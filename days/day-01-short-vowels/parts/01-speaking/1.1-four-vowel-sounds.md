## One-line answer

Keep the beginning and end of bit, bet, bat and but alike; change the sound in the middle.

## The story

Imagine meeting a neighbour outside your home. Their child has a cricket bat. You try to say
bat, but it comes out like bet. You try again, louder. The middle still sounds the same.
The neighbour may guess from the thing in the child's hand. On the phone, that clue would be
gone. Changing the middle sound helps more than adding force.

This is an imagined practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

A **vowel sound** is a voice sound made with air flowing through your mouth without a full
closure or a narrow, noisy squeeze. Your tongue and lips help shape it. Today you learn four
of these sounds. **Short vowel** is the teaching name for this group; do not try to make all
four sound alike by cutting them off.

The definition was checked in [vowel](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/vowel)
on 2026-09-18.

The marks between slashes below are **sound symbols**: marks that tell you which sound to
make. You can use the example words first. You do not need to learn a whole set of symbols.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 speaking check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) asks for a
spoken introduction a stranger can follow. Distinct words help that stranger follow you
without seeing what you are pointing at.

## The rule

Use the British pronunciation buttons on the four linked entries if you want to hear a model.
Listen to one word, then say it with your phone's voice recorder on. The text of the entries
was checked on 2026-09-18; the audio was not evaluated during lesson preparation.

| Word | Sounds | Meaning here | Mouth cue to try |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [bit](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bit_1) | /bɪt/ | A small amount | Keep the tongue fairly high and towards the front. |
| [bet](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bet_1) | /bet/ | Risk money on a result | Try a lower tongue position than for bit. |
| [bat](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bat_1) | /bæt/ | The thing used to hit a cricket ball | Open the mouth more than for bet; keep the tongue low towards the front. |
| [but](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/but_1) | /bʌt/ | A word joining contrasting ideas | Try a low, more central tongue position, with relaxed lips. |

These are starting cues, not exact measurements. Use a mirror to notice your mouth opening.
Keep your lips from forming a tight round shape. Use the sound you hear as the target.

This simple map shows the direction to try, not a measured picture of a mouth:

| Tongue height | Towards the front | More towards the middle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Higher | bit | |
| Lower | bet | |
| Low | bat | but |

Say the words as one practice row:

> bit — bet — bat — but

Then change the order. Keep the beginning /b/ and ending /t/ as alike as you can. That lets
you listen for the middle. Mouth-position guidance draws on
[IPA Vowel symbols](https://www.mq.edu.au/faculty-of-medicine-health-and-human-sciences/departments-and-schools/department-of-linguistics/our-research/phonetics-and-phonology/speech/phonetics-and-phonology/transcription/ipa-vowel-symbols)
and [Vowels](https://teaching.ncl.ac.uk/ipa/vowels.html), checked 2026-09-18. The map is a
practice aid, not a claim that every speaker uses the same position.

## When it breaks

Deliberate wrong version, if you meant bat:

> bet

That is another English word. A listener may hear that word or ask you to repeat it. Try
opening your mouth more and lowering the tongue, then say bat again. Shouting bet does not
turn it into bat. If all four recordings sound the same, the check is RED: return to one
pair and compare it with the linked models.

## In real life

A listener can often use the rest of a message to guess a word. A name or an isolated word
gives them less help. Aim for words they can recognise; you do not need to remove your accent.

The entry for but lists both /bʌt/ and /bət/. Today use /bʌt/ when saying the word by itself.
The other form occurs in speech too. Do not mark every different-sounding but as a mistake.
Your own name may contain none of today's four sounds. Keep its own pronunciation.
