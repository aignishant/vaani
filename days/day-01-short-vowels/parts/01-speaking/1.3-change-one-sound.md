## One-line answer

Make the wrong middle sound on purpose, hear the change, then record the word you meant.

## The story

Imagine your neighbour repeats a word back to you, but it is not the word you meant.
You nod because stopping feels awkward. Now you have agreed to the wrong word. A small
correction would let both of you continue with the same meaning.

This is an imagined situation. Today's wrong version is a deliberate exercise, not a
mistake already found in your recording.

## The idea in plain language

A **repair** is a small correction that helps someone understand what you meant. Here you
repair one word. Review the [vowel sounds and mouth map](1.1-four-vowel-sounds.md) if the
four words are new to you. [Your name aloud](1.2-your-name-aloud.md) shows how to record a
message about yourself.

A useful check must be able to say RED. Here RED means the intended word cannot be told
apart from the word you deliberately substituted. GREEN means the difference is audible
on playback and the corrected word matches the target you were trying to say.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 speaking check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), a stranger
may ask you to repeat a word. Practising a repair gives you a way to continue without
throwing away your whole introduction.

## The rule

Use a pair that you found hard. Here is an example with bat as the intended word:

> bat — bet — bat

The middle word is the deliberate wrong version. The last word is the repair. Keep the
beginning and ending alike so the middle is the part being tested. For mouth cues, use
[part 1.1](1.1-four-vowel-sounds.md).

1. Record your chosen word, the other word, and your chosen word again.
2. Play the recording without looking at the word row. Can you hear which one changed?
3. Compare the repaired word with its linked pronunciation model in part 1.1.
4. If the words merge together, mark this check RED. Try the mouth cue for one pair and
   record it again. If you are unsure, keep it as needing another try.
5. Finish with a fresh greeting using your own name. Keep the final greeting clear of the
   deliberate wrong version so it can stand alone as your introduction.

For a stronger check, record both bat–bet–bat and bat–bat–bat, then replay them in a changed
order without looking at their labels. One row should contain a change; the other should not.
Hearing three different sounds where you intended three identical words needs another try too.

The words and their contrasting sounds were checked on 2026-09-18 in
[bat](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bat_1) and
[bet](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bet_1).

## When it breaks

You meant bat, bet, bat, but your recording sounds like:

> bet — bet — bet

Saying the row three times has not passed the check. The difference is still missing.
Try just bat and bet with the mouth change from part 1.1. Then return to the row.
Do not log a successful repair until you can hear it.

## In real life

In a conversation you do not announce RED or GREEN. You repeat the unclear word and check
whether the other person understood. You can stay calm and keep the correction small.
The other person's answer is more useful evidence than how smooth the speech felt to you.

An accent difference alone is not a failed conversation. Ask whether the listener caught
the word. This exercise holds the surrounding sounds steady to make the change easier to
notice; a real conversation supplies more clues and more distractions.
