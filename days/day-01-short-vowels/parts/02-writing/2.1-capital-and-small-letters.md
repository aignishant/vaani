## One-line answer

Each of the 26 English letters has a capital form and a small form.

## The story

Imagine putting your name on a notebook. A name on the cover uses big, straight shapes,
but the sample name inside looks different. You try shrinking the cover's shapes to match
it. Some still look different. You need to learn which two shapes belong to the same letter.

This is a practice scene, not a mistake reported from your page.

## The idea in plain language

A **letter** is a written sign used to make words. The **alphabet** is the ordered set of
letters used in a language. English has 26.

A **capital letter** is the form shown in the left column below. A **small letter** is its
partner in the right column. **Case** means this choice between capital and small forms.
Capital is also called uppercase; small is also called lowercase.

“Small” names a form, not the size of your handwriting. You can draw a small-letter form
very large and it is still that form.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 written page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) needs readable

## The rule

Read across each row. The two signs are partners. Read down the table for the order.

| Capital | Small | Capital | Small |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | a | N | n |
| B | b | O | o |
| C | c | P | p |
| D | d | Q | q |
| E | e | R | r |
| F | f | S | s |
| G | g | T | t |
| H | h | U | u |
| I | i | V | v |
| J | j | W | w |
| K | k | X | x |
| L | l | Y | y |
| M | m | Z | z |

The first pair of columns runs A to M. The next pair continues N to Z. Point to A and then
a. Notice that their shapes differ. Now point to C and c. Their shapes are alike, but they
are still different forms.

Shape map:

> A ↔ a: same letter, different forms.
>
> D ↔ d: same letter, different forms.
>
> B ↔ b: same letter, different forms.

Copy each pair once to make your own reference. Separate the pairs with spaces. Then use
the table to find the letters in your own name. A **spelling** is the letters in a word or
name in their order. Keep that order when you write your name.

Checked 2026-09-18: [English Alphabet](https://www.worldometers.info/languages/english-alphabet/)
for all 26 capital/small pairs. This lesson uses only the letter table, not its sound column.

## When it breaks

A deliberately wrong partner for D:

> D → b

The reader sees a different letter. Shrinking D does not create d either. Look back at the
correct pair and write d beside D. For these small letters, notice the side with the tall
line: b has it on the left; d has it on the right. Compare them directly: **b | d**.

This is a constructed exercise, not a claim that you confuse those letters.

## In real life

Letters on a phone and letters in a notebook can look different. Use clear, separate shapes
while learning. Fancy handwriting makes a weak match harder to spot. If you cannot tell
which letter a shape is, compare it with your reference instead of guessing from the word.

A reader may work out a familiar word despite a poor letter. Your name may be new to them,
so give them clear shapes and the correct order. A neatly drawn wrong letter is still the
wrong letter.
