## One-line answer

Write your name with its usual spelling and capitals, keeping a space between separate names.

## The story

Imagine making a name label inside your notebook. You fit every letter into one long run.
It saves space, but someone returning the notebook cannot see where one name ends and the
next begins. Making the whole label bigger does not create that boundary. Leaving a gap does.

This is an imagined example. Your own name may be one word, and that is fine.

## The idea in plain language

Use the [capital and small forms](2.1-capital-and-small-letters.md) to make a label that
another person can read. The label should carry your name, not the model person's name.

A **space** is a blank gap between written words. On paper, leave a visible gap. When
typing, use the space key. Inside each name, keep the letters in their spelling order.

## Why Vaani needs it

Your [day 20 page about yourself](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) must
make sense to someone else. Your name is the first piece of that page which belongs to you.
Copying the example person's identity would not build your page.

## The rule

For an ordinary name such as the example below, start each separate name with a capital and
use small forms for the rest. Keep your own name's established spelling and case if it has
a different pattern. Names are not all built the same way.

> Priya Rao

Here is the layout of that model:

| First name | Gap | Next name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P**riya | space | **R**ao |
| Capital first | blank | Capital first |

Look at the capital P and R. The remaining forms are small. If your name has one word,
write one word. If it has more, preserve its own order and spaces. Do not add a second name
just to copy the model.

Write your own name once while checking your usual spelling. Cover it. Write it again from
memory. Compare the two, letter by letter, from left to right. Correct any missed or changed
letter before trying again.

Now put your own name into this frame:

> My name is … .

Replace the dots with your name. The line tells a reader who you are. Keep the final dot
beside the last letter, with no extra space. The full model looks like this:

> My name is Priya Rao.

The model shows the shape only. Your page must use your name. Keep your alphabet reference
and these lines together on one page, on paper or in `lab/writing/day-01.md`.

Checked 2026-09-18: [Capital letters and apostrophes](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/capital-letters-apostrophes)
for capitals in personal names; [name](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/name_1)
for the “My name is” frame. Letter forms were checked in
[English Alphabet](https://www.worldometers.info/languages/english-alphabet/).

## When it breaks

Constructed wrong version for the model name:

> priya rao

The spelling is still recognisable, but its starting letters lack the usual name capitals.
Change just those starting forms to P and R. Do not turn every letter into a capital to
hide the missing choice.

Another constructed problem is PriyaRao: both capitals are present, but the gap is missing.
Restore the gap for this two-word name. Check your own name's pattern before changing it.

## In real life

A friend might understand a name typed entirely in small letters. A page meant for a formal
reader should use the person's established name spelling and case. Do not assume a casual
message is a model for every kind of page.

Some forms ask for all capitals. Follow that instruction on that form. It is a different
instruction from today's ordinary notebook label. A signature can be decorative; for this
exercise, give a stranger letters they can read.
