## §1 Where we are

You call a friend after a trip. One word comes out too short, and she hears a different word.
The speaking folder is about holding a sound long enough to be the word you meant. Think of a
long vowel as a note you hold, not a note you tap.

A parcel is coming to your home, and the delivery person cannot read the box. The writing
folder is about the small list that is your address, and the numbers that go with it.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

label with your address on it, so you can copy the order your country uses. Have your phone
handy to copy your own number from the screen.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** Play the recording. Is sheep longer than ship? Is the deliberate short middle in
[part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-cut-it-short-on-purpose.md) audible as a change? If all three
words in the row sound alike, the check is RED. Go back to one pair and try again.

**Writing:** Cover your correct address. Read the deliberately broken one in
[part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-a-form-nobody-can-use.md) with your finger under each word. Did
you find all three breaks? If you marked a broken line GREEN, the check is RED. Run it again.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Making the long vowel loud instead of long. Loud does not change the word. Length does.
- Reading the sound symbol instead of pressing the sound button. Listen first, then say.
- Writing the phone number as one block of ten digits. Groups are what make it checkable.
- The spellings four, forty and ninety. Part 2.2 has them side by side.
- Reading your own address from memory instead of from the page. Use your finger.
