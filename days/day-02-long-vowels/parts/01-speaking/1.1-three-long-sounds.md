## One-line answer

Some English vowel sounds are held longer. Beat, boot and bought each hold a long sound in the middle.

## The story

You call a friend after a trip. You say, "We saw a sheep." But the middle sound is short. Your
friend hears "ship". She asks, "A ship? Near the hills?" You say it again, louder. She still
hears "ship". Loud does not help. Long helps. You hold the middle sound a little longer. Now she
hears "sheep".

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **hold** — keep a sound going, not stop it fast.
- **stretch** — make a sound longer.
- **lips** — the two soft parts around your mouth.
- **round** — make your lips into a small circle, like for a kiss.

A **vowel sound** is a sound made with open air in your mouth. You met four of them on
[day 1](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/01-speaking/1.1-four-vowel-sounds.md). Those four
were short. Today you learn three **long vowels**. A long vowel is a vowel sound you hold a
little longer. The **sound symbol** for a long vowel has two dots after it, like this: /iː/.
A sound symbol is a mark that tells you which sound to make. The dots mean "hold it".

Long does not mean loud. It means the sound goes on a little more.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 speaking check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), a stranger
must follow your ten sentences. Many everyday words only differ by a long sound. If every
vowel is short, "sheep" and "ship" become one word. The stranger has to guess.

## The rule

Open each linked word on your phone. Press the sound button. Listen. Then say the word with
your voice recorder on. Play both back. These pages were opened on 2026-09-22; the text was
checked, and each page carries a sound button.

| Word | Sound | Meaning here | Mouth cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [beat](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/beat_1) | /biːt/ | hit many times | Smile a little. Hold the "ee". |
| [boot](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/boot_1) | /buːt/ | a tall shoe | Round your lips and push them forward. Hold the "oo". |
| [bought](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bought) | /bɔːt/ | paid money for something | Round your lips, open your mouth more. Hold the "aw". |

Use a mirror. Look at your lips:

| Sound | What the mirror shows |
| --- | --- |
| /iː/ as in beat | lips wide, like a small smile |
| /uː/ as in boot | lips round and pushed forward |
| /ɔː/ as in bought | lips round, mouth more open |

Say the three words in a row. Hold the middle of each one:

> beat — boot — bought

Now say each word two ways. First short and quick. Then long, holding the middle. The long one
is the target.

You can also watch the mouth on video. The free series
[The Sounds of English](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/pronunciation)
has one short video for each sound. Open the page and choose the "Long Vowels" videos. The
page was opened on 2026-09-22. Each video names its sound on screen, not in the page text, so
find the one that shows /iː/, /uː/ or /ɔː/.

## When it breaks

You mean beat, but you cut the sound short:

> bit

That is a different English word. You met it on day 1. The listener hears "bit". Loudness does
not fix it. The fix is small: hold the "ee" a little longer, with a small smile. Say beat again.

If your three long words sound the same as three short words on playback, mark this check RED.
Go back to one pair. Listen to the model. Try again.

## In real life

Fast speakers still hold long vowels. They shorten other things, not these. So a long vowel
is not "slow talking". It is part of the word.

A listener uses the rest of your sentence to guess. "I bought a boot" makes sense. "I bit a
bit" does not. So a wrong length inside a full sentence is often forgiven. A wrong length in a
single word, or a name, is not. Give the long sound its time when the word stands alone.

Your accent can stay. Only the length needs to be there.
