## One-line answer

Say the long word with a short middle on purpose, hear the other word appear, then say it long again.

## The story

You tell your friend on the phone, "I saw a ship." You meant sheep. She says, "A ship? Where?"
You feel silly. You say, "No, no — sheep!" This time you hold the sound. She laughs. "Oh,
sheep." The talk goes on. A small repair fixed it. Nobody needed to start again.

This is an imagined scene. Today's wrong version is a deliberate exercise. It rests on what
beginners often do, not on a mistake found in your own recording.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **repair** — a small fix that helps someone understand you. You met this on
  [day 1](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/01-speaking/1.3-change-one-sound.md).
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check failed. GREEN means it passed.

You know the three long sounds from [part 1.1](1.1-three-long-sounds.md). You put them next
to their short partners in [part 1.2](1.2-long-and-short-in-your-words.md). Now you break one
on purpose. You listen to the break. Then you fix it. A check that can go RED is a real check.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), a stranger may say
"Sorry?" You will need to repair one word without starting your whole talk again. Practising
the repair now makes it small and calm later.

## The rule

Pick the pair you found hardest in part 1.2. Here is the shape with sheep:

> sheep — ship — sheep

The middle word is the deliberate wrong version. The last word is the repair.

1. Record the row. Long, short, long.
2. Play it back without looking at the words. Can you hear the middle one change?
3. Now record the row with all three long: sheep — sheep — sheep.
4. Play both rows. One should have a change. One should not. If you cannot tell which is
   which, the check is RED.
5. Finish with your own true sentence from part 1.2, said with the long sound held.

The model words were checked on 2026-09-22 in
[sheep](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/sheep) and
[ship](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/ship). Both pages carry a
sound button.

## When it breaks

You meant sheep — ship — sheep, but the playback sounds like:

> ship — ship — ship

The change never came. Every middle was short. Saying it three more times will not help. Go
back to the mirror. Smile a little for the "ee". Hold it. Say only sheep until it is long.
Then try the row again.

Also RED: sheep — sheep — sheep, when you meant to put a short one in the middle. That means
you cannot yet make the short one on purpose. Both directions matter.

## In real life

Nobody says RED or GREEN in a conversation. You hear the wrong word come back to you, and you
say the right one, a little longer, with a small "no, sorry". Then you go on. That is the whole
repair.

Under pressure, in a fast talk, people shorten everything. Long vowels are the ones to keep.
A native listener will forgive most things. A "ship" that should be "sheep" they will notice,
and they may make a joke. That is fine. Make the repair and keep going.
