## One-line answer

An address is written as short lines, one part per line, with a capital letter on every place name.

## The story

A parcel is coming to your home. The delivery person calls. "Where is this? I can't read it."
The address on the box is one long run of words. Nothing shows where the house number ends and
the street begins. The city name starts with a small letter, so it looks like any other word.
He stands on the wrong road, reading it again and again.

This is a practice scene. We have not seen your handwriting.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **address** — the words and numbers that say where you live, so post can reach you.
- **line** — one row of writing. A new line starts under the last one.
- **digit** — one of the number signs 0 to 9.
- **postal code** — the number your area uses for post. Different countries name it differently.

An **address** is a small list. Each part of it goes on its own **line**. The reader's eye
moves down the list and finds each part. You need the
[capital and small letters](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.1-capital-and-small-letters.md)
from day 1: a **capital letter** is the big form, such as A. Place names start with a capital.

House numbers and postal codes are written in **digits**, not in words.

The word address was checked on 2026-09-22 in
[address](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/address_1): "details of
where somebody lives or works and where letters, etc. can be sent".

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 page about yourself](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) says where
you live. Every form you ever fill in asks for this. If the reader cannot find the city in your
address, the rest of the page has already lost them.

## The rule

Write your address from the smallest part to the biggest. One part per line. Here is the
shape. This person and this place are not real; the layout is the point:

> 14, Rose Lane
> Shivaji Nagar
> Pune 411005
> India

Look at each line:

| Line | What it carries | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | house or flat number, then the street | number in digits; a capital on Rose and on Lane |
| 2 | the area or locality | a capital on each word of the name |
| 3 | the city, then the postal code | a capital on the city; the code in digits |
| 4 | the country | a capital on the country |

The rule for capitals on places was checked on 2026-09-22 in
[Capital letters and apostrophes](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/capital-letters-apostrophes):
"We capitalise the names of people and places, including streets, planets, continents and
countries."

Copy your own address the way it appears on a letter or a bill you have received. If the
order in your country is different, keep your country's order. The rule that does not change
is: one part per line, digits for numbers, a capital on every place name.

## When it breaks

A deliberately wrong version of the model address:

> 14 rose lane shivaji nagar pune 411005 india

All the parts are there. But there are no lines, and there are no capitals. The reader cannot
see where one part ends. The city looks like an ordinary word. The smallest fix has two steps.
First, break it into lines, one part each. Second, put a capital on Rose, Lane, Shivaji,
Nagar, Pune and India.

## In real life

On a form, the lines are printed for you. You fill each box. On an envelope or a parcel, you
make the lines yourself. A delivery person reads addresses all day and reads them fast. Lines
and capitals are what they scan for.

A friend who knows your home can read a messy address. A stranger with fifty parcels cannot.
Some forms ask for everything in capital letters. Follow the form's instruction there. On a
plain page, use the layout above.
