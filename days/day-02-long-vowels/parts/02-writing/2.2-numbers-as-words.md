## One-line answer

A phone number is written in digits; a small number in a sentence is written as a word, with its own spelling.

## The story

The parcel form has a box for your phone number. You write it as one long run of ten digits.
The delivery person misreads one digit and calls a stranger. Under it, the form asks "How many
parcels?" You write "tow". He reads it twice. Is it two? Is it ten? He guesses.

This is a practice scene. It is not a report of your page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **phone number** — the digits people press to call you.
- **group** — a few digits kept together, with a space before the next few.
- **number word** — a number written with letters, like two, not 2.
- **hyphen** — a short line between two words that join, like this: twenty-one.

You met **digits** in [part 2.1](2.1-your-address-line-by-line.md): the signs 0 to 9. A phone
number is always digits. A small number inside a sentence is usually a word. Each number word
has its own **spelling**, which you met on
[day 1](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.1-capital-and-small-letters.md): the
letters in their order. The spelling of a number word is fixed. You cannot guess it from the
sound.

## The rule

**Your phone number.** Write it in digits, in small groups, with a space between groups. The
groups make it easy to read aloud and easy to check. This number is not real:

> 98765 43210

Copy your own number from your phone. Then read it back one digit at a time against the
screen.

Three spellings trap almost everyone. Look at them closely:

| Digits | Right | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | four | has a u |
| 40 | forty | has no u |
| 90 | ninety | keeps the e from nine |

For 21 to 99, join the tens word and the units word with a hyphen: twenty-one, forty-five,
ninety-nine. The dictionary's own page for
[ninety](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/ninety) writes
"ninety-nine" with the hyphen, checked 2026-09-22.

Now put a number word into a true sentence. The shape, with a person who is not you:

> There are four people in my home.

## When it breaks

A deliberately wrong version:

> There are fourty people in my home.

The word fourty is not in the dictionary. A reader knows what you mean and also knows the
spelling is wrong. The smallest fix is to take out the u: forty. Compare the two words letter
by letter.

Another wrong version is a phone number as words: nine eight seven six five four three two
one zero. It is correct English and nobody will use it. Digits, in groups.

## In real life

Forms want digits. Sentences want words for small numbers. Big numbers in a sentence, like a
price or a year, usually stay as digits. When you are not sure, ask: is a reader going to copy
this number? If yes, digits. If they are just reading, a word is fine.

Under pressure, on a form at a counter, people write the phone number as one block and drop a
digit. Groups catch that. Count your groups. Two groups of five, or three groups, whatever
your country uses. Then count the digits.
