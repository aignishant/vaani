## One-line answer

Write your address and a number wrong on purpose, read it as a stranger would, mark what fails, then fix only that.

## The story

You fill in the parcel form and hand it over. You read it once before you let go. Your eye
slides over the address. It is your address. You know it. So you do not see that the city is
missing its capital, and that the number of parcels says "tow". The delivery person sees both
at once. He does not know your address. That is why he sees it.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on what beginners
often do, not on a mistake found in your own page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **read back** — read your own page again, slowly, as if it were someone else's.
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You wrote your address in [part 2.1](2.1-your-address-line-by-line.md) and your numbers in
[part 2.2](2.2-numbers-as-words.md). Now you break both on purpose. Then you read back and
point at each thing a stranger would need. If you find the break, the check works. If you
miss it, the check is RED, and you learn where your eye slides.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) is read by a stranger. You
will read it back before you hand it over. A read-back that cannot find a planted mistake will
not find a real one either.

## The rule

Work on a fresh line under your correct address and sentences.

1. Copy your address again, but make two deliberate changes: run two lines into one, and
   change one place-name capital to a small letter.
2. Copy one number sentence again, but change the number word to a wrong spelling.
3. Cover the correct versions. Now read the wrong versions as a stranger. Put your finger on
   each line. Ask: where does this part end? Does this name start with a capital? Is this
   number word in the table?
4. Mark each thing you find RED. There should be three.
5. Uncover the correct versions. Fix only the RED things, on a new line. Mark each GREEN.

The model, with the changes shown:

> 14, Rose Lane Shivaji Nagar
> pune 411005
> India

> There are fourty people in my home.

Three RED marks: the joined line, the small p on pune, the u in fourty.

The place-capital rule was checked on 2026-09-22 in
[Capital letters and apostrophes](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/capital-letters-apostrophes).
The spelling forty was checked in
[forty](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/forty).

## When it breaks

You read the wrong version back and mark it GREEN:

> 14, Rose Lane Shivaji Nagar — GREEN

Your eye read the address you know, not the line on the page. The check itself failed. Do it
again with your finger under each word. Say each part out loud: "house number. street. area."
When you say "street" and "area" for the same line, you have found it.

Another break: you fix the capital on pune, and while fixing it you also change 411005 to
something else. A fix should touch one thing. Compare every digit with your correct version.

## In real life

Nobody plants mistakes on a real form. But every real form is read fast by someone who does not
know you, and that reader finds what you did not. The read-back with a finger is how you become
that reader for a moment.

A friend forgives a small p on a city. A courier, a bank, an office does not: the form comes
back, or the parcel goes to the wrong place. Keep the wrong version on your practice page. It
is proof that your check can go RED.
