# Talking over someone on purpose

`PR-02`, `FL-01` · Day 3

## The one-line answer

Shadowing is repeating a speaker out loud while they are still talking, a beat behind, without
pausing them and without understanding everything — and it is the single highest-return thing
you will do in this course.

## The model

Every other way of practising pronunciation has the same flaw: you get to think first. You read
the word, you assemble the sounds, you produce it. That is a fine way to learn a sound and a
useless way to learn to speak, because in a real conversation there is no gap in which to
assemble anything.

Shadowing removes the gap. The audio does not wait for you. You start about a word behind the
speaker and stay there, and if you fall further behind you do not stop and catch up — you drop
what you missed and rejoin at the next word you catch.

That dropping is not a failure of the exercise. **It is the exercise.** You are training the
thing that fails in every real conversation: continuing without complete information.

Two things happen that nothing else produces.

Your mouth learns the rhythm before your brain approves it. English rhythm — which syllables get
hit, where words run together — is copied physically, the way you copy a dance step, and it goes
in far faster that way than through any rule.

And your tolerance for not-understanding goes up. On Day 40 someone will interrupt you and you
will keep going, and the reason will be that you spent seventy mornings not stopping.

Choosing a clip. It should be **speech, not singing**, at natural speed, with one speaker you
can hear clearly. Something under a minute. Something you find slightly interesting, because you
are going to hear it many times.

> Do not slow it down. Slowed audio has different rhythm, and rhythm is the thing you came for.

**Today's phrases** — all of these go into `docs/CHUNKS.md` tonight.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| Sorry, I missed that last bit. | You lost part of what they said and want it again |
| Say that again? | The short casual version, among friends |
| Hang on, let me catch up. | You are behind and buying two seconds |
| I got the first part, not the rest. | Precise repair — tells them exactly where to restart |

Those four are not decoration. They are the spoken form of what shadowing teaches: you fell
behind, and you rejoined without stopping the conversation.

## Say it now

One clip, four passes. Same clip every time.

1. **Listen only.** No speaking. Just hear where the speaker pauses and which words they hit
   hardest.
2. **Mumble along.** Do not form words. Hum the rhythm at the right pitch, mouth closed. This
   feels ridiculous and it is the most important pass.
3. **Shadow full.** Speak it, a beat behind, out loud, at full volume. When you fall off, drop
   what you missed and rejoin at the next word you catch. **Do not pause the audio. Ever.**
4. **Shadow again.** The same clip. You will be measurably closer behind than in pass three, and
   noticing that is the point of doing it twice.

Full volume matters. Shadowing under your breath trains a mouth that only works under your
breath.

Two takes, back to back, in the same file. Do not stop between them.

> **Take one:** shadow the clip one more time — this take is just the shadowing, recorded.
> **Take two:** with the audio off, say what the clip was about, in your own words, as much as

Take two will be shorter than you expect and will have gaps. Leave the gaps in. On Day 44 you

- **Solo form:** as written.
- **With a partner:** they listen to take two without having heard the clip, then tell you what
  they think it was about. The distance between their version and the real one is your evidence.

## The deliberate failure

Shadow the clip once more, but this time **try to understand every word as you go.** Actively
translate. Do not let anything past you.

**What breaking sounds like:** you last about eight seconds. Then you stop — not fall behind,
*stop* — and the audio carries on without you, and there is a small feeling of panic.

That feeling is exactly what happens on a phone call in English. It is not caused by weak
listening. It is caused by refusing to proceed without full comprehension. Shadowing is the drill
that cures it, and you just watched it break so you know what you are curing.

## When it breaks in the wild

Someone says a sentence with one unknown word in the middle. You stop to work out that word. By
the time you have it, they have said two more sentences and you have heard none of them — so now
you have lost the whole turn instead of one word.

**The repair:**

> I got the first part, not the rest — say that again?

Specific beats general. "Sorry?" makes them repeat everything and teaches you nothing. Naming
where you fell off gets you a targeted repeat and tells them you were listening.
