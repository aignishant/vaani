## One-line answer

Run your three sentences together on purpose, read them back as a stranger, mark where the reader stumbles, then put the marks back.

## The story

You write the note and read it back. It looks fine. It is your note; you know what it says.
Then you imagine you are the other person, reading it cold, standing at the table with a bag
of shopping. You read it out loud in their voice. At "six I made" you stumble. That stumble
is the missing full stop, and you only found it by pretending to be someone else.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on what beginners
often write, not on a mistake found in your own page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **stumble** — stop or trip while reading, because something is not clear.
- **read back** — read your own page again, slowly, as if it were someone else's.
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You wrote three sentences in [part 2.2](2.2-three-sentences-about-today.md). Now you write
them again as one run-on, with no full stops and only one capital. Then you read back out
loud, as a stranger. Every stumble is a RED mark. Then you fix only what is marked.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) is checked by reading
back. If your read-back cannot find three missing full stops that you put there yourself, it
will not find the one you did not mean to leave out.

## The rule

Work under your three correct sentences.

1. Copy them as one line. Take out both full stops in the middle. Make the two middle capitals
   small. Leave one capital at the start and one full stop at the end.
2. Cover the correct version.
3. Read the run-on out loud, in the voice of someone who has never seen it. Read at normal
   speed. Every time you stumble or have to go back, mark the spot RED.
4. You should find two RED spots, one for each missing full stop.
5. Uncover the correct version. On a new line, write the three sentences again, with all
   three capitals and three full stops. Mark it GREEN.

The model, broken on purpose:

> I got up at six i made tea for my mother the bus was late.

Two RED spots: between six and i, and between mother and the. The lost capital on i is a
third thing to mark.

The marks were checked on 2026-09-22 in
[sentence](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/sentence_1) and
[full stop](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/full-stop_2).

## When it breaks

You read the run-on back and do not stumble:

> I got up at six i made tea for my mother the bus was late. — GREEN

You read it in your own voice, knowing what it says. The check itself failed. Do it again
with your finger under each word and out loud. At "six i", say the two words and ask: is
"six i made" one thing?

Another break: you fix it and add a full stop after every word or two:

> I got up. At six. I made tea.

Now the stops cut one thing into pieces. "At six" is not a complete thing. A full stop goes
after a complete thing, not after every few words.

## In real life

Nobody leaves out full stops on purpose in a real note. But everyone leaves one out by
accident, and it is always the reader who finds it. Reading your own page out loud in a
stranger's voice is the closest you can get to being that reader before you hand it over.

A friend will phone you and ask what you meant. An office will not phone; the form comes
back, or it is read wrongly and acted on. Keep the broken note on your practice page as proof
that your read-back can go RED.

## Check yourself

- **Write:** `TODO(me)` Add your deliberately run-on note, your RED marks, and the fixed
  three sentences marked GREEN to your page. Note what you actually changed. Label it
  deliberate when you add it to the error ledger.
- **Say out loud:** Where did you stumble on the read-back, and what was missing there?
