## One-line answer

Say v as w and w as v on purpose, hear the wrong words, then put the teeth and the lips back where they go.

## The story

You leave the clothes shop with the vest. Outside it is raining. You say to your friend,
"It's wery vet." She laughs, not unkindly. "Very wet," she says. You say it back with your
teeth on your lip for very and round lips for wet. It feels strange and slow. It sounds right.
The strange feeling is the sign that something changed.

This is an imagined scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on a swap many
beginners make, not on a mistake found in your own recording.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **swap** — put each thing in the other's place.
- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **repair** — a small fix that helps someone understand you. You met it on
  [day 1](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/01-speaking/1.3-change-one-sound.md).
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check failed. GREEN means it passed.

You made /v/ in [part 1.1](1.1-v-teeth-on-the-lip.md) and /w/ in
[part 1.2](1.2-w-round-lips-no-teeth.md). Now you swap them on purpose in one short phrase.
You listen for the swap. Then you say the phrase right. If you can swap on purpose, you can
hear the swap when it happens by accident.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), "very" and "we"
will be everywhere. A swap you cannot hear is a swap you will make on the day.

## The rule

The phrase is very wet. The deliberate wrong version is wery vet. The row is:

> very wet — wery vet — very wet

1. Mirror in front of you. Record the row. Right, wrong, right.
2. On the wrong one, do the swap fully: round lips and no teeth on wery, teeth on the lip
   on vet.
3. Play it back without looking. Can you hear the middle phrase change?
4. Record the row with all three right. Play both rows. One changes; one does not. If you
   cannot tell which is which, the check is RED.
5. Finish with your own true "We …" and "very …" sentences from part 1.2, said right.

The model words were checked on 2026-09-22 in
[very](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/very_1) and
[wet](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/wet_1). Each page has a
sound button.

## When it breaks

You meant very wet — wery vet — very wet, but the playback is:

> wery vet — wery vet — wery vet

The swap never came back. All three are wrong. This is common when v and w are one sound in
your first language: the mouth makes its own sound for both. Go back to the mirror. Say only
very, watching for teeth on the lip. Say only wet, watching for round lips and no teeth. Then
the row.

Another break: all three come out right, and you could not make the wrong one on purpose.
That is a good sign for your v and w, but the check still needs a change to hear. Try again,
slower, and make the swap big.

## In real life

Nobody will correct your v and w in a conversation. Friends get used to it. So the recording
is the only place you will hear it, and the first sign you are improving is that "wery"
starts to sound wrong to you on playback. Before that, it sounds normal.

Under pressure, most people slide back to the sound their first language has. That is fine
in a fast chat. On a name, an address or a single word said alone, take the time: teeth for
v, round lips for w.
