## One-line answer

A few common nouns do not take -s; their plural is a different word you learn by heart: men, women, children, people, feet.

## The story

Still packing for the train. You write a note for the ticket office: "4 persons: 2 mans,
1 womans, 1 childs." The clerk understands. She also smiles, because every word on the note
is one that English changes in a different way. Four people. Two men. One woman. One child.
No -s anywhere.

This is a practice scene, not a report of your page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **irregular** — not following the usual rule.
- **learn by heart** — remember a thing exactly, because there is no rule to work it out.
- **list** — a set of things written one under the other.

In [part 2.1](2.1-one-and-many-add-s.md) you added -s, -es or -ies to make a **plural**. That
works for almost every **noun**. A few nouns are **irregular**: their plural is a different
word. There is no rule to make it. You learn each pair by heart. The good news is that the
irregular ones are the words you use most, so you will see them every day.

## Why Vaani needs it

On the [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) you will write about
the people in your family, and about children if you have them. "Two childs" and "three
persons" are the first things a reader's eye stops on.

## The rule

Five pairs. Cover the right column and say the plural. Then uncover and check. All five were
checked on 2026-09-22 in
[Count nouns](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/count-nouns),
which lists them as "some common nouns have irregular plurals".

| One | More than one |
| --- | --- |
| man | men |
| woman | women |
| child | children |
| person | people |
| foot | feet |

The shape, in sentences about a family that is not yours:

> There are five people in my family.

> I have two children.

Notice: no -s on people, no -s on children. The number in front does the counting; the noun
changes its whole shape.

Now write your own. Count the people in your home. Write the number as a word, from
[day 2](../../../day-02-long-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.2-numbers-as-words.md), and the noun
in its plural.

## When it breaks

The note from the story:

> 4 persons: 2 mans, 1 womans, 1 childs

Persons is used in some official writing, but the everyday plural is people. Mans is not a
word. Womans is not a word. Childs is not a word. The fix is to replace each with its pair
from the table: people, men, woman (one, so singular), child (one, so singular).

A second break, very common:

> two childrens

Children is already the plural. Adding -s makes a word that does not exist. If the word is in
the right column of the table, it is finished; do not add anything.

## In real life

These five come up in nearly every conversation about family, work or a crowd. A reader
sees "childs" or "peoples" and knows at once. They understand you; they also know you are
new. Learning five pairs by heart removes that in a single day.

A note about people: a sign or a form may say "persons", as in "4 persons". That is a formal
use. In your own sentences, use people.
