## One-line answer

Write your list with every plural wrong on purpose, read it as the person packing, mark each wrong ending, then fix only those.

## The story

You hand your brother the packing list and watch him read it. He reads "2 bag" and picks up
one bag. He reads "3 childs" and frowns. You see, from his face, every ending you got wrong.
You did not see them when you wrote the list, because you knew what you meant. He did not
know, and so he saw the words.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on endings beginners
often get wrong, not on a mistake found in your own page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **read back** — read your own page again, slowly, as if it were someone else's.
- **ending** — the last letter or letters of a word.
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You made regular plurals in [part 2.1](2.1-one-and-many-add-s.md) and irregular ones in
[part 2.2](2.2-the-ones-that-break-it.md). Now you write a list with every plural wrong, in
three different ways. Then you read it back as the person who has to act on it. Each wrong
ending is a RED mark. Then you fix only what is marked.

## The rule

Under your correct list and sentences from parts 2.1 and 2.2, write a new list with three
deliberate breaks:

1. One regular noun with no -s after a number bigger than one.
2. One noun that needs -es or -ies, given plain -s.
3. One irregular noun given an -s, or an irregular plural given an extra -s.

Then:

4. Cover the correct versions.
5. Read the broken list as the person packing. Put your finger on each noun. Look at the
   number in front. Look at the ending. Ask: does the ending match the number? Is this noun
   in the irregular table?
6. Mark each wrong ending RED. There should be three.
7. Uncover. Fix only the three, on a new line, and mark it GREEN.

The model, broken on purpose:

> 2 bag, 3 boxs, 4 childrens

Three RED marks: bag needs -s, box needs -es, children needs nothing.

The endings were checked on 2026-09-22 in
[Count nouns](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/count-nouns).

## When it breaks

You read the broken list back and mark it GREEN:

> 2 bag, 3 boxs, 4 childrens — GREEN

Your eye read the numbers and stopped. The check itself failed. Do it again, finger on the
last letter of each noun, saying the letter out loud. "g. Is there an s? No. Two bag. RED."

Another break: you fix it and over-fix:

> 2 bags, 3 boxes, 4 childrens

Two fixed, one still wrong, because children looked finished. Check every RED mark against
the table, not against your feeling.

## In real life

Lists are read by people in a hurry, and the person in a hurry reads endings, not numbers.
"3 copy" gets one copy. "2 ticket" gets one ticket. The mistake is small on the page and
costs a trip back to the counter.

Nobody plants mistakes on a real list. But the read-back with a finger on the last letter is
the same move you will make on a real list, and it takes seconds once it is a habit. Keep the
broken list on your practice page as proof that your check can go RED.
