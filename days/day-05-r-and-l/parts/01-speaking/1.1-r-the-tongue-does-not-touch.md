## One-line answer

For the English r, the tip of your tongue curls up towards the roof of your mouth and stops just before it touches.

## The story

You are on a new road and you stop a man to ask the way. "Is this the right road to the
station?" He hears the sentence, but two of the words come out with a tongue that taps or
rolls, the way r is made in your first language. He asks you to say it again. The second
time you hold your tongue back from the roof of your mouth. "Right road." He points.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make. If your first language
already has an r like the English one, this part will feel easy, and that is fine.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **roof of the mouth** — the hard top inside your mouth, behind your top teeth.
- **curl** — bend the tongue tip upwards and a little back.
- **tap** — touch quickly, once.
- **roll** — make the tongue tip shake against the roof, many times, very fast.

Today's sound is a **consonant**, defined on
[day 3](../../../day-03-two-th-sounds/parts/01-speaking/1.1-tongue-between-the-teeth.md).
Its **sound symbol** is /r/. Many languages make their r by tapping or rolling the tongue
tip on the roof of the mouth. English does not. The English r is made with the tongue tip
curled up towards the roof, close to it, and never touching. The air passes over the tongue
with no tap and no roll. The lips are a little round at the start.

If nothing touches, there is nothing to tap. That is the whole rule.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 speaking check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), a stranger
follows you more easily when your r sounds like theirs. A tapped or rolled r is understood.
It also makes the listener work a little on every word with an r in it, and there are many:
right, road, room, work, friend, brother.

## The rule

Look in the mirror. Open your mouth a little. Curl your tongue tip up and back, towards the
roof, and stop before it touches. Round your lips a little. Now hum through it: rrrrr. Nothing
should tap.

| Part of the mouth | For /r/ as in right |
| --- | --- |
| tongue tip | curled up towards the roof, not touching |
| lips | a little round at the start |
| air | flows over the tongue with no stop and no tap |
| voice | on |

Say them in a row. Between each word, hold rrrrr for a moment and feel that nothing touches:

> rice — right — road

## When it breaks

You mean right, but the tongue taps the roof:

> rright, with a tap or a roll at the start

The listener still hears right. They also hear an r from another language, and on a fast
word they may miss it. The fix: pull the tongue tip back a little so it cannot reach the roof,
and let the air flow. Hold rrrrr first, then add the word.

If, when you hold rrrrr, you can feel your tongue touch the roof even once, the check is RED.

## In real life

An r from your first language is not a mistake in the way a swapped v is. Many good speakers
of English keep it all their lives and are understood. The English r is worth learning for
two reasons. It is easier for a stranger to follow at speed. And in some words — at the end,
or before another consonant — many English speakers do not sound the r at all, and a rolled r
there stands out.

A native listener notices a rolled r and forgives it completely. What they cannot forgive is
not hearing the word. So on a name, a road, a number, slow the r down and keep it off the
roof.
