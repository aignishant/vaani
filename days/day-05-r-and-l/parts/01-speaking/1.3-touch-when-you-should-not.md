## One-line answer

Let the tongue land where it should float, hear right road become light load, then lift it and say it again.

## The story

You are telling the man the road you want: "the right road". You say it on purpose with the
tongue landing: "the light load". He frowns. You say it again with the tongue lifted: "the
right road". He nods and points. You did the wrong one on purpose this time. The next time
it happens by accident, you will hear it, because you have heard it before.

This is an imagined scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on a swap some
beginners make, not on a mistake found in your own recording. If your first language
separates r and l, make the swap anyway; the check needs a change it can hear.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **land** — the tongue tip touches the ridge.
- **float** — the tongue tip stays up in the air, touching nothing.
- **repair** — a small fix that helps someone understand you. You met it on
  [day 1](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/01-speaking/1.3-change-one-sound.md).
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check failed. GREEN means it passed.

You floated the tongue for /r/ in [part 1.1](1.1-r-the-tongue-does-not-touch.md) and landed
it for /l/ in [part 1.2](1.2-l-the-tongue-touches.md). Now you land it where it should float.
Both words in the phrase change. Then you lift it and repair.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), a stranger may ask
where you live, and you will say a road, a left, a right. A swap you can make on purpose is a
swap you can hear by accident.

## The rule

The phrase is right road. The deliberate wrong version is light load. The row is:

> right road — light load — right road

1. Record the row. On the middle phrase, land the tongue on the ridge for both words. On the
   outer phrases, keep it floating for both.
2. Play it back without looking. Can you hear the middle phrase change?
3. Record the row with all three right. Play both rows. One changes; one does not. If you
   cannot tell which is which, the check is RED.
4. Add a second row the other way: light — right — light, where the middle one is wrong.
5. Finish with your own true sentences about where you live from part 1.2, said right.

The model words were checked on 2026-09-22 in
[right](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/right_1),
[road](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/road),
[light](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/light_1) and
[load](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/load_1). Each page has a
sound button.

## When it breaks

You meant right road — light load — right road, but the playback is:

> right road — right road — right road

You could not land the tongue on purpose. That can mean your l and r are already clear, or
that the tongue floats for both. Test it with part 1.2's pairs: if light and right sound the
same, the tongue is floating for both, and the l needs work, not the r.

The other break:

> light load — light load — light load

The tongue landed every time. Go back to part 1.1. Hold rrrrr with nothing touching. Then the
row.

A third: the tongue floats, but it taps or rolls on the way. That is an r from another
language. Not wrong, but not today's target. Pull the tip back a little further.

## In real life

Nobody in a real conversation will tell you that your r landed. Friends get used to it. The
recording is where you hear it. And after some days of this, you will feel the tongue land as
it happens, before you hear it. That is what the check is for.

Under pressure, giving directions to a driver, the words right, left, light and road come
fast and close together. A native listener will guess from the hand you point with. A
recording has no hands. Make each one clear.
