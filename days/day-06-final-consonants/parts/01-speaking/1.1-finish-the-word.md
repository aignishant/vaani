## One-line answer

The last sound of an English word is part of the word; bad, bat and back are three different words because of how they end.

## The story

You are in an auto-rickshaw on a noisy road. You tell the driver, "Stop near the bank." The
end of the word gets lost in the noise, and what reaches him is "ba…". Bank? Back? Bag? He
slows and looks at you. You say it again and finish the word: "bank", with the k. He turns
in.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **final** — last; at the end.
- **finish** — make the last sound fully, not drop it.
- **drop** — leave a sound out.
- **pop** — the small burst of air when a stopped sound lets go.

A **consonant**, defined on
[day 3](../../../day-03-two-th-sounds/parts/01-speaking/1.1-tongue-between-the-teeth.md),
is a sound made by stopping or squeezing the air. A **final consonant** is a consonant at
the end of a word. In English the end of a word carries meaning. Bad, bat and back start the
same and have the same middle, the short vowel from
[day 1](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/01-speaking/1.1-four-vowel-sounds.md). Only the
last sound is different, and it makes three words.

In some languages the end of a word is soft, or gets a small extra vowel, or is dropped in
fast speech. In English, a dropped ending is a missing piece of the word.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 speaking check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), the stranger
hears each word once. If your endings are dropped, "I like" and "I live" and "I light" fall
together, and so do bad and bag and back. The endings are what make a word one word.

## The rule

Three endings, three places in the mouth. Each one stops the air and then lets it go with a
small pop. The pop is quiet at the end of a word, but it is there.

| Word | Sound | The last sound | Where it is made |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [bad](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bad_1) | /bæd/ | /d/ | tongue tip on the ridge behind the top teeth, voice on |
| [bat](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bat_1) | /bæt/ | /t/ | tongue tip on the same ridge, voice off |
| [back](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/back_1) | /bæk/ | /k/ | the back of the tongue up against the roof, voice off |

Say the three words in a row. Finish each one. Let the last sound go before you start the
next:

> bad — bat — back

Now say each word and hold your hand in front of your mouth. On bat and back you should feel
a small puff of air at the end. On bad it is softer. If you feel nothing on any of them, the
ending is not there yet.

and look under "Voiced Consonants" for /d/ and "Voiceless Consonants" for /t/ and /k/. The
page was opened on 2026-09-22. Each video names its sound on screen.

## When it breaks

You mean back, but the ending is dropped:

> ba

The listener hears half a word and must guess: bad, bat, back, bag, bank. In a noisy place
they cannot. The fix: put the back of your tongue up at the end and let the k pop. Say back
again.

A second break, common when your first language ends words softly:

> back-uh

A small vowel is added after the k. Now the word has an extra piece. The listener hears
"backer" or "bucker". The fix: stop the air with the k and do not let the voice start again.

If, on playback, bad, bat and back sound like the same word, the check is RED.

## In real life

Fast speakers make the final pop very small. Sometimes they hold the stop and never release
it. But they always make the stop; the tongue goes to the place. That is what a listener
hears. So "finish the word" does not mean "say the ending loudly". It means "put the tongue
where the ending is made".

A native listener forgives a soft ending in the middle of a sentence. They cannot forgive a
missing one on a word that stands alone: a name, a number, a place. Bank, bag, back: give
the driver the k.
