## One-line answer

Say the word without its last sound on purpose, hear how little is left, then say it again and finish it.

## The story

You get out of the rickshaw and tell your friend about the ride. "The driver went the wrong
way and the road was ba…" You stop the word early on purpose. Your friend waits. "Bad? Or
back?" You finish it: "Bad." The half-word made her wait. The whole word did not.

This is an imagined scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on what many
beginners do at speed, not on a mistake found in your own recording.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **half-word** — a word with its ending missing.
- **repair** — a small fix that helps someone understand you. You met it on
  [day 1](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/01-speaking/1.3-change-one-sound.md).
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check failed. GREEN means it passed.

You finished the word in [part 1.1](1.1-finish-the-word.md) and chose its ending with the
voice in [part 1.2](1.2-voice-on-voice-off-at-the-end.md). Now you drop the ending on
purpose, and also swap it. You hear how much of the word is gone. Then you finish it.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), you will speak for
longer than you have so far, and endings are the first thing that goes when you speak for a
while. A dropped ending you can make on purpose is one you can hear when it happens by
itself.

## The rule

Two rows. In the first, the middle word has no ending. In the second, the middle word has
the wrong ending.

> back — ba — back

> bad — bat — bad

1. Record both rows. On "ba", stop the word before the tongue moves for the k. On "bat", cut
   the buzz before the ending.
2. Play them back without looking. Can you hear the middle word lose its ending, and change
   its ending?
3. Record both rows with all three words right. Play all four rows. Two change; two do not.
   If you cannot tell which is which, the check is RED.
4. Finish with your own true sentences from part 1.2, every ending finished.

The model words were checked on 2026-09-22 in
[back](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/back_1),
[bad](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bad_1) and
[bat](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/bat_1). Each page has a
sound button.

## When it breaks

You meant back — ba — back, but the playback is:

> ba — ba — ba

No ending on any of them. The repair did not happen. Go back to part 1.1 with your hand in
front of your mouth. Say only back until you feel the puff of air. Then the row.

The other way:

> back — back — back

You could not drop the ending on purpose. That is a good sign for your endings, but the check
needs a change to hear. Try again and stop the word early, as if someone cut you off.

A third break: the ending is there, but with a small vowel after it: "back-uh". That is an
added piece, not a finished word. Stop the air on the k and keep the voice off.

## In real life

In a conversation, nobody will tell you that your endings are dropping. They will ask you to
repeat, or they will guess, and half the time they will guess right and you will never know.
The recording is where you find out. After some days, you will start to feel the tongue not
reaching its place, and that feeling is the fix arriving.

Under pressure, when you are tired or speaking fast, endings drop first. Names and places
suffer most, because the listener has nothing else to go on. Give the last sound its place,
and the listener has the whole word.
