## One-line answer

Write your message with the verb be missing or wrong on purpose, read it as someone who does not know you, mark every gap, then put am, is or are back.

## The story

Before you send your message to the group, you read it back. It reads fine to you. Then you
read it as the person who joined the chat yesterday and knows nobody. "I new here. My
brother driver. They from Delhi." In that voice, each sentence stops short, like a step with
no stair. You feel the gaps because you are reading as someone who cannot fill them in.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on what beginners
whose first language can leave this verb out often write, not on a mistake found in your own
page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **gap** — a place where a word should be and is not.
- **read back** — read your own page again, slowly, as if it were someone else's.
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You wrote the full forms in [part 2.1](2.1-am-is-are.md) and the short forms in
[part 2.2](2.2-short-forms-and-not.md). Now you write your sentences again with three
deliberate breaks: one verb missing, one wrong form, one apostrophe missing. You read back as
a stranger. Each break is a RED mark. Then you fix only the marks.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) will have am, is or are
in nearly every sentence. A read-back that cannot find three planted gaps will not find the
real one.

## The rule

Under your correct sentences from parts 2.1 and 2.2:

1. Copy them with three deliberate breaks: leave one am, is or are out; give one sentence the
   wrong form (are for one person, or is for I); write one short form without its apostrophe.
2. Cover the correct version.
3. Read the broken version out loud, in the voice of someone who has never met you. After
   each person or thing at the start of a sentence, put your finger down and ask: is the verb
   here? Is it the right one for one or for more? If it is short, is the apostrophe there?
4. Mark each break RED. There should be three.
5. Uncover. Fix only the three, on a new line, and mark it GREEN.

The model, broken on purpose:

> I new here. My brother are a driver. Were from Pune.

Three RED marks: am is missing after I; are should be is, because one brother; Were should
be We're, with the apostrophe, or it reads as a different word.

The forms were checked on 2026-09-22 in
[Present simple: 'to be'](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple-be).

## When it breaks

You read the broken version back and mark it GREEN:

> I new here. My brother are a driver. Were from Pune. — GREEN

If your first language can drop this verb, "I new here" sounds complete to you. The check
itself failed. Do it again, finger after each subject, asking "verb?" out loud.

Another break: you fix it and change more than the marks:

> I'm new here. My brothers is a driver. We're from Pune.

The am was fixed, but brother gained an -s while its verb was fixed. One fix, one thing.
Compare every word with your correct version.

## In real life

Nobody plants a missing verb on a real page. But everyone whose first language can leave it
out drops it by accident, especially in short quick messages, and readers feel every gap as
a stumble. The finger after each subject is the habit that catches it. It is slow today and
automatic in a week.

A friend in a chat reads past "I new here". A new colleague reads it and decides something
about you before you have said anything else. Keep the broken message on your practice page
as proof that your read-back can go RED.

## Check yourself

- **Write:** `TODO(me)` Add your deliberately broken message, your three RED marks, and the
  fixed version marked GREEN to your page. Note what you actually changed. Label it
  deliberate when you add it to the error ledger.
- **Say out loud:** Which of the three breaks did your eye slide past first, and what
  question made you see it?
