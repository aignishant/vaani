## §1 Where we are

You are at a railway station with a broken board, and a man is calling out names. Some you
catch, some you don't, and the difference is not how loud he is. The speaking folder is about
the one loud syllable every English word carries, and what a listener loses when it is missing
or moved. Think of a word as a row of taps with one hard tap in it.

You are filling in a library form, and the box says "About you". The writing folder is about
five sentences that hold five facts a stranger could check, in the order a stranger expects.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- Saying every syllable with the same weight. It feels careful. It sounds flat, and the word
  is hard to catch.
- Guessing the stress from the spelling. Hotel is hoTEL. Check the mark ˈ in the dictionary.
- Making two syllables loud when you move the stress. One word, one stress.
- Writing an opinion where a fact belongs: "I am a good person." A stranger cannot check it.
- Dropping am: "I from Nashik." The verb is never left out on paper.
- Joining two facts with "and" so five sentences become four.
