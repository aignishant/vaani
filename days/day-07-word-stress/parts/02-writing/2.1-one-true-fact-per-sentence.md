## One-line answer

A sentence about yourself has three parts, I, a verb, and one fact, and it is true only if you could prove it to a stranger.

## The story

You are joining the local library. The form has a box that says "About you", with five lines
in it. You freeze. What do they want? You write "I am good" on the first line and stop. The
woman at the desk reads it, smiles, and says, "Good at what?" Your line was a sentence, but it
held no fact. She cannot picture you from it.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **fact** — a thing that is true and can be checked.
- **prove** — show that something is true.
- **picture** — see in your mind.
- **stranger** — a person who does not know you.
- **box** — a space on a form for writing.

A **sentence**, from [day 3](../../../day-03-two-th-sounds/parts/02-writing/2.1-what-a-sentence-is.md),
starts with a [capital letter](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.1-capital-and-small-letters.md)
and ends with a [full stop](../../../day-03-two-th-sounds/parts/02-writing/2.1-what-a-sentence-is.md).
A sentence **about you** starts with I. Then comes a
[verb](../../../day-06-final-consonants/parts/02-writing/2.1-am-is-are.md), a word that says
what you do or are. Then one fact.

A fact is something a stranger could check. "I live in Nashik" is a fact. They could go and
look. "I am good" is not a fact yet. Good at what? For whom? The library form does not want
your opinion of yourself. It wants facts a stranger can picture.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 writing check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) is a
ten-sentence page about you, and every sentence must carry a capital letter and a full stop.
Ten sentences with no facts in them is a page a stranger cannot use. Five true sentences today
are half of that page, and the shape you build today is the shape you will reuse.

## The rule

Five kinds of fact, one per line. Each is a shape you fill with your own truth:

| Line | What the fact is about | The shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | your name | I am + name. |
| 2 | your place | I live in + place. / I am from + place. |
| 3 | your work or study | I work as + job. / I am a student. / I work in + place. |
| 4 | your people | I live with + people. / I have + number + people. |
| 5 | one thing you like | I like + thing. |

Three of the shapes filled in, for a person who is not you:

> I am Meera.
> I live in Nashik.
> I work in a shop.

The model lines are not yours. Your lines hold your own name, your own town, your own job.

Three checks on every line:

1. Does it start with I and a capital letter?
2. Is there a verb: am, live, work, have, like?
3. Could a stranger check it? If not, it is not a fact yet.

The shapes "I'm from" and "I live in" and "I work as" were confirmed as the sentence shapes of
a beginner's self-introduction on 2026-09-22 at
[Online course introductions](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/writing/a1/online-course-introductions),
which shows model introductions and says to "give a little bit of information about yourself".
The forms of am, is and are were checked at
[Present simple: 'to be'](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple-be)
on the same date.

Here is the trap. A fact must be true today. "I am a doctor" when you are studying to be one is
a wish, not a fact. Write "I am a student" or "I study medicine". A stranger who finds one
untrue line stops trusting the other four.

## When it breaks

You write an opinion where a fact should be:

> I am a good person.

The reader cannot check it, and cannot picture you. The fix: swap it for something they can
see. "I help my mother in the kitchen." Now they see a kitchen and a person in it.

You write a line with no verb:

> I from Nashik.

The reader stops. Something is missing between I and from. Many beginners drop am here because
their first language does not need it. The fix: put am back. "I am from Nashik."

If any of your five lines has no verb or no checkable fact, that line is RED.

## In real life

Nobody fills a form with "I am a good person". But in a first message to a new group, a new
colleague, or a landlord, beginners write exactly that, because it feels safe. Fluent writers
give facts. "I'm Meera. I've just moved to the third floor. I work nights, so I'm quiet in the
day." Three facts, and the reader knows what to expect from their new neighbour.

A friend reading your message forgives an opinion. A landlord or an office reads for facts,
and a page with none makes them ask questions you could have answered. Facts save you the
questions.
