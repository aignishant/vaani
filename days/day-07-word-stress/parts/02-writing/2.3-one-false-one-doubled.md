## One-line answer

Copy your block with one sentence made untrue and one sentence carrying two facts, read it as a stranger, mark both, then restore it.

## The story

You hand the library form back. The woman reads line three and looks up. "You work in a
hospital? The form says cloth shop on the other side." You had copied a line from a friend's
form. It was a fine sentence. It was not about you. On line four she frowns again: "You live
with your parents and you like cricket and you have a dog?" Three things in one breath, and she
lost count. Two kinds of break, and a stranger caught both in one look.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong versions are deliberate. They rest on what beginners
commonly write, not on a mistake found in your own page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **false** — not true.
- **doubled** — carrying two things where one belongs.
- **restore** — put back the way it was.
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You have a block of five true sentences from [part 2.2](2.2-five-sentences-on-paper.md). Now
you break it in two ways that beginners really do. First, you copy a model line instead of
writing your own, so one sentence is false. Second, you jam two facts into one sentence with
"and", so one sentence is doubled. Then you
[read back](../../../day-02-long-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.3-a-form-nobody-can-use.md) as the
woman at the desk, who knows nothing about you and has no patience for a puzzle.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) is judged by a stranger
who can follow it. A copied line breaks trust, and a doubled line breaks the count. If your
read-back cannot catch a planted false line and a planted doubled line today, it will not catch
the real ones on a page twice as long.

## The rule

Under your correct block:

1. Copy the block. Change one sentence so it is no longer true about you. Take it from the
   model in part 2.2 if you like: give yourself Meera's job.
2. Join two of the remaining sentences with "and" so one sentence carries two kinds of fact.
3. Cover the correct block.
4. Read the broken block out loud in the desk woman's voice. After each sentence, ask two
   questions: "Is this true of the person in front of me?" and "How many facts did I just
   get?" One is right. Two is a break.
5. Mark the false sentence RED and the doubled sentence RED. Two marks.
6. Uncover. Restore only those two sentences, on a new line, and mark the block GREEN.

The model, broken on purpose:

> I am Meera. I live in Nashik. I work in a hospital. I live with my parents and I like cricket.

Two RED marks: "hospital" is false for Meera, who works in a cloth shop; and the last sentence
holds people and a like, two facts, so the block now has four sentences, not five.

The sentence shapes were checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Online course introductions](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/writing/a1/online-course-introductions).

## When it breaks

You read the broken block back and mark it GREEN:

> I am Meera. I live in Nashik. I work in a hospital. I live with my parents and I like cricket. — GREEN

The false line slipped past because it is a good sentence, and a good sentence feels true. The
doubled line slipped past because "and" is small. The check itself failed. Do it again with the
two questions said out loud after every sentence.

Another break: you restore the block and fix more than the marks.

> I am Meera. I live in Nashik city. I work in a cloth shop. I live with my parents. I like cricket.

The two marks were fixed, but "city" appeared on line two, which was never marked. One fix per
mark. Compare each word with your correct block.

If either planted break was marked GREEN, the check is RED.

## In real life

Nobody plants a false line in a real message. But beginners copy model sentences all the time,
from a lesson, from a friend's form, from a message they received, and a copied sentence is
true of someone else. Readers notice when a line does not fit the rest. And everyone joins
facts with "and" when they are in a hurry. One "and" is fine. A sentence with three facts is a
list pretending to be a sentence, and a reader loses count.

A friend forgives both. A form, an office or a landlord does not; they act on what you wrote,
and a false line becomes a wrong record with your name on it. Keep the broken block on your
practice page as proof that your read-back can go RED.

  deliberate when you add it to the error ledger.
- **Say out loud:** Which break did your eye pass over first, the false line or the doubled
  one, and which question caught it?
