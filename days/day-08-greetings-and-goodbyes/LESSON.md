## §1 Where we are

You meet your neighbour on the stairs every morning, and something small is going wrong that
neither of you can name. The speaking folder is about the greeting and the goodbye as pairs of
turns: what you say, what comes back, and the two words that keep a door open. Think of a
greeting as a ball you throw and catch.

You are at the vegetable market with someone else's list, and the list cannot point. The
writing folder is about this, that, these and those: four words that are a finger on the page.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** Play the recording. In the rows from
[part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-greeting-that-does-not-come-back.md), can you hear the
silence after the version with no return, and hear it vanish in the repaired one? If both
versions sound the same to you, the check is RED. Record again.

**Writing:** Cover your correct note. Read the broken note from
[part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-point-at-the-wrong-one.md) as the shopper, stopping at each
pointing word to ask "one or many?" and "near or far?". Did you find all three? If you
marked it GREEN, the check is RED. Run it again.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Answering "How are you?" with one word and no return. The door closes.
- Saying "Good night" to arrive. It only ever means you are leaving or sleeping.
- A flat, falling "See you later" that sounds like you hope not to.
- Writing this or that with a plural noun: "this onions".
- Fixing the noun when the pointing word was wrong: "this tomato" when you wanted many.
- Measuring near and far from the reader. They are measured from the writer.
