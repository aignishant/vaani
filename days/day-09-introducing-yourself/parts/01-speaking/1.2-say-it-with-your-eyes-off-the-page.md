## One-line answer

Learn the five sentences in chunks until you can say them looking at a person, not at paper, and record the version where your eyes were up.

## The story

Second day at the new job. A colleague from another floor comes to your desk and says, "Hi, I
don't think we've met." You reach for your phone, where your five sentences are typed. You
read them out. She listens politely to the top of your head. When you look up, she has already
started talking to someone else. Everything you said was right. You said it to the phone.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **chunk** — a few words that go together and are learned as one piece.
- **eyes up** — looking at the person, not at paper.
- **from memory** — without reading.
- **build up** — add one piece at a time.

In [part 1.1](1.1-the-five-spoken-sentences.md) you wrote and said your five sentences. Now
you take them off the page. Reading and speaking are different acts. When you read, your eyes
do the remembering and your voice goes flat. When you speak, your eyes are on the listener and
your voice moves. A stranger hears the difference at once.

You do not learn the five sentences word by word. You learn them in chunks: "I'm from" is one
piece, "I live with" is one piece. A chunk comes out of your mouth whole, without building it.
That is what makes speaking feel easy.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 recording](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) must sound like a
person speaking, not a page being read. A stranger can tell. If your eyes are down on the day,
the recording will say so, and the check is about following a speaker, not a reader.

## The rule

Build up in four steps, all at the mirror, recorder on for the last one:

1. **Read it.** Say the five sentences from the page, once.
2. **Cover and build.** Cover the page. Say sentence one. Uncover, check. Cover, say one and
   two. Uncover, check. Keep adding one until you say all five with the page covered.
3. **Eyes up.** Look at your own eyes in the mirror and say all five. If a sentence goes,
   don't look down; say "Sorry", take a breath, and go on from where you were.
4. **Record it.** Eyes on the mirror, all five, with the pause after each. This is the take
   you keep.

The chunks to learn as pieces, so they come out whole:

> I'm from … / I live in …
> I work in … / I'm a …
> I live with …

The short forms I'm, you're, he's were confirmed on 2026-09-22 at
[Present simple: 'to be'](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple-be).

One more thing. Say the five to a real person today, if you can: a family member, a friend, a
shopkeeper you know. Eyes up. It will feel odd. It is the whole point.

The trap: learning the sentences so well that you say them at one speed with no pauses, like a
prayer. The pauses are part of what you learned. Keep them.

## When it breaks

Your eyes drop on sentence four every time:

> I'm Arjun. I'm from Nagpur. I work in a bank. ... (looks down) I live with my wife and my son.

The listener sees you leave them and come back. The fix: sentence four is the one to learn as
a chunk today. Say it alone ten times, eyes up, before you say the five again.

You lose a sentence and stop dead:

> I'm Arjun. I'm from Nagpur. ... ... ...

The silence grows and the listener gets uncomfortable. The fix: "Sorry," one breath, and go
to the next one you remember. A lost sentence is one fact lost. A stop is the whole
introduction lost.

If your kept recording has your eyes down on more than one sentence, the check is RED. Record
again.

## In real life

Fluent speakers have said their introduction hundreds of times. It is not memory for them; it
is habit. That is where you are going. Under pressure, a big room, an interview, even fluent
speakers lose a sentence, and what they do is exactly step three: a small "sorry" and on to
the next. Nobody minds. They mind a long silence and a face turned to the floor.

A native listener does not notice a perfect introduction. They notice eyes. Eyes up with one
lost sentence beats eyes down with all five.
