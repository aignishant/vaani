## One-line answer

Record your five sentences with no pauses and with I'm dropped, on purpose, play it back as the stranger, count what you could catch, then record it right beside it.

## The story

Third day. A new person joins and the manager says "Go round" again. This time you know your
five. You are nervous, so you say them fast, all in one breath, to get it over with. The new
person nods and smiles and, at lunch, calls you by the wrong name and asks which city you
said. You gave her all five facts. She caught none, because there was nowhere to put them
down.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong versions are deliberate. They rest on what beginners
commonly do, not on a mistake found in your own recordings.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **run together** — said with no gaps, so the pieces join.
- **one breath** — without stopping to breathe.
- **catch** — hear and remember.
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You have the five sentences from [part 1.1](1.1-the-five-spoken-sentences.md), off the page
from [part 1.2](1.2-say-it-with-your-eyes-off-the-page.md). Now you break them the two ways
nervous beginners really do. First, no pauses. Second, no I'm and no I: just the facts. You
listen back as the new colleague. You count how many of the five you could have written down.
Then you record the right version beside it.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), nerves will do to
you what they did in the story. A learner who has heard their own run-together introduction on
a recording knows what nerves sound like, and slows down on the day. A learner who has not
does not know it is happening.

## The rule

Two rows, recorder on. In each, wrong first, then right, with a clear gap between the two.

**Row 1 — no pauses.** Say all five in one breath, as fast as you can:

> I'm Arjun I'm from Nagpur I work in a bank I live with my wife and my son I like old songs.

Then the right version: five sentences, one beat after each.

**Row 2 — no I'm.** Say the facts with the small words gone:

> Arjun. Nagpur. Bank. Wife and son. Old songs.

Then the right version, with I'm and I back in.

Play each row. Listen as the new colleague, with a pen, and try to write the five facts down
from the wrong version. How many did you get? Then from the right version. If you got all five
from both, your wrong version was not fast enough or not bare enough. Do it again, worse.

Then the repair, in one breath, as it happens when you hear yourself going too fast:

> I'm Arjun I'm from — sorry. I'm Arjun. I'm from Nagpur.

The short forms were confirmed on 2026-09-22 at
[Present simple: 'to be'](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple-be).

## When it breaks

The break this part is about: your wrong and right versions sound the same on playback.

> I'm Arjun. I'm from Nagpur. I work in a bank. — I'm Arjun I'm from Nagpur I work in a bank.

If the colleague with the pen gets five from both, you have not yet heard what fast sounds like
from outside. Record the wrong one again at the speed you would use if the bus were leaving.

A second break: the repair restarts from the top.

> I'm Arjun I'm from Nagpur I work — sorry. I'm Arjun. I'm from Nagpur. I work in a bank. I
> live with …

You said the first two sentences twice. The listener now has them twice and the rest not yet.
The fix: repair only the sentence you rushed, and go on.

If you cannot tell the wrong version from the right one on playback, the check is RED.

## In real life

Nobody rushes on purpose. Everybody rushes when nervous, and nervous is the normal state in an
introduction round. Fluent speakers rush too; the difference is that they hear it and slow
down after the first sentence, often with a small laugh. That is the repair you practised.

A native listener forgives a rushed first sentence. They lose a rushed five. And they never
say so; they smile, nod, and forget your name. The recording is the only person who will tell
you the truth about your speed, so ask it today.
