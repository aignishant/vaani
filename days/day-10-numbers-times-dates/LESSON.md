## §1 Where we are

You are at a clinic desk and the nurse is writing down what you say. Some of it comes out
wrong on her page, and it is not her hearing. The speaking folder is about numbers, times and
dates out loud: the teens that sound like tens, the small words that turn digits into a time,
and the habit of saying a number two ways. Think of every number as a parcel with a label on
it.

You have started a diary in a notebook by your bed, and next month you will be its stranger.
The writing folder is about the date, the time and one true sentence, written so that you in a
year can read them.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** Play the rows from [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-thirteen-or-thirty.md) and
write down what you hear from each wrong version before you hear the right one. If the nurse
wrote the same thing from both versions in any row, the check is RED. Say the wrong one more
wrong and record again.

**Writing:** Cover your correct entry. Read the broken entry from
[part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-date-nobody-can-read.md) as yourself in a year, asking
"which month?", "morning or evening?" and "what happened?". Did you find all three? If you
marked it GREEN, the check is RED. Run it again.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Thirteen with the loud piece at the front. It is thirty to the listener.
- A phone number in one breath. The listener keeps the first two digits.
- "Four thirty" and "three March" to a person. Not wrong, but a screen talking. Half past
  four; the third of March.
- Past after the half hour: "a quarter past three" for 3.45.
- The month as a number: 9/10 is two different days to two readers.
- A time with no a.m. or p.m., and a diary line with no verb.
