## One-line answer

Write the date as day, then month, then year, with the month as a word starting with a capital letter, and the reader can never mix up the day and the month.

## The story

You start a small diary in a notebook by your bed. On the first night you write "9/10" at the
top of the page. A week later you look back and cannot tell: was that the ninth of October or
the tenth of September? Your own diary, and you cannot read the date. You cross it out and
write "9 October 2026". Now it will be readable in ten years.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **diary** — a notebook where you write what happened each day.
- **date** — the day, month and year, such as 9 October 2026.
- **month** — one of the twelve parts of a year: January, February and so on.
- **order** — which comes first: day, month or year.
- **cross out** — draw a line through a mistake.

A **date** on paper has three parts: the day as a number, the month, the year. The safe order
is day, month, year: 9 October 2026. The month is a word, not a number, and it starts with a
[capital letter](../../../day-01-short-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.1-capital-and-small-letters.md),
because months are names.

Why a word? Because numbers for months can be read two ways. Some countries write the month
first, some the day first, and 9/10 means two different days depending on who reads it. A word
cannot be mixed up. October is October to everyone.

## Why Vaani needs it

Every page you write from here starts with a date, because a diary or a portfolio with no
dates is a pile of pages. The [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases)
is dated. Your `lab/writing/` folder is dated. A date the reader cannot trust makes the whole
page hard to trust.

## The rule

| Part | How to write it | Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| day | the number, no -th needed | 9 |
| month | the word, capital letter | October |
| year | four figures | 2026 |

Together, with spaces and no commas:

> 9 October 2026
> 22 September 2026

You may add the day of the week before it, also with a capital:

> Thursday 9 October 2026

The order and the month-as-word were checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Writing the Date](https://www.englishclub.com/vocabulary/time-date.php), which lists the
British forms "14 March 2016" and "14th March 2016" as day-month-year, and the American forms
"March 14, 2016" as month-day-year, and shows that "14/3/2016" and "3/14/2016" are the same
date written in the two orders. The day-month order in a real message, "Thursday 14 March",
was seen at
[An email to confirm an appointment](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/writing/a1/email-confirm-appointment),
opened the same day.

The twelve months, each with a capital: January, February, March, April, May, June, July,
August, September, October, November, December.

The trap: numbers-only dates like 9/10/2026 look neat and short. They are the ones that get
read wrong. In this course the month is always a word.

## When it breaks

You write the date as numbers only:

> 9/10/2026

Half your readers see the ninth of October and half the tenth of September. The fix: 9
October 2026.

You write the month without its capital:

> 9 october 2026

The reader can still read it, but a month is a name, and a name without its capital looks
like a mistake before it looks like a month. The fix: October.

You put the year first, the way a computer file might:

> 2026 October 9

Not wrong on a file. On a page for a person, it reads backwards. The fix: day, month, year.

If your date has a number for the month or a small letter starting the month, that date is
RED.

## In real life

Fluent writers use several forms: "9 Oct 2026", "9th October", "Thurs 9 Oct". In a chat, "9
Oct" is enough. On a form, they follow the boxes the form gives. On a letter or a diary page,
day, month as a word, year is what they write, because it is the form nobody misreads.

A friend reading 9/10 will ask. A bank, a school or an office will not ask; they will read it
in their own order and act on it, and the appointment or the payment lands on the wrong day.
