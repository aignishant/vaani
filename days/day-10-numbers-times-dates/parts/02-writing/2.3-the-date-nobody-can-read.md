## One-line answer

Copy a diary entry with the month as a number, the time without a.m. or p.m., and the sentence cut to words, read it as yourself in a year, mark all three, then restore it.

## The story

You find an old notebook from years ago, in your own handwriting. The top of a page says
"3/4". Below it, "6.30 — market — Papa". You turn it over and over. Was it March or April?
Morning or evening? What happened with Papa at the market? You wrote it. You cannot read it.
The person who could read it was you on that day, and that person is gone.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on what beginners
commonly write, not on a mistake found in your own page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **in a year** — twelve months from now.
- **restore** — put back the way it was.
- **read back** — read your own page again, slowly, as if it were someone else's, from
  [day 2](../../../day-02-long-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.3-a-form-nobody-can-use.md).
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You have the date from [part 2.1](2.1-writing-the-date.md) and the time and diary line from
[part 2.2](2.2-the-time-and-a-one-line-diary.md). Now you break an entry in the three ways
tired diary-writers really do, and you read it as the reader who matters most: you, in a year,
with no memory of the day. That reader is a stranger. Then you fix only what they could not
read.

## Why Vaani needs it

Every page in your `lab/writing/` folder is dated, and the folder is your portfolio. On
[day 20](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), and again at every later gate,
you look back at earlier pages. A date you cannot read makes a page you cannot place. If your
read-back cannot catch three planted breaks today, it will not catch the real one on a tired
night.

## The rule

Under your correct entry from part 2.2:

1. Copy the entry with three deliberate breaks: write the month as a number; drop a.m. or
   p.m. from the time; cut the sentence to two or three words with no verb.
2. Cover the correct entry.
3. Read the broken entry out loud as yourself one year from now, who remembers nothing. At
   the date ask: "Which month?" At the time ask: "Morning or evening?" At the sentence ask:
   "What happened?" If you have to guess, that is a break.
4. Mark each RED. There should be three.
5. Uncover. Restore only the three, on a new line, and mark it GREEN.

The model, broken on purpose:

> 9/10/2026
> 7.30 Late. Bus.

Three RED marks: 9/10 could be two dates; 7.30 could be morning or evening; "Late. Bus." has no
verb and no full sentence, so nothing happened that a reader can see.

The date forms were checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Writing the Date](https://www.englishclub.com/vocabulary/time-date.php), which shows
"14/3/2016" and "3/14/2016" as the same date in two orders. The a.m. and p.m. form was seen at
[An email to confirm an appointment](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/writing/a1/email-confirm-appointment).

## When it breaks

You read the broken entry back and mark it GREEN:

> 9/10/2026 · 7.30 Late. Bus. — GREEN

You marked it GREEN because you remember today. The check failed, because the reader is not
you today. Read again and say the three questions out loud. If any answer is "I know because
I was there", it is RED.

Another break: you restore more than the marks.

> Thursday 9 October 2026
> 7.30 a.m. I got up late, missed the first bus, and was late for work.

The day of the week and the extra fact were never marked. They are fine in a real entry, but
this is a repair, and a repair changes only what was broken. One fix per mark.

If any planted break was marked GREEN, the check is RED.

## In real life

Nobody breaks a diary entry on purpose. Everybody writes "3/4" and "6.30 — market" on a tired
night, because it is faster and they will remember. They do not remember. Fluent writers who
keep diaries learn this once, from an old notebook they cannot read, and after that they write
the month as a word and the sentence with a verb, even tired.

A friend reading your message with "3/4" asks. A form, a booking or an office does not; they
read it in their order. Keep the broken entry on your practice page as proof that your
read-back can go RED, and as the old notebook you will now never have.
