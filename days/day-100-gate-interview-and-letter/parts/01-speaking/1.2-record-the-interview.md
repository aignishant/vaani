## One-line answer

Give a friend your five questions on a card, and ask them to play the interviewer and add one follow-up.
Record the whole interview in one take, both voices. Then write the transcript of your answers, mark
every sentence F for full or X for fragment, and play the recording once to a stranger who writes down
the main point of each answer.

## The story

Your neighbour's daughter had a nursing interview. She practised with her father playing the panel. He
read the questions from a card in his best official voice, and at one point he asked *Can you give me an
example of that?*, which wasn't on the card.

She recorded it on her phone. That evening she wrote down every word she'd said and put an X next to
every sentence that wasn't a sentence. There were six. Four were in the strengths answer: *Patient.
Calm. Good with families. Hard-working.*

She recorded the strengths answer again with each one inside a sentence and an example: *I'm patient
with families. On my placement, a father asked me the same question four times, and I answered him
four times.* Then her aunt, who'd never heard any of it, listened once and told her the point of each
answer. She got all five.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **gate**, **take**, **transcript**, **standard question**, **mock interview**, **full sentence
  (spoken)**, **fragment**: from [part 1.1](1.1-what-the-gate-asks-for.md).
- **follow-up**: a question the interviewer adds after your answer, to ask for more.
- **F / X marking**: marking each sentence in the transcript F if it's full, with a subject and a verb,
  and X if it's a fragment.
- **point-of-each-answer test**: a stranger hears the recording once and writes the main point of each
  of the five answers.

## Why Vaani needs it

This is the gate take. The marking shows whether the *full sentences* condition holds. The stranger
shows whether the answers carried their points to someone who doesn't know you.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-24.**
[How to prepare for a job interview in English](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/level/improve-your-english-level/how-prepare-job-interview-english):
"Practise speaking English aloud until you feel comfortable and confident."

**Step one: the interviewer's card.** Write the job and the five questions on a card for your friend.
Ask them to add one follow-up after any answer, from these: *Can you give me an example?* / *What did
you learn from that?* / *Why is that important for this job?*

**Step two: record, one take.** Phone on the table between you. Greeting, five questions, the follow-up,
a thank-you. Don't stop.

**Step three: the transcript and the marking.** Write every word of your answers. Put F or X next to each
sentence. For each X, write the full sentence it should have been beside it, but don't change the
transcript.

**Step four: the point-of-each-answer test.** Play the take once to a stranger who doesn't know you well.
They write down the main point of each answer, in their own words.

**If either check fails, record a new take.** Any X in the gate take, or any answer whose point the
stranger can't write, means a new take. Keep every take, and number them.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the take stopped and restarted**. A real interview doesn't restart. If a
sentence goes wrong, say it again and carry on.

The second is **a transcript written from memory**. You'll write what you meant, not what you said.
Pause the recording and write it word by word.

The third is **a friend who reads the questions too kindly**, smiling and nodding. Ask them to be
polite, neutral and a little serious, like a real panel.

These are errors beginners commonly make in a first gate recording. This day was written before your
own error ledger had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard you make.

## In real life

Fluent candidates still use fragments sometimes, in conversation. *Punctual? Yes, always.* What the
gate asks is that every answer is carried by sentences. A fragment used as an answer is where the
meaning gets lost.

Under pressure, the follow-up is what knocks people off. It's not on the card. Answer it the same way
as the others: a sentence, then an example.

A stranger who can write the point of each answer is doing what a real interviewer does at the end of
the day, when they try to remember you among ten other people.
