## One-line answer

Draft both pieces in one sitting. After a gap, check each one in four separate passes: the greeting,
the sign-off against the greeting, every apostrophe, and a last read for what it says. Write the fair
copies into `lab/writing/`, count the words, and give each piece to the real reader it was written
for, or send it if it's live.

## The story

Your friend applied for a receptionist job. She wrote the cover letter late at night and sent it at
once. The next morning she read it on her phone and saw it: *I'm confident I'd be a good fit.* Two
contractions in her best sentence, in a letter for a job about writing formal emails.

For the next application she did it differently. She wrote the draft, went to bed, and checked it in
the morning, one thing at a time. First the greeting. Then the sign-off. Then she put her finger on
every apostrophe. There were four. Three were contractions, and she changed them. One was *the
hotel's guests*, which stayed. Then she read it once more for what it said, and sent it.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **frame (of an email)**, **matching pair**, **cover letter**, **contraction**, **greeting**,
  **sign-off**: from [part 2.1](2.1-what-both-pieces-must-do.md).
- **draft**: a first version, written to be changed.
- **fair copy**: the clean final version, written out again properly after the draft, from
  [day 20](../../../day-20-gate-self-introduction/parts/02-writing/2.2-write-the-page.md).
- **pass**: one read of the page looking for one thing only.
- **apostrophe**: the small mark ' that shows where letters are missing in a short form, from
  [day 6](../../../day-06-final-consonants/parts/02-writing/2.2-short-forms-and-not.md). It also shows
  belonging: *the company's*.

One thing per pass. If you look for everything at once, you'll find the thing you're best at and miss
the rest.

## Why Vaani needs it

These are the gate pieces. The passes are how the three gate conditions, greeting, sign-off and no
contractions, get checked one at a time instead of hoped for.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[How to write a cover letter](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/covering-letter):
"It's a short letter, usually 3 to 5 paragraphs long."; write "a new one for every job you apply for and
make sure it's tailored to the company and the specific role".
[Unit 4: Starting and finishing emails](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/business/english-emails/unit-4-starting-finishing-emails):
"We normally write a comma after the opening phrase".

**Step one: draft both.** One sitting. The email in the formal frame: subject line, greeting, a reason
line, one or two short paragraphs, a closing line, the sign-off, your name. The cover letter in three to
five paragraphs for one real job, in the same frame.

**Step two: a gap.** Sleep on it, or at least do something else. The passes need fresh eyes.

**Step three: four passes, each piece, one at a time.**

| 4. What it says | one read: the reason is clear; the job is named; nothing is invented | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**Step four: the fair copies.** Write both into `lab/writing/day-100.md`. Count the words in each and
write the count under it.

**Step five: the real reader.** Send the email, or give it to the person it's for. Send the cover letter
with a real application, or give it to someone who hires people and ask *Would you read my CV after
this?* Write down what happened.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the passes done all at once**. You read the letter once, looking for
everything, and a contraction slips through in the sentence you're proudest of.

The second is **the possessive changed by mistake**. *The company's website* becomes *the company is
website*. An apostrophe of belonging stays.

The third is **the fair copy never sent**. A gate piece that's checked and kept in a notebook hasn't met
its reader.

These are errors beginners commonly make in a first gate attempt. This day was written before your own
error ledger had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Fluent writers do the apostrophe pass with a search: they look for every ' in the document. On paper,
a finger does the same job.

Under pressure, with a deadline tonight, the gap gets skipped. Even a short walk helps. The contraction
you can't see now will be obvious later.

A cover letter that reaches a real employer is what this phase was building toward. It counts when it's
sent, not when it's finished.
