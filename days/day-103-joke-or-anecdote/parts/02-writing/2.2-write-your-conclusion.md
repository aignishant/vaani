## One-line answer

Write the conclusion to your essay, three to five sentences: your answer again in new words, one or two
sentences pulling your points together, and a so-what line. Then run the so-what test: a reader reads
each sentence and says *So what?* after it. The last sentence should be the one they can't say it to.

## The story

Your friend's tutor ran the so-what test out loud. She read the old conclusion one sentence at a time,
and after each one she said *So what?* After the copied thesis: *So what? I read that.* After the
list: *So what? I read those too.* After the electric buses: *So what? Where did that come from?*

With the new conclusion, she stopped at the last sentence, about the bus arriving at nine. She didn't
say *So what?* She said: *Yes.*

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **conclusion**, **restate**, **pull together**, **so-what line**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-answer-again-say-why-it-matters.md).
- **introduction**: the first paragraph of an essay, from
  [day 101](../../../day-101-speaking-without-translating/parts/02-writing/2.1-general-to-your-answer.md).
- **so-what test**: a reader reads a conclusion one sentence at a time and says *So what?* after each
  one that doesn't say something new about why the answer matters.

If you wrote an introduction on day 101 and a body paragraph on day 102, this is the last paragraph of
that essay. If you didn't, write a thesis with a map, and three topic sentences, before you start. A
conclusion needs something to conclude.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Conclusions](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/conclusions/): ask a friend to say "'So what?'
or 'Why should anybody care?'" after each statement. The "full circle" strategy: "if you begin by
describing a scenario, you can end with the same scenario".
[An opinion essay](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing/b1-writing/opinion-essay):
the model's last paragraph begins *To sum up* and gives the writer's opinion again.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **a conclusion shorter than one sentence per move**: just the thesis
again. It gets a *So what?* straight away.

The second is **a so-what line that's too big**: *This will change the world.* The reader doesn't
believe it. Stay with your scene.

The third is **a reader who didn't see the introduction**, so the full-circle ending means nothing to
them. Give them both paragraphs.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Fluent writers read their first and last paragraphs together, before anything else, when they check an
essay. If the two don't fit, one of them changes.

Under pressure, you'll be tempted to stop after the last body paragraph. Three sentences of conclusion
are worth writing, even in a hurry.

A reader remembers the last sentence of an essay longer than any other. Make it the one you'd want them
to repeat.
