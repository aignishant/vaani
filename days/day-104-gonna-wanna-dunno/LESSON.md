## §1 Where we are

A friend's voice note from the bus sounds like one long word until you rebuild it: *gonna* is *going
to*, *dunno* is *don't know*. Then you hear yourself tell your landlord *I'm gonna pay on Monday*, and
wonder which room that belongs in. The speaking folder is about connected speech: hearing the full form
inside the relaxed one, choosing the full form when the room is formal, and never putting *gonna* before
a place. You record the same plan for a friend and for a landlord, then break it on purpose for a formal
question and let listeners guess who you were talking to.

Your cousin missed the last train and wrote *If I didn't stay for the photo, I would catch the train*,
and her brother asked whether she was going to catch it. The writing folder is about the third and
mixed conditionals: *If I had known, I would have come*, for a past that can't change, and the mixed
form for a past choice with a result now. You write a paragraph of your own regrets and lucky escapes,
then break its verbs on purpose and let a reader tell you what still sounds possible.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-gonna-the-market.md), two listeners each hear one take,
guess who you were talking to, and name any sentence that sounded wrong. RED is when your full-form take's
listener guesses a friend or names a wrong sentence. GREEN is when they guess someone formal and name
nothing, and the broken take's listener guesses someone informal or names the *gonna* before a place.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-regret-that-sounded-like-now.md), two readers answer
*Can this still change?* for each conditional and circle any wrong verb. RED is when your real
paragraph's reader answers *yes* anywhere or circles a verb. GREEN is when they answer *no* every time
and circle nothing, and the broken copy's reader answers *yes* or circles a verb.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A relaxed form heard as one strange word.
- *Gonna* before a place.
- Relaxed forms in a formal answer.
- Relaxed forms forced into every sentence.
- Full forms said so slowly they sound read.
- Relaxed forms coming back when nervous.
- The second conditional used for a past event.
- *If I would have known*.
- *Would have went*, *would have took*.
- *I'd* read as *would* when it means *had*.
- A mixed conditional with no time word.
- A missing comma after the *if* clause.
