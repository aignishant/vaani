## One-line answer

Record two voice notes about the same real plan. Note F is for a friend: relaxed forms where they come
naturally. Note O is for someone formal, such as a landlord, a manager or an office: full forms, every
one clear. Then write out both notes, circle every relaxed form in F, and check that O has none. Finally,
play a real relaxed voice note or a clip you like, and write down the full form of every lump you hear.

## The story

After the voice note to your landlord, you tried something. You recorded the same message twice. To
your friend:

> Hey, I'm gonna be home late, I gotta finish some work. You wanna eat without me?

To your landlord:

> Hello, it's the tenant from flat four. I'm going to pay the rent on Monday. I have to wait for my
> salary. I hope that's all right.

You played them back to back. Same person, same week, two rooms. Both sounded right, and you could hear
exactly why.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **connected speech**, **relaxed form**, **full form**: from
  [part 1.1](1.1-the-full-form-inside.md).
- **register**: how formal or informal your words are for the person and the place, from
  [day 81](../../../day-81-formal-and-informal/parts/01-speaking/1.1-the-request-ladder.md).
- **voice note**: a short recorded message sent from your phone instead of a written one.
- **transcript**: every word of a recording, written down exactly as it was said.

## Why Vaani needs it

In the phase 6 conversation, you'll move between relaxed chat and careful answers. This part trains the
switch, and trains your ear to rebuild relaxed forms from real speech.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a note O with one relaxed form that slipped in**. You
won't notice it until the transcript. That's the point of writing it out.

The second is **a note F with a relaxed form in every sentence**, forced. It sounds like an imitation.
Relaxed forms come on their own when you're relaxed.

The third is **a listening rep with no lumps found**. Choose faster, more casual speech.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers don't plan their relaxed forms. They come with speed and ease. Full forms are the ones
they choose, on purpose, when the room is formal.

Under pressure, on a formal call, nerves make you speak faster, and relaxed forms slip in. Slow down on
*going to* and *want to*.

A native listener on a formal call won't correct a *gonna*. They'll just file you as a little less
careful than they expected.
