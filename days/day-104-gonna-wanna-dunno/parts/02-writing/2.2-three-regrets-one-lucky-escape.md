## One-line answer

Write a short paragraph about your own past, of five to seven sentences, with four conditionals in it:
three regrets or lessons in the third conditional, and one mixed conditional that jumps to now. Then
underline every *had*, box every *would have* and *would*, and check each sentence against the pattern.
Finally, a reader answers one question for each sentence: *Can this still change?*

## The story

Your cousin's diary grew. After the missed train, she wrote about the job she almost took in another
city, the exam she didn't prepare for, and the time she missed a bus that later broke down on the
highway. Each one was a different past. For the job, she wrote *If I had taken it, I would be living
alone now*, and she realised she was glad she hadn't.

She gave the page to her brother with a question for each line: *Can this still change?* He answered
*no* every time. That was right. It was all past.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **past perfect**, **third conditional**, **mixed conditional**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-if-i-had-known.md).
- **past participle**: the third form of a verb, from
  [day 63](../../../day-63-weekend-and-year-plans/parts/02-writing/2.1-have-or-has-and-the-third-form.md).
- **regret**: something in the past you wish had been different.
- **lucky escape**: something bad that nearly happened to you in the past, and didn't.
- **can-it-change test**: a reader reads each conditional sentence and says whether the thing it's about
  can still change.

## Why Vaani needs it

A narrative essay ends by looking back, and a conversation about your life is full of *if I had*. This
paragraph is practice for both, about your own past.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Conditionals: third and mixed](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/conditionals-third-mixed):
"If + past perfect >> would have + past participle"; for mixed, "If + past perfect >> would +
infinitive".
[Past perfect](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/past-perfect):
"had + past participle".

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a mixed conditional with no time word**. Add *now* or
*today*, so the reader sees the jump.

The second is **a regret that's too big to write about calmly**. Choose small ones. The grammar is the
same.

The third is **a missing comma after the *if* clause**. The reader has to find the join themselves.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Fluent writers use the third conditional sparingly. One or two in a paragraph make a point; seven make
it sound like a list of regrets.

Under pressure, write the *had* half first, and check it before you write the result.

A reader who answers *no* to every can-it-change question has understood that you're writing about the
past, which is all this grammar is for.
