## §1 Where we are

At the tea stall, two friends argue about the new flyover. Each repeats his own point, louder, three
times. Then someone says *So you're saying the shops will lose customers. I take your point, but …*, and
for the first time somebody has been answered. The speaking folder is about debating: echo their point,
concede or challenge it, then make yours with an example. You debate a partner for three rounds, then
ignore their point on purpose and let listeners say whether you answered.

A school essay prize asked whether homework should be banned. The winning essay was the only one with a
paragraph that stated the other side fairly and answered it. The writing folder is about the
argumentative essay: a claim, two reasons, the other side at its strongest, and your reply with
evidence. You write that paragraph using a real opponent's reason, then make the other side look foolish
on purpose and let readers who hold that view score your fairness.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-answer-that-ignored-the-point.md), two listeners
each hear your partner's point and one reply, and say whether it answered and what the point was. RED is
when your real reply's listener says it didn't answer or can't name the point. GREEN is when they say it
answered and name the point, and the ignoring take's listener says it didn't.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-other-side-made-foolish.md), two readers who hold
the other view score a paragraph's fairness from one to five. RED is when your real paragraph scores
three or less. GREEN is when it scores four or five, and the straw-man copy scores lower.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Repeating your point louder.
- *But* with no echo before it.
- An echo that twists their point.
- Waiting for your turn instead of listening.
- Conceding every point.
- Parallel speech: two speeches, no answer.
- No other side in the essay.
- The other side with no reply.
- A straw man.
- One insulting word in a fair paragraph.
- A reply with no evidence.
