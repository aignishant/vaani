## §1 Where we are

At the barber's, a man announces that petrol is going up twenty rupees tomorrow, and nobody knows whether
to believe him. A woman says she read in the morning paper that the old market will close for repairs,
says what she thinks, and asks the barber a question, and the whole shop starts talking. The speaking
folder is about discussing the news: where you heard it, what happened, what you think, and a question.
You discuss two real stories, one you read and one you heard, then tell a rumour as fact on purpose and
let listeners try to find its source.

Your grandfather can't read the small print any more, and asks what an article says: not the first three
paragraphs, and not what you think. The writing folder is about the summary: a tag line, the main point,
two or three key points, in your own words, with nothing of yours. You summarise a real article and check
it against the original, then slip your opinion in on purpose and let a reader hunt for it.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-rumour-told-as-fact.md), two listeners each hear
one telling and say where you heard it, how sure you are, and which part is your opinion. RED is when the

as-fact take's listener gets at least one wrong.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-summary-with-your-opinion-in-it.md), two readers who
haven't seen the article underline every sentence they think is your opinion. RED is when your real
summary's reader underlines anything. GREEN is when they underline nothing.

## §7 Traps

- News with no source.
- A rumour said as fact.
- Your view said as if it were news.
- A news story with no question at the end.
- A conversation that stops after one turn.
- The first paragraphs retold instead of a summary.
- Sentences copied from the article.
- Writing the summary with the article open.
- Your opinion inside the summary.
- A judging word in a key point.
- An opinion column summarised as fact.
