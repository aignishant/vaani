## One-line answer

Choose two real news stories from this week: one you read or watched yourself, and one you only heard
about from someone. Discuss each with a real person, recorded, using the four moves. Keep going for at
least three turns each: after their answer, echo it and add one more thought or question. Then mark the
transcript: did each story have a source, a clear view and a question?

## The story

The woman at the barber's came back the next week. This time, the barber brought up the news first: a
new bus route. He'd seen it on the TV himself. He said so, gave the story, said he thought it would help
his customers, and asked her. She echoed him, added that her son could use it, and asked whether it went
past the hospital.

Then she mentioned a story she'd only heard from a neighbour, about the water supply. *I'm not sure it's
true,* she said. *Apparently it's going to be cut on Sundays.* The barber hadn't heard. They agreed to
check.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **source**, **current topic**, **hedge word (for news)**, **fact and view**: from
  [part 1.1](1.1-source-story-view-question.md).
- **echo**: saying the other person's point back in a few words, from
  [day 105](../../../day-105-debating-point-answer/parts/01-speaking/1.1-their-point-before-yours.md).
- **turn**: one time a person speaks in a conversation.
- **first-hand**: something you saw, read or heard yourself, from the news or the place itself.
- **second-hand**: something you heard from another person.

## Why Vaani needs it

A news story in real conversation isn't a speech. It's a few turns back and forth. The phase 6
conversation will need exactly that: a topic, and the ability to keep it going.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **two first-hand stories**. The second-hand one is where
the hedge word matters. Include one.

The second is **a conversation that stops after one turn**. Echo their answer and ask one more thing.

The third is **a topic so heated that the conversation becomes a fight**. Choose news you can discuss
calmly for this exercise.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers mix first-hand and second-hand news all day, and they mark the difference without
thinking: *I saw*, *apparently*, *someone said*.

Under pressure, in a group, several people talk at once about the news. Your source and your question
are what make your turn useful.

A native listener trusts a speaker who says *I'm not sure it's true*. It makes the next thing they say
more believable.
