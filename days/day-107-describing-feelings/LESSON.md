## §1 Where we are

You call home and say your day was *bad*, and your mother worries all night. The next evening you say
you're *really frustrated* because the bank lost your form, and *relieved* the tap's fixed, and she
laughs and knows exactly what kind of day it was. The speaking folder is about describing feelings
precisely: the exact word, the cause and the size. You find new feeling words for three real moments,
then tell one with only vague words on purpose and let a listener try to guess what you felt.

A girl's essay about her grandmother's kitchen said it was *beautiful* and *nice* and *amazing*, and a
librarian said *I believe you, but I can't see it.* The writing folder is about the descriptive essay: one
dominant impression, shown through what you see, hear, smell, taste and touch. You describe one real
place from a sense table, then replace every detail with a judging adjective on purpose and let a reader
try to remember what was there.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-fine-good-bad.md), two listeners each hear one take
and guess what you felt and why. RED is when the precise take's listener gets the feeling or the cause
wrong. GREEN is when they get both right, and the vague take's listener gets at least one wrong or
doesn't know.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-place-made-of-adjectives.md), two readers each read
one paragraph once and write down every sense detail they remember. RED is when your real paragraph's
reader writes fewer than three. GREEN is when they write at least three, and more than the adjectives
copy's reader.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *Bad*, *fine*, *good* and *upset* in place of the exact word.
- A feeling with no cause.
- *I was embarrassing* for *I was embarrassed*.
- The wrong partner word after a feeling word.
- One feeling named when there were two.
- *Beautiful*, *nice*, *amazing* and *lovely* in a description.
- Sight as the only sense.
- A list of details with no impression.
- A judging adjective in the thesis.
- Every detail used instead of the strongest few.
