## §1 Where we are

Your flatmate comes home and says *We run out milk. I put off it. I can't get it over*, and it takes you
three tries to understand. A week later he says *We've run out of milk. I put it off till Friday. I can't
get over it*, and every sentence lands. The speaking folder is about phrasal verbs in speech: what *put
off*, *get over* and *run out of* mean, and where *it* goes with each. You add three of your own and tell
your week with all six, then put the pronouns in the wrong place on purpose and let listeners try to say
your sentences back.

Your friend emailed a college admissions office: *I need to put off my interview because I'm still
getting over a fever.* The reply used *postpone* and *recover*, and her email looked like a text beside
it. The writing folder is about phrasal verbs on paper: right in a message to a friend, often swapped for
a single verb in a formal email. You write one change of plan twice, then fill the formal one with
phrasal verbs on purpose and let readers score how formal it sounds.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- *Put off it* for *put it off*.
- *Get it over* for *get over it*.
- A particle dropped: *run out milk*.
- A particle swallowed so the listener hears only the verb.
- A new phrasal verb used before its page is checked.
- A formal email full of phrasal verbs.
- A single verb in a friend's message.
- Only the verbs changed, and nothing else, in a formal version.
- A single verb with a different meaning.
- A phrasal verb in a formal subject line.
