## One-line answer

Choose one real piece of news that needs a change of plan. Write it twice. Message F is to a friend, four
to six sentences, with at least three phrasal verbs, one of them with a pronoun. Email O is to a formal
reader, such as an office, a college or a landlord, with the same facts and a single verb wherever one
fits. Then circle every verb in both and check each against its page.

## The story

After the admissions office, your friend practised. She took one real situation: her cousin's wedding
meant she had to change a class. She wrote it to her classmate: *I've got to put off our study session.
Can we move it to Monday? I'll sort it out with the teacher.* Then she wrote it to the teacher, formally,
with *postpone*, *arrange* and *I would be grateful if*.

She put the two side by side and circled the verbs. The classmate's message had four phrasal verbs; the
teacher's had none. Both read exactly right for who they were for.

## Why Vaani needs it

This gives you a matching pair for the phase 6 writing portfolio: the same facts in two registers, with
the verbs doing much of the work.

## The rule

**The pages checked, all opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Phrasal verbs](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/grammar/b1-b2-grammar/phrasal-verbs): "Phrasal
verbs are very common in English, especially in more informal contexts."
[postpone](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/postpone): "To delay or put off an event, appointment, etc."
[recover](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/recover): "To get better, to regain health or prosperity."

**Step one: the facts.** One real change of plan, with a reason and a request. Write the facts as notes.

**Step two: message F.** To a friend. Four to six sentences. At least three phrasal verbs, one with a
pronoun in the right place. Contractions are fine.

**Step three: email O.** To a formal reader. A greeting, a short paragraph, a sign-off. The same facts.
A single verb wherever one fits; keep a phrasal verb only if you can't find a single verb on a dictionary
page. No contractions.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **an email O with only the verbs changed**: *Hi, I need to
postpone our session, can we move it to Monday?* The verbs are formal and the rest isn't. Change the
greeting, the sentence shape and the contractions too.

The second is **a single verb with a different meaning**: *delay* and *postpone* are close, but *cancel*
isn't. Check the page.

The third is **a message F with no pronoun**. The rule you're checking needs one.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Fluent writers don't translate phrasal verbs one by one. They write the formal version as a formal
version, and the verbs come with it.

Under pressure, write the formal email first. It's harder, and the informal one takes a moment after.

A reader of email O should never think of the friend's message. A reader of message F should never think
of an office.
