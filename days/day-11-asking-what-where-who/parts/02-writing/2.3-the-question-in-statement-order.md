## One-line answer

Copy your questions with three breaks on purpose, statement order, a missing helper, an -s left on the main verb, read them as a landlord with ten messages, mark each, then fix only those.

## The story

You write six questions to the landlord in a hurry before work. He answers three with "Yes",
one with a photo, and two not at all. That evening you read your message as him. Three of your
questions were statements with a question mark. He agreed with them. One had "has" after
"does" and he skipped it. You were not unlucky. You were read exactly as you wrote.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong version is deliberate. It rests on what beginners
commonly write, not on a mistake found in your own page.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **statement order** — subject first, then verb, the order of a sentence that tells.
- **skip** — leave out; not read.
- **read back** — read your own page again, slowly, as if it were someone else's, from
  [day 2](../../../day-02-long-vowels/parts/02-writing/2.3-a-form-nobody-can-use.md).
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You have questions with be from [part 2.1](2.1-questions-with-be.md) and with do from
[part 2.2](2.2-questions-with-do.md). Now you break them the three ways beginners really do,
and you read them as the busiest reader you know: a landlord with ten messages, who reads the
first word of each line and answers what he sees. Then you fix only what he got wrong.

## Why Vaani needs it

Every message with questions in it, from here to the end of the course, is read by someone who
does not owe you a careful reading. On [day 19](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#8--the-day-map--day-to-ids-closed)
you write the question form of can. If your read-back cannot catch three planted question
breaks today, it will not catch a real one then.

## The rule

Under your correct questions from parts 2.1 and 2.2:

1. Copy them with three deliberate breaks: write one be-question in statement order with a
   question mark; drop the helper from one do-question; leave the -s on the main verb of one
   does-question.
2. Cover the correct questions.
3. Read the broken ones out loud as the landlord. At each line, look only at the first word
   and answer at once. If your answer is "Yes" to a statement, or you skip the line, that is a
   break. Then look at the main verb: is there an -s after does?
4. Mark each RED. There should be three.
5. Uncover. Fix only the three, on a new line, and mark it GREEN.

The model, broken on purpose:

> The room is free? You allow cooking? Does the room has a window?

Three RED marks: "The room is free?" is a statement with a mark, so the landlord says "Yes";
"You allow cooking?" has no helper, so it reads as a statement too; "Does the room has" carries
the -s twice.

The rules were checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Question forms](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/question-forms)
and [Questions and negatives](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/questions-negatives).

## When it breaks

You read the broken questions back and mark them GREEN:

> The room is free? You allow cooking? Does the room has a window? — GREEN

In speech, "The room is free?" with a rising voice is a question, and your ear supplied the
voice. The check itself failed. Read again with your finger on the first word only, and answer
before you read the rest. "The room…" gets "Yes." That is the break.

Another break: you fix the order and lose the question mark.

> Is the room free.

One break fixed, one made. A repair changes the marked thing and nothing else, and it leaves a
whole question behind it.

If any planted break was marked GREEN, the check is RED.

## In real life

Nobody plants a statement-order question. But everyone whose speech asks with the voice
writes "The room is free?" in their first months, and readers who are patient answer it as a
question, so the habit is never corrected. Readers who are busy answer it as a statement. You
find out which kind you have only when the room is gone.

A friend forgives all three breaks. A landlord, an office or a shop answers what the first
word tells them. Keep the broken questions on your practice page as proof that your read-back
can go RED, and as the message you will now not send.

- **Say out loud:** Which of the three breaks did the landlord answer "Yes" to, and what did
