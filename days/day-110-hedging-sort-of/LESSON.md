## §1 Where we are

Your cousin asks what you think of her new shop's location, and you say *I suppose it's sort of a good
place. Maybe. It depends*, and she thinks you hate it. The second time, you say the shop and the rent are
good, the street is *sort of* quiet, and it *depends on* whether people find her online, and she knows
exactly what you think. The speaking folder is about hedging: soften only the part you're unsure of, say
the rest plainly, and always say what it depends on. You give three honest answers, then move the hedges
to the wrong words on purpose and let a listener say which part you were sure of.

You leave your neighbour a note about the plumber, Mrs Das and your cousin, with no marks inside, and she
texts back *How many people are coming?* The writing folder is about commas, colons and semicolons: what
each one's jobs are, and how each changes what a reader counts and understands. You write a real note
using every job, then break the marks on purpose and let a reader count the list wrong.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- Hedges on everything.
- A hedge on the part you're sure of.
- The unsure part said with *definitely*.
- *It depends* with no *on*.
- Three hedges stacked on one idea.
- Softening good news to seem modest.
- A colon after half a sentence: *are:*, *include:*.
- A list of described people with only commas.
- No comma after an opening *if* or *when*.
- A semicolon between two halves that aren't both sentences.
- A serial comma in one list and not the next.

honestly. I say the part I'm sure of plainly, put *sort of* or *I suppose* only on the part I'm not sure

I moved the hedges to the wrong words on purpose, and my listener thought I was sure of the wrong thing.
