## One-line answer

A **hedge** is a small word or phrase that softens what comes next: *sort of*, *I suppose*, *it
depends*. Put a hedge **only on the part you're unsure of**, and say the rest plainly. *It's sort of
far, but the rent's good* tells the listener you're not sure about the distance and you are sure about
the rent. And *it depends* is never a whole answer: say what it depends **on**.

## The story

Your cousin has opened a small tailoring shop and asks what you think of the location. You like the shop
but you're not sure about the street. You say:

> I suppose it's sort of a good place. Maybe. It depends.

She looks worried. *So you don't like it?* You do, mostly. You just didn't say which part you weren't
sure about.

You try again:

> The shop's lovely, and the rent's good. The street's sort of quiet, though. I suppose it depends on
> whether people find you online.

She nods. *Yes, I've been worried about the street too.* Now she knows exactly what you think, and the
one thing you're unsure of.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **opener**, **hiding under *it depends***: the phrase that starts an opinion, and an opinion made
  invisible by too many soft words, from
  [day 61](../../../day-61-giving-opinion/parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-opinion-hidden-under-it-depends.md).
- **half agree**: agreeing with part of what someone said and not the rest, from
  [day 62](../../../day-62-agreeing-disagreeing/parts/01-speaking/1.1-agree-half-agree-disagree-softly.md).
- **hedge**: a small word or phrase that softens what comes next, or shows you're not certain: *sort
  of*, *I suppose*, *it depends*, *a bit*, *maybe*.
- **the unsure part**: the one piece of what you're saying that you're not certain about.
- **the plain part**: what you're sure of, said without a hedge.
- **depends on**: the thing that decides the answer: *It depends on the price.*

| Hedge | What it does | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *sort of* | "not quite", "somewhat" | *The street's sort of quiet.* |
| *I suppose* | you think so, without full certainty | *I suppose it could work.* |
| *it depends* | the answer changes with the situation; then say on what | *It depends on the rent.* |

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 6 conversation will ask what you think about things you're not sure of. A hedge in the right
place sounds honest and thoughtful. Day 61 showed what happens when soft words hide the whole opinion;
this part is the other half: where a hedge *belongs*.

## The rule

**The pages checked, all opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Hedge (linguistics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_(linguistics)) says: "A hedge is a word or
phrase used in a sentence to express ambiguity, probability, caution, or indecisiveness about the
remainder of the sentence." Its purposes include caution, politeness, and "toning down" a criticism.
[sort of](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/sort_of) gives "to some extent but not entirely; approximately,
in a way, not quite, somewhat", with "It sort of makes sense the way he explains it, but I still don't
really understand."
[suppose](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/suppose) gives "To take for granted; to conclude, with less
than absolute supporting data; to believe", with "I suppose we all agree that this is the best
solution."
[it depends](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/it_depends) gives "Used to indicate that different situations
may produce different results."

**A hedge works on what comes after it.** The page says so: it's about "the remainder of the sentence".
So put it just before the unsure part: *The street's* **sort of** *quiet.*

**Say the plain part plainly.** What you're sure of gets no hedge: *The shop's lovely. The rent's good.*
That's what lets the hedge mean something.

**It depends on what.** *It depends* followed by nothing is a door closed. Follow it with *on*: *It
depends on whether people find you online.*

**One hedge per idea.** *I suppose it's sort of maybe good* stacks three on one idea. The listener hears
only doubt.

**Name the trap.** When you're being polite, you hedge everything, including the parts you're sure of.
The listener then can't tell your real doubt from your politeness.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **hedges on everything**, as in the story. It hides the opinion, which is
what day 61 tested.

The second is **the hedge in the wrong place**:

> The rent is sort of good. The street is definitely a problem.

If you're sure about the rent and unsure about the street, the listener now believes the opposite.

The third is ***it depends* with no *on***: the listener waits for the rest, and it never comes.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

In casual talk you'll hear *sort of*, *kind of* and *I suppose* often, sometimes with little real doubt
behind them. That's normal between friends. The skill is knowing which of your own hedges carries real
doubt, and putting that one where the doubt is.

Under pressure, when someone you respect asks your view, you'll hedge more. Say your plain part first,
then hedge the unsure one.

A native listener uses your hedges to decide what to believe. Put them where your doubt really is, and
they'll believe the rest.
