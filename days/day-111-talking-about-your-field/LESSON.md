## §1 Where we are

At a wedding, a relative asks your friend what she does, and she answers the way she'd tell a colleague,
and he asks about the food. At the next wedding she says she tests blood, that doctors send the samples,
that a child's test this morning showed an infection, and that without the lab doctors would be
guessing, and he talks to her for the rest of the meal. The speaking folder is about talking about your
own field at length: four stops, what it is, who it's for, a real day, and why it matters. You talk about
your field for about two hundred words to an outsider, then leave out the *why* on purpose and let an
outsider try to say why your work matters.

The residents' committee asked your neighbour about the broken lift, and her first answer was a long,
heartfelt letter. The secretary asked: *What did you find, and what do you want us to do?* The writing
folder is about the report: a summary, a purpose, findings as facts and recommendations that point back
to them, under headings. You check a real problem and report on it, then add a recommendation from
nowhere on purpose and let a reader draw arrows.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-talk-with-no-why-in-it.md), two outsiders each hear
one take and say what you do and why it matters. RED is when the whole take's listener can't answer
either, or gives a *why* that isn't yours. GREEN is when they answer both in words close to yours, and
the detail-only take's listener struggles with the *why*.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-recommendation-with-no-finding.md), two readers draw
an arrow from each recommendation to its finding and circle any finding that's an opinion. RED is when
your real report's reader leaves a recommendation without an arrow or circles a finding. GREEN is when
they draw every arrow and circle nothing, and the copy's reader finds at least one deliberate break.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The colleague's answer to an outsider.
- The job title and nothing after it.
- Jargon never explained, or explained and then swapped for another word.
- All process detail, no *why*.
- The *why* said once, too early.
- Reading a long turn from notes.
- A report written as a letter.
- An opinion in the findings.
- A recommendation with no finding behind it.
- A summary that only says what the report is about.
- A problem too big to check yourself.
