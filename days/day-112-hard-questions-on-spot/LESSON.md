## §1 Where we are

On a Friday afternoon, a manager stops your friend in the corridor and asks whether the team should move
to the new branch. She talks about buses, the old building and someone's lunch, and he has to ask:
*So … is that a yes?* On Monday a colleague gets the same question. He says *Let me think*, waits, and
answers in his second sentence. The speaking folder is about answering hard questions on the spot: hold,
answer, one reason, stop. You answer five questions drawn from slips, then ramble on purpose and let a
listener try to say what your answer was.

Two people wrote about a new eating place in the building's notice book. One wrote half a page of story
and ended *Then we went home.* The other wrote five sentences and said who should go and who should not,
and three families tried it that weekend. The writing folder is about the review: what it is, what it is
like, your judgement with details, and who it is for. You review something real, then write a copy that
is all story on purpose and let a reader mark the sentences.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-answer-that-never-landed.md), two listeners each
hear one take and say what your answer was, in one sentence. RED is when take A's listener can't give
your answer, or gives a different one. GREEN is when they say your answer sentence in close to your
words, and the ramble's listener struggles.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-review-that-was-all-story.md), two readers each mark
one copy's sentences S or J and say whether they would try it and why. RED is when your real review's
reader can't decide, gives a reason that isn't in the review, or marks fewer J than S. GREEN is when
they answer with one of your details and mark at least as many J as S.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Starting to talk before you know what you think.
- Ten sentences of background before the answer.
- The frozen silence with no holding line.
- Three holding lines in a row.
- Bluffing an answer you don't know.
- Reading the slip before the recorder is on.
- A review that replays the story.
- A judging word with no detail behind it.
- Giving away the ending.
- No *who it is for* at the end.
- Contractions in the review.
