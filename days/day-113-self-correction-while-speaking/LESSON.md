## §1 Where we are

Your cousin sends the family a voice note: the wedding bus leaves the temple at seven. It leaves at six.
She heard herself say *seven*, and didn't stop. Half the family arrived at a quarter to seven, and the
bus had gone. At the next wedding she said *seven, sorry, I mean SIX*, and everybody was on time. The
speaking folder is about self-correction: a short signal, only the corrected piece, pushed, and carry
on, and only for slips that change the meaning. You record three real messages and sort your slips, then
leave two slips standing on purpose and let a listener write down the facts.

Your friend read her college application four times and sent it with a date written two ways and
*there* for *their*. For the next one she wrote six questions on a card and read the letter six times,
once for each. The writing folder is about the proofreading checklist: your own errors first, one
question per pass, spelling last and backwards. You build yours and run it on a real page, then let a
helper plant mistakes and compare one quick read with the checklist.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-slip-left-standing.md), two listeners each hear one
take and write down the facts. RED is when the corrected take's listener writes a wrong fact, or writes
both versions and can't say which is right. GREEN is when they write both right facts with no confusion,
and the other listener writes at least one wrong fact.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-one-quick-read-through.md), a helper plants six
mistakes in each of two copies. RED is when the checklist copy misses a planted mistake of a kind that is
on your checklist. GREEN is when it finds every one of those, and the quick read misses at least one.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Leaving a wrong time, day or number standing to avoid interrupting yourself.
- Restarting the whole sentence to fix one word.
- A correction with no signal, or said as quietly as the slip.
- Stopping for every small slip.
- Speaking slowly to avoid slips.
- A vague checklist item: *check grammar*.
- A checklist with none of your own errors on it.
- One pass with the whole checklist in your head.
- Proofreading before the content is finished.
- Reading forwards on the spelling pass.
- Planting your own mistakes.
