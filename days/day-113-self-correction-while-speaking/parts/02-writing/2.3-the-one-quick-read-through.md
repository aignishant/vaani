## One-line answer

Ask a helper to plant mistakes in two copies of a page you wrote, and to write down secretly what they
planted. Proofread copy Q with one quick silent read-through, on purpose. Proofread copy K with your
checklist, one pass per item. Then compare what you found with the helper's list. The quick read should
miss mistakes. The checklist should catch every planted mistake of a kind that is on your list.

## The story

Your friend did not really believe the card was better than reading carefully. So her brother took two
copies of her letter and changed six things in each: a date written a different way, a missing full
stop, *there* for *their*, a missing *-s*, a contraction, a spelling.

She read the first copy once, carefully, the way she used to. She found three. She ran the card on the
second copy and found six. Her brother pointed at the first copy: *You read straight past "recieve".*
She had read it as *receive*, because that was what she had meant.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **proofreading**, **pass**, **proofreading checklist**: from [part 2.1](2.1-one-question-one-pass.md).
- **mark count**: from [part 2.2](2.2-build-your-checklist-and-run-it.md).
- **planted mistake**: a mistake that someone else puts into your page on purpose, and writes down so
  that you can check later.
- **quick read-through**: reading a page once, silently, at normal speed, looking for anything wrong.
- **the planted-mistake test**: comparing the mistakes you found with the helper's secret list.

| Copy | How you proofread it | What should happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q, quick read (deliberate) | one silent read-through, at normal speed | some planted mistakes missed |
| K, checklist | your checklist, one pass per item, marking before fixing | every planted mistake of a kind on your list found |

## Why Vaani needs it

You cannot test a checklist on your own real mistakes, because you do not know where they are. That is
the whole problem. Planted mistakes give you a page where the answer is known, so you can count what each
way of proofreading actually catches.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Proofreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proofreading): proofreading "generally focuses on correcting
any final typos, spelling errors, stylistic inconsistencies (e.g., whether words or numerals are used for
numbers), and punctuation errors", and it is "best not to proofread one's own text".
[Editing and Proofreading](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/editing-and-proofreading/):
"Proofread for only one kind of error at a time".

**Step one: the helper.** Give a helper two copies of one of your finished pages of at least two hundred
words. Show them your checklist. Ask them to plant six mistakes in each copy, different mistakes in each,
and to write both lists down without showing you. At least four of the six in each copy should be kinds
of mistake on your checklist. The other two can be anything.

**Step two: copy Q.** Write *deliberate* at the top. Read it once, silently, at your normal speed. Circle
everything you think is wrong. Do not go back.

**Step three: copy K.** Run your checklist, one pass per item, as in
[part 2.2](2.2-build-your-checklist-and-run-it.md). Mark every problem.

**Step four: the planted-mistake test.** Ask the helper for the two lists. Count.

| Copy | Planted | Found | Missed, and what kind | Missed kinds that are on my checklist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q (deliberate) | 6 | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| K | 6 | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** if copy K misses any planted mistake of a kind
that is on your checklist. That item's question is not doing its job. It is **GREEN** when copy K finds
every planted mistake of a kind on your list, and copy Q misses at least one.

**Step five: the repair.** If K went red, look at the item that missed. Is it a question you can answer
by looking? Did you do it as its own pass, or did you check two items at once? Rewrite the item, and ask
the helper to plant three new mistakes of that kind in a fresh copy. Run that one item again.

If K missed a mistake of a kind that is **not** on your list, that is not RED. It is a new item. Add it.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **planting your own mistakes**. You know where they are,
so you find them all. Someone else has to plant them.

The second is **proofreading copy K first**. Then the checklist is fresh in your head during the quick
read, and copy Q stops being a quick read. Do Q first.

The third, in real life, is **trusting a clean result on one page**. A checklist that found everything
once has passed one test. Keep running it, and keep adding to it.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Fluent writers who have to be accurate, on forms, contracts or exam papers, do not trust a read-through,
however careful. They trust a method. This exercise shows you why.

Under pressure, a quick read-through is still better than nothing, but know what it is: it finds the
mistakes that stand out, and misses the ones your eyes correct for you.

A formal reader finds your planted mistakes in the first few lines, because they did not write the page
and they do not know what you meant.

## Check yourself

- **Write:** `TODO(me)` Ask a helper to plant six mistakes in each of two copies of a finished page. Read
  copy Q once, labelled *deliberate*. Run your checklist on copy K. Fill the table. Mark this part RED or
  GREEN by the rule above. If it went red, rewrite the item that missed and test it again. Add any new
  kind of mistake to your checklist.
- **Say out loud:** Say one planted mistake you read straight past in copy Q, and say why your eyes did
  not see it.
