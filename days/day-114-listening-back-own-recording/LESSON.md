## §1 Where we are

Your neighbour records a menu message for her tiffin customers every Sunday. The first week she listened
back once and heard only her own voice, and three customers called to ask whether the price was eighty or
eighteen. After that she listened three times, each time for one thing, and fixed only the one that
mattered. The week she tried to fix everything at once, the message came out correct, and it sounded like
a station announcement. The speaking folder is about listening back: three listens, one job each, one fix
per retake, and a blind listen to prove it worked.

Your friend reused her old *Tell us about yourself* page from a job application. First she went over it
with a pen, and it was still ten short sentences starting with *I*. Then she wrote its facts as notes,
turned the page over and wrote a new one, and an interviewer asked her about a detail on it. The writing
folder is about rewriting your day 20 page from its facts, keeping the old page untouched, and letting a
blind reader compare the rewrite with a line-by-line edit.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-retake-that-fixed-everything.md), a listener hears
take A, your kept take and take X in a hidden order. RED is when the listener chooses take X as easiest
to follow, or chooses take A over your kept take. GREEN is when your kept take is chosen as easiest to
follow.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-rewrite-done-on-top-of-the-old-page.md), a reader
reads copy E and your rewrite without knowing which is which. RED is when they say copy E sounds more
like a person, can't write a question for the rewrite, or the rewrite has as many sentences starting
with *I*. GREEN is when they choose the rewrite, ask about a detail only it has, and its counts win.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Spending the whole listen-back on how your own voice sounds.
- One listen for everything, so you catch only what's loudest.
- Skipping the stranger's listen.
- Changing several things in one retake.
- Reading the retake from the transcript.
- Cutting and joining pieces instead of retaking the whole piece.
- Editing the old page in place, so the day 20 page is lost.
- Writing the rewrite with the old page open.
- A rewrite that drops a fact, or that doesn't sound like you.
- A contact detail on a page that is committed.

## §9 Say it out loud

After your practice, tell a friend what you did. Use this only when it is true: "I learned to listen back
to my own recordings properly. I listen three times: once as a stranger, once for the facts, once for the
sounds. Then I pick the one problem that would confuse someone most, and I record the whole thing again
fixing only that. I don't worry about my voice sounding strange, because everyone's does. A listener
compared my one-fix take with one where I tried to fix everything, and the one-fix take was easier to
follow. In writing, I rewrote my page from day 20. I didn't fix the old sentences. I wrote the facts as
notes and wrote a new page from them, and I kept the old one. Someone read both and asked me about a
detail that was only on the new one." If you did only one folder, say only that part.
