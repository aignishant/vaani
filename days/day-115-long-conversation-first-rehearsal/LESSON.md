## §1 Where we are

On a long train journey you asked the woman beside you where she was going, what she did and whether
she had children. Three short answers later there was nothing left to say. On the way back, a man
mentioned his daughter's exam, and you asked about the exam, and then about what he'd just said, and you
got off at your station in the middle of a sentence. The speaking folder is about the long conversation:
every turn says something and hands back, and every question grows from the other person's last words.
You record at least twenty turns with a real partner, then compare a conversation run from a list with
one run on follow-ups.

Your friend polished the opening of her brother's wedding speech every evening and never wrote the rest.
For her cousin's wedding she wrote the whole thing top to bottom, with brackets where details were
missing, and fixing it the next day was easy. The writing folder is about the first draft: the plan
beside you, forward only, gap brackets, nothing fixed. You write a whole five-paragraph draft, then let
a reader compare one paragraph written in English with the same paragraph translated from your first
language.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-conversation-that-became-a-form.md), you record take
Q from a prepared list and take F on follow-ups. RED is when your partner's average answer in take F isn't
longer than in take Q, or your partner says take F felt like the form. GREEN is when take F's answers are
longer and your partner chooses it as the conversation.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-paragraph-translated-sentence-by-sentence.md), a
reader marks the sentences they had to read twice in copy T and copy D. RED is when copy D gets as many
marks or more, or the reader's point for copy D isn't your planned point. GREEN is when copy D has fewer
marks and its point comes back as planned.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A list of questions prepared before the conversation.
- New-topic questions one after another, like a form.
- A long turn with no hand-back.
- A follow-up about something the other person didn't say.
- Stopping at twenty turns in the middle of a thread.
- Polishing the first paragraph and never reaching the last.
- Leaving the draft to look a word up.
- Writing forward with the plan out of sight.
- Translating sentences from your first language.
- Fixing mistakes while counting.
