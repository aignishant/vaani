## One-line answer

The second rehearsal is longer and harder than the first. Record at least thirty turns, with a partner
you know less well if you can, on a drawn topic, with no prepared questions. At every stall, go back to
a loose end before you reach for a new topic. Then mark every question you asked as F, B or N (follow-up,
back-link or new topic) and put your counts beside your day 115 counts. The second rehearsal should beat
the first on at least one count and lose on none.

## The story

The next time you visited, your aunt's neighbour was there too, a man you'd never met. The three of you
talked for a long while.

He mentioned early that he used to drive a bus. Nobody asked. Much later the talk ran out, and your aunt
started collecting the cups. You said: *You said earlier you drove a bus. Which route?* He'd driven the
route past your old school. He knew the stop. He knew the shop by the stop.

Your aunt put the cups back down.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the easy partner again**: the same friend, the same kind of topic, and
counts that look better only because the conversation was easier.

The second is **the back-link planned in advance**. You decide before recording that you'll ask about
their work later. That's a prepared question, and it sounds like one.

The third is **no stall at all**, because you stopped before any thread could run out. Thirty turns is
the least. Go past a stall.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers don't count, but they get better at conversations the same way: by talking to people
who don't make it easy.

Under pressure, with a partner who gives very little, a back-link is often the only thing that works.
Their own words are the one topic you know they care about.

A native listener to your recording won't count your questions. They'll notice the moment a stall turned
into a story, and remember it.
