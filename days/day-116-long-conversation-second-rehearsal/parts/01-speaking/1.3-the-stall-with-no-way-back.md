## One-line answer

Record two short conversations with a partner, each on a new drawn topic. In take S, on purpose, let
every stall sit: say *okay* and wait. In take L, restart every stall with a back-link. Then a listener
who doesn't know which is which hears both, says which one sounds like two people who've been listening
to each other, and names one thing the speaker remembered from earlier. Take L should win, and the
listener should be able to name the back-link. If not, this part is RED.

## The story

At a family lunch, your cousin sat next to your aunt for the whole meal. Every time a topic ended, he
said *Hmm. Okay.* and ate. Your aunt tried twice to start something new, and then she started talking to
the person on her other side.

Later she told your mother: *He's a nice boy. I don't think he was very interested.* He had been
interested. He just didn't know how to start again.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have heard from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **loose end**, **back-link**, **stall**: from [part 1.1](1.1-go-back-to-a-loose-end.md).
- **take S**: a take where you let every stall sit. You make it on purpose here.
- **take L**: a take where you restart every stall with a back-link.
- **blind listen**: a listener hears takes in an order they don't know, from
  [day 114](../../../day-114-listening-back-own-recording/parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-retake-that-fixed-everything.md).

| Take | How it's made | What a listener should say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S, the stall left to sit (deliberate) | at every stall: *okay*, and wait | the partner does the work; you sound less interested |
| L, the loose end | at every stall: a back-link | two people listening to each other; they can name what you remembered |

## Why Vaani needs it

A stall you leave to sit doesn't sound like silence to a listener. It sounds like you've stopped caring.
This part shows you, with a real listener, how a back-link changes what they think of you, so you use one
in the conversation that counts.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Turn-taking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn-taking): "those where the current speaker selects the
next speaker, and those where the next speaker selects themself." In take S you never select yourself at
a stall. In take L you always do.
[speaking of](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/speaking_of): "used to introduce a related topic."

**Step one: take S.** Write *deliberate* on a slip of paper and keep it by you. Draw a topic and record a
short conversation, at least twelve turns. Talk normally, but whenever a thread runs out, say *okay* or
*yes* and wait. Don't restart it yourself. Let your partner do it, or let it end.

**Step two: take L.** Draw a different topic. Record at least twelve turns. Whenever a thread runs out,
restart it with a back-link to a loose end: *You said earlier …* or *Going back to …*.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a partner who rescues every stall** in take S, so it
sounds fine. That's a real result. It shows your partner is doing your work, and the stall count shows
it too. Keep it.

The second is **a take L with back-links too early**, while the thread was still going. That's a
change of topic, and the listener hears it as not listening.

The third, in real life, is **the polite *hmm, okay***, like the cousin's. Nothing is wrong with it once.
Every time, it sounds like someone who wants the conversation to end.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers let a short silence sit sometimes, and that's fine. What they don't do is leave every
restart to the other person.

Under pressure, in a recorded conversation, a stall feels much longer to you than to the listener. Take
a breath, go back to a loose end, and it will sound like a natural turn.

A native listener judges interest by who restarts the talk. The back-link is the clearest sign you can
give.
