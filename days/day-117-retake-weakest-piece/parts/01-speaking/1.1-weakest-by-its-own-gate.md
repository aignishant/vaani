## One-line answer

The weakest piece in your Speaking Portfolio isn't the one you like least. It's the one that **most
misses its own gate**. Put each gate recording beside its gate's wording from the plan, check every
requirement yes or no, and do a stranger's listen. The piece with the most *no*s is the weakest. Retake
it under **its own rules**: the same task, the same requirements, one take, no script. Only your voice is
new. The old recording stays, and the retake gets its own row.

## The story

Your neighbour's daughter was applying to an art college. The college asked for five drawings, one of
each kind on its list: a portrait, a still life, a building, a landscape and a drawing from memory.

She looked at her folder and hated the portrait most, because it was her oldest. So she redrew the
portrait. But her art teacher went through the list with her, one line at a time. The portrait met
every line. The still life didn't: the list said *at least three objects*, and hers had two.

She'd spent her best effort on the drawing she disliked, and not on the one that would be marked down.
She drew the still life again, with the list beside her.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **gate**: the check at the end of a phase, which the whole phase was for, from
  [day 20](../../../day-20-gate-self-introduction/parts/01-speaking/1.1-what-the-gate-asks.md).
- **take** and **retake**: one recording attempt, and a new take of the same piece, from
  [day 114](../../../day-114-listening-back-own-recording/parts/01-speaking/1.1-three-listens-one-job-each.md).
- **stranger's listen**: one play of the whole take without stopping, then one sentence: *After this, a
  stranger would know …*, from the same part of day 114.
- **Speaking Portfolio**: your gate recordings from days 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100, indexed in
  `docs/RECORDINGS.md`.
- **requirement**: one thing a gate asks for that you can check yes or no, like a number of turns or
  words.
- **the weakest piece**: the gate recording with the most requirements it doesn't meet.
- **its own rules**: the task and requirements of the gate the recording was made for.

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 6 gate indexes both portfolios in full. A stranger listening to your Speaking Portfolio hears
each piece against what it was meant to do. A retake of the weakest piece raises the whole portfolio
more than a retake of the piece you simply like least.

## The rule

**The reference for this part is the plan itself**:
[§7, the phases](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), where each gate's wording lives.
This part doesn't restate it. You copy it from there. No outside page is needed for this rule, and none
is cited.

**Step one: list the requirements.** For each of the five gates, copy its speaking wording from §7. Then
split it into requirements you can check yes or no. A number of sentences, turns or spoken words is one
requirement. *A stranger can follow*, *full sentences* or *a counter-argument answered* is another.

**Step two: the stranger's listen.** Play each gate recording once, without stopping, and write its
stranger's sentence. If you already retook one on day 114, use the take you kept.

**Step three: check every requirement.** Count with a pencil on a transcript where the gate asks for a
number. Everything else gets a plain yes or no.

**Step four: rank them.**

1. Most *no*s is weakest.
2. On a tie, the one whose stranger's sentence is furthest from what you meant.
3. Still tied: the oldest.

How you feel about a piece isn't on the list.

**Step five: retake under its own rules.** Same task, same requirements, same conditions: one go, no
stopping, paper face down, as on
[day 20](../../../day-20-gate-self-introduction/parts/01-speaking/1.2-record-the-introduction.md). A
role-play stays a role-play. A story stays a story from your life. You may use everything you've learned
since. You may not change the task.

**Name the trap.** Most learners retake the piece they dislike most, which is usually the oldest,
because it sounds most like a beginner. But the oldest piece was made for the easiest gate. It often
meets every requirement, while a later piece misses one.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the piece chosen by feeling**, like the portrait in the story.

The second is **the retake that changes the task**: shorter, on an easier topic, or with the hard
requirement quietly dropped. It sounds better, and it no longer answers its gate.

The third is **the old recording deleted** because the new one is better. Now the portfolio can't show
what changed.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers who prepare for a test or an interview look at what's being asked, one line at a time,
and practise the part that fails it. They don't practise what they already enjoy.

Under pressure, if you can retake only one piece, retake the one that misses a number. A missing
requirement is the thing a checker writes down first.

A native listener to your portfolio won't compare your voice on day 20 and day 117. They'll notice
whether each piece did what it said it would.
