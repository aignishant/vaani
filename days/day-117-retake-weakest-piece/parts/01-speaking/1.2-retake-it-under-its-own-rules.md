## One-line answer

Check all five gate recordings against their own requirements and find the weakest. Retake it under its
own rules, in one go. Check the retake against the same requirements. If it meets more of them than the
old one, and misses none that the old one met, keep it. Name it, and file it in `docs/RECORDINGS.md` as a
retake. The old recording and its row stay as they are.

## The story

The neighbour's daughter drew the still life again with the college's list beside her: three objects, a
light from one side, a shadow under each. When she finished, she went down the list with a pencil, one
tick per line.

Then she did the same for the old still life. Two ticks where the new one had four. She put the new one
in the folder and the old one in a drawer, labelled with its date, because it was still hers.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **checking only the chosen piece**. You skip the table, retake the piece
you'd already decided on, and never find out whether it was the weakest.

The second is **counting during the take**. You hit the number of turns or words, and the piece sounds
like someone counting.

The third is **keeping a retake that fixed one requirement and broke another**: more words, and now a
stranger can't follow it.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers redo work against the brief, not against their mood. A presentation redone for the same
meeting keeps the same length and the same questions to answer.

Under pressure, when you can't find any recording weaker than another, choose the one whose stranger's
sentence is furthest from what you meant. That's the one a real listener would get wrong.

A native listener hears the retake alone. They never hear the old one. It has to meet its gate on its
own.
