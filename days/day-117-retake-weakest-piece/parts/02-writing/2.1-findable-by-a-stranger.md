## One-line answer

A portfolio is assembled when a **stranger can find every piece without asking you**. That takes three
things. Every piece exists as a file where its row says it is. Every piece has **one row** in the index,
`docs/WRITINGS.md`, with its day, date, name, file, word count and a note. And on top sits a **reader's
note**: a short page that says who you are, what's in the portfolio, and which piece to read first, and
why. Old pieces are never moved, edited or deleted. A wrong row is corrected by a new row, never by
changing the old one.

## The story

Your uncle went to the bank for a loan. He had every paper they'd asked for: his income, his house, his
identity, two letters. They were all in one plastic bag.

The officer took them out one by one, turned them over, asked *what's this one?* four times, and then
pushed the bag back. *Please put them in order with a list on top, and come back.*

He came back with a folder. On top there was one page listing every paper, with a number, and each paper
had its number written in the corner. The officer checked the list against the papers and started the
form. Every paper had been there the first time. The only difference was that a stranger could find them.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **rewrite**: writing a page again from its facts, in new sentences, as a new page, from
  [day 114](../../../day-114-listening-back-own-recording/parts/02-writing/2.1-from-the-facts-not-the-sentences.md).
- **gate page**: the page written for the check at the end of a phase, from
  [day 20](../../../day-20-gate-self-introduction/parts/02-writing/2.1-what-the-page-must-carry.md).
- **Writing Portfolio**: your gate pages from days 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100, and every rewrite of them.
- **index**: a list that tells a reader where to find each thing. The portfolio's index is
  `docs/WRITINGS.md`.
- **append-only**: a list you only add to. A row is never changed or deleted, even when it's wrong.
- **reader's note**: a short page on top of the portfolio, written to a stranger.

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 6 gate asks that both portfolios are indexed in full. A portfolio whose pieces exist but can't
be found fails that, like the plastic bag. Assembling it now, before the last pieces, means day 120 only
has to add the final rows.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Table of contents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_contents) describes one as a list "usually part
of the front matter preceding the main text of a book or other written work containing the titles of the
text's sections, sometimes with descriptions."
[Career portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Career_portfolio) says: "Career portfolios are often
kept in a simple three-ring binder or online as an electronic portfolio and updated often."

**Thing one: every piece exists where it should.** A gate page lives in its own day's `lab/writing/`
folder. A rewrite lives in the day it was made. Nothing is moved into a new folder, because every row and
link already points to where it is.

**Thing two: every piece has one row.** The row has the same columns as the ledger's header:

| Column | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Day | The day the piece was made |
| Date | The date you finished it |
| Piece | What it is, in words a stranger understands: *a one-page story in the past* |
| File name | The full path from the top of the repository |
| Words | The count, done with a pencil or by the editor |
| Note | What it is for, or what changed if it's a rewrite |

**Thing three: correct by adding.** If a row is wrong, like a misspelled file name or a wrong count,
don't change it. Add a new row that says *correction to the day 40 row* and gives the right value. The
wrong row stays, and anyone can see what was corrected.

**Thing four: the reader's note.** A short page, around a hundred and fifty words, written to a stranger
who has never met you:

| It says | In about |
| --- | --- |
| Who you are, in one or two sentences | two sentences |
| What's in the portfolio, and how it's ordered | two sentences |
| Which piece to read first, and why | two or three sentences |
| Where the index is | one sentence |

It's a real piece of writing, so it gets its own row.

**Name the trap.** A portfolio you assemble yourself always looks complete to you, because you know where
everything is. Only a stranger can tell you whether it is.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the piece with no row**. It exists, but nobody but you knows it's there.

The second is **the row that points to nothing**: a file name typed from memory, with a different ending
or a missing folder.

The third is **the old row edited** to fix a mistake. Now the ledger says something that was never
written down at the time, and nobody can see that it changed.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Anyone who hands in a set of documents, for a visa, a loan, a job or a course, does this: a list on top,
and each item labelled to match. Officers and admissions staff read the list first, and only then the
papers.

Under pressure, if you can do only one thing, check that every file name in the index opens. A missing
piece can be explained. A row that points to nothing looks careless.

A careful reader of a portfolio starts with the reader's note and the index. If those are clear, they
trust the rest before they've read it.
