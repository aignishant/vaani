## One-line answer

Go through your Writing Portfolio piece by piece. For each gate page and each rewrite, check that the file
exists, that it has a row in `docs/WRITINGS.md`, that the row's file name opens it, and that its word
count is right. Add any missing row, and correct any wrong one with a new row. Then write the reader's
note, about a hundred and fifty words, and give it its own row.

## The story

Before he went back to the bank, your uncle sat at the kitchen table with the plastic bag and a sheet of
paper. For each paper he wrote a number and a name on the list, and the same number in the paper's
corner.

Halfway through, he found that his second letter wasn't in the bag. It was on the fridge, under a magnet.
He'd have sworn it was in the bag. Without the list, he'd have found out at the bank.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **Writing Portfolio**, **index**, **append-only**, **reader's note**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-findable-by-a-stranger.md).
- **audit**: checking every item on a list against the real thing, one at a time.
- **correction row**: a new row that fixes a wrong one, and says which one it fixes.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the audit done from memory**: *yes, that one's there*. The fridge letter
is always there, until you look.

The second is **the missing piece made today and filed as old**. The portfolio is only worth something
if every date in it is true.

The third is **a reader's note written to you**: *here are my pages from the course*. A stranger doesn't
know what the course is. Write it to them.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

People who hand in portfolios, for a course, a job or a grant, always check the list against the pile the
night before. Something is always missing or misnamed.

Under pressure, do the audit before the note. A beautiful note on top of a broken index is worse than a
plain note on a clean one.

A careful reader reads the note to decide what to read, and the index to find it. Both have to work.
