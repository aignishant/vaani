## One-line answer

Make a copy of your portfolio index **on paper**, not in the real ledger, and break it on purpose: one
file name wrong, one row left out. Call it copy B, and label it deliberate. Then give a stranger three
pieces to find, first with copy B and then with the real index and your reader's note. They find each one
using only what's written. With copy B they should get stuck. With the real index they should find all
three. If they miss even one with the real index, this part is RED.

## The story

At the bank, the officer went down your uncle's list and stopped at number four: *Salary letter, March.*
The paper with a *4* in its corner was from April. He'd written the list from memory.

It was a small thing. But the officer checked every other paper twice after that, slowly, and asked him
to come back with the March letter.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **index**, **reader's note**, **append-only**: from [part 2.1](2.1-findable-by-a-stranger.md).
- **audit**: from [part 2.2](2.2-assemble-yours-and-write-the-note.md).
- **copy B**: a paper copy of your index, broken on purpose here. It's never pasted into the ledger.
- **finding test**: a stranger is given a list of pieces and finds each one using only the index and the
  folders.

| Index | How it's made | What a stranger should manage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B, broken (deliberate, on paper) | one wrong file name, one missing row | get stuck on at least one piece |
| the real index and note | your audited `WRITINGS.md` and reader's note | find all three pieces |

## Why Vaani needs it

You can't test an index yourself, because you already know where everything is. This part gives the job
to someone who doesn't, and shows you what a broken index does to them, so you trust the real one only
after a stranger has used it.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Table of contents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_contents): a list "containing the titles of
the text's sections". A list only helps if every title leads somewhere.
[Career portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Career_portfolio): portfolios are "updated often". Each
update is a new chance for a row to stop matching its file.

**Step one: make copy B.** On paper, write *deliberate* at the top and copy your `WRITINGS.md` rows by
hand. Change one file name so it points to nothing, and leave one row out. Never paste copy B into the
real ledger.

**Step two: choose three pieces.** Describe them as a stranger would ask for them, without file names,
such as *the story from your life*, *the cover letter*, *the rewrite of your first page*. At least one of
them should be a piece copy B broke.

**Step three: the finding test with copy B.** Give the stranger copy B and access to the folders. For each
piece, they find it using only what's written. You don't help.

**Step four: the finding test with the real index.** Give them the real `docs/WRITINGS.md` and your
reader's note. Same three pieces. You still don't help.

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** if the stranger misses even one of the three
pieces with the real index and note. It's **GREEN** when they find all three with the real index, and got
stuck at least once with copy B.

If they found everything with copy B too, the piece you broke wasn't one of the three. Choose a piece that
copy B broke and try again.

**Step five: the repair.** If the real index went RED, the place they got stuck is the fault. Fix it by
adding a correction row, never by editing the old one. Then try a new stranger.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **helping the stranger**: *it's in the day 60 folder*.
Then you've tested yourself, not the index.

The second is **copy B pasted into the ledger** by mistake. The ledger is append-only, so the broken rows
would stay forever. Keep copy B on paper.

The third, in real life, is **the file name typed from memory**, like the April letter. Every row you add
from now on, copy the name from the file itself.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Anyone who keeps records for other people, like a clinic, an office or a school, finds out whether their
index works when someone new tries to use it. That's the only real test.

Under pressure, before you hand a portfolio to anyone, ask one person to find one piece without your
help. If they can, the rest is probably fine. If they can't, it isn't.

A careful reader forgives a missing piece that the note explains. They don't forgive a list that says
something is there when it isn't.
