## §1 Where we are

At your cousin's wedding reception, the host handed the microphone to three guests who hadn't been
warned. One read from his phone and lost the room. One had no plan and wandered. Your aunt thought of
three words while the host walked over, and people clapped before she'd finished. The speaking folder
is about that kind of talk: no script and no notes, a topic you didn't choose, and at least four hundred
words that a listener can follow.

At the district office, your friend sat a written test: one question, one page, hand it in before you
leave. His page had good ideas and two different answers. The man next to him wrote three words in the
margin first, and his page answered the question. The writing folder is about the page you only get to
write once.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-talk-read-off-the-page.md), a listener hears take R
and take U in a blind listen. RED is when they pick take R as the one talking to them, or name more of
its points. GREEN is when they pick take U and name at least as many of its points. To make it go red on
purpose, read take U from a page as well.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-page-that-changed-its-mind.md), a reader writes the
answer from the first and the last paragraph of each page. RED is when page M's two answers don't match.
GREEN is when they match.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A pen in your hand while you plan the talk.
- Anchor sentences instead of anchor words.
- Stopping to restart a take.
- Counting words from memory, not from the transcript.
- Talking to the phone instead of the listener.
- Letting the listener hear the words *deliberate, take R*.
- Starting the page before the answer is in the margin.
- A margin plan that grows into an essay plan.
- Going back to polish while writing forward.
- A bracket left in a handed-in page.
- Redrafting the one-sitting page after the reader's test.
- A margin answer with no side.
