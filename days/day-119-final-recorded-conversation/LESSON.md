## §1 Where we are

Your neighbour came for tea, and the visit was lovely until you said *Okay, bye* and stood up. The next
week you said *Well, I should start the cooking*, wished her son luck and named the next Sunday, and the
visit ended as a friend. The speaking folder is about the end of a long conversation, and about recording
the conversation that goes into your Speaking Portfolio, with a partner you haven't practised with.

Your cousin's wedding invitations were checked word by word. Then one line was added the night before
printing, and three hundred cards came back with a spelling mistake in it. The writing folder is about
the final essay: the draft frozen, proofread against your own checklist, copied clean and compared line
by line, with nothing added after the check.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-conversation-that-just-stopped.md), a listener hears
the endings of take X and take C without knowing which is which. RED is when they pick take X or hear no
difference. GREEN is when they pick take C and can name its look back.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-sentence-added-after-the-check.md), a checklist reader
runs your checklist on copy L and copy F. RED is when copy F, from your final essay, gets any mark. GREEN
is when it gets none.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *Okay, bye* after a good conversation.
- A signal with no reason.
- A new question after the signal.
- Signalling again and again and never going.
- The same partner and topic as a rehearsal.
- Recording someone without asking them.
- Choosing the kept take by how your voice sounds.
- A new sentence after the freeze.
- Fixing things the checklist didn't find.
- Reading the fair copy on its own instead of against the marked draft.
- A checklist sheet filled from memory.
- Overwriting Draft 1 or Draft 2.

## §9 Say it out loud

After your practice, tell a friend what you did. Use this only when it is true: "I recorded a long
conversation with someone I'd never practised with, on a topic we drew from slips. More than thirty
turns. When it ran out I went back to something they'd said earlier, and at the end I didn't just say
bye. I gave a reason, mentioned something they'd told me, thanked them and said when we'd meet again. A
listener could hear the difference from an ending that just stopped. In writing, I froze my essay, ran my
own checklist on it one item at a time, copied it clean and checked the copy line by line. Then I added
two sentences to one paragraph on purpose, and a reader with my checklist found the mistakes in exactly
those sentences, and none in my final essay." If you did only one folder, say only that part.
