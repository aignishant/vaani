## One-line answer

A long conversation isn't ended with *bye*. It's closed in four moves. **Signal**: a small word, *Well*,
*Anyway* or *Okay*, and a reason, so the other person knows the end is coming. **Look back**: one line
about something they said. **Thank**: say what you enjoyed. **Go**: the goodbye from day 8, with the next
meeting in it. The other person agrees at each step. A conversation closed like this ends as warmly as it
went.

## The story

Your neighbour came round for tea on Sunday. You talked about her son's new job, the price of onions,
and the mango tree that fell in the storm. It was a good visit.

Then your phone rang in the other room. You stood up and said, *Okay, bye!* She blinked, picked up her
bag and left. On Monday she was a little quiet on the stairs.

The week after, she came again. This time, when it was time, you said, *Well, I should start the
cooking.* She said, *Oh yes, me too.* You said, *Tell your son good luck on Monday.* She smiled. *Thank
you for the tea. This was nice.* *It was. See you on Sunday?* *See you on Sunday.*

Same neighbour, same tea, same ending time. The second one ended as a friend.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **goodbye**: the last line, which names the next meeting or wishes the other person well, from
  [day 8](../../../day-08-greetings-and-goodbyes/parts/01-speaking/1.2-goodbye-and-see-you.md).
- **turn**: one person's part of a talk, and then it's the other person's turn, from [day 115](../../../day-115-long-conversation-first-rehearsal/parts/01-speaking/1.1-pick-up-their-last-words.md).
- **loose end**: something the other person mentioned earlier, from
  [day 116](../../../day-116-long-conversation-second-rehearsal/parts/01-speaking/1.1-go-back-to-a-loose-end.md).
- **closing**: the last few turns of a conversation, which bring it to an end.
- **pre-closing**: the first move of a closing. A small word like *well*, *anyway* or *okay* that says
  *I'm ready to finish*, often with a reason.
- **abrupt ending**: a conversation that stops with *bye* and no closing.

## The rule

**The pages checked, opened on 2026-09-25.**
[9.4: Closing Conversation](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Communication/Introduction_to_Communication/Communicating_to_Connect_-_Interpersonal_Communication_for_Today_(Usera)/09:_Conversation_Skills/9.04:_Closing_Conversation)
lists the kinds of pre-closing speakers use. Among them are announcing a next activity (*I need to go
study*), summaries, appreciations, well-wishes (*have a nice day*) and arrangements for meeting again. It
describes an abrupt *bye* as sounding as if one speaker "was rude in some way".

**The four moves.**

| Move | What it does | What it sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Signal | says the end is near, with a reason | *Well, I should let you get back to work.* |
| 2. Look back | shows you listened | *Good luck with the new flat.* |
| 3. Thank | says what you enjoyed | *Thanks, this was really nice.* |
| 4. Go | the goodbye, with the next meeting | *See you on Friday.* |

**The other person agrees at each step.** After your signal, wait. If they say *Oh yes* or *Me too*, go
on to the look back. If they start a new topic, they weren't ready. Let them finish it, then signal again.
A closing is done by two people, not one.

**Look back to a loose end, not the last thing said.** The best look back is a loose end from earlier.
It shows you held on to the whole conversation. Build it from their words, not from a list.

**Name the trap.** Many learners think closing takes a special phrase, and wait for one. It doesn't.
*Well* and a reason is enough. The other trap is the reverse: signalling again and again and never going.
Three signals and no goodbye is as awkward as no signal at all.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the abrupt ending**:

> Okay. Bye.

The other person hears that something went wrong, and wonders what they said.

The second is **the signal with no reason**: *Okay … okay … so …* The other person can't tell if you're
finishing or thinking.

The third is **a new question after the signal**: *Well, I should go. Oh, and what do you think about the
new road?* Now the conversation has started again, and you have to close it twice.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers close almost without noticing, and they do it the same way on the phone, at the door and
at the end of a meeting. *Right, I'll let you go.* *Anyway, I'd better get on.* Listen for them this week.
You'll hear them everywhere.

Under pressure, when you really must leave at once, the reason does the work. *Sorry, that's my bus, I
have to run. Good luck on Monday!* It's fast, and it's still a closing.

A native listener remembers the end of a conversation more than the middle. A warm closing covers many
small mistakes that came before it.
