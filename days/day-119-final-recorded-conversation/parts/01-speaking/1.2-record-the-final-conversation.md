## One-line answer

Record the conversation that goes into your Speaking Portfolio. Use a partner who didn't do day 115 or
116 with you, a topic drawn from slips, no prepared questions, at least thirty turns, back-links at the
stalls, and a real closing of four moves. Then mark your questions F, B or N, compare the counts with your
two rehearsals, and ask your partner how the ending felt. The kept take gets its own row in
`docs/RECORDINGS.md`.

## The story

After two Sunday visits, your neighbour asked if you'd help her niece practise English. The niece came
with her. You'd never met her. You drew a topic from a jar on the table: *food from home*.

You talked. She said her grandmother made the best pickle. Later, when the talk ran out, you went back to
the grandmother. At the end, you said, *Well, your aunt's waiting. Say hello to your grandmother for me.
This was fun. See you next Sunday?*

Your neighbour, listening from the kitchen, said it was the best conversation she'd heard you have.

**Words to know**

- **F, B, N**: marks for your questions: F for a follow-up built from their last words, B for a back-link
  to a loose end, N for a new topic, from
  [day 116](../../../day-116-long-conversation-second-rehearsal/parts/01-speaking/1.2-thirty-turns-and-a-loose-end.md).
- **rehearsal**: a practice run of something you'll do for real later, done in full, from
  [day 115](../../../day-115-long-conversation-first-rehearsal/parts/01-speaking/1.2-twenty-turns-with-a-real-partner.md).
- **kept take**: the take you file in the portfolio, with its own row.
- **final conversation**: the recorded conversation that goes into your Speaking Portfolio.

## The rule

The four closing moves and the page checked are in [part 1.1](1.1-signal-look-back-thank-go.md). This
part is the rep.

**Step one: the partner.** Someone who didn't take part in your two rehearsals, if you can find one. Ask
them before you record, and tell them the recording goes into your portfolio. If they'd rather it
didn't, choose another partner.

**Step two: the topic.** Write six topic slips, or ask your partner to write them. Draw one together.
Neither of you prepares.

**Step three: record.** One take. At least thirty turns between you. No list of questions. Build
questions from their last words. At each stall, go back to a loose end before a new topic. Close with the
four moves, and let your partner agree at each step.

**Step four: mark and count.** Play it back. Write down every question you asked and mark it F, B or N.
Then fill the table from your notes on days 115 and 116.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the rehearsed partner**. You pick the friend from day 116 and the same
topic. It's easier, and it isn't the final conversation. It's a third rehearsal.

The second is **the closing that starts too early**, at turn twelve, because you're nervous. Wait until a
thread has run out and a back-link has been used.

The third is **the kept take chosen by how your voice sounds**. Choose it by the counts and your
partner's answers.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

A fluent speaker doesn't count turns in a real conversation. But they've had thousands. Your thirty turns
stand for that: long enough that the middle has to hold, and the end has to be managed.

Under pressure, with a new person, the first few turns are the hardest. Ask one follow-up early, from
their very first answer. It sets the pattern.

A native listener to your portfolio hears two people who listened to each other, and an ending that was
agreed. That's what a conversation is.
