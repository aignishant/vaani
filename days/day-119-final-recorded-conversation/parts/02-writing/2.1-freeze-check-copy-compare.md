## One-line answer

The final essay is made from Draft 2 in four steps, and the first step is the one people skip.
**Freeze**: from now on, no new words, no new sentences, no new ideas. **Check**: run your proofreading
checklist on the frozen draft, one pass per item, and mark every fix. **Copy**: write the fair copy with
every fix in it. **Compare**: read the fair copy against the marked draft, line by line, because copying
makes new mistakes. A sentence added after the check was never checked.

## The story

Your cousin's wedding invitations went to the printer. The family checked the proof carefully: the
names, the date, the address, every spelling. Your aunt read it twice. It was perfect.

Then, the evening before printing, your uncle phoned the printer. *Please add one line: Lunch will be
served after the ceremony.* The printer added it. Nobody checked it again, because the card had already
been checked.

Three hundred cards came back with *Lunch will be served after the cermony.*

Every word the family had checked was right. The only mistake was in the only line nobody had checked.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **Draft 2**: the second draft of your essay, from
  [day 116](../../../day-116-long-conversation-second-rehearsal/parts/02-writing/2.1-margin-notes-first-sentences-second.md).
- **proofreading checklist**, **pass**: your own list of yes-or-no questions, run one at a time, one
  read of the page each, from
  [day 113](../../../day-113-self-correction-while-speaking/parts/02-writing/2.2-build-your-checklist-and-run-it.md).
- **fair copy**: the clean final version, written out again properly after the draft, from
  [day 100](../../../day-100-gate-interview-and-letter/parts/02-writing/2.2-write-the-email-and-the-letter.md).
- **freeze**: the moment after which a draft gets no new words, only fixes that the checklist finds.
- **marked draft**: the frozen draft, with every checklist fix marked on it.
- **compare**: reading the fair copy beside the marked draft, one line at a time, to find anything
  copied wrong.
- **final essay**: the fair copy of your essay that goes into your Writing Portfolio.

## The rule

**The pages checked, opened on 2026-09-25.**
[Proofreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proofreading) describes proofreading in publishing as
comparing proofs "against the original manuscripts" to "identify transcription errors", errors made in
copying. [Foul papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foul_papers) says an author's working drafts were
copied into "a transcript or 'fair copy'" once the writing was finished. Both show the same order: finish
the writing, then make the clean copy, then check the copy against what it came from.

**The four steps.**

| Step | What you do | What's not allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Freeze | Write *Frozen* and today's date at the top of Draft 2. | a new sentence, a new example, a new idea |
| 2. Check | One pass per checklist item, your own errors first. Mark every fix on the draft. Count the marks for each item. | fixing anything the checklist didn't find |
| 3. Copy | Write the fair copy with every marked fix in it. Title it *Final essay*. | changing anything while copying |
| 4. Compare | Put the fair copy beside the marked draft. Read one line of each, then the next. Mark anything that's different. | reading the fair copy on its own |

**What the frozen draft looks like on the page.**

```text
Frozen 2026-__-__                                   checklist marks
Para 1   ...the problem [is] that ...  ← missing verb    item 2 (verb)
Para 2   ...three year[s] ago ...      ← plural -s        item 1 (-s)
...
```

**If the checklist finds a sentence that doesn't work at all**, you may rewrite that sentence only, in
the fewest words that fix it, and mark it. Then run the pass for that item again on the new sentence.

**Name the trap.** At this stage, almost everyone has a better idea. It feels like improving the essay.
It's actually adding the one sentence nobody will check. If the idea is really better, it belongs in a
Draft 3, and the freeze starts again.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the sentence added after the check**, like the lunch line:

> Also, many young people today spend there time on phones.

It came in while copying. It has *there* for *their*, a mistake the checklist would have caught, if it
had been run on it.

The second is **the copying slip**: a word left out or doubled in the fair copy that wasn't wrong in the
draft. *The the bus was late.* Only the compare step finds it.

The third is **the checklist run on the fair copy only**. Then there's no marked draft to compare
against, and no record of what the checklist found.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Anyone who sends out a final version, like a printer, an office sending a contract or a school printing
results, freezes it before checking. A change after the freeze means the check starts again.

Under pressure, the night before something is due, the temptation to add *one more line* is strongest.
That's the line with the mistake in it.

A formal reader can't tell which sentence was added last. They only see the mistake, and it counts the
same as any other.
