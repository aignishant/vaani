## One-line answer

To make a sentence with am, is or are say no, put not right after the verb, and on paper the short forms are I'm not, isn't and aren't.

## The story

You share a flat. You leave a note on the fridge: "The milk is fresh." Your flatmate drinks it
and is ill all afternoon. You meant the opposite. The milk was two days old and you wanted to
warn her. One word was missing, and the note said the reverse of what you knew. The next note
you write says "The milk isn't fresh," and she pours it away.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **negative** — a sentence that says no, or that something is not so.
- **reverse** — the opposite.
- **warn** — tell someone about a danger.
- **flatmate** — a person you share a flat with.
- **fridge** — the cold box in the kitchen.

A **negative** sentence says that something is not so. In English the word that does it is
**not**, and with the verb [be](../../../day-06-final-consonants/parts/02-writing/2.1-am-is-are.md),
am, is or are, not goes **right after the verb**. The milk is not fresh. I am not hungry. They
are not home.

On paper, the [short forms](../../../day-06-final-consonants/parts/02-writing/2.2-short-forms-and-not.md)
from day 6 are what a reader expects in a note or a message: **I'm not**, **isn't**, **aren't**.
The [apostrophe](../../../day-06-final-consonants/parts/02-writing/2.2-short-forms-and-not.md)
shows where letters are missing. The reader's eye finds n't and knows the sentence says no
before they reach the end.

## Why Vaani needs it

Half of what you write to people who share your life is warnings and corrections: the milk
isn't fresh, the door isn't locked, I'm not at home. The [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases)
about you needs at least one thing you are not. And on [day 19](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#8--the-day-map--day-to-ids-closed),
can't is the same not on a new verb.

## The rule

| Person | Full form | Short form on paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | I am not | I'm not |
| he, she, it, one thing | is not | isn't |
| you, we, they, more than one | are not | aren't |

> The milk isn't fresh.
> I'm not at home this evening.
> The keys aren't on the hook.

Not goes after the verb, and nothing else moves. The rest of the sentence stays where it was.

The rule was checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Questions and negatives](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/questions-negatives):
"We make negatives by putting not after the first part of the verb", with "They are not
working hard." The short forms "I'm not", "You aren't", "He/She/It isn't" were seen at
[The verb 'be'](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/verb-be),
and again at [Present simple: 'to be'](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple-be),
opened the same day.

The check for every negative you write: finger on the verb, am, is or are. Is not, or n't,
right after it? Then the sentence says no, and a reader sees it.

The trap: I'm not is the only one that does not shorten to "amn't". Write I'm not, never
"I amn't", and never "I isn't".

## When it breaks

You leave not out because in your head the sentence was already negative:

> The milk is fresh.

The reader does the opposite of what you meant. This is the most dangerous break on the page,
because the sentence is perfect and wrong. The fix: isn't.

You put not in the wrong place:

> The milk not is fresh.

The reader sees the no before the verb and stumbles. The fix: not after the verb. "The milk
isn't fresh."

You write the short form without the apostrophe:

> The milk isnt fresh.

The reader reads it, and marks you as careless. The fix: isn't. The apostrophe is small and it
is looked for.

If a negative you wrote has not before the verb, or a short form with no apostrophe, that
sentence is RED.

## In real life

Fluent writers use the short forms in every note and message, and the full forms only when
they want weight: "The milk is NOT fresh" on a note to a flatmate who ignored the last one. In a
form or a formal letter, they write the full form: "I am not available on Friday."

A friend who drinks the old milk forgives you. A note that says the opposite of what you
meant, to a landlord, a colleague or a doctor, is acted on. "The patient is allergic" and "the
patient isn't allergic" are one word apart and a world apart.
