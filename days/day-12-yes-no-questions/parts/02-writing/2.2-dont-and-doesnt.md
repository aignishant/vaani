## One-line answer

With any verb that is not am, is or are, the negative is don't or doesn't before the plain verb, and doesn't takes the -s so the main verb loses it.

## The story

The fridge note this time is about the cat. You write: "The cat not eat fish." Your flatmate
reads it three times. Does the cat eat fish or not? Is this a rule or a report? You try
again: "The cat doesn't eat fish." Now it is clear, and the tin of tuna stays in the cupboard.
Not on its own was not enough. It needed a helper.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **helper word** — do or does, added to carry not when the verb is not be.
- **plain form** — the verb with no -s: eat, not eats.
- **move** — the -s leaves the main verb and goes into does.
- **report** — a sentence that tells what is so.
- **tin** — a metal can of food.

In [part 2.1](2.1-not-with-be.md), not went after am, is or are. But most verbs are not be:
eat, like, live, work, want. Not cannot stand after them on its own. It needs a **helper
word**: **do**, or **does** for he, she, it and one name. Together they make **don't** and
**doesn't**, and they go **before** the main verb.

The helper takes the job of showing the person. So the main verb goes back to its plain form.
The cat eats fish. The cat doesn't eat fish. The -s moved from eats into does.

## Why Vaani needs it

Most of what you are not, and do not, is a verb that is not be: I don't smoke, I don't eat
meat, my brother doesn't live here. The [day 20 page](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases)
will have one or two of these. On [day 15](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#8--the-day-map--day-to-ids-closed)
you write routines with the -s on he, she and it, and today's doesn't is where that -s goes
when the routine is a no.

## The rule

The shape, as a picture:

```text
Yes:   The cat        eats     fish.
       subject        verb+s

No:    The cat   doesn't   eat      fish.
       subject   helper    plain verb
```

| Subject | Helper + not | Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I, you, we, they | don't | I don't eat meat. |
| he, she, it, one name | doesn't | The cat doesn't eat fish. |

> I don't eat meat.
> The cat doesn't eat fish.
> They don't live here now.

The rule was checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Present simple](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple):
"For the negative, we use don't or doesn't", and at
[Questions and negatives](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/questions-negatives):
"We make negatives by putting not after the first part of the verb", with the example "They
don't work hard."

Two fingers for every negative: one on don't or doesn't, matching the subject; one on the main
verb, which must be plain, no -s.

The trap is the -s. In a yes sentence, he, she and it put an -s on the verb: she likes, he
works. In the no sentence, that -s lives inside doesn't, and it must not also be on the main
verb. She doesn't like. Not she doesn't likes. One -s per sentence.

## When it breaks

You write not with no helper:

> The cat not eat fish.

The reader cannot tell if it is a rule, a report or a broken sentence. The fix: "The cat doesn't
eat fish."

You keep the -s on the main verb:

> The cat doesn't eats fish.

Two words show the person, and the reader trips on the second. The fix: doesn't eat.

You use don't for one thing:

> The cat don't eat fish.

Common in speech in some places, and on paper it reads as a mistake. The fix: doesn't, for one
cat.

If a negative you wrote has not with no helper, don't where doesn't belongs, or an -s on the
main verb, that sentence is RED.

## In real life

Fluent writers put don't and doesn't in without thinking, and they use them in every note,
message and form: "I don't drive." "She doesn't work Fridays." Under pressure, writing fast,
even fluent writers let the -s slip back onto the main verb, and a careful one reads back and
removes it.

A friend reading "The cat not eat fish" understands. A vet's form, a school note about what
your child does not eat, a message to a landlord about what does not work in the flat: these
are read by people who act on them, and "not eat" is a sentence they will ask about instead of
acting on.
