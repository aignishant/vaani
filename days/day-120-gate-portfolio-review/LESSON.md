and *wanna*, a debate, the news, feelings, suspense, phrasal verbs, hedging, your own field, hard

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-talk-that-ran-dry.md), a gate-check listener with
only the §7 wording marks every requirement the gate take and take D miss. RED is when the gate take
misses any. GREEN is when it misses none and take D misses at least one. To make it go red on purpose,
stop the gate take when the first point runs out.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-essay-with-four-paragraphs.md), a gate-check reader
with only the §7 wording marks every requirement the final essay and copy B miss. RED is when the final
essay misses any. GREEN is when it misses none and copy B misses at least one.

## §7 Traps

- A topic you chose or wrote yourself.
- A note, a card or a phone screen in your hand.
- A count rounded up to four hundred.
- A listener given your own count.
- A conversation or an essay made today and filed as day 119's.
- An essay improved during the review.
- A checklist run once on the fair copy, or not shown at all.
- A paragraph break a reader can't see.
- An index audit done from memory, or stopped at the gate pieces.
- An old row, page or recording changed instead of a new row added.
- The day 117 reader's note edited instead of a new closing line.
- A gate made kinder because it was written early.
