## One-line answer

The phase 6 speaking gate asks for an unscripted talk of at least four hundred spoken words on a topic
drawn at random, plus a recorded conversation, and a Speaking Portfolio indexed in full. This part
teaches nothing new, because a gate that teaches isn't a gate. It splits the gate into requirements you
can check yes or no, and names the evidence for each, so that when you press record there's nothing left
to decide except what you say.

## The story

A hundred and nineteen days ago, you said your name into your phone. Since then you've greeted a
neighbour, ordered food, argued, complained, interviewed, told stories, closed a conversation. Some of it
is on your phone, in six folders.

Today someone who has never met you could sit down with your phone and your index, and hear all of it in
order. They'd hear what you could do on day 20 and what you can do now. The last piece they'd hear is a
talk on a topic you didn't choose, with nothing in your hand.

This folder is about making that last piece, and making sure the stranger can find every piece before it.

**This is a rehearsal, and it says so.** This day was written before your error ledger had rows in it.
Until the `docs/ERRORS.md` re-read on the checklist is ticked, the checks below are the general ones
rather than your own recurring mistakes, and the gate is not yet a gate.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **gate**: a check at the end of a phase that the work has to pass before the phase is finished, from
  [day 20](../../../day-20-gate-self-introduction/parts/01-speaking/1.1-what-the-gate-asks.md).
- **requirement**: one thing a gate asks for that you can check yes or no, from
  [day 117](../../../day-117-retake-weakest-piece/parts/01-speaking/1.1-weakest-by-its-own-gate.md).
- **Speaking Portfolio**: your gate recordings and their retakes, indexed in `docs/RECORDINGS.md`, from
  the same part of day 117.
- **unscripted talk**, **answer sentence**, **anchor word**: a talk with no script and no notes on a topic
  you didn't choose, held as one sentence and three words, from
  [day 118](../../../day-118-unscripted-talk-no-notes/parts/01-speaking/1.1-three-words-in-your-head.md).
- **transcript**: every word of a recording, written down as it was said, from
  [day 114](../../../day-114-listening-back-own-recording/parts/01-speaking/1.1-three-listens-one-job-each.md).
- **final conversation**: the recorded conversation that goes into your Speaking Portfolio, from
  [day 119](../../../day-119-final-recorded-conversation/parts/01-speaking/1.2-record-the-final-conversation.md).
- **indexed in full**: every piece has a row in the index, and every row leads to a piece that plays.

The gate wording is in the plan's §7, and it's copied here without a word changed:

> Speaking: an unscripted talk of at least four hundred spoken words on a topic drawn at random, plus a
> recorded conversation.

The same row of §7 ends: *Both portfolios indexed in full.* In this folder, that means your Speaking
Portfolio.

| Requirement | What it means | The evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| an unscripted talk | no script, no notes, nothing in your hand | the take, and a helper who saw you make it |
| at least four hundred spoken words | counted, not guessed | the transcript, with the count at the bottom |
| on a topic drawn at random | a slip you didn't write, drawn without looking | the slip, kept with the transcript |
| plus a recorded conversation | the final conversation from day 119 | its file, and its row in `docs/RECORDINGS.md` |
| indexed in full | a row for every gate recording and retake, each leading to a file that plays | the index audit in part 1.2 |

## Why Vaani needs it

This is the end of the course. Everything in the speaking folder, from the first vowel sound on day 1,
exists so that you can talk for four hundred words to a stranger on something you didn't choose, and
hold a conversation with someone you've never practised with. Until it's recorded and indexed, nobody
knows whether you can.

## The rule

There's no new rule today. What follows is the setup.

**The conversation comes from day 119.** This day reviews it and never makes it. If there's no kept
conversation with a `RECORDINGS.md` row when you reach this part, go back to day 119 and make it there.
A conversation made today and filed as day 119's isn't honest, and it isn't the gate.

**The slips come from someone else.** Ask a helper to write six everyday topics, fold them and mix them.
You haven't seen them. That's what *drawn at random* means here. A topic you wrote yourself, you've
already started planning.

**Nothing in your hand.** You may find your answer sentence and three anchor words in your head after
the draw, exactly as on day 118. No pen, no paper, no phone screen.

**Name the trap.** A gate written early is easy to make kinder: a topic you like, a count that's nearly
four hundred, a conversation with your best friend. None of those is in §7. If you notice you're making
the gate easier, stop and read §7 again.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the chosen topic**: *I'll just talk about my job, it's easier.* The talk
may be good. It isn't on a topic drawn at random.

The second is **the missing conversation**. The learner reaches day 120, finds no day 119 recording, and
records one today. It's a conversation, but it's not the one the course asked for, and the index now
lies about its day.

The third is **the index checked from memory**: *They're all there.* Open each file from its row.

These are errors beginners commonly make at a final gate. This day was written before your own error
ledger had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Any real test of speaking works like this gate. An examiner gives you a topic you didn't choose, and
listens for whether you can keep going and stay clear. A job interview asks a question you didn't
prepare. A neighbour starts a conversation on the stairs. None of them lets you choose the topic.

Under pressure, the four-hundred-word count matters less than it feels. If you keep your three points,
the words come. If you lose them, go back to your answer sentence.

A stranger listening to your portfolio won't grade your accent. They'll notice that the person on day
120 can do things the person on day 20 couldn't.
