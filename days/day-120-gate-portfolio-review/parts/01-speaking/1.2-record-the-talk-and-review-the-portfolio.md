## One-line answer

Draw one of your helper's slips and record the gate talk in one take, with nothing in your hand, to a
listener. Write the transcript and count the words. Ask the listener to say your answer and your three
points. Then review the whole Speaking Portfolio: open every row in `docs/RECORDINGS.md`, play its file,
and check it against its own gate. Add a row for today's talk. The portfolio is reviewed when every piece
from day 20 to day 120 has a row, and every row plays.

## The story

On the last evening, your helper sat at the table with six folded slips in a bowl. You drew one. You
said it out loud, stood still for a breath, and started.

When it was done, she told you your three points before you asked. Then she picked up your phone and
your index, and played the day 20 recording. Then day 40. She didn't need you to show her where anything
was.

## The rule

The requirements are in [part 1.1](1.1-what-the-gate-asks-for.md). They aren't restated here.

**Step one: the conversation is there.** Find the day 119 row in `docs/RECORDINGS.md` and play the file
it names. If it's missing, stop and go back to day 119.

**Step two: the draw.** Your helper's six slips, folded, in a bowl. Draw one. Say it out loud. Keep the
slip.

**Step three: the gate take.** Face your listener. Find your answer sentence and three anchor words in
your head. Record the whole talk in one take, nothing in your hand, no stopping.

**Step four: the listener.** Before they hear the recording, ask them: *What was I saying about the
topic? What were my three points?* Write their words down.

**Step five: the transcript and the count.** Write the transcript from the recording and count every
word. A counted total below four hundred means a new take on a new slip, not a rounded number.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the rounded count**: *three hundred and eighty, that's about four
hundred.* It isn't. §7 says *at least*.

The second is **the helper who helps**: a slip they know you'll like, or a nod when you pause. Ask them to
write six ordinary topics and to say nothing during the take.

The third is **the audit that stops at the first five rows**. Retakes and the final conversation are
part of the portfolio too.

These are errors beginners commonly make at a final gate. This day was written before your own error
ledger had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

When fluent speakers give a talk they didn't prepare, nobody counts their words, but everybody feels
whether they kept going. The count is how you prove to yourself what a listener would feel.

Under pressure, if you lose your place at the gate, say your answer sentence again and reach for the next
anchor word. A pause of one breath at a turn sounds planned. A restart doesn't.

A stranger reviewing your portfolio trusts it more when a note says *day 60: missing* than when every
row looks perfect and one file won't play.
