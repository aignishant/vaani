## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **requirement**, **indexed in full**: from [part 2.1](2.1-what-the-gate-asks-for.md).
- **audit**, **correction row**: checking every item on a list against the real thing, one at a time,
  and a new row that fixes a wrong one and says which one it fixes, from
  [day 117](../../../day-117-retake-weakest-piece/parts/02-writing/2.2-assemble-yours-and-write-the-note.md).

- **closing line**: one or two sentences for the stranger, on a new page, saying the portfolio is
  complete and where it ends.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the essay improved during the review**. The reviewer sees a weak
sentence and rewrites it. Now the essay has a sentence the checklist never saw. A review finds; it
doesn't write.

The second is **the audit that stops at the gate pages**. Rewrites and the reader's note are in the
portfolio too.

The third is **the reader's note edited** to add the last piece. It was a page of the portfolio on day
117. It stays that way. The closing line is a new page.

These are errors beginners commonly make at a final gate. This day was written before your own error
ledger had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Anyone who hands over a finished set of documents, like an application, a file for a lawyer or a folder
for a new job, goes down the list one last time and opens every item. It's slow, and it's the step that
catches the missing page.

Under pressure, when the review finds a missing page, the honest row is the strong one: *day 60: not
found*. A stranger can work with that. They can't work with a row that points at nothing.

A formal reader trusts a portfolio when the first three things they look for are where the list says.
