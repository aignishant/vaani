## One-line answer

Take five sentences you really say at home, mark the meaning words, tap them as you speak, and let the small words hurry between the taps.

## The story

Now it is your turn to call across the kitchen. The fan is on, your brother is at the far end,
and you need the big spoon. You say, "Can you give me the big spoon from the drawer?" with
every word the same, and he says "What?" You try again, tapping in your head: "Can you GIVE me
the BIG SPOON from the DRAWER?" He hands it over without turning round. Same words. This time
four of them arrived.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **mark** — put a sign on a word to show it is loud.
- **drawer** — the sliding box in a kitchen unit.
- **hurry** — go fast.
- **across** — from one side to the other.

In [part 1.1](1.1-loud-words-and-quiet-words.md) you learned which words are loud and which
are quiet. Now you do it on your own sentences: the things you actually say at home, to real
people, across real rooms. The rule does not change. Find the words that carry the meaning.
Tap them. Let the rest hurry.

The quickest test for a loud word: if you sent the sentence as a short message and could only
afford four words, which four would you send? Those are the loud ones.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 recording](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) is ten sentences
about you, and every one of them has a beat. If you practise the beat on someone else's
sentences, you will have to find it again on yours on the day. Find it on yours now.

## The rule

1. Write five sentences you really say at home. Requests, questions, news: "Dinner is ready."
   "Where are my keys?" "I'm going to the shop."
2. For each, underline the meaning words. Things, actions, describing words, question words.
   Usually two to four in a short sentence.
3. Say the sentence at the mirror, tapping the table on each underlined word. Make the taps
   even, like a slow clock.
4. Record all five, tapping. Then record them again without tapping, keeping the beat in your
   voice.

Two models, for a home that is not yours, with the taps in CAPITALS:

> DINNER'S READY.
> WHERE are my KEYS?

Notice the second one: "are my" is two quiet words squeezed between WHERE and KEYS. Say it and
feel them hurry.

The content-word and structure-word rule was checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Sentence Stress](https://www.englishclub.com/pronunciation/sentence-stress.php), which says
the loud words fall on "a constant beat" and the small words are said "more quickly" to keep
it. The word [never](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/never)
/ˈnevə(r)/ was opened the same day and carries a sound button; it is a describing word for
time and is always loud: "I NEVER eat late."

Two more things about which words are loud. **Not** and **n't** are loud, because they change
the meaning: "I DON'T LIKE it." **New information** is louder than old: if someone asks "Whose
keys?", the answer "MY keys" makes my loud, even though it is usually quiet.

The trap: tapping every word that is longer than one syllable. Length is not the rule.
Meaning is. "Under" has two syllables and is usually quiet; "salt" has one and is loud.

## When it breaks

You mark the wrong words as loud:

> Can YOU give ME the big spoon FROM the drawer?

The listener hears you insisting on who and where, and looks for a spoon-shaped argument.
The fix: the meaning words are give, big, spoon, drawer.

You keep the beat when tapping and lose it when you stop:

> Can you give me the big spoon from the drawer? (all equal)

The tap was doing the work, not your voice. The fix: tap under the table, then in your pocket,
then only in your head. The beat moves from the hand to the voice.

If, on playback, your five sentences without tapping sound like the five with tapping, the
check is GREEN. If they sound flat again, it is RED.

## In real life

Fluent speakers change which words are loud depending on what they mean. "I didn't take YOUR
keys" and "I didn't TAKE your keys" are different sentences to them. You will get to that. For
now, the meaning words loud and the small words quick is enough to be understood across any
kitchen.

A native listener across a noisy room catches your loud words and guesses the rest, and they
guess right most of the time. Give them the right four words and they will hand you the spoon
without turning round.
