## §1 Where we are

A colleague shows you a photo of her family at lunch, and then it is your turn, and you have
the people but not all the words. The speaking folder is about the family words and the
two-sentence tour that shows a photo: who this is, and one fact, with he or she pointing at the
right face. Think of it as walking someone along a row of photos, one finger per frame.

Four of you share a kitchen and things keep going missing. The writing folder is about the
words that say whose: my, your, his, her, and the apostrophe s on a name, and the one mark that
separates Priya's cup from many Priyas.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- Two brother, one sisters. The -s follows the number.
- Mudder, brudder. The th in the middle is tongue between the teeth.
- "Cousin brother." A listener from elsewhere does not know it. My cousin; then he or she.
- He for a sister, she for a brother. The listener follows the word to the wrong face.
- His and her matched to the thing, not the owner. Priya's bag is her bag whatever colour.
- Priyas cup. No apostrophe means many Priyas.
- Two cup's. A plural has no apostrophe.
- It's for its. Its bowl has no apostrophe.
