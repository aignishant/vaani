## One-line answer

Take a real photo of your family, and for each person say who they are with this is my, then he or she, then one true fact, and the listener meets your whole family in six sentences.

## The story

Next day at lunch you show the photo again, ready this time. "This is my mother. She lives with
us. This is my father. He drives a taxi. This is my younger brother. He's still at school."
Your colleague points at the last person. "And that's your uncle, your dad's brother, right?"
You said that yesterday, and she remembered, because yesterday you gave her the word and today
you gave everyone a fact.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **fact** — a thing that is true and that someone else could check.
- **he** — the word for a man or boy already named.
- **she** — the word for a woman or girl already named.
- **tour** — going through the photo one person at a time.
- **still** — up to now, and not finished.

You have the family words from [part 1.1](1.1-the-family-words.md). Now you use them on your
real family, in a shape a listener can follow. Two sentences per person. First, who they are:
"This is my" plus the family word. Second, one fact about them, starting with **he** for a man
or boy and **she** for a woman or girl. He and she stand in for the person you have just named,
so you do not say "my mother" twice.

One fact is enough. Where they live, what they do, how old they are, one thing they like. The
listener holds one thing per person. Two things and the faces start to blur.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 self-introduction](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) needs a
sentence about your people, and a stranger's first question after it is nearly always about
your family. Six ready sentences turn that question into the easiest part of the recording.

## The rule

Pick a real photo with three to six family members in it, or picture one. For each person, two
sentences, recorder on:

| Sentence | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| who | This is my + family word. |
| one fact | He / She + verb + the rest. |

> This is my mother. She lives with us.
> This is my father. He drives a taxi.
> This is my younger brother. He's still at school.

Point at the mirror as if it were the photo, one person per point. Give each person a
[pause](../../../day-09-introducing-yourself/parts/01-speaking/1.1-the-five-spoken-sentences.md)
before the next.

The rule for he and she was checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Personal pronouns](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/personal-pronouns):
"We use he/him to refer to men, and she/her to refer to women." The fact sentence uses the
-s on the verb for one person, lives, drives, which was seen at
[Present simple](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple):
"we add s or es for he, she and it".

The family words in the model carry sound buttons, opened the same day:
[mother](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/mother),
[father](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/father),
[brother](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/brother).

The trap: he and she. If your first language uses one word for both, or none, the choice feels
like extra work, and under pressure the wrong one comes out. The listener does not hear a slip;
they hear a different person. Take the beat before the fact sentence and choose.

## When it breaks

You say the family word twice instead of he or she:

> This is my mother. My mother lives with us.

Correct, and heavy; it sounds like a form. The fix: she in the second sentence.

You give three facts for one person:

> This is my father. He drives a taxi and he likes cricket and he is fifty and he…

The listener has lost the first fact by the fourth. The fix: one fact, then the next person.
You can come back to him later if they ask.

You forget the -s on the fact verb:

> This is my father. He drive a taxi.

Understood, and marked. The fix: he drives.

If, on playback, any person has no fact, or any fact has the wrong he or she, or the fact verb
has no -s, the check is RED.

## In real life

Fluent speakers give a photo tour exactly like this, and then stop and let the listener ask.
"This is my sister. She's a nurse." Then a pause, and the listener says "Oh, where?" and the
conversation goes where they want. The two-sentence shape is not a school exercise; it is how
people show photos.

A native listener remembers one fact per face. Give them one and they will ask for the second.
Give them four and they will remember none, and smile.
