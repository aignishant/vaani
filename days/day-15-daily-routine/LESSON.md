## §1 Where we are

A doctor asks you to describe a normal day, and the answer you give decides what she can help
with. The speaking folder is about the verbs of a day and the order they go in: get up, have
breakfast, go to work, come home, go to bed, joined by then and after that. Think of it as
walking someone along your day from morning to night without going back.

A notice goes up in your building about the milkman, and one missing letter starts an
argument. The writing folder is about the -s that says one other person does this, and the
words that say how often: always, usually, sometimes, never.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- Times with no verbs: "Six. Nine. Late." A timetable with nothing on it.
- "In six" for a time. Times take at.
- Splitting a two-word verb: "I up get." The pair stays together.
- Then on every sentence. One every two or three.
- "After that" at the end. It goes at the front.
- The verb with no -s for one other person: "the milkman come".
- Both -s letters at once: "my brothers works".
- The how-often word after the main verb: "closes usually at ten".
- Always in front of be: "he always is late".
- Never beside doesn't. Never already means no.
