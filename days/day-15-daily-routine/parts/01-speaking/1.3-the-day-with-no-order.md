## One-line answer

Record your eight sentences shuffled out of order and with every verb dropped to a bare time, on purpose, play them back as the doctor with a pen, try to draw the day, then record the ordered version beside them.

## The story

You try it on your brother. You tell him your day with the sentences shuffled: bed, then
lunch, then getting up, then dinner. He listens and says, "Wait, when do you actually leave the
house?" Then you tell him the times with no verbs, and he says nothing at all, because there is
nothing to answer. Then you tell him the eight in order, and he says, "You don't eat until
seven? No wonder." Same eight facts, three deliveries, one of which reached him.

This is a practice scene. Today's wrong versions are deliberate. They rest on what beginners
commonly do, not on a mistake found in your own recordings.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate** — done on purpose.
- **shuffle** — mix up the order.
- **lose the thread** — stop being able to follow.
- **draw** — here, write the day down as a line from morning to night.
- **RED** and **GREEN** — RED means the check found a problem. GREEN means it passed.

You have the verbs from [part 1.1](1.1-the-routine-verbs.md) and the ordered eight from
[part 1.2](1.2-your-day-in-order.md). Now you break them the two ways beginners really do
under pressure: the order goes, and the verbs go. You listen as the doctor with a pen and try
to draw the day as a line. Whatever you cannot draw is what the listener did not get.

## Why Vaani needs it

At the [day 20 check](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases), a stranger listens
once and cannot ask you to start again. A learner who has tried to draw their own shuffled day
from a recording knows that order is not decoration. It is how the listener holds eight things
at once.

## The rule

Two rows. Recorder on. Wrong first, then right, with a gap.

**Row 1 — shuffled.** Say your eight sentences in a mixed-up order, each still a full
sentence:

> I go to bed at eleven. I have lunch at one. I get up at six. I come home at seven. I have
> breakfast at seven. I watch TV. I go to work at nine. I have dinner at eight.
> (then the same eight in order)

**Row 2 — no verbs.** Say only the times and places:

> Six. Seven. Nine. One. Seven. Eight. Eleven.
> (then the same eight as full sentences)

Play row 1. Be the doctor. Take a pen and draw a line for the day, morning at the left, night
at the right, and put each thing on it as you hear it. Notice how often your pen has to go
back. Then draw the ordered version. Your pen should move only forward.

Play row 2 and try to draw anything at all.

Then the repair, in one breath, as it happens when you see the listener frown:

> I go to bed at eleven, I have lunch — sorry, let me start from the morning. I get up at six.

whose model day runs in order from getting up to going to bed.

## When it breaks

The break this part is about: you draw the shuffled day easily.

> bed, lunch, get up, home → a clean line

You drew it easily because it is your day and you know it. The doctor does not. Play it again
and put each thing on the line in the order you hear it, moving your pen back when you must.
Count how many times the pen goes backwards. Every backward move is the listener rebuilding.

A second break: the repair restarts and then shuffles again.

> Sorry, let me start from the morning. I get up at six. I go to bed at eleven. I have tea.

Starting again is right; keeping the order after that is the point. The fix: from the morning,
forward, no jumps.

If your pen moved the same way on the wrong and right versions, the check is RED.

## In real life

Nobody shuffles a day on purpose. Everybody shuffles when they are remembering out loud,
because the memories arrive in the order they arrive. Fluent speakers do it too, and then say
"sorry, from the top" and start at getting up. That sentence is the repair, and it is a normal
thing to say.

A native listener draws your day in their head whether you order it or not. If you order it,
the drawing is free. If you do not, they spend their attention on rebuilding and have none left
for the thing you actually wanted them to notice, the late dinner, the early start, the long
commute.
