## One-line answer

A full answer about liking has three pieces: the rung, the thing, and one short reason, and the reason is what gives the other person something to say back.

## The story

At the same friend's house, later. His mother asks what food you do like. You say, "I like
fish." She waits. You stop. Silence again, for a different reason this time: you gave her a
fact with nothing to hold. Then you try, "I like fish, because my mother cooks it on Sundays,"
and she is off — what fish, what does your mother put in it, does she use coconut. One reason
opened twenty minutes of talk.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **reason** — why you like or do not like something.
- **because** — the word that introduces a reason.
- **hold** — here, something for the listener to take and use.
- **open** — start something; make more possible.
- **coconut** — the big brown nut used in cooking.

You have the ladder from [part 1.1](1.1-the-ladder-of-liking.md). An answer with only a rung
and a thing is complete and small: I like fish. An answer with a **reason** is a door. The
word that carries a reason is **because**, and after it comes a small fact about your life.

Three pieces, in order: the rung, the thing, because, the reason.

The reason does not have to be big. "Because my mother cooks it on Sundays." "Because I can
sleep on the bus." "Because it's quiet." A small true reason works better than a clever one,
because the listener can ask about it.

## Why Vaani needs it

The [day 20 recording](../../../../docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md#7--the-phases) is ten sentences a
stranger can follow, and the ones about what you like are where a listener finds you. A
recording of ten flat facts is followable and forgettable. One because makes it a person
talking.

## The rule

The shape:

> I like fish, because my mother cooks it on Sundays.
> I'm not keen on long bus journeys, because I get a headache.
> I love the early morning, because it's quiet.

Build five of your own, one at each of the rungs you actually use: one love, two like or all
right, one not keen, one don't like. Each about a real thing, each with a real reason.

Say them at the mirror. The thing is a loud word, and so is the key word in the reason, from
[day 13](../../../day-13-sentence-rhythm/parts/01-speaking/1.1-loud-words-and-quiet-words.md).
"I like FISH because my mother COOKS it on SUNDAYS."

The -ing form after these verbs when the thing is an action was checked on 2026-09-22 at
[Verbs followed by '-ing' or infinitive](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/verbs-followed-ing-or-infinitive)
and ['-ing' forms](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/ing-forms):
"We enjoy learning English." The words
[love](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/love_1),
[like](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/like_1) and
[enjoy](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/enjoy) carry sound
buttons, opened the same day.

One more piece that costs nothing: after your answer, hand the question back, the way you
learned to on [day 8](../../../day-08-greetings-and-goodbyes/parts/01-speaking/1.1-hello-and-how-are-you.md).
"…and you? What do you like?"

The trap: a reason that repeats the rung. "I like it because I like it." The listener has
nothing new. The reason must add a fact: a person, a place, a time, a feeling.

## When it breaks

You give the rung and the thing and stop:

> I like fish.

True, complete, and closed. The listener says "Oh, nice" and looks for another subject. The
fix: because, and one small fact.

Your reason repeats the rung:

> I don't like it, because it is not nice.

Nothing new arrived. The fix: a fact. "Because it's very spicy for me."

Your reason is longer than your answer:

> I like fish, because when I was small we lived near the sea and my mother had a friend who…

The listener has lost the fish. The fix: one short reason. The story comes if they ask.

If, on playback, more than one of your five answers has no because, or a reason that repeats
the rung, the check is RED.

## In real life

Fluent speakers give the reason without being asked, and often skip the because: "I like fish
— my mum used to cook it on Sundays." The dash does the work. You can use because; it is
always correct and never sounds odd.

A native listener takes the reason and asks about it. That is how small talk works: you hand
them a hook, they pull it. Answers with no reason make them do all the work, and after two or
three of them they stop.
