# Six shapes that keep it going

`IN-01` · Day 16

## The one-line answer

Asking is how you stay in a conversation you cannot yet carry, because a question hands the work
back — and there are only six shapes you need, five of which cost you almost no English at all.

## The model

A conversation is a rally, not a performance. Everything up to now has been about your side of
the net: your minute, your account, your recovery when it breaks. Today is the return.

Here is the thing nobody tells a learner. **The person who asks the questions is not the weaker
speaker in the exchange.** They are the one deciding what the conversation is about. You have
been avoiding questions because they felt like admitting you had nothing to say, and they are in
fact the cheapest way to buy a long turn of listening — during which you are still fully in the
conversation, still doing your job, and not producing a single sentence of your own.

Six shapes. Learn them as shapes, not as sentences.

**One — the opener that cannot be answered short.** *What's it like …?* It asks for description,
and description takes a while.

> What's it like, working nights?

**Two — the reason, without the interrogation.** *Why* is a fine word that lands hard; it can
sound like you are asking someone to justify themselves. *How come* asks the same thing and
sounds curious instead.

> How come you moved out that way?

**Three — the one that asks for a story.** *What made you …?* This is the highest-yield shape on
the list. It cannot be answered with a fact. It gets you a beginning, a middle and an end,
because the person has to explain a decision.

> What made you go for that one?

**Four — the follow-up that costs two words.** *Such as?* *In what way?* *Like what?* You use
these when someone has said something vague, and they are the closest thing to free English that
exists. You did not have to build a sentence. You did not have to know the topic. The rally
continues and it is still not your turn.

> — It was a bit of a nightmare, honestly.
> — In what way?

**Five — the narrower.** *Which bit …?* *Whereabouts …?* When you did not follow the whole thing,
you do not have to admit to the whole thing. Narrow it, and you get the part you missed without
resetting the conversation. Day 3 gave you the repair lines for when you missed something; this is
the shape for when you got most of it.

> Which bit were you not sure about?

**Six — the ask.** *Any chance you could …?* Requests are where learners get stiff and either
over-apologise or come out blunt. This shape is neither. It is what people actually say to a
colleague, a neighbour, a stranger holding a door.

> Any chance you could send that over again?

Notice what is not here: *do you*, *did you*, *have you*, *is it*. Those are not banned, they are
just endings — they close the rally, because the honest answer to most of them is one word.

**Today's phrases** — check `docs/CHUNKS.md` first. Day 6 already gave you *What about you?*,
which is the handover, not a question shape. Cite it, don't re-learn it.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| What's it like …? | Opening a topic you know nothing about |
| How come …? | Asking why without sounding like you want a defence |
| What made you …? | Fishing for a decision, and therefore a story |
| Such as? | They were vague and you want an example |
| In what way? | They were vague and you want the reasoning |
| Which bit …? | Narrowing, when you followed most of it but not all |
| Whereabouts …? | Placing something, without a full sentence |
| Any chance you could …? | Asking for something, warmly, without grovelling |

## Say it now

Say each three times, filling the slots with something real about a person you actually know. No
writing.

1. *What's it like, {{…}}?*
2. *How come you {{…}}?*
3. *What made you {{…}}?*
4. *Such as? … In what way?*
5. *Which bit {{…}}? Whereabouts {{…}}?*
6. *Any chance you could {{…}}?*

Then run all six as one stretch, as if to the same person, twice. The rule for the join: no two
questions in a row may use the same shape, and you may not put a statement between them. Six
returns, no serves.

## Say it yours

**Recorder on. File: `audio/d16-free.m4a`.**

> **Pick a job, a place or a hobby you genuinely know nothing about.** Record yourself
> interviewing someone about it — only your side. Ask a question, leave a real silence for the
> answer you are not going to get, then ask the next one, built on the answer you imagined.
> Keep going until you run out. Every shape above must appear at least once.

The silences are not dead space. They are the rep. Asking a question and then filling the gap
yourself is the exact habit this day exists to break.

- **Solo form:** as written.
- **With a partner:** they answer, but they answer as briefly as they can get away with. Your job
  is to keep the rally alive off short returns. They are allowed to be difficult.

## The deliberate failure

Record a run of questions to the same imagined person in which **every single one can be answered
yes or no.** *Do you like it? Is it far? Have you been there long? Did you enjoy it? Would you do
it again?* Leave the silences.

**What breaking sounds like:** you will hear your own voice rise at the end of every question and
then stop dead, over and over, with nothing built between them. Each one is a serve, not a return
— you are starting the rally from scratch every time, and you can hear the effort of it in how
often you have to begin.

Now run the same topic with the six shapes. Listen for the difference in your own voice: the
questions get longer, the rises stop being identical, and — this is the part worth catching — you
sound like someone in a conversation rather than someone administering one.

## When it breaks in the wild

You are in a group of people who all speak faster than you. You have followed maybe half of it.
You go quiet, because you have decided that the only way back in is to produce something
interesting, and you cannot produce something interesting in a language you are still assembling.

So you sit there, and the longer you sit the harder it gets to re-enter, and you leave believing
your English let you down when what actually happened is that you never took a shot.

You do not need something interesting. You need four words.

**The repair:**

> Sorry — what's it like, actually? I don't know anything about this.

That puts you back in, admits nothing you should be ashamed of, and buys you a long stretch of
listening in which you are visibly participating.
