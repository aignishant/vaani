## §1 Where we are

You are at a small grocery counter asking for three things, and the man keeps saying "Sorry?"
Your words are right. The stops between them are not. The speaking folder is about the join —
the place where a word ending in a consonant slides into a word starting with a vowel — and
about hearing, on a recording, what a chopped sentence costs the person listening.

Your sister is at home looking for the house keys and all she has is the line you sent her. The
writing folder is about four small words that decide where she looks: in, on, under, next to.
Think of them as the address on an envelope. Everything else in the sentence is the letter.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- Marking the join by the letter instead of the sound: *an hour* links, *a university* does not.
- Linking everything, including consonant to consonant, until the sentence is one long word.
- Practising the joins only on the six given phrases, then chopping your own sentences.
- Joined but flat — every word equally loud is a different failure, and it is day 13's.
- *On* for a container: *on the drawer* when the thing is inside it.
- *In* for a surface: *in the wall* when the photo hangs on it.
- Leaving the place word out: *The keys are the drawer.*
- A true place sentence that is not enough: one drawer in your head, three in the kitchen.
- Doing the deliberate walk in your head instead of standing up.
