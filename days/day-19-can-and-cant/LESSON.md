## §1 Where we are

You are at a counter paying a bill. She says something quick, you catch the word *can*, and you
take out your card — and she had said *can't*. The speaking folder is about the size of those
two words: in a real sentence *can* shrinks until it is almost not there, and *can't* grows until
it is the loudest thing in the line. The difference you hear is length, not the *t*.

You are filling in a form for a flat and you write *she cans cook*. The writing folder is about
the plain verb after *can*, the apostrophe in *can't*, the word *cannot* on a notice, and the
four errors that are the only four anybody makes.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- Saying *can* big and clear in an ordinary sentence, so it lands as an argument.
- A short, quick *can't* whose *t* disappears into the next word, reversing your meaning.
- Listening for the *t* instead of for the length.
- A permission question with a flat voice, which arrives as an announcement.
- The bare claim with no limit: *I can drive*, four years after the last time.
- *I can to swim* — no *to* after *can*, ever.
- *She cans cook* — the day 15 *-s* rule does not apply here.
- *cant* and *ca'nt* — the apostrophe stands where the letters were taken out.
- *can not* as two words on a notice, where *cannot* belongs.
- A request with no reason, and a rule with no alternative.
- A read-back that reads generally instead of hunting for one named error at a time.
