## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** Answer the stranger's three questions from each of the four takes in
[part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-take-a-stranger-cannot-follow.md), naming the sentence each
answer came from. If a broken take answers all three, that failure is invisible to you — RED,
and write it down. If your real take fails a question, that is also RED, and it is the most
useful result of the day: retake, fixing that one thing.

**Writing:** Run the three counts on the broken page in
[part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-page-that-fails-its-own-check.md). If the sentence count does
not collapse, you left full stops in — RED, do it again. Then hunt the two repeated facts
separately. Finding only one is RED, and it means your eye passes over repetition.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Nine unfinished takes and nothing in the portfolio, because every small mistake caused a restart.
- Retakes that get shorter each time, quietly dropping the sentence that went wrong.
- A take read off the page, which passes today and is worthless to compare against later.
- Ten "sentences" that are really three, joined by *and*.
- Ten facts with no verbs: a list a stranger cannot follow and cannot ask about.
- An introduction that opens on a small detail, so the stranger builds the wrong person.
- Counting the page by eye. It is always seven.
- Padding to reach ten by splitting one fact into two sentences.
- A missing full stop on the last sentence — the commonest one, because you had finished.
- A phone number or street address on a page that is committed to the repository.
- A sentence that was true last year. The truth pass is the only check nobody else can do.
