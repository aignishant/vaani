# The take — three exchanges, one file

`CF-07` · Day 24

## The one-line answer

Three exchanges recorded back to back with the belt never stopping, because what is being tested
is not whether you can steer once — it is whether the steering is still there the third time.

## The model

This is portfolio recording 05, and it is the first piece where you are not talking about
yourself. The three earlier recordings were all monologue with the shape decided in advance. This
one has another voice in it, and it goes wrong on purpose.

How to run it:

- **One file, three exchanges, no stopping between them.** The joins are part of the take. If you
  stop the recorder to think, you have removed the only pressure in the exercise.
- **You play both sides.** The voices do not have to sound different. Turning your head is enough.
- **Decide the three counters before you start** — somewhere you buy something, somewhere you
  collect something, somewhere you ask for something to be sorted out. Decide nothing else. The
  trouble is already assigned: you change your mind, you miss what is said, they have not got it.
- **Keep the first take**, as on Day 6. You may record a second afterwards. The first one is not
  deleted and it is the one that goes in row 05.

And there is a fourth kind of trouble, which the three roleplays deliberately do not cover,
because it arrives after the talking is finished. **The goods can be wrong.** You are handed
something that is not what you asked for, or the change is wrong, or it is somebody else's order
entirely. Almost everyone takes it and leaves. The exchange is not over when you have agreed — it
is over when you have what you asked for, and the phrases below exist for that last stretch of
belt.

> Sorry — the small one, was it?
> Sorry, I asked for the other one.
> I think there's been a mix-up.
> No, you're alright, thanks. That's everything.

**Today's phrases** — these go into `docs/CHUNKS.md` tonight with part 01's.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| That's everything, thanks. | Closing the exchange out loud instead of standing there |
| Sorry — {{…}}, was it? | Saying a number or a name back to check you got it |
| Sorry, I asked for {{…}}. | You have been handed the wrong thing |
| I think there's been a mix-up. | Bigger than one wrong item, and said without blaming anyone |
| No, you're alright, thanks. | Turning down an extra you did not ask for |
| Sorry to be a pain — {{…}}. | Asking for a second change, when you have already asked once |

## Say it now

Before the take, say the closing line on its own, five times, out loud:

1. *That's everything, thanks.*

It is the shortest phrase in the day and the one most likely to go missing under pressure. Then
the two that only ever get used after something has already gone wrong, three times each:

2. *Sorry — {{…}}, was it?*
3. *Sorry, I asked for {{…}}.*

Then run a short imaginary exchange that ends with all three of them in a row: check the number,
find the wrong thing, close it. Straight through, no pause between them.

## Say it yours

**This is the take. Recorder on. File: `audio/d24-counter.m4a`.**

> **Three counter-service exchanges, back to back, in one file.** In the first you change your
> mind. In the second you miss what is said and have to get it repeated. In the third they have
> not got what you asked for. Every one of them ends with you closing it out loud.

Rules:

- No notes, and no rehearsing an exchange immediately before recording it.
- Do not stop the recorder between the exchanges.
- If something goes wrong that was not in the plan, stay in it. That is the best material this
  take can produce, and it cannot be planned.
- The first take is the one that goes in row 05 of `docs/RECORDINGS.md`, whatever happens in it.

- **Solo form:** both voices, as written.
- **With a partner:** they run the counter, and they are told only which of the three troubles to
  cause, never how. They may also hand you the wrong thing at the end of any exchange, without
  warning you first.

## The deliberate failure

Record a fourth exchange in which you are **banned from every recovery line in the day**. When you
mishear, say yes. When they have not got it, take whatever they offer instead. When they hand you
the wrong thing, take that too, and leave.

**What breaking sounds like:** nothing. That is the finding. It is the smoothest exchange on the
whole recording — short, no hesitations, no repairs, no apologies — and at the end of it you have
the wrong thing. Play it directly after one of the real ones. The one that sounds most fluent is
the one that failed, and every counter you have ever walked away from with the wrong item in your
hand sounded exactly like that.

Now run the same exchange again with the lines allowed. It is longer and bumpier and it has three
*sorry*s in it, and it is the one that ends with you holding what you asked for. Bumpy and correct
beats smooth and wrong, and until you have heard the two side by side that is only an opinion.

## When it breaks in the wild

You are handed the wrong thing. You can see it is wrong. You take it, say thanks, and leave — and
on the way out you explain to yourself that it was not worth the trouble.

It was not the trouble. It was that you did not have the sentence, and the moment for it is small
and it closes quickly.

**The repair:**

> Sorry, I asked for {{…}}.

Four words and the thing you wanted. No blame in it, no explanation, no apology for existing —
and said while you are still standing there, it costs nothing at all.
