## §1 Where we are

You say "I'll be there tomorrow" on a level voice and the person on the phone says "sorry — is
that a question?" The speaking folder is about the tune: falling means *I have finished*, rising
means *your turn*, and level means the listener has to guess. It is the part of English that
survives a bad line when every word is lost.

You write "are there much people in it?" and the reader understands you perfectly and notices it
anyway. The writing folder is the second half of day 22's rule: two sets of amount words, one for
things you count and one for things you do not, and *a lot of* covering both.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- A level ending. In English that is not neutral — it is a gap the listener has to fill.
- Rising on statements, which makes you sound unsure of your own life.
- Rising on *where*, *what*, *how* questions. Those fall.
- Marking the tune by the question mark instead of by the kind of question.
- Judging your own pitch while speaking. Everybody sounds more expressive to themselves.
- Sorting your own recording with the words in front of you. The eye answers before the ear.
- *Much people* — *people* is countable and plural.
- *Many time*, *many work*, *many information* — all uncountable, all take *much*.
- *Much* in a positive sentence, where *a lot of* is what people actually write.
- *Few* without its *a*, which says almost none when you meant some.
- An amount word where the reader needed a number: *some chairs* covers two and ten.
- Hunting the words *much* and *many* instead of hunting the nouns.
