## §1 Where we are

Four of you are outside deciding where to eat and everybody is being polite, and then somebody
says *let's go to the place by the station* and it is decided. The speaking folder is about the
three ways to suggest — *let's* decides, *shall we* asks, *how about* offers — and about the four
pieces a plan needs before it will actually happen.

Your sister has moved abroad and asks what everyone is doing, and you write *my father watches
television*, which tells her nothing she did not know. The writing folder is about the tense for
right now, and the one sentence in the ordinary present that tells a reader whether the moment is
worth noticing.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- *How about meet at six* — *how about* takes the -ing form or a noun, never the plain verb.
- *Let's* in a group still deciding: the first firm suggestion wins, and somebody was still waiting.
- A bare *no, I can't*, which ends the planning and makes somebody else start again.
- *Shall we…?* said flat. Without the rise it is a *let's*.
- A suggestion with no day in it. *Sometime* never happens.
- Four pieces settled and no confirmation, so you are outside different entrances.
- Nobody saying out loud that they will book it.
- *She working today* — the missing *am*, *is* or *are*.
- *He is works* — the day 15 *-s* rule does not apply here.
- *I am knowing*, *I am wanting* — state verbs stay in the simple.
- A paragraph about now written in the simple tense, which tells the reader nothing new.
- No anchor sentence, so a reader far away cannot tell an ordinary evening from a bad one.
- Three anchors, which turns the moment back into a general description.
