## §1 Where we are

You are at a window and something is going on down in the street, and somebody behind you asks
what is happening. The speaking folder is about the two things that answer them: the small word
that puts a sentence in front of the listener instead of in a book, and the order that turns four
true sentences into a scene somebody can watch.

Somebody far away is holding their phone and cannot see what you can see. The writing folder is
about the four moves that let a reader build a picture out of words, and about the test that finds
the hole in your description before a stranger does — a pencil, a sheet of paper, and somebody who
has never seen the place.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** Fill in the claim table in
[part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-commentary-that-froze.md) from the two recordings. Fewer than
four claims changed is RED — you changed the words instead of the verb shape, so listen again and
swap only the shape. And if the frozen take sounds better to you than the live one, that is RED
and it is the finding worth keeping.

**Writing:** Run the pencil test in
[part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-paragraph-nobody-can-draw.md) with a real person. The drawing
from the RED version must come out visibly wrong or unfinished. If it comes out right, your
scramble was too gentle — take the first sentence out entirely and run it again.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *She waiting outside* — the missing *am*, *is* or *are*.
- *He is waits* — the day 15 *-s* arriving where the *-ing* is already doing the job.
- *A man walks to the door* said about a man who is walking right now.
- The *be* word said so weakly that it disappears: *they coming*.
- A commentary that jumps between four things and finishes none of them.
- Stopping the recording because one word is missing, instead of saying *I don't know the word*.
- *I am wanting*, *I am knowing* — the over-correction that comes the day after this one.
- A description that starts with a detail, so the reader has nothing to hang it on.
- A list instead of a paragraph: nothing is behind, beside or under anything else.
- *It*, *there* or *above it* pointing at something that has not been written yet.
- A paragraph that changes direction halfway and sends the reader back to the start.
- Seven details, which is the same as none.
