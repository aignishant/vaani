# The take, and hearing the joins

`CF-08` · Day 30

## The one-line answer

This is the sixth piece in your portfolio, and the only thing you judge tonight is the joins — the
places where the ball went over the net.

## The model

Portfolio recording 06. One claim, an opinion each, and the turn changing hands as many times as
it will.

You do not need a good argument. You need a rally that stays in the air, and the two are
unrelated — some of the best exchanges you have ever been in were about nothing much and they
never stopped moving.

Set it up like this.

**One claim, agreed before the recorder goes on.** Something you both have opinions about and
neither of you has rehearsed. Not a debate topic — something small and live, like whether the way
you both do a particular ordinary thing is actually the better way. Pick it, say it out loud once,
then start.

**No notes.** As with every take so far.

**Keep the first take.** Same rule as Day 6, and the same reason: the first one is the honest one,
and a second is a different exercise.

**When it stalls, it stalls on the recording.** A three-minute exchange has a moment where neither
of you has anything ready. It is normal, it is not a failure, and you do not stop the recorder for
it. You have three cheap returns for exactly this.

> Where were we?
> Go on —
> That's a different question, though.

*Go on* is the cheapest return there is. Two words, no content at all, and it puts the ball back
over the net while you get your feet under you. It is not weak. A returned ball is a rally, and
that is the only thing this piece is measuring.

*That's a different question, though* is for the drift. Long exchanges wander, and at some point
you will notice you are both now talking about something else. Naming that out loud is a real move
and it makes you sound like somebody who has been listening. Following the drift silently is how
an exchange quietly turns back into two monologues.

**Then the playback, once.** You already know the job from Day 6: listen with a task, never for a
verdict. Tonight the task is narrow and countable.

**Count the joins.** Every place the turn changed hands. Not what was said, not who was right.
Just: how many times did the ball cross the net, and for each one — was it *handed over*, *dropped*
or *taken*? A handed-over join has a question or a clean stop in it. A dropped one has a voice
trailing off and a gap where nobody knew whose turn it was. A taken one is where somebody spoke
over the other. Three numbers. They go into `docs/RECORDINGS.md` tonight beside the date.

**Today's phrases** — appended tonight with the others.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| Where were we? | Restarting the rally after it has stopped dead |
| Go on — | The cheapest return there is, when you have nothing ready |
| That's a different question, though. | Naming a drift instead of quietly following it |

## Say it now

Before the recorder goes on, say each of these three times, flat and unhurried, so they arrive
without a run-up when the exchange stalls.

1. *Where were we? {{the last thing one of you actually said}}.*
2. *Go on —*
3. *That's a different question, though. {{the question you were on}}.*

Then say all three one after another, twice, as a single run. These are the only lines you rehearse
today. Everything else in this recording is live.

## Say it yours

> **A three-minute opinion exchange.** One claim, an opinion each, the turn changing hands as many
> times as it will. Position first every time. Hand back before you run out, not after.

Rules:

- No notes, and no plan beyond the claim itself.
- **Keep the first take.** You may record a second afterwards; the first one is not deleted and is
  the one that goes in the portfolio row.
- Stalls stay in. Do not stop the recorder to start again cleanly.

- **Solo form:** you can do this alone and it is a harder version, not a lesser one. Write six
  claims on separate slips, shuffle them face down, and draw one at a time. Answer each aloud as
  if to a person — position, reason, example, handback — then answer your own handback from the
  other side, honestly arguing the side you did not take. Draw the next slip when a rally dies.
  Keep going, recorder running, until the three minutes are on the file.
- **With a partner:** as written. Two instructions for them: do not go easy on you, and do not
  fill your silences.

## The deliberate failure

One extra turn, recorded on the end after the take is finished. Hand the turn back **without
leaving a gap** — put the question in and then keep talking straight through the place the
silence should have been. *So where do you stand on that, because I mean obviously it depends
a bit, and…*

**What breaking sounds like:** with somebody else in the room you will hear the collision — both
of you starting, both stopping. Alone you hear something subtler and just as useful: the question
stops being a question. Buried inside a run of speech it flattens out and lands as filler, and
nobody returns filler.

Then hand back once more, properly. Land the last word and stop. That silence is the shortest
thing on the whole recording and it does more work than any phrase in it.

## When it breaks in the wild

A call where the sound arrives a beat late. You finish, they do not hear the end, you both start
at once, you both stop, you both start again. On the third collision you give up your turn
altogether and the thing you were going to say never gets said by anybody.

**The repair:**

> Go on — you first.

Then actually stop talking. Two words, said early, cost you one turn and save the next four. The
alternative is both of you deferring politely into the same gap, over and over, and that costs the
conversation.
