## §1 Where we are

Somebody gives you directions and half the syllables seem to be missing. The speaking folder is
about the sound that is in those gaps — the dullest vowel in the language, the one that has no
spelling of its own — and about the small words that collapse into it, which is why fast English
sounds fast.

A guest is coming to stay and you leave a note about the street. The writing folder is about the
two sentences English uses to say that something exists in a place, the one that says something
does not, and the two mistakes in this area that nobody ever corrects and everybody notices.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-every-syllable-shouting.md), play the
deliberately flat take and the normal take to one real person, once each, and write down what they
give back. It is RED when the flat take returns fewer facts — that is the break working. It is
also RED, and more useful, if your listener hears no difference between the two takes: that means
your ordinary voice is already flat.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-there-have-many-shops.md), give the deliberately
fossilised note to a real reader. GREEN is when they understand it completely and say nothing
about the English — because the point is that this failure is invisible. Write down exactly what
they said, including if they did stop at one sentence.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Saying the spelling vowel in unstressed syllables: *ah-BOUT*, *ba-NA-nah*.
- Stress on the wrong syllable, which puts the schwa where the loud sound belongs.
- Schwa everywhere, including the stressed syllable, so the word has no beat at all.
- Every small word in its strong form: *I have to go TO the bank FOR my mother*.
- Weakening a content word, so the sentence has no beat left.
- A weak form at the end of a sentence: *who's it fə?*
- Squeezing a small word until it disappears: *I'm going bank*.
- *Near the house have a shop* — *have* used for existence.
- *There is many shops* — the plural noun with the singular verb.
- *Is a chemist also* — the missing *there*.
- *There're* written down, which has no written short form.
- A place with no distance, and a note with no negative in it.
