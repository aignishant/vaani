## §1 Where we are

Somebody behind a counter says nine words and you catch two. The speaking folder is about the
three different repairs — *I didn't hear you*, *you were too fast*, *I don't know that word* —
about the three-part ask that gets you a real answer, and about the third question you did not ask
and what it cost you the next morning.

A request goes out on a screen where nobody can see your face. The writing folder is about the
words that carry politeness when the voice cannot, the four blocks that make a request answerable,
and the two ways a request fails: as an order, and as a fog.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

**Writing:** Run the reader test in
[part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-request-that-vanished.md) with two or three real people. GREEN
is when they can state the ask and the date from your clear version and cannot from the fogged
one. If they get the ask right from the fog, the fog was too clear. If they get it wrong from your
GREEN version, that is the RED worth having.

## §7 Traps

- *Sorry?* asked three times for a problem that was never about volume.
- Silence and a nod, which the other person reads as understanding.
- *Repeat, please.* — correct, and heard as impatience.
- Asking for the whole sentence again when you needed one word.
- Apologising for your English instead of asking your question.
- An ask with no goal, so the helper solves a different problem.
- Walking away without repeating the answer back.
- *Send me the list today.* — the bare imperative, which reads as an order.
- A request with no reason, so the reader decides whether you matter.
- *As soon as possible*, which every reader reads as *whenever*.
- The ask buried in a friendly paragraph with no blank lines around it.
- A clear request undone by *but no rush at all* in the last line.
