## §1 Where we are

speaking folder is about instructions that survive one hearing — one action per sentence, a word

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-step-you-skipped.md), remove one step on
purpose and have a real person follow the instructions while you say nothing. GREEN is when they
improvise without asking and the result comes out wrong. If they stop and ask you, the hole was
too visible — remove a quieter, earlier step and run it again.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-note-that-assumes.md), give the
pointer-word note to somebody who has never done the task. GREEN is when the result is visibly
different from yours, or they cannot start. If they get it right, add more pointer words and run it
again — and either way, do the hunt through your good note, because that is where the real finding
is.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Five actions joined with *and*, so the listener keeps only the last one.
- The first step left out because the expert's hands already did it.
- No checks, so you find out at the end that they stopped at step two.
- A warning said after the step it was warning about.
- *Leave it for a bit* instead of a sign the listener can see.
- A word they do not have — *strain*, *whisk* — used without a plain-words version.
- Answering an interruption and never returning to the sequence.
- A note of facts with no verbs telling anybody to do anything.
- *You have to* on every line, which reads as nagging.
- An *if* condition written backwards, so nobody knows what to do.
- A step with three actions on one line; the reader does the first and the last.
- *Usual*, *normal*, *just*, *as always* — words that point at knowledge instead of carrying it.
