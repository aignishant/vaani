## §1 Where we are

Somebody at a wedding asks what you do, and the answer decides whether there is a conversation.
The speaking folder is about the three pieces of a usable answer, the three sizes to have ready,
and the version made of words from inside your workplace, which is correct, accurate, and leaves a
listener with nothing to ask.

A course group or a new team wants a few lines about you. The writing folder is about the sentence
that carries a paragraph, the small words that show how your facts are related, and the test that
tells you whether anything survived: take the paper away and ask.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-answer-nobody-could-follow.md), play both
takes to somebody who does not do your work and ask what they would ask you next. GREEN is when
the jargon take produces one weak question or none, and the three-line take produces a real one.
If both produce good questions, the takes were not different enough — try a second listener and
make the jargon take harder.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-paragraph-that-lists.md), run the recall test:
one reading, paper taken away, a real gap, then *what does this person do?* GREEN is when the
reader of the good version gives back your spine and the reader of the listed version gives back
two or three words. If both give back the same thing, your good version has no spine either.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *I am working in a company* — a title with no field and no verb.
- A job title from inside your building, which means nothing outside it.
- *I am working here* when you mean *I work here*: the temporary shape for a permanent job.
- The whole work history given in answer to a question about now.
- An apology attached to the answer, so the listener has to reassure you.
- The one-line version used in a sitting-down conversation, and the long one used in a doorway.
- Never asking *what about you?*, which turns a conversation into an interview.
- A paragraph whose first sentence is a label, so nothing can lean on it.
- The fond sentence that survives every draft and holds nothing up.
- Ten short sentences with no joins, or one sentence with five *ands*.
- *But* used where the reader expected *because*.
- Every sentence starting with *I*, which a reader feels as a list.
