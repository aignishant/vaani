## §1 Where we are

You are at a stall holding something you want, and the seller says a number fast. The speaking
folder is about the short question that asks the price, the two syllables that confirm it, the
four moves that ask for a better one, and the nod that feels cheap in the moment and is not.

An order goes out in writing and comes back with numbers in the wrong units. The writing folder is
about the two question words English uses for quantity, the unit that has to be inside the
question, and the swap that gets answered correctly and delivered wrongly.

an item that is stored in two different units and run it again.

## §7 Traps

- The nod at a number you did not catch, which cannot be undone later.
- *How much?* shouted across a stall with twenty items on it.
- Asking each price and never asking for the total.
- *Thirty* and *thirteen*, *forty* and *fourteen* — the stress, not the word.
- Saying yes before asking whether the price can move.
- A counter-number with no question in it, which turns a chat into a contest.
- Criticising the thing you are trying to buy.
- Asking for a discount in a shop with printed price labels.
- *How much chairs*, *how many rice* — the swap.
- *How many money* — money is uncountable in English.
- *Informations*, *advices*, *furnitures* — plurals English does not have.
- A quantity question with no unit in it, answered in whatever unit the reader stores it in.
