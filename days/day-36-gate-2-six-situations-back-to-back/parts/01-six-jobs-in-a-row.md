# Six jobs in a row, and whether the tool comes to hand

`CF-09` · Day 36

## The one-line answer

Today nobody teaches you a new tool: six ordinary jobs are handed to you back to back, and the
only question the gate asks is whether the right thing came to hand without you rummaging.

## The model

This is a measurement, not a lesson. Everything the gate reaches for was put in the box on a day
that has already happened. **No new phrases today.** `docs/CHUNKS.md` is unchanged tonight.

Phase 2 was called The Toolkit for a reason. Eighteen days went into filling a box — a way to ask
for something, a way to send somebody down a street, a way to point at a person in a crowd, a way
to say what you are doing on Saturday, a way to say what you think and then not die on the hill,
a way to tell a small story that goes somewhere. Each of those arrived on its own day, on its own,
with nothing else going on.

A full box is not the skill. **Reaching without looking is the skill.** You have had eighteen days
of being handed one job at a time, told which drawer it is in, and given as long as you liked to
open it. Today you get six jobs in a row, you are not told which drawer, and the next one starts
before you have put the last thing down.

**The six jobs.** These are the gate, in this order, and you do not change the order:

1. **You are buying something.** You want a thing, you are not sure they have it, and there is one
   complication — the size is wrong, or the price surprised you, or you want two of one and one of
   the other. (Day 19.)
2. **Somebody stops you and asks how to get somewhere.** A real place you actually know. Send them
   there out loud, with turns and landmarks, and finish with what they will see when they arrive.
   (Day 21.)
3. **Describe a person so a stranger could pick them out.** Somebody you know. Not their character
   — the bit that would let a stranger walk up to the right one. (Day 23.)
4. **What you are doing next.** Something already fixed, something you are only thinking about,
   and one thing you decide out loud while you are speaking. (Day 25.)
5. **Say what you think about something, then take it back a notch.** State the opinion. Then
   soften it, and then half-agree with the objection you can hear coming. (Days 27 and 28.)
6. **A short story that has a point.** Something that actually happened. Set it up, turn it, and
   land the point in a sentence. (Day 35.)

Those six were drawn from what Phase 2 actually taught. Requests and refusals, small talk, and
recommending one thing over another are in the box too — they are your re-run material if today
does not go the way you want, and they turn up inside the six anyway.

**What "unscripted" means here.** The six situations are fixed and you can read them now. The
*content* is not, and you do not choose it in advance. The thing you are buying, the place you are
sending them to, the person you are describing, the story you are telling — all four get drawn at
the moment you arrive at them, not before. Draw the same way you drew for Gate 1: face-down
scraps, or a scroll through your phone's photo gallery with your eyes shut, or a partner naming
one out loud.

**Back to back means back to back.** One file, one take, and the recorder does not stop between
the six. You may take a breath between jobs. You may not take a think. The point of the whole
thing is the reach, and if you get to sit in silence and open the drawers one at a time then the
gate has measured nothing.

**Running it honestly** is the hard part, same as last time, and the temptations are the same
ones. Do not draft any of the six beforehand. Do not run it once for practice and record the
second go. Do not stop the recorder when one goes badly — a bad job number three followed by a
good job number four is exactly the evidence you came for. Do not delete anything.

**The tools the gate listens for.** All of these are yours already. This is a reminder, not a
lesson, and there is nothing new in it.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| Could I get …, please? | Day 19 · job one, the opening reach |
| I'll take … | Day 19 · job one, deciding out loud at the counter |
| It's just round the corner from … | Day 21 · job two, placing something against a landmark |
| She's the one with the … | Day 23 · job three, the detail that picks a person out |
| I'm supposed to … | Day 25 · job four, a plan somebody else fixed |
| I'm thinking of … | Day 25 · job four, the one you have not committed to |
| If you ask me, … | Day 27 · job five, the opinion going in |
| Yeah, up to a point. | Day 28 · job five, half-agreeing with the pushback |
| The other day, … | Day 14 · job six, opening a story |
| Long story short, … | Day 5 · job six, landing it |

If three or four of these arrive in the take **without you deciding to use them**, that is the
sound of a tool coming to hand. It is also the whole of what Phase 2 was for.

## Say it now

Standing up, before you draw anything and before you press record. Fill the slots with whatever
is true right now.

1. *Six jobs, one file, and I don't stop the recorder between them, {{…}}.*
2. *I draw each one when I get to it — not before.*
3. *If a job goes badly, the next one still starts. That's the whole point.*
4. *If the phrase doesn't come, I say it the long way round and move on.*
5. *Mistakes are fine. Rummaging in silence isn't. That's the only rule today, {{…}}.*

Now say all five as one continuous stretch, on your feet, once. Then set out your six draws face
down, press record, and start job one on the next breath.

## Say it yours

**Recorder on. File: `audio/d36-free.m4a`.**

> **The six jobs, in the order above, back to back, on one file. Draw each one's content as you
> arrive at it. One take — you do not stop it, you do not restart it, and you do not delete it.**

The take is the gate. A rough take recorded honestly is worth more today than a smooth one you
got to on the third attempt, because only one of them tells you what is actually in the box.

- **Solo form:** the draws go face down in front of you. Speak each job to the wall as though the
  other person were there. For job two, send an imagined stranger to a real place.
- **With a partner:** they name each job's content out loud, one at a time, and then say nothing —
  except in job five, where they push back once, briefly, so you have something to half-agree
  with. No helping with words. They mark on their fingers each job where you went silent before
  starting.

If you do stop the recording, that stop is today's answer. Keep the file anyway. The gate can be
re-run on a later day, and that re-run gets its own row in `docs/PROGRESS.md`.

## The deliberate failure

After the gate take, and only after, record a **seventh job** — one nobody has handed you a tool
for. Explain how something technical in your work actually functions, to somebody who does not do
your job. No preparation and no stopping.

**What breaking sounds like:** listen to job seven straight after job six, and the difference is
not what you expect. Job seven is not more hesitant than the others. It is *emptier* — long,
correct sentences that circle, with no phrase arriving to move it forward, because there is
nothing in the box for this one yet. That is what "I do not own that situation" sounds like, and
it sounds nothing like being nervous.

This matters because from the inside, all six of the gate jobs will have felt bad too. Fluent
people also feel bad while speaking. Job seven is your reference sample for the other thing — the
sound of a genuinely missing tool — and once you have heard the two side by side you can tell them
apart on tape for the rest of this course. Job seven is not part of the gate and it is not
judged. It is calibration.

## When it breaks in the wild

A day where every one of these turns up in a row and none of them is announced. You buy something
on the way in, somebody asks you where the lifts are, a colleague asks what you are up to at the
weekend, and then somebody says something you disagree with. You handle the first two, and by the
fourth you have gone quiet and you are nodding — not because that one was harder, but because
three switches have already happened and you spent everything you had on the joins.

**The repair:**

> Give me a second — you've just caught me between two things.

The switch is what costs, not the situation. Naming it out loud buys the second you need and
sounds like a busy person rather than a stuck one.

**If the gate does not pass**, this is what happens, and it is short.

Name two or three days and re-run them, chosen by what you actually heard. If the joins were the
problem — long silences between jobs — re-run Day 24 and Day 30, because both of those are about
speaking across a switch. If one particular job ran dry, re-run the day that built it: Day 19 for
the counter, Day 21 for directions, Day 23 for describing, Day 25 for plans, Days 27 and 28 for
the opinion, Day 35 for the story. If several jobs came out as correct sentences that went nowhere,
re-run Day 27 and Day 35. Each re-run gets its own dated row in `docs/PROGRESS.md`, with its own
honest note. Then run the gate again on a later day, with fresh draws, and that take gets a row
too.

That is all. It is not a restart, nothing resets, and Day 37 is still Day 37 when you reach it.
A box you have now found two holes in is a better box than one you have never tipped out.
