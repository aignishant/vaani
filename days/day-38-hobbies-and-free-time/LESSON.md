## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-answer-that-went-on-too-long.md), play all
three takes to a real person and ask what they would say next. GREEN is when the repaired take
gets a genuine question or a story back and you can name the handhold they took. RED is when the
long take leaves them with nothing but *that's nice* or a pause. To make it go red on purpose, let
take B run past ten sentences with no question anywhere in it.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-everything-i-like.md), give two readers both
paragraphs, wait a day, and ask what they remember about you. GREEN is when both name something
concrete from the real paragraph. RED is when neither can name one specific thing from the list
paragraph.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The one-word answer, which is true and ends the conversation.
- The list of six hobbies, which says less than one sentence about one of them.
- *I don't have any hobbies*, which almost always means *nothing impressive*.
- A frequency invented to sound better, and the follow-up question that exposes it.
- A good answer with no hand-back, which puts the ball down.
- Asking four questions in a row and giving nothing back.
- Answering your own question before the other person can.
- *I am going to the gym every day* for a habit, where English wants *I go*.
- *Because I like it* — a reason that goes in a circle.
- *Very*, *really*, *a lot*, *quite often* — the words that make writing vanish.
- A paragraph that takes a second subject after *also*.
- No capital letter, no full stop, and a reader who answers more slowly because of it.
