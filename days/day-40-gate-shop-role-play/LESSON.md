## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-take-that-fell-apart-at-turn-five.md), record
the take where the counter voice refuses twice, find the turn where it collapsed, and play it up to
that turn to a real person. GREEN is when both refusals are met with a next move, the take passes
twelve turns, and the matter is closed one way or the other. RED is when it collapses at or before
turn eight, or ends unsettled. To make it go red on purpose, refuse flatly, with no reason, twice.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-note-that-did-two-jobs.md), give three readers
one version each and ask what they have to do and how. GREEN is when the sent note's reader names
all three jobs unprompted. RED is when the two-job note's reader says yes confidently and cannot
say *how*.

**And the gate itself**, which is the plan's §7 and is not restated more kindly here:

> Speaking: a recorded shop or café role-play, both voices yours, at least twelve turns.
> Writing: a hundred-word note that asks for something, thanks someone and gives a direction.

A gate is never passed because time ran out. If it goes red, that is the gate working: name two or
three days from days 21 to 39 to re-run, and give each re-run its own `PROGRESS.md` row.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A transaction with no problem in it, which genuinely finishes at turn five.
- Padding the end with six turns of goodbye to reach twelve.
- Counting the turns from memory instead of from the recording.
- Stopping the take because one word came out wrong.
- Both voices at the same pace, so the recording has one speaker in it.
- A counter voice that cannot bring itself to refuse.
- Filling the silence after a refusal with *sorry, sorry, OK, thank you*.
- Leaving the matter unclosed, with no next step for either side.
- A thank-you that could have been sent to anybody.
- A direction that assumes: *the usual place*, *you know the one*.
- The request buried in the middle of a warm paragraph.
- A note that is finished, checked, counted — and never sent.
