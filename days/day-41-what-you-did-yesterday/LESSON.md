## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-day-that-stayed-in-the-present.md), play the
all-present take and the past take to two different people, once each, and ask when it happened
and what the main thing was. GREEN is when the past take gets both answers right unprompted. RED
is when the listener cannot place the present take in time, or thinks it is happening now. To make
it go red on purpose, take every time word out of the present take.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-i-stoped-and-tryed.md), give a real reader your
unchecked page and ask them to mark anything that looks wrong. GREEN is when the checked page
comes back with nothing marked. RED is when your own unchecked page contains an *-ed* spelling you
did not see and they did.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The whole day told in the present, which a listener has to keep moving backwards.
- The complete day from waking to sleeping, with no turn in it anywhere.
- *I didn't went* — the past form kept after *did*, which has its own day later.
- One half of a sentence in the past and the other half not.
- One length of account for everybody, usually the long one.
- A stretched story that starts at breakfast instead of near the turn.
- *Stoped*, *planed* — the doubling rule dropped, and *planed* is a real word.
- *Tryed*, *studyed* — the consonant + *y* rule dropped.
- An *s* on a past verb: *he workeds*.
- *-ed* added to a verb that has its own past: *goed*, *taked*.
- Notes handed in as sentences, with no subject and no full stop.
- Proofreading by reading, which repairs the page inside your head and not on it.
