# What five minutes asks that three didn't

`CF-10` · Day 42

## The one-line answer

Recording 06 had a claim in it, and a claim is an engine — tonight there is no claim, so the
thing that keeps five minutes alive is changing the subject well, and that is a move rather than
an accident.

## The model

Look at what recording 06 actually was. One claim, an opinion each, and the turn changing hands.
It had fuel built into it: as long as you disagreed even slightly, there was always something to
say next. Three minutes is comfortably inside what one subject will carry.

Tonight there is no claim and no subject set in advance. Two people, a recorder, and five minutes
of conversation. And a conversation is a card game — each turn, somebody puts a card on the table.
It carries on for exactly as long as one of you can play something. Nobody loses dramatically. It
just goes quiet, and then it is over.

Here is the arithmetic that makes tonight different. One subject, played honestly, gives you a
handful of cards. At three minutes you can live on that. At five you will play the suit out, and
then you are both sitting there holding nothing, which is when people start looking at their
phones. **A five-minute conversation is not a longer version of a three-minute one. It is a
conversation that has to change subject, and probably more than once.**

There are three ways the subject changes, and they sound completely different on tape.

**Carried — you follow suit.** Something in what they just said opens the next subject, and you
say the word you turned on so they can hear the join.

> Speaking of {{…}}, —
> Oh, that reminds me —

This is the good one, and not because it is polite. It is the good one because their own word
comes back out of your mouth, which is the clearest available proof that you were listening. A
carried change costs nothing and the conversation does not have to restart.

**Jumped — you start a new suit from your hand.** Nothing in the last thing they said led here.
You just have a card and you are playing it.

> Right — completely different question.
> Whatever happened with {{…}}?
> How's {{the thing}} going these days?

Jumps are fine. Real conversations are full of them. The only rule is that a jump gets announced —
you say something in front of it, so the other person knows a new suit has started and does not
spend their next turn working out whether they missed a link.

**Rescued — nobody had a card, and somebody picked up from the deck.** The table went quiet, and
the change happens out of the silence rather than out of anything that was said. There is nothing
wrong with a rescue. But a conversation where every change is a rescue is one person doing all the
work, and you will hear that clearly enough on playback.

**The opposite mistake, which is just as real.** Changing too early. A subject that still had two
or three cards in it, dropped because a silence made you nervous, is a subject you abandoned rather
than finished. You are allowed to refuse a change.

> I'm still stuck on {{…}}, actually.
> You never told me about {{…}}.

Both of those hold the table where it is, and both sound like interest rather than obstruction.

**Where the next subject comes from.** Almost always from something they already mentioned in
passing and did not develop — the job they named on the way to something else, the trip they said
half a sentence about. Day 37 taught you to listen for the gist rather than build your reply; the
by-product of listening that way is that these loose ends land in your head instead of going past
you. Tonight you spend them.

**Today's phrases** — the joins between subjects, which is the only part of tonight you rehearse.
All of these go into `docs/CHUNKS.md` tonight.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| Oh, that reminds me — | Carried change, when their word opens a door you want to walk through |
| Speaking of {{…}}, — | Carried change, naming out loud the word you turned on |
| Whatever happened with {{…}}? | Reopening something from earlier, or from the last time you met |
| How's {{the thing}} going these days? | A jump onto ground you know they can walk on |
| Right — completely different question. | An honest jump, announced rather than smuggled in |
| You never told me about {{…}}. | Picking up the thing they skipped past |
| I'm still stuck on {{…}}, actually. | Refusing a change, because the subject wasn't finished |

## Say it now

Pick any subject at all for the {{slots}} — the point is the join, not the content.

1. *Oh, that reminds me — {{the thing it reminded you of}}.*
2. *Speaking of {{their word}}, {{the new subject}}.*
3. *Whatever happened with {{the thing they mentioned once}}?*
4. *How's {{the thing}} going these days?*
5. *Right — completely different question. {{the question}}.*
6. *You never told me about {{the thing they skipped}}.*
7. *I'm still stuck on {{the last subject}}, actually — {{what you want to know}}.*

Then say lines 1 to 6 again as a single run, with two or three sentences of your own between each
one, so that every join arrives out of real speech rather than out of silence. Joins are easy on
their own and hard in traffic, and it is the traffic version you need tonight.

## Say it yours

**Recorder on. File: `audio/d42-free.m4a`.**

> **Six subjects, six changes, one continuous take.** Start anywhere. Two or three sentences on
> the subject, then change — out loud, with a phrase in front of it. Do it six times without
> stopping the recorder. At least four of the six changes have to be carried out of a word you
> yourself just said.

- **Solo form:** as written. Say the word you turned on, so the join is on the tape and you can
  find it later. Do not plan the six subjects. The whole exercise is finding the next one in the
  sentence you have just finished.
- **With a partner:** they talk about their week and you do nothing at all except change the
  subject six times. No opinions of your own, no stories. Every change carried out of a word they
  used, and you say the word.

## The deliberate failure

Three changes of subject with nothing in front of them. No phrase, no join — finish a sentence
about one thing and start the next sentence about something else, as though the two were connected.

**What breaking sounds like:** on your own, the tape goes flat and strange. You cannot tell,
listening back, whether the second subject grew out of the first or simply replaced it, and neither
could anybody else. With a partner you get something sharper: a small dead spot before they answer,
while they work out whether they missed something.

The finding underneath is worth sitting with. **An unmarked change is heard as a verdict.** You
meant nothing by it — you just had a new thought — but what arrives is *that thing you were saying
wasn't worth carrying on with*. Six words in front of it would have prevented all of that, which is
why those six words are the whole phrase bank today.

## When it breaks in the wild

You are with somebody you do not know well and the subject you were on has run out. You cannot
think of a new one, so you ask them something they have already answered. They answer it again,
slightly more briefly. Now you have used up the only card you had and confirmed that you were not
listening the first time.

**The repair:**

> Right — completely different question. {{the question}}.

Announce it and ask something genuinely new. An honest jump is a perfectly good move and it costs
nothing. Recycling a question you have already had answered is the one thing in this whole area
that actually does damage.
