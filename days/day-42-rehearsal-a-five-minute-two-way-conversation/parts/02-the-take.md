# The take, and counting the changes

`CF-10` · Day 42

## The one-line answer

This is the eighth piece in your portfolio, and the only thing you judge tonight is where the
subject changed and how it changed.

## The model

Portfolio recording 08. Five minutes, two people, and no subject agreed in advance.

That last part is deliberate and it is the whole difference from recording 06. On Day 30 you
agreed a claim before the recorder went on, and the claim carried you. Tonight nothing is agreed.
You sit down with somebody and start, the way conversations actually start, and the piece has to
find its own material as it goes.

Set it up like this.

**Nothing agreed except that you are recording.** No subject, no plan. If you find yourself
picking a topic beforehand, you have quietly turned tonight back into Day 30.

**No notes.** As with every take so far.

**Keep the first take.** Same rule since Day 6, same reason. The first one is the honest one.

**Dead spots stay in.** A five-minute conversation with a stranger's-worth of material in it will
go quiet somewhere. That is not a failure and you do not stop the recorder for it. You have six
cheap ways back.

> Right, what haven't we talked about?
> Ask me something.
> We've gone quiet, haven't we.

*Ask me something* is the cheapest of the lot and the one people find hardest to say. It is two
words, it admits the obvious, and it hands the whole job across. Every silence you have ever sat in
would have ended there if one of you had said it.

*Right, what haven't we talked about?* does something slightly different — it says out loud that
the subject is finished, which stops you both politely pretending it isn't. And *We've gone quiet,
haven't we* names the silence itself, on a dropped tag from Day 26, which turns an awkward gap into
a thing you are both looking at together rather than something happening to you.

> I've got one — {{…}}
> What have you got on this week?
> Right, that's that one done.

*I've got one* is for when you do actually hold a card and are hesitating over whether it is
interesting enough. It is. Play it. *What have you got on this week?* is the subject that is always
available, because everybody has one and nobody has finished it. And *Right, that's that one done*
closes a subject cleanly, so the next one starts on its own ground instead of colliding with the
last of the old one.

**Then the playback, once, all the way through.** You know the job from Day 6: listen with a task,
never for a verdict. Tonight's task is a different count from Day 30's, and the change of count is
the point of tonight.

**Why not the joins again.** On Day 30 you counted joins — every place the turn changed hands —
because at three minutes the way a short exchange dies is at the handover. That is no longer the
thing most likely to kill you. You have spent four days on turn-taking, on holding the floor and on
being interrupted, and the handovers will mostly look after themselves. **At five minutes what
kills a conversation is a subject running out with nobody able to open another one.** So that is
what gets counted.

**Count the changes of subject.** Every place the conversation moved onto something new. For each
one, one label:

- **Carried** — it grew out of a word somebody had just said, and you can hear that word.
- **Jumped** — announced, but from nowhere in particular.
- **Rescued** — it came out of a silence rather than out of anything said.

Three numbers. They go into row 08 of `docs/RECORDINGS.md` tonight, beside the date. And one more
thing you say out loud but do not have to write: how many of the changes were yours.

**Today's phrases** — the ways back in when the table has gone quiet. All of these go into
`docs/CHUNKS.md` tonight.

## Say it now

Before the recorder goes on, say each of these three times, flat and unhurried, so they arrive
without a run-up when the conversation goes quiet.

1. *Right, what haven't we talked about?*
2. *Ask me something.*
3. *We've gone quiet, haven't we.*
4. *I've got one — {{something you have been meaning to ask them}}.*
5. *What have you got on this week?*
6. *Right, that's that one done.*

Then say all six one after another, twice, as a single run. These are the only lines you rehearse
today. Everything else on this recording is live, and the joins you drilled in part 01 have to
arrive on their own.

## Say it yours

**This is the take. Recorder on. File: `audio/d42-conversation.m4a`.**

> **A five-minute two-way conversation.** No agreed subject. Change it whenever it needs changing,
> carry the change out of a word where you can, announce it where you can't, and let the quiet bits
> stay quiet until one of you picks up.

Rules:

- No notes and no subject chosen beforehand.
- **Keep the first take.** You may record a second afterwards; the first is not deleted and is the
  one that goes in the portfolio row.
- Dead spots stay in. Do not stop the recorder to start again somewhere better.

- **Solo form:** you can do this alone and it is the harder version. Write eight subjects on
  separate slips, shuffle them face down, and draw one. Talk about it as if to a person, then hand
  over out loud and answer yourself from the other side, honestly. **When a subject dies, do not
  draw.** Change it yourself, carried out of a word you have just said, and only draw a new slip
  when you genuinely cannot find one. Keep the recorder running throughout. The number of slips you
  had to draw is itself a finding.
- **With a partner:** as written. One instruction for them: do not rescue every silence. If they
  fill all of them, the take measures their conversation rather than yours.

## The deliberate failure

Recorded on the end, after the take is finished. Start a subject, let it die properly, and then sit
in the silence without rescuing it. Do not use any of today's rescue lines. Do not stop the
recorder. Wait it out.

**What breaking sounds like:** you fill it anyway. Almost everybody does. What comes out is half a
sentence about nothing — *yeah… no, it's… yeah, exactly* — and on playback you can hear precisely
what it is: a noise that ends a silence without putting anything on the table. So the silence comes
straight back, usually worse, because now you have both had a go.

Then do it once more and rescue it on purpose with one of today's lines. Listen to the two back
to back. One of them ends the silence. The other one only interrupts it. That difference is the
whole reason those six lines are worth having in your mouth rather than in your notes.

## When it breaks in the wild

A long ride somewhere with a colleague you like well enough but do not know. You cover the obvious
subject, it runs out, and then there is nothing. You both reach for your phones with a small
apologetic movement, and the rest of the journey is spent not looking at each other. Neither of you
minded the other's company. The conversation just ran out of cards and nobody picked up.

**The repair:**

> Ask me something.

Two words, said cheerfully, into the middle of the silence. It is not awkward — it is the opposite
of awkward, because it names the situation you are both already in and hands the next move to
somebody who is definitely holding one.
