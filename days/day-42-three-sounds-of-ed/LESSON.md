## §1 Where we are

Two people are walking back from a cricket ground, arguing about the match. The speaking folder is
about the three ways *-ed* is said — *t*, *d*, and the extra beat *id* — about sorting your own
verbs before your mouth gets involved, and about what a listener hears when every past verb gets
an extra beat.

A message has to explain what happened with a booking. The writing folder is about the twenty
verbs that make their past by changing shape, about writing a real account that uses ten of them,
and about the two ways this goes wrong on paper: inventing a past, and quietly avoiding the verb
altogether.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-every-verb-got-an-extra-beat.md), play both takes
to two real people and ask whether the meaning differs and which one sounds like reading aloud.
RED is when they say the meaning is identical and both point at the same take as the read-aloud
one. GREEN is when your correct take carries every ending right, checked verb by verb against your
sorting sheet. To make it go red on purpose, give every past verb in the account the *id* ending.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-i-goed-and-telled.md), give three readers one
version each and ask what happened and what you want. GREEN is when the real account's reader
answers both immediately and names the date. RED is when the avoidance version — every word of it
correct — leaves a reader unable to say cleanly what happened.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The extra beat on every past verb, which confuses nobody and changes how people speak to you.
- *Want* said where *wanted* was meant, which moves the sentence into the present.
- *d* where *t* belongs: *askd*, *finishd*.
- A three-beat *id*: *want-ted*, *need-ded*.
- Correct in the list and wrong in the sentence, because attention moved to the meaning.
- A pause before every past verb while the rule gets checked.
- *Goed*, *telled*, *buyed*, *thinked* — words that do not exist.
- *Did you went?* and *I didn't saw* — the past form kept after *did*.
- *I have went*, *I seen him* — the third column borrowed into a plain past sentence.
- *Purchased*, *proceeded*, *submitted* — avoidance dressed as formality.
- An account with no dates, which cannot be acted on.
- An account with no last sentence saying what you want.
