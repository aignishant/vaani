## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-story-with-no-middle.md), play the no-middle
take, the no-end take and the whole story to three different people and write down the first thing
each says. GREEN is when the whole story gets a reaction — a laugh, their own story back, a
comment. RED is when the two broken takes both get a question instead: *so what happened?* To make
it go red on purpose, compress everything that happened into *in the end*.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-six-sentences-of-setting.md), give two readers one
version each and ask what happened. GREEN is when the six-job reader can say what happened, in
order, including the turn. RED is when the all-setting reader cannot say what happened, although
every sentence was clear.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A beginning four sentences long, which is where listeners leave.
- The ending given away in the first sentence, so the middle pulls nobody.
- A story with no problem in it, which is an account of a day.
- *Anyway* used as an ending.
- An ending that depends on a shrug, which does not exist on the phone.
- Rehearsing between two tellings, which trains reading aloud from memory.
- Telling the story to somebody who was there.
- Six sentences of feelings — *it was very bad*, *anyway it's fine now* — and no events.
- No sentence with a *but* or a *then* in it, so nothing turns.
- A last sentence that summarises instead of leaving a fact behind.
- One sentence doing three jobs and the next doing none.
- Four background sentences out of six, which feels like careful writing.
