## One-line answer

Write three different six-sentence stories from your own life, send one of them to somebody who
was not there, and keep the question they ask — because the question always points at the job you
left undone.

## The story

The water tank message gets written properly and goes to the family group.

Two people answer with sympathy. One asks:

> Which wall? The one behind the stairs?

That question is worth more than the two sympathetic replies. It says sentence three did half its
job: the reader knows the joint cracked and does not know where the water went.

One sentence is added, the message is re-sent, and nobody asks anything else.

This is what a reader is for. Not praise, and not correction — a question that names the missing
job.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **draft** — a first version.
- **send** — to give it to a real reader.
- **missing job** — a job from the six that no sentence actually did.
- **revise** — to change a piece of writing after somebody has read it.

The work is three stories, one send, and one revision.

1. **Story one** — something that went wrong, from the last month.
2. **Story two** — something that went well or was funny.
3. **Story three** — something from years ago that you still tell people.

Then **send one** to somebody who was not there, and write down the first question they ask.

Three stories, because one story teaches you only about that story. Three shows you which of the
six jobs you always drop.

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 3 writing gate is a one-page story, in paragraphs, with speech marks used once. Three
six-sentence stories is the practice that makes a page possible — and one of these three will
probably become the page.

And a story that has been sent is a different thing from a story that was written. Everything you
avoid saying clearly gets found by one real reader in about nine seconds.

## The rule

**The page checked for this, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[My last holiday](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/writing/a2/my-last-holiday),
whose model opens with the event and the place — "My last holiday was a five-day trip to Prague in
the Czech Republic." — and whose tips are:

> Try to make your writing interesting for the reader. To do this, you can make it personal with
> your own memories and experiences.
> Use adjectives to add detail to your descriptions.
> Write clear and simple sentences and organise your ideas in short paragraphs. Give each
> paragraph a different topic.
> Use *so, but, and, because* and other linking words.

**Write the labels first, every time.** Six words down the side of the page:

```text
   set-up      ______________________________________
   found out   ______________________________________
   what was wrong ___________________________________
   THE TURN    ______________________________________
   settled     ______________________________________
   left behind ______________________________________
```

Labels first is the difference between writing a story and writing until you stop.

**Then the three checks on your own six**, before anybody sees them:

| Check | How to do it |
| --- | --- |
| Six jobs, six sentences | one job per sentence, none doing two |
| The turn is there | sentence four contains *but* or *then*, and something gets worse |
| The verbs are past | underline every verb; check the irregular ones against a list |

**Then choose one and send it.** To one person who was not there. Say nothing except the story.

**Then the question.** Write down the first thing they ask, exactly, and mark which of the six
jobs it points at:

| What they asked | Which job it points at | The sentence I added |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**Then revise and re-send.** One sentence added or changed, not a rewrite. The point is to see how
small the repair is.

**And the variation that stretches you**, once the three are written: take story three — the one
you have told for years — and write it in exactly six sentences, without the parts you usually
tell. Long-told stories are full of sentences that survive because you always say them, not
because they do a job.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **writing all three about the same kind of event**. Three
complaints in a row teach you one shape. The three kinds above are different on purpose: things
that went wrong, things that were funny, things that are old.

The second is **sending it to somebody who was there**:

> Yes, I remember, the wall is still wet.

No question, no finding. The reader has to be somebody who only has your six sentences.

The third is **rewriting the whole thing after the question**. One sentence. If your revision is a
new story, you are avoiding the finding rather than using it.

The fourth is **six sentences that are each two sentences long**:

> The tank on the roof, which my uncle installed about six years ago when he was staying with us,
> started leaking on Tuesday night, and because we were asleep we did not notice for a while, and
> then I heard water on the stairs.

The six-sentence limit means nothing if the sentences are unlimited. Aim for about fifteen words
each.

These are the errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger
had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

The stories people actually write are short and sent in a hurry, and they carry real weight: what
happened with the delivery, what the doctor said, why somebody is not coming. Six sentences is not
a classroom length. It is the real length of most written accounts.

Under pressure, writers drop the turn and the fact left behind — the two jobs that require them to
say something uncomfortable. That is why those two produce most of the questions a reader asks.

A formal reader will not ask a question at all. They will act on the version they were given. That
is the reason to practise the question with a friendly reader now, in a message where the cost of
a missing job is one more message.
