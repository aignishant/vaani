## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-and-then-and-then-and-then.md), give the same
five-step sequence as a chain to one person and signposted to another, wait thirty seconds, and
ask each to say it back. GREEN is when the signposted version comes back with all five steps in
order. RED is when the chain comes back with fewer than four, or with the stage change lost. To
make it go red on purpose, join every step with *and then* and leave no gap anywhere.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-did-you-went.md), give a real reader the vague
record and the dated one and ask what they would check if they worked at the shop. GREEN is when
the dated record gives them at least two things to check. RED is when the vague one gives them
none.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *And then* five times in a row, which gives a listener no stages at all.
- A time word said inside the flow with no gap after it, which signposts nothing.
- *In the end* used in the middle, after which nobody is still tracking.
- *Den* instead of *then*, with the tongue behind the teeth instead of between them.
- Asking *understand?* instead of asking for the sequence back.
- Six steps where four would do, so the important one gets lost.
- Repeating the whole sequence when only one step broke.
- *Did you went*, *I didn't saw* — the past carried twice.
- *Why you didn't call?* — the question word order slipping.
- Three questions inside one paragraph, of which one gets answered.
- A denial with no date, no person and no action, which cannot be checked.
- *Did you were* — *be* does not take *did*.
