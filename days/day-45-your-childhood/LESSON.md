## §1 Where we are

Six hours on a train, and the person opposite asks where you grew up. The speaking folder is about
the three pieces of a childhood answer — a place, a person doing something, one thing that
happened — about having three answers ready for the three questions people actually ask, and about
what a listener remembers a day after hearing a childhood made only of dates.

An old photograph is going into an album and somebody has to write the few lines underneath. The
writing folder is about one moment rather than one decade, about the two readers who find two
different faults, and about the paragraph nobody can draw.

## §3 Setup

For speaking: your phone's voice recorder, three places from your childhood, one long scene you
remember well, the English words for two or three childhood things you do not have yet — looked up
before you start — and two people who do not know your history.

For writing: a notebook and pen or a plain text editor, an old photograph if you have one, three
memories with a time of day attached, one person who was there, one who was not, and two readers
with a pencil.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-childhood-with-nobody-in-it.md), play the
facts-only take and the real take to two people who do not know your history, wait a day, and ask
what they remember about that person. GREEN is when the real take's listener names the person and
the event. RED is when the facts take's listener cannot name one thing that happened. To make it
go red on purpose, use only dates, jobs and numbers, said flatly.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-those-were-happy-days.md), give two readers one
paragraph each with a pencil and ask them to draw what they read. GREEN is when the specific
paragraph gets at least two things from the writing drawn. RED is when the general paragraph
leaves the pencil unmoved.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The childhood given as a list of dates, jobs and numbers of brothers.
- A whole decade in one breath, ending on a conclusion about the present.
- People described — *very kind*, *very strict* — instead of doing something.
- An answer with no place in it at all.
- The long answer given to the short question, and the two-word answer to the long one.
- The memorised answer, said the same way every time.
- A long story with nobody speaking in it.
- A memory that covers ten years, so there is no time of day.
- An ending that explains the meaning instead of leaving it.
- Three adjectives where one concrete noun would do.
- Invented speech in a memory, which always sounds invented.
- A paragraph nobody can draw.
