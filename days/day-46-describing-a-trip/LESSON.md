## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-trip-that-was-a-timetable.md), give the same
real trip as a timetable to one person and in four moves to another, and count the questions each
asks. RED is when the timetable take gets no questions. GREEN is when the four-move take gets at
least two. To make it go red on purpose, leave out every person outside your group and everything
that went wrong.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-there-was-two-beds.md), plant four errors in your
own description and give it to a reader. RED is when any planted error is not found when they are
asked, or when the present-in-the-past error changes what they would do. GREEN is when you fix all
four without your hidden list and the reader agrees the fixed copy says what you meant.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The itinerary: every day and every stop, and nothing that happened.
- The journey told in full when nothing happened on it.
- A trip account with no person in it outside your own group.
- Answering a listener's question with a whole new timetable.
- *There was* with a plural: *there was two beds*.
- *We was* and *they was*.
- A description that starts in the past and drifts into the present.
- *Did* used with *be*: *did there was*, *it didn't was*.
- A description with no negatives, so the reader arrives without what was missing.
- Fixing the loud error and leaving the quiet one.
