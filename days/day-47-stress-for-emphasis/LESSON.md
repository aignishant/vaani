## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-stress-on-the-wrong-word.md), record one
correction four ways and ask a listener what you want them to change after each. GREEN is when only
the right-word take gets the detail named straight away. RED is when the listener cannot name it
from that take, or names it as easily from the wrong-word or flat take. To make it go red on
purpose, keep the words after the pushed word loud.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-paragraph-that-broke-its-promise.md), show a
reader the first line of a paragraph whose real point is at the end, then the whole paragraph. RED
is when what they would do from the first line differs from what they would do from the whole.
GREEN is when your rewrite makes the two the same.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The correction said flat, so it sounds like a repeat.
- Pushing the wrong part of the word: *tues-DAY*.
- Pushing the right word and then the next three as well.
- Repeating the whole detail louder instead of pushing the one part that is wrong.
- Pushing *said*, *told* or *was*, which turns a correction into an argument.
- In a long number, pushing the last digit out of habit.
- The warm-up first line: *there are many things in life*.
- The announcement: *in this paragraph I will write about*.
- A first line that is one small fact and cannot hold the paragraph.
- The point buried in the last sentence, because that is the order it happened.
- A sentence starting *also* that brings in a new topic.
