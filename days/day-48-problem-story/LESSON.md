## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-problem-story-with-no-you-in-it.md), tell one
real problem as pure bad luck to one person and in four beats to another. RED is when the bad-luck
take gets no question about the story and the listener changes the topic. GREEN is when the
four-beat take gets at least one question about something you did. To make it go red on purpose,
make every sentence's subject the problem, a thing or the weather.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-i-was-knowing-and-i-cooking.md), plant four tense
errors in a real paragraph and ask a reader what happened first. RED is when their order differs
from what really happened, or they cannot find a quiet error when asked. GREEN is when your fixed
copy gets the right order from a second reader.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The problem with no action: two beats and *it was bad*.
- A story that is all feeling and no scene.
- Hiding the attempt that failed, which was the best part.
- A list of attempts with no reaction between them.
- Every sentence's subject a thing: *the tyre*, *my phone*, *the shop*.
- The past simple for the background, which turns a picture into steps.
- The event in the past continuous, so the moment never lands.
- The missing *was*: *I cooking dinner*.
- A state verb with *-ing*: *I was knowing*, *I was needing*.
- A picture so long the reader forgets to expect an event.
