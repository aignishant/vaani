# The take, and counting the words they didn't have

`CF-11` · Day 48

## The one-line answer

This is the ninth piece in your portfolio, and the only thing you judge tonight is one number —
how many specialist words you handed over that the other person had no way to hold.

## The model

Portfolio recording 09. Your work, explained to a stranger, start to finish, no notes.

You are not being judged on how interesting your work is. You're being judged on whether somebody
standing on completely different ground could walk across to it. Those are unrelated. Some of the
best explanations you've ever heard were of things you had no interest in whatsoever, and you
followed every step.

Set it up like this.

**A real outsider if you can get one.** Somebody who genuinely does not know — a neighbour, a
relative, somebody at a table. Not a friend who half knows and will fill the gaps for you out of
kindness. Kindness is the enemy of this recording.

**No notes.** As with every take so far.

**Keep the first take.** Same rule as Day 6, same reason. The first one is the honest one.

**Stalls stay in.** You'll hit a place where you can't find a way down. Don't stop the recorder.
You've got three cheap lines for exactly this.

> Hang on — I've used a word there.
> Is that further than you wanted to go?
> Ask me the thing you'd be embarrassed to ask.

The first one is a catch. You'll notice a specialist word roughly one sentence after it's out of
your mouth, which feels too late and isn't. Going back and hooking it up mid-flow is completely
normal speech, and it's far better than the alternative, which is quietly hoping.

The second is the depth check. Say it when the face in front of you is being polite and you've got
nothing to steer by — and remember from part 01 that a nod is not an answer.

The third is the one that changes the recording. Say it near the end and then be quiet. The
questions people ask when they've been given permission to ask something stupid are the exact
questions that show you where your explanation had a hole. You want those on the tape. They're
worth more than anything you say.

**Then the playback, once, with one job.**

**Count the unglossed words.** Every word on the recording that somebody outside your field could
not define, that you used and never explained. Not roughly. Actually count them, on your fingers,
playing the tape through once.

That's one number, and it goes into row 09 of `docs/RECORDINGS.md` tonight beside the date.

Two things about the number. It will be higher than you expect — everybody's is, the first time.
And it is not a score to be ashamed of, because you cannot lower a count you've never taken. This
is the only day in the whole plan where you find out what your actual figure is, and every take
after tonight is measured against it.

**Today's phrases** — the three you rehearse before the take, and nothing else. All of these go
into `docs/CHUNKS.md` tonight, with part 01's.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| Hang on — I've used a word there. | Catching a specialist word one sentence too late |
| Is that further than you wanted to go? | Checking depth, when the face in front of you is only being polite |
| Ask me the thing you'd be embarrassed to ask. | Opening the end, so the questions you get are the real ones |

## Say it yours

**This is the take. Recorder on. File: `audio/d48-stranger.m4a`.**

> **Explain your work to a stranger.** What it is, what it's for, and one real example — then
> open the floor and answer whatever comes back, including the questions you have no prepared
> answer for.

Rules:

- No notes, and no rehearsing the explanation itself beforehand.
- **Keep the first take.** You may record a second afterwards; the first is not deleted and is the
  one that goes in the portfolio row.
- Stalls, backtracks and catches stay in. Do not stop and start again cleanly.
- The questions at the end go on the recording, theirs and your answers both.

- **Solo form:** you can do this alone and it's the harder version, not a lesser one. Explain it
  to an empty chair, all the way through. Then swap sides and play the stranger — out loud, in the
  stranger's voice — and ask three questions you have no answer ready for. Not soft ones. The kind
  that start *but why would anyone*. Then swap back and answer them, live, with the recorder still
  running.
- **With a partner:** as written. Two instructions for them: don't help you, and don't pretend to
  follow. If they've lost you, they say so, mid-sentence, without waiting for a polite moment.

## The deliberate failure

One extra rep, recorded on the end after the take is finished. Take the single thing they asked
about — the one that didn't land — and explain it again by **saying the same sentence again, only
slower and a bit louder.** Word for word. Commit to it.

**What breaking sounds like:** nothing. It sounds like the first version at half pace. Every word
that failed the first time is still there, doing exactly what it did before, and the extra volume
lands on the listener rather than on the problem. If somebody's in the room you'll see it — the
same polite face, held slightly longer.

That's the finding, and it's the reason Day 44 exists. A second attempt has to be made out of
different words or it isn't a second attempt. Repeating is what everyone does under pressure,
because repeating requires nothing of you and feels like effort.

Now say it a third time, properly — different words, different comparison, and this time say the
why before the what. Play the three versions back one after another. The second one adds nothing
to the first. The third one is the only one that was ever going to work.

## When it breaks in the wild

You give somebody the good version, they follow it, and they say something like *so it's basically
{{something else entirely}}, isn't it* — and it's said warmly, as agreement, as though the two of
you have arrived somewhere together. Correct it too hard and you've just told a friendly person
they weren't listening. Let it go and they walk away holding the wrong thing and repeating it to
somebody else.

**The repair:**

> Sort of — the bit that's different is {{the one difference}}.

Take the half they got. Fix the one part that matters and no more. You do not need to reclaim the
whole explanation, and trying to is how a pleasant conversation turns into a correction.
