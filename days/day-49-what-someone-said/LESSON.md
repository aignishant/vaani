## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-who-said-that.md), tell one real conversation flat
to one listener and with edges to another, and have each hand every line back to its speaker. RED
is when the flat take gets at least one line given to the wrong person. GREEN is when the take with
edges gets every line right. To make it go red on purpose, drop every reporting verb after the first
and keep one voice.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-conversation-with-no-marks.md), give a reader the
conversation with no speech marks and no new lines and ask who said each line. RED is when at least
one line goes to the wrong person. GREEN is when the correct copy gets every line right from a third
reader and you fix all four planted errors without your list.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Their words and yours with no reporting verb between them.
- *He said me*, where *he told me* or *he said to me* belongs.
- *You* and *I* inside a quote that end up meaning the wrong person.
- The quote said in your own flat voice with no pause.
- *He* for two different men in the same story.
- *The teacher is saying* for something she said once.
- The full stop inside the mark before *said*: *'The market.' I said.*
- The comma or question mark outside the closing mark.
- A capital on the reporting verb: *'Market again?' He asked.*
- Two speakers in one paragraph.
- A reporting verb on every single line.
- Switching between single and double marks on one page.
