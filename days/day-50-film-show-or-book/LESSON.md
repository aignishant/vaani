## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-whole-plot-start-to-finish.md), answer the same
question as the whole plot to one listener and in three moves to another, and ask each what you thought
of it. RED is when the plot listener cannot say, or no longer wants to watch it. GREEN is when the
three-move listener can say what you thought and whether it is for them. To make it go red on purpose,
join every event with *then* and give no opinion.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-i-was-very-boring.md), swap every *-ing* and *-ed* word
in your real message and ask a reader what you think of each person. RED is when a swapped *-ing* word on
a person is read as an opinion. GREEN is when your restored copy leaves a second reader with only what you
meant.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *Very nice, very good, you should watch* — an answer with nothing in it.
- The whole plot, joined with *then*.
- Giving away the ending without asking.
- No move three, so the listener still has to ask *would I like it?*
- Recommending your own favourite to everybody.
- A warning with no reason, or one that is rude about people who like it.
- *I was boring* for *I was bored*.
- *I am interesting in* for *I am interested in*.
- *-ed* on a thing: *the film was bored*.
- Only *-ing* words in a message about people.
