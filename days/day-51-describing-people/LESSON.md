## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-everybody-is-nice-and-tall.md), describe one person
in a real group photo with only general details to one listener and with distinctive details to another,
and ask each to point. RED is when the general description gets the wrong face or no choice. GREEN is
when the distinctive one gets the right face first time. To make it go red on purpose, use only details
that fit at least three people in the photo.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-kind-helpful-and-hardworking.md), give a reader who
knows two real people two unnamed paragraphs of character words and ask which is which. RED is when they
cannot tell or guess. GREEN is when they match the two evidence paragraphs correctly and can say which
sentence told them.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Answering *what does she look like?* when the question was *what is she like?*
- Character words with no example.
- Appearance in a jumble, with the glasses before the height.
- *He has tall*, *she has forty years*.
- A hard character word said flat: *she's strict*.
- The same description for every purpose.
- A distinctive detail that is unkind.
- The adjective pile: *kind, generous, hardworking, honest*.
- A paragraph about what the person means to you, not about the person.
- An ending that turns into a speech about life.
- Two people in one story both described as *kind and helpful*.
