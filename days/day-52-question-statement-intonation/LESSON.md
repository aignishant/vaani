## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-check-that-sounded-like-an-order.md), record six
real checking questions in three tunes each and ask a listener after every line whether it was a
question and what about. RED is when fewer than five of the six rising-on-the-turning-word takes are heard
correctly. GREEN is when at least five are. To make it go red on purpose, let the voice fall at the end.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-more-cheaper-than.md), plant four comparative errors in
your real message and give it to a reader. RED is when they miss a planted error when asked or misread a
comparison. GREEN is when your fixed copy gets the same choice and reasons from a second reader.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A checking question said falling, so the listener hears agreement.
- The rise saved for the very last syllable.
- The rise on the wrong loud word, which asks about something else.
- A rise too small to hear as a question.
- Echoing a whole instruction with one rise at the end.
- A permission question said falling, which sounds like a decision.
- *More bigger*, *more cheaper*: two comparatives at once.
- *More big*, *more cheap*: *more* with a one-syllable word.
- *Then* for *than*.
- *Gooder*, *badder*, *more good*.
- Describing each option separately instead of comparing.
