## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-two-descriptions-and-no-comparison.md), answer *which is
better?* about one real pair as two descriptions to one listener and as a comparison to another, and ask each
which they would choose and why. RED is when the two-descriptions listener cannot choose, asks which one, or
chooses on a point that did not matter. GREEN is when the comparison listener chooses and names one of your
three points. To make it go red on purpose, use only *is*, *has* and *there is*, and no verdict.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-most-cheapest.md), plant four superlative errors in your
real guide note and ask a reader what they would do. RED is when a line cannot be acted on or a planted error
is missed when asked. GREEN is when a second reader can act on every line of your fixed copy.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Two descriptions in a row instead of one comparison per point.
- A verdict with no condition: *A is better*.
- Five or more points, so the first is forgotten.
- The same comparison for everyone, led by your own priority.
- A verdict that contradicts the points.
- *The most cheapest*, *the most best*.
- *Nearest chemist is on corner*: the missing *the*.
- *The cheaper* for a whole group.
- *The quickest is through the park*: the group missing.
- *Very cheap* where *the cheapest* was meant.
- Every line *the best*.
