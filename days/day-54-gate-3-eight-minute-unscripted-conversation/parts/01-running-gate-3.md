# Running Gate 3, and what the tape is weighed for

`CF-12` · Day 54

## The one-line answer

Today nothing is taught: you and one other person pick up something heavy at both ends and carry
it for eight minutes, and the only question the gate asks is whether it reached the top of the
stairs without either of you letting go.

## The model

This is a measurement, not a lesson. Every move the gate listens for was taught on a day that has
already happened. **No new phrases today** — `docs/CHUNKS.md` is unchanged tonight.

**What the gate is.** An eight-minute unscripted conversation with one other person, on a subject
you did not know about beforehand. One take. Recorded, both voices, as `audio/d54-free.m4a`.

**What it weighs, and what it does not.** Phase 3 made one promise: that another person talking
would stop being an emergency. That is what is on the scale today, and it is the only thing on it.
The gate does not measure your grammar. It does not measure your accent. It does not measure
whether you were interesting, or right, or whether you used a good word. You can make a mistake in
every sentence of this recording and pass comfortably.

What it measures is whether the thing stayed off the floor.

Two people carrying a wardrobe up a staircase are doing one job with two pairs of hands. Neither
of them is carrying it. If one of them quietly stops taking any weight, the other one feels it
immediately — and that is the sound this gate is listening for. A conversation where the other
person did all the lifting is a failed carry, no matter how correct your English was while they
did it.

**So here is what a drop sounds like.** The other person finishes something. Nothing comes back at
them — or something short comes back, and it is finished, and it hands them nothing. Now the whole
weight is theirs again, so they go and find a new subject and start it, because somebody has to.
That is a **rescue**, and rescues are countable from the tape by anybody.

Three or more rescues in eight minutes and the thing is on the floor, whatever else the recording
sounds like.

**These are not drops**, and this matters, because half of what feels like failure from the inside
is not:

- Not knowing a word, and going round it the way Day 52 taught. The weight never moved.
- Mishearing them and asking for it again. Asking is lifting.
- A grammar mistake, said at speed and walked past. Phase 3 does not weigh it.
- Both of you laughing and saying nothing for a moment. That is two people resting, not dropping.
- Talking about something ordinary. The gate does not measure interesting, and a conversation
  about a bus journey that goes somewhere passes over a conversation about politics that dies.

**Running it honestly** is the difficult part of today, and the temptations are specific.

Do not agree the topic with your partner beforehand. Do not run one "to see how it goes" and then
record the second. Do not stop the recorder when it goes badly, and do not delete anything. And
this one is today's own particular trap: **do not tell your partner to go easy on you.** A partner
who has been asked to help will rescue you constantly, out of kindness, and you will pass a gate
that measured them.

**Getting a person, and getting a topic.** The partner does not need good English. They do not
need to know anything about the subject, and they do not need to be in the room. What they need is
to be willing to talk to you and to have been told, plainly: *do not help me, and do not fill my
silences.*

For the topic: write six ordinary subjects on scraps, turn them face down, and have your partner
draw one and read it out. Or they name a subject of their own choosing and tell you nothing about
it in advance. Either way, the recorder is already running when the topic is spoken aloud, and you
start on the next breath.

**The phrases the gate listens for.** All of these are yours already. This is a reminder, not a
lesson, and nothing here is new.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| The thing is, … | Day 1 · you need a running start into a sentence |
| To be fair, … | Day 4 · turning a point round without stopping |
| Bear with me. | Day 9 · the thought needs a moment and you keep the turn while it comes |
| …, or rather, … | Day 17 · the wrong word, fixed inside its own sentence |
| Do you reckon …? | Day 51 · handing the turn back, in the clothes this conversation is wearing |

Everything Day 43 gave you about follow-up questions is doing most of the work today, and Day 49
is what you reach for when one of you has misunderstood the other. If three or four of these
arrive in the take **without you deciding to use them**, the carry is going well, whatever else
you can hear.

## Say it now

Standing up, before your partner draws the topic and before you press record. Fill the slots with
whatever is true right now.

1. *I'm not being graded on being right today — {{the thing you are actually worried about}}.*
2. *If the word doesn't come, I'll describe it and keep going.*
3. *Every time they finish, something goes back — a question, or the other half of it.*
4. *If it dies, I pick it up. That's my end of it, {{…}}.*

Now say all four as one continuous stretch, once, on your feet. Then bring your partner in, press
record, and have them read the topic out loud onto the tape.

## Say it yours

**Recorder on. File: `audio/d54-free.m4a`.**

> **Eight minutes of conversation with one other person on the topic they just drew. One take.
> Both voices on the recording. You do not stop it, you do not restart it, and you do not delete
> it.**

The take is the gate. A rough conversation honestly recorded is worth more here than a smooth one
you reached on the third attempt, because only one of them tells you where you are.

- **Solo form:** there isn't one, and that is the whole point of Phase 3 — the gate is another
  person. What you can vary is who. Somebody at home, a neighbour, somebody you work near, or a
  voice call with anybody at all who will talk to you. The cheapest version needs nothing but a
  phone and somebody willing to pick up.
- **With a partner:** they are told two things and nothing else — draw a topic and read it out,
  and do not help. No slowing down for you, no finishing your sentences, no supplying words unless
  you ask out loud, no kind new questions when it goes quiet.

## The deliberate failure

After the gate take, and only after, record a short follow-up stretch on the same topic with one
rule: **you answer everything fully and correctly, and you never send anything back.** No
questions. No half-sentence handed over. Every answer complete and closed.

**What breaking sounds like:** your partner working. Listen to that tape and count who starts each
subject — it is all of them. You can hear the effort in their voice as it goes on, the questions
getting shorter and a bit more desperate, until the stretch ends with them saying something like
*right, yeah* into a gap. That is what carrying a wardrobe alone sounds like, and it is completely
audible on tape.

And here is the part that is worth the discomfort: **your English is at its best in that
recording.** Full answers, no scrambling, time to get the words right. The conversation is still
dying. Correctness was never what was holding it up.

## When it breaks in the wild

You spend a long stretch talking to somebody you would actually like to know — a friend of a
friend, somebody at a table. You answer everything they ask, and you answer well. You never ask
anything back, because you are concentrating on your English and because their questions keep
arriving so it never seems to be your job. They leave thinking you were not interested in them.
You leave thinking it went fine. Neither of you is right, and you will never find out.

**The repair:**

> That's more or less my whole answer — what about you, though? Do you reckon it's the same?

The handover is the lift. It costs one sentence, and it is the sentence that tells somebody you
are in the conversation with them rather than being examined by them.

## Check yourself

Play back `audio/d54-free.m4a` once, all the way. Pass if:

- [ ] **Two rescues or fewer.** Count the times your partner had to start a fresh subject because
      nothing came back from you. Count them on your fingers as you listen and write the number in
      `docs/PROGRESS.md` whether it passes or not.
- [ ] **Six questions or more from you**, and at least three of them are follow-ups to something
      they had just said rather than new subjects of your own. Quote one of the three out loud.
- [ ] **The subject at the end is not the subject that was drawn.** Say both out loud — the topic
      on the scrap, and what the two of you were actually talking about when the recording ended.
      If they are the same, the conversation was answered rather than carried.

Nothing about accuracy is on this list, and that is deliberate. Do not add it.

**If the gate does not pass**, this is what happens, and it is short.

You name two or three days between 37 and 53 and re-run them. If the rescues came from short
closed answers, re-run Day 43 and Day 50. If they came from you losing the thread of what they
said while your own reply was loading, re-run Day 37 and Day 45. If they came from stopping dead
on a missing word, re-run Day 52 and Day 44. If they came from never getting a turn at all, re-run
Day 38 and Day 53. Each re-run gets its own row in `docs/PROGRESS.md`, dated, with its own honest
note. Then you run the gate again on a later day, with a new person or a new topic, and that take
gets its own row too.

That is all. **It is not a restart**, nothing resets, and Day 55 is still Day 55 when you get to
it. There are no streaks in this repository and no counters. A gate passed on the second attempt
is a better record than one passed on the first, because the second one has evidence underneath
it. The only failure genuinely available today is a partner who was told to be kind.
