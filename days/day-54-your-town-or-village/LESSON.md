## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-town-thats-the-same-as-every-town.md), play two
general descriptions of two real places to a listener and ask for one difference, then play your specific
take and ask what they would recognise. RED is when they cannot name a real difference between the general
takes. GREEN is when they name one specific thing from yours. To make it go red on purpose, use only
*peaceful*, *green*, *friendly*, *good food* and *many temples*.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-brochure-paragraph.md), give the brochure paragraph and
your real paragraph to two readers and ask each to draw what they read. RED is when the brochure reader draws
nothing specific to your town. GREEN is when your real paragraph's reader draws at least two things in it.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The town's name with no anchor a stranger knows.
- *Peaceful, green, good people* — the words every town gets.
- Only good things, so nothing sounds true.
- The same description for a visitor and for somebody moving there.
- A visitor's version with no warning.
- A detail too local to mean anything to a stranger.
- The brochure paragraph: *beautiful, famous, rich culture*.
- The history lesson that never reaches today.
- A list of places with no sentences.
- *There is three temples*.
- The past tense for things that are still there.
