## One-line answer

Write the full paragraph about your real town or village for a real reader who has never been there, then
ask them to draw a map or a picture from it and tell you three things they now know — because what they
draw and what they list is exactly what your paragraph actually said.

## The story

You rewrite the paragraph for your cousin's daughter:

> My family's town is a small market town that lives on its river. It is about two hours south of the
> capital, on the old road to the coast. There is one long market along the river bank, and every stall
> has a blue plastic roof. There are three white temples on the hill above the market, and you can hear
> their bells from the bus stand. In the monsoon, the river comes up to the market steps.

A week later your cousin sends a photo. Her daughter has drawn a river, a row of blue roofs, a hill with
three white shapes on it, and a bus. Under it she has written, *My family's town*. Every object in the
drawing came from one of your sentences.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **the five pieces** — topic sentence, location, things you would see, a detail each, a last line, from
  [part 2.1](2.1-where-what-and-one-true-detail.md).
- **reader** — the person the paragraph is for.
- **draft** — a first version you expect to change.
- **map test** — asking a reader to draw what your paragraph describes.

This part is the paragraph itself, written for a real person, and tested by what they can draw. A drawing
is a strict test: a reader can only draw what the words actually put there.

The phase 3 gate is a one-page story in paragraphs, read by a stranger. Its setting paragraph has to

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-23.**
[Using 'there is' and 'there are'](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/using-there-there-are),
for singular and plural:

> **There is a restaurant** in the station.
> **There are two cafés** in the shopping centre.

[My last holiday](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/writing/a2/my-last-holiday):
"Try to make your writing interesting for the reader. To do this, you can make it personal with your own
memories and experiences."

**Step one — choose a real reader** who has never been to your town or village.

**Step two — plan in five lines**, not sentences:

**Step three — write the paragraph**, six to eight sentences, all in the present simple. At least two
sentences with *there is* or *there are*, one with *there isn't* or *there aren't* if something the reader
might expect is missing.

**Step four — the *there* check.** Underline every *there is* and *there are*. Read the word after each:
one thing, or more than one?

**Step five — the map test.** Give it to your reader with a blank page and ask:

> Could you draw what you just read? Stick figures are fine.

Then ask:

> Tell me three things you now know about this place.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the paragraph that answers *what do I feel about my town?*** instead of
*what is in it?*. Feelings belong in the last line, if anywhere.

The second is **every detail about the same thing**: four sentences about the temple, nothing else. The
reader can draw a temple and not a town.

The third is **the past tense creeping in** from your memories: *there was a cinema*. If it is still there,
*there is*. If it has gone, that is a different paragraph, about then.

These are the errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger
had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Good descriptions of places are drawable. That is not a rule anyone writes down, and it is how readers
judge them without knowing it: they either see something or they do not.

Under pressure, writers describe their town the way they feel it. A stranger needs the way it looks. Keep
one line for the feeling, at the end, and give the rest to the eyes.

A reader from far away is the best test of all, because they cannot fill any gap from their own knowledge.
If they can draw it, anybody can.
