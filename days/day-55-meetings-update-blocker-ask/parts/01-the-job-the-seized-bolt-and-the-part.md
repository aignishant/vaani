# The job, the seized bolt, and the part you need ordered

`FN-16` · Day 55

## The one-line answer

When your turn comes round in a meeting you say three things in this order — where the job has got
to, what is stopping it, and what you need somebody to do — and the third one is the one everybody
drops.

## The model

Phase 4 starts today, and the change is that somebody is now judging. Not unkindly, and usually
not on purpose. But in a meeting there is a person whose job is to decide what happens next, and
your turn is the only evidence they get from you.

So think of a workshop. A vehicle is up on the ramp, somebody rings to ask how it is going, and
the mechanic says three things. *It's nearly done — everything's back together bar the exhaust.
The bolt's seized so I can't get the old one off. I need you to say yes to drilling it
out, and I'll need somebody to give me a hand with it.* Where it is. What is stuck. What they need
from you.

Nobody taught that mechanic a framework. It is simply what you say when your turn comes and you
want something to happen. And it is exactly what a meeting turn is, whatever your work actually is.

**The update is where it has got to, not what you have been doing.** This is the first thing that
goes wrong, and it is very audible. Under pressure people narrate: *so on Monday I started looking
at it, and then I spoke to someone, and then I found that actually —* That is not an update. That
is a defence, and the room hears it as one, because the only reason anybody lists their own effort
is that they think effort is what is in question.

> Where I've got to is: the first half is finished and being checked.

State. Not history. If your first sentence has a *then* in it, you are narrating.

**The blocker is the seized bolt, and it is a fact, not a complaint.** Name the thing, and name
what it is waiting on. Do not name it as a person's failure, because the moment it sounds like
blame the room stops listening to the update and starts managing the relationship.

> What's holding it up is that I can't test it until the new data comes across.

And if nothing is stuck, say so out loud. *Nothing's blocking me.* Silence about blockers is read
as everything being fine, so a person with a real blocker who says nothing has actively told the
room the wrong thing.

**Then the ask.** This is the part that goes missing, and today exists for it.

An ask is a sentence with a verb in it that somebody other than you has to do. That is the whole
test. *It would be good to have the data soon* has no verb anybody has to do. *Can you send me the
data, or tell me who owns it?* does.

> So the ask is: I need a decision from you on which one we're doing.

Without it you have described a car to its owner and then stood there in silence. The owner says
*right, thanks*, puts the phone down, and the bolt is still seized tomorrow. **That is why an
update changed nothing** — not because nobody was listening, but because nothing was requested.

**And then stop, out loud.** A turn that trails off makes the chair ask whether you have finished,
which is a small cost you pay every single time.

> That's me.

**Three sentences is a whole turn.** One for each part. You are not being judged on how much you
say; you are being judged on whether the room can act on it — and a long turn is where the ask
gets buried, because by the time you reach it everybody is waiting for you to stop.

**Today's phrases** — the three parts, and the way out. All of these go into `docs/CHUNKS.md`
tonight.

| Phrase | You use it when |
| --- | --- |
| Update from me — | Opening your turn without a run-up |
| Where I've got to is … | The state of the job, in place of the story of the week |
| It's done bar the … | Nearly finished, and you want the remainder named rather than guessed at |
| What's holding it up is … | Naming the blocker as a fact about the work |
| I can't move until … | The blocker is somebody else's decision or delivery |
| Nothing's blocking me. | Nothing is stuck, and you say so rather than leaving a silence |
| So the ask is … | Turning the corner into the request, out loud, so it cannot be missed |
| What I need from you is … | Aiming the ask at one person rather than at the room |
| Who's picking that up? | Nobody owns it yet and you want that decided while everybody is here |
| That's me. | Handing the turn back audibly so the chair does not have to ask |

## Say it now

Take one real thing you have going on and run all three parts on it. Every line lands flat and
finishes — no rising tone at the end, or the room will hear a question.

1. *Update from me — where I've got to is {{the state}}.*
2. *It's done bar the {{remaining piece}}.*
3. *What's holding it up is {{the blocker}}.*
4. *I can't move until {{the thing you're waiting on}}.*
5. *Nothing's blocking me.*
6. *So the ask is: {{the verb somebody else has to do}}.*
7. *What I need from you is {{the specific thing}}.*
8. *Who's picking that up?*
9. *That's me.*

Now build a whole turn out of three of those lines — one update, one blocker, one ask — and say it
as a single unbroken stretch, twice. Then say the same three in the wrong order, ask first, and
listen to what happens: the request arrives before anybody knows what it is for, so you have to
explain it afterwards, and the explanation sounds like an apology.

## Say it yours

**Recorder on. File: `audio/d55-free.m4a`.**

> Give your turn on **three different things you actually have going on** — three separate turns,
> one after another, on the same recording. Each turn is an update, a blocker and an ask. Each ask
> names who you want to do it. Each turn ends with you handing the turn back out loud.

- **Solo form:** as written, standing, at the volume of a room rather than a desk. Do not write
  anything down first — an update you have drafted is a document being read, and the difference is
  clearly audible.
- **With a partner:** they play the chair. Their only job is to say *right, thanks* after each turn
  and move straight on. If they cannot tell you what you asked them for, the ask was not in it,
  whatever you meant to say.

## The deliberate failure

Take the same three things and give each turn as a full narrative. Everything you did, in order,
with the reasons. Include the blocker somewhere in the middle, as a passing difficulty. End on
what you are going to try next. **No ask at all.**

**What breaking sounds like:** hard-working. That is the trap, and it is why this survives in so
many people's meetings for years. It sounds diligent. It sounds like somebody across the details.
Nobody in the room would criticise it.

And nothing happens. Play it back and try to answer one question: **what would somebody have to do
after hearing this?** There is no answer in the recording. Nobody in a meeting will ever chase you
for an ask you did not make — they will say *great, thanks* and move to the next person, and the
blocker will still be there next week, and the week after, and it will start to feel like a thing
about you rather than a sentence you did not say.

## When it breaks in the wild

You raise the same blocker three weeks running. Every time you mention it, honestly and clearly.
Every time somebody nods. Nothing changes, and by the third week you have quietly decided that
this room does not listen to you, or does not rate you, or that your English is not good enough to
be taken seriously in it. Then somebody else joins, mentions the same blocker once, ends with
*so can you sign that off today?* — and it is sorted before anyone has left the room.

Nothing about them was better. They finished the sentence.

**The repair:**

> Sorry — I've mentioned this before and I've never actually said what I need. What I need from
> you is a yes or a no on it, today.
