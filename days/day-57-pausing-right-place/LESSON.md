## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-pause-that-changed-who-was-there.md), record
four real sentences with true and deliberately wrong pauses, and ask a listener how many people or
things were in each. RED is when they give the wrong number for a line you said right. GREEN is when
every right line gets the true number and at least half the wrong lines get a different one. To make
it go red on purpose, flatten the list tune and swallow the blinks.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-break-in-the-wrong-place.md), give a reader a
copy of your page with one planted join and one planted split, ask them to name each paragraph, and
ask one fact question. RED is when the joined block gets one name or the fact question is answered
wrong. GREEN is when a second reader names every paragraph of the repaired copy to match your labels.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A story told at one level, with small gaps but no real stop.
- Filling every stop with *um*.
- Coming back after a stop at the same low pitch.
- Reading the marked script instead of looking up.
- A long stop after every sentence.
- A list said with no lift, so the items blur.
- A name and its description said as a list, so one person becomes two.
- The wall: a whole page in one block.
- A break in the middle of one idea.
- A new line with no visible gap.
- Labels so wide that nothing is ever broken.
