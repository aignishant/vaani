## §1 Where we are

A colleague's story from the tea break, retold at home that evening, and your mother thinks you went
to a police station. The speaking folder is about the frame that says whose story it is and the
switch from their *I* to *he* or *she*. It's also about retelling two real stories to people who
don't know the tellers, and about the story that becomes yours when the frame and the *she* go
missing.

A long forwarded message, a story in a schoolbook, a newspaper item, and a reader who hasn't seen
it. The writing folder is about closing the page before you write, so the words are yours and the
facts stay exact. It's also about retelling a real story in about a hundred words with a fact check,
and about the deliberate copy with synonyms swapped in, set beside your real retelling and
underlined.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §3 Setup

For speaking: your phone's voice recorder, paper for five-line notes, two or three real stories
people told you recently, and four or five listeners who don't know the tellers.

For writing: a notebook and pen or a plain text editor, one real story of two hundred words or more
that you can read and close (a forwarded message, a news item, a schoolbook story), a real reader
who hasn't read it, and two more readers for the underline test.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-story-that-became-yours.md), play three
versions of one real retold story to three different listeners and ask who it happened to. RED is
when the right version's listener doesn't name the teller, or the deliberately wrong version's
listener still does. GREEN is when the right version gets the teller at once and the no-frame
version gets *you*.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-retelling-that-was-a-copy.md), give a reader the
original, a deliberate synonym copy and your real retelling, and ask them to underline every run of
five or more matching words. RED is when any run is underlined in your real retelling. GREEN is when
the copy is full of runs and the retelling, after repair, has none for a second reader.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Starting straight in with the teller's *I*, and no frame.
- *She* at the start, and the teller's *I* back at the exciting moment.
- Filling a gap you don't remember with something that sounds right.
- Your own opinions all the way through, so the story is longer than the first telling.
- The ending told in the middle.
- Writing with the original open.
- Swapping single words for synonyms and calling it your own words.
- A number or name changed from memory and never checked.
- Cutting the ending to save words.
- Opening the original to check a fact, and copying the sentence as well.
