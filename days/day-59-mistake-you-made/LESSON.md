## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-mistake-with-no-mistake-in-it.md), play a vague
and a concrete version of one real mistake to two different listeners and ask what exactly you did
and what you do now. RED is when the concrete version's listener can't say back both. GREEN is when
they can, and the vague version's listener can't.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-i-use-to-drink-tea-every-morning.md), plant four
*used to* errors, one of them *use to* for a habit you still have, and ask a reader what you still do
today. RED is when that habit is missing from their list, or two planted errors go uncircled. GREEN is
when a second reader lists every present habit from the repaired page and circles nothing.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *Did a mistake* for *made a mistake*.
- Stopping after the cost, with no *so now I …*.
- The mistake told without *I*.
- A fake mistake that is really a boast.
- A long cost that turns into a complaint.
- *Some mistakes*, *things*, *you learn*: the vague version.
- *I use to live* in a yes-sentence.
- *Didn't used to* with an extra *d*.
- *Used to went*: a past verb after *used to*.
- *Use to* as a word for *usually*.
- *Used to* in the *now* half.
