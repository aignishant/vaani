## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-take-that-ran-out-of-story.md), compare the gate
take with a take told without notes. RED is when the gate take is under two hundred words or its
stranger couldn't give three parts. GREEN is when the gate take passed both and take B has shown you
your collapse line. To make it go red on purpose, record with no notes and no long stops.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-page-that-slipped-into-the-present.md), give a
slipped copy and the fair copy to two different readers and ask when it happened and whether any of
it is still going on. RED is when the fair copy's reader thinks any of it is still going on, or finds
a really wrong verb. GREEN is when they say it's all over.

**And the gate itself**, which is the plan's §7 and is not restated more kindly here:

> Speaking: a recorded story from your life with a beginning, middle and end, at least two hundred
> spoken words.
> Writing: a one-page story in the past, in paragraphs, with speech marks used once.

A gate is never passed because time ran out. If it goes red, that's the gate working: name two or
three days from days 41 to 59 to re-run, and give each re-run its own `PROGRESS.md` row.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A story with no problem in it, which ends at forty words.
- A retold story, a film or somebody else's life.
- Stopping the recording at a stumble, or editing takes together.
- Counting the words from memory instead of from a transcript.
- Reading a full script aloud, flat and even.
- Padding to two hundred: *so yeah, anyway, basically*.
- Playing the take to someone who already knows the story.
- The middle paragraph slipping into the present.
- The page in one block.
- Speech with no marks, or with the comma outside.
- A page and a half, because the story was good.
- A page that's finished, checked and never given to anybody.
