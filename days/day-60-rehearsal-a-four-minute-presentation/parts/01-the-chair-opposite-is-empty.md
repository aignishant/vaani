# The chair opposite you is empty

`CF-13` · Day 60

## The one-line answer

Ten recordings in, every single one was made across a table from somebody or to nobody at all —
tonight the chair opposite you is empty, there are chairs behind it with people in them, and
nobody in any of them is going to help you.

## The model

Have a look at what the other person has been doing for you this whole time.

They gave you gaps. Every time they spoke, you got to breathe and think, and you have never once
had to arrange that for yourself. They told you when you had lost them — a face does that without
meaning to, and you have been reading it and adjusting without knowing you were. They filled your
stalls; when you ran dry they said something and you got back on. They decided how long your turns
were: you spoke until they were ready, and that was a shared decision. And they ended it. Somebody
always ends a conversation, and about half the time it isn't you.

Take the person out of the chair opposite. All five of those jobs come back to you at once, and
that is the whole difference between recording 10 and recording 11.

Then put people in the chairs behind, and add the second thing: they are judging. Not maliciously.
They are just doing what an audience does, which is sit there quietly forming a view while you
talk. In every previous take, the other person had to hold up their half, so their attention was
occupied. An audience's attention is entirely free, and it is pointed at you.

**So the five jobs, back to you.**

**Your gaps you now make.** Days 45 and 58 gave you this. There is nobody else's speech to breathe
in, so the silences are yours to place, and they will feel much longer than they are.

**Your structure they now hear only because you say it.** Day 57. In a conversation your listener
could ask *sorry, which bit?* Tonight they can't, and won't.

**Your stalls you now recover from alone.** This is the new one, and it needs a line. In an
exchange you had *Where were we?* from Day 30 — a question, aimed at another person, who answers
it. Alone, nobody answers. So the solo version is not a question. It is you saying where you are,
out loud, to yourself, in front of everybody.

> Where was I — right, the second one.

Say it flat and keep going. The lost thread is not the failure. The silence you leave while you
hunt for it privately is the failure, and it is what an audience remembers, because from out there
it is indistinguishable from you having nothing left.

> Let me back up a step.

That one is for when you notice you have gone somewhere they cannot follow — you took two steps
that only make sense from inside your own head. Going back is a strong move and it always sounds
like it.

**Your turn length is now entirely your problem.** Nobody is waiting to speak, so nothing tells you
to stop, and the two failures here are opposite. One is running long because you have material
left. The other is stranger and more common: **finishing before you expected to, and then filling.**
The talk is done, the room is quiet, and the quiet is unbearable, so you add a fourth thing that
was never on the map. Everything you say after your ending is the worst part of the recording, and
it is nearly always the last thing the room hears.

The move is to have somewhere for the extra material to go.

> I've got a bit more on this if it's useful.

That is an offer. It puts the choice in the room, and if nobody wants it, you have finished
gracefully instead of trailing.

**And you now end it.** Nobody else will.

> That's me — I'll stop there.

Land it and stop. Not *so, yeah* — you will want to say *so, yeah*, everybody does, and it is the
sound of an ending that was not planned.

**The silence is not a verdict.** One more thing, because it is the thing that breaks people the
first time. A room listening to you makes no noise at all. In a conversation, silence meant
something had gone wrong — the other person had disengaged, or you had said something odd. In a
presentation, silence is what listening sounds like. It will feel exactly like the bad kind. It
isn't, and you cannot tell them apart from where you are standing, so you do not get to try.

> I'll keep going and take questions at the end.

Say that early, once, and you have told the room what the silence is for. It is as much for you as
for them.

opposite. All of these go into `docs/CHUNKS.md` tonight.

## Say it now

Use the same three-part talk you built on Day 57. Stand up, face an empty chair with two or three
more behind it, and run these in order — with real content between them, thin content, whatever
arrives.

1. *I'll keep going and take questions at the end.*
2. *{{first part}} … Where was I — right, {{the second one}}.*
3. *{{second part}} … Let me back up a step. {{the same thing, from further back}}.*
4. *That's interesting but it's not this — I'll come back to that at the end.*
5. *{{third part}} … I've got a bit more on this if it's useful.*
6. *That's me — I'll stop there.*

Then run lines 2, 3 and 6 again, and this time **plant a real stall**: stop dead in the middle of a
sentence, wait until it genuinely feels bad, and then come back with *Where was I — right,*. You
are rehearsing the recovery, not the talk. A recovery you have never said out loud is not a
recovery, and tomorrow is not the place to find that out — tonight is.

  you can actually let one sit there.
- **With a partner:** they sit, say nothing, and give you no face at all — no nodding, no
  encouragement, nothing. That is the instruction and it will be harder for them than for you.
  Afterwards they say one thing only: where they lost you, if they did.

## The deliberate failure

Run part one of your talk again, and when you lose the thread this time, **do not say anything.**
Hunt for it silently. Stand there. Wait until you find it, however long that takes, and then carry
on as if nothing had happened.

**What breaking sounds like:** on the tape it is a hole. Not a pause — a pause has shape and sits
between two things. This is nothing, and it is unaccountable: there is no voice in it, no breath,
no signal, no explanation. Play it to yourself and notice that you cannot tell, from the recording
alone, whether the speaker was thinking or had stopped. That is exactly the information the room
has, and a room with no information about a silence assumes the worse of the two.

Then run the same stretch and lose it again, and this time say *Where was I — right,* out loud
the instant it goes. Listen back to both. The recovery costs you four words and it converts a hole
into a person organising themselves, which nobody minds at all. Four words. That is the entire
distance between the two takes, and it is the reason to say it aloud tonight rather than promising
yourself you will remember tomorrow.

## When it breaks in the wild

Presenting down a line to people who are all muted with their cameras off. No faces, no sound, no
sign that anybody is there. Every signal you have ever used to steer by is gone at once, and after
a while you start talking to the possibility that nobody is listening — you speed up, you drop the
gaps, you skip the third part to get it over with.

**The repair:**

> I'll keep going and take questions at the end.

Say it out loud even though nobody answers. It is not really for them. It tells your own mouth
that the silence has been accounted for, and it stops you presenting to an audience you have
started to assume is not there.
