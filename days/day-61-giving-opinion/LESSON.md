## §1 Where we are

Sunday lunch, and your brother has been offered a job in another city. The speaking folder is about
the three pieces of a spoken opinion: an opener, a reason with *because*, and one real example. It's
also about giving real opinions on three questions people around you are really asking, and about the
opinion hidden under *it depends* that nobody can find.

A message to your mother about coming home and your father's medicine. The writing folder is about
*going to* for plans decided before, and *will* for decisions made now, promises, offers and
predictions. It's also about writing a real message with both so a reader can sort *fixed* from
*promised*, and about the planted errors, one of which turns a booked plumber into a guess.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-opinion-hidden-under-it-depends.md), play a hidden
and an open version of one real opinion to two different listeners and ask what you think and how
strongly. RED is when the open version's listener can't say your view in one sentence. GREEN is when
they can, and the hidden version's listener can't.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-i-will-going-to-call-you.md), plant four future-form
errors, one of them a fixed plan written as a *will* guess, and ask a reader what's fixed. RED is when
that plan isn't in their *fixed* list, or two errors go uncircled. GREEN is when a second reader sorts
the repaired message the way you meant.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- An opinion with no opener, heard as a rule.
- An opinion with no reason, heard as a mood.
- *To be honest* in every sentence.
- A practice question nobody around you is asking.
- No *what do you think?* at the end.
- *It depends* in place of the opinion.
- The real view said last, after the hedge.
- *Will going to*: two futures at once.
- *I going to*, with the *be* missing.
- *Will to*, with an extra *to*.
- Every line in *will*, so nothing reads as fixed.
- A promise with no day on it.
