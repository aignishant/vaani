## One-line answer

An opinion out loud has three pieces: an opener that shows it's your view (*I think*, *in my view*,
*to be honest*), a reason with *because*, and one example from real life. Without the opener it
sounds like a fact, and without the reason it sounds like a mood.

## The story

Sunday lunch. Your brother has been offered a job in another city, with more money and no family
nearby. Your father asks around the table:

> Should he go?

Your aunt says:

> He should go. Money is important.

It sounds like a rule, and your brother goes quiet.

Your cousin says:

> I think he should go, because he's young and it's only two years. My friend did the same,
> and he came back with savings and a better job here.

Your brother turns to him and asks, *which friend?* The talk opens up.

Both said *go*. One sounded like an order. The other sounded like a person with a view, a
reason, and something real behind it.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **opinion**: what you think or feel about something, which other people might not share.
- **opener**: the few words at the start that show an opinion is coming: *I think*, *in my view*.
- **reason**: why something is so, from
  [day 56](../../../day-56-explaining-why/parts/01-speaking/1.1-reason-and-result.md). Here, why you
  think what you think.
- **example**: one real case that shows your reason is true.
- **fact**: something true that could be checked.

An opinion said out loud has three pieces, in this order:

| Piece | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| opener | tells the listener *this is my view, not a fact* | *I think …* |
| reason | tells them *why* | *… because he's young* |
| example | shows them it's real | *My friend did the same …* |

The opener changes how the listener hears everything after it. *He should go* sounds like a
fact or a rule. *I think he should go* sounds like a person talking, and invites an answer.

## The rule

**The pages checked, all opened on 2026-09-23.**
[Opinions](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/exams/speaking-exams/opinions) lists phrases
for giving your opinion: "I think... / I don't think...", "I believe... / I don't believe...", "In my
opinion,", "For me,", "Personally, I think...". Its phrases for asking include "What do you think?"
and "What's your view?".
[to be honest](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/to_be_honest): "Frankly, honestly", with the usage note
that "overusing it undermines its purpose".
[in my view](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/in_my_view), which opens the entry *in someone's view*:
"the way or manner in which someone feels about something", with the synonym "in someone's opinion".

**Choose the opener by how the listener will feel.**

| Opener | When | Sounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *I think* | almost any time | ordinary, open |
| *In my view* / *In my opinion* | a more serious question | careful, thought-through |
| *Personally* / *For me* | when you know others may disagree | *this is just me* |
| *To be honest* | when the listener may not like it | frank, a little risky |

**Say the opener lightly, and push the opinion.** *I think* is quick and quiet. The loud word is the
one that carries your view:

> I think he should GO.
> To be honest, I think it's too FAR.

**Then *because*.** One reason, said plainly:

> … because he's young, and it's only two years.

**Then one example.** Something you saw, heard or did:

> My friend did the same, and he came back with savings.

**Stop there.** Opener, reason, example. Three sentences at most.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the opinion with no opener**:

> He should go. Money is important.

The listener hears a rule. If they disagree, they feel they have to fight it.

The second is **the opinion with no reason**:

> I think he should go.

Then silence. The listener waits, then asks *why?* It sounds like a feeling, not a view.

The third is **to be honest in every sentence**. As the reference page says, overusing it undermines
it. A listener starts to wonder about the sentences without it.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard you make.

## In real life

Fluent speakers give opinions many times a day, usually with *I think* and a quick *because*. The
example is what makes them persuasive: people remember *my friend did the same* much longer than
*money is important*.

Under pressure, in a disagreement or at a meeting, people drop the opener and the example and repeat
the opinion louder. That's when talks turn into arguments. The opener costs two words and keeps the
temperature down.

A native listener hears *I think* as an invitation to reply. An opinion said as a fact is heard as
the end of the conversation.
