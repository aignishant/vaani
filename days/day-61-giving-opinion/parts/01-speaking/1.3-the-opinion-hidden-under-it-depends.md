## One-line answer

Record your real opinion on one question twice: once deliberately hidden under *it depends*, *maybe*
and *both are good*, and once with an opener, a reason and an example. Ask two listeners *so what do I
think?* An opinion hidden to stay safe doesn't sound polite. It sounds like you don't have one.

## The story

Your manager asks the team at a meeting: *Should we move the Saturday shift to Sunday?* She goes round
the table. When it's your turn, you're nervous, and you say:

> Hmm, it depends. Both have good points. Maybe Saturday is OK, but Sunday is also fine. It depends on
> everyone.

She nods and moves on. Afterwards, the schedule changes to Sunday, the day your children are home. A
colleague says, *you should have said something.*

You did say something. Thirty words. Nobody could tell what you thought, so your view counted for
nothing.

What you meant was:

> To be honest, I'd prefer Saturday, because Sunday is the only day my children are at home. I'd lose
> my whole family day.

Two sentences, and she would have heard it.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have heard from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **hedge**: to avoid saying clearly what you think, so you can't be wrong.
- **opener**, **reason**, **example**: from [part 1.1](1.1-opener-reason-example.md).
- **stake**: something you'll gain or lose because of the answer.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

Some hedging is normal and polite. *It depends* is a real answer when it really does depend, and you
say *on what*. The problem is the hedge that *replaces* the opinion:

| Hedge | Is there an opinion after it? | What the listener hears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *It depends on the weather. If it rains, I'd stay in.* | yes | a real, careful view |
| *It depends. Both are good. Maybe.* | no | nothing, or *I don't care* |

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 4 gate asks for an opinion with three reasons. A hidden opinion can't carry reasons,
because there's nothing to give reasons for. Hearing, on purpose, that a listener can't find your
opinion is how you learn to catch yourself hiding it.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-23.**
[Opinions](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/exams/speaking-exams/opinions): "I think...",
"In my opinion,", "For me,", "Personally, I think...".
[to be honest](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/to_be_honest): "Frankly, honestly".

**Step one: choose one real question** where you have a real stake and where you're tempted to hide
your view: a family decision, a work change, a choice with a friend.

**Step two: record two versions.**

1. **H**, deliberately hidden: *it depends*, *both are good*, *maybe*, *whatever everyone thinks*. No
   reason, no example.
2. **O**, the real opinion: an opener, one reason with *because*, and one example.

**Step three: the what-do-I-think test.** Play H to one listener and O to a different listener, both
people who don't know your view. Ask each:

> So what do I think? Say it in one sentence.

Then ask:

> How strongly do I feel about it: not at all, a little, or a lot?

**Step four: fill the table.**

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** when O's listener can't say your real view in
one sentence, or says *not at all* for something that matters to you. Then your real version is still
too hidden. It is **GREEN** when O's listener gets your view and its strength, and H's listener
doesn't.

**Step five: the repair.** If O failed, change the opener to *to be honest* or *personally*, push the
word that carries your view, and put your stake in the reason: *because my children …*. Try it on a
new listener.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **choosing a question you don't care about**. Then H and
O sound the same, because there's nothing to hide. Choose one where speaking up costs you something.

The second is **a listener who knows your life** and fills in your view from what they know.

The third, in real speech, is **the real view said last, after the hedge**: *it depends, both are good,
maybe … I'd prefer Saturday.* By then the listener has stopped listening. Put the view first.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows in
it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

In many families and workplaces it feels safer not to disagree with the person in charge. But the
people who are asked for their view and give none are usually counted as agreeing.

Under pressure, speaking to a manager or an elder, choose the softest opener, *personally* or *for
me*, and still say the view and the stake. Soft opener, clear view.

A native listener hears a string of *it depends* as either shyness or not caring. They almost never
hear it as politeness, which is often what it means in the speaker's first language.
