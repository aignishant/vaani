## §1 Where we are

A colleague at lunch hates the new timetable, and you don't. The speaking folder is about the scale from
*I agree* to *let's agree to disagree*, and the three steps of a soft disagreement: show you heard them,
a gentle *but*, your view with a reason. It's also about disagreeing with three real people, and about
the blunt *no, that's wrong* played to listeners who say how it would feel.

A question from the parents' group at school, and a vote about the tree by the gate. The writing folder
is about the five moves of an opinion paragraph: view, reason, example, other side, close. It's also
about writing one for a reader who might disagree, and about the circular reason that has the shape of a
reason and nothing inside it.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-no-thats-wrong.md), play a blunt, a mixed and a soft
version of one real disagreement to three listeners and ask how they'd feel from 1 to 5 and what your
reason was. RED is when the soft version scores 3 or more, or its listener can't give your reason. GREEN
is when it scores 1 or 2, its listener gives the reason, and the blunt version scores higher.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-because-i-think-so.md), give a circular copy and your real
paragraph to two readers and ask why the writer thinks this. RED is when your real paragraph's reader can
only give your view back, or finds nothing to check. GREEN is when they give a different, checkable
reason, and the circular copy's reader can't.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *No, that's wrong*, heard as an insult.
- *But* with nothing before it.
- Soft words in a loud, fast voice.
- *I see what you mean* said quickly, with nothing true after it.
- Five reasons after *but*.
- Not knowing when to agree to disagree.
- The view said four times, and no reason.
- *For example* followed by the view again.
- The other side with no *but* and no answer.
- Testing the paragraph on someone who already agrees.
- A circular *because*.

After your practice, tell a friend what you did. Use this only when it is true: "I learned to disagree
in three steps: say one true thing about their side, a gentle *but*, and then my view with a reason, with
my voice level. I disagreed with three real people, and they answered my reasons instead of getting
defensive. Then I said the same thing bluntly and found out how it felt to the people who heard it. In
writing, I learned the five moves of an opinion paragraph: view, reason, example, the other side, and a
close. I wrote one for somebody who disagreed with me, and they argued with my example. Then I made the
reason circular on purpose, and a reader couldn't say why I thought what I thought." If you did only one
