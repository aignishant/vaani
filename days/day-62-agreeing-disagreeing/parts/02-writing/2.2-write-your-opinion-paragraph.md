## One-line answer

Write a real opinion paragraph of about a hundred words for a real reader who might disagree, using the
five moves. Then ask the reader to answer it in one sentence. If they argue with your reason or your
example, the paragraph worked. If they only say *I disagree*, it gave them nothing to argue with.

## The story

Your building is voting on whether to cut down the big tree by the gate. Some neighbours say its roots
are breaking the path. You want to keep it.

You write a paragraph for the residents' group:

> I think we should keep the tree by the gate, because it keeps the whole car park cool in summer. For
> example, last May the cars under it were ten degrees cooler than the others. Some neighbours think
> the roots will break the path, but we can repair the path for much less than a new tree would cost.
> So I believe we should fix the path and save the tree.

Mr Iyer from the second floor, who wanted it cut, replies:

> Ten degrees is a lot. Who measured that?

He's still on the other side. But he's arguing with your example, not just saying *no*. And two other
neighbours change their vote.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **the five moves**: view, reason, example, other side, close, from
  [part 2.1](2.1-view-reason-example-other-side-close.md).
- **draft**: a first version you plan to change, from
  [day 38](../../../day-38-hobbies-and-free-time/parts/02-writing/2.2-write-your-paragraph.md).
- **reply test**: giving your writing to a reader and asking them to answer it.

The best reader for an opinion paragraph is somebody who might disagree. A reader who already agrees
will say *yes, exactly*, and tell you nothing about whether your reason works.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[An opinion essay](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing/b1-writing/opinion-essay):
write "in clear paragraphs", give your view with "I think," "In my opinion," or "I believe," and mention
other viewpoints with "Some people think".

**Step one: choose a real question and a real reader** who might disagree with you: a relative, a

**Step three: write the paragraph**, about a hundred words, as one block with no breaks. Count the words
and write the number at the bottom.

**Step four: the move check.** Label each sentence 1 to 5 in the margin. Is every move there, in order?
Does move 3 have something a reader could check: a number, a place, a person, a date?

**Step five: the comma check.** A comma after an opener at the start of a sentence, and before *but*
when it joins two full sentences.

**Step six: the reply test.** Give it to your reader and ask:

> Could you answer this in one sentence?

Write their sentence down and decide what it answers:

| Their reply answers … | Means |
| --- | --- |
| my reason or my example | the paragraph gave them something to discuss |
| only my view: *I disagree* | the reason or example is missing or weak |
| something I never wrote | move 1 wasn't clear |

**Step seven: rewrite** the weak move if the reply was *I disagree* or went off topic.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **testing it on someone who agrees**. They say *very good*, and you learn
nothing. Choose somebody on the other side.

The second is **the example with no detail**: *For example, it's hot in summer.* Nobody can check it,
so nobody can argue with it, so it persuades nobody.

The third is **an unfair other side**: *Some people think we should destroy nature for no reason, but
…*. Nobody on the other side would say that, so they stop reading. Write their view the way they'd say it.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows in
it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Residents' groups, school meetings, office discussions and family decisions all run on short opinion
paragraphs, often typed on a phone. The ones with a checkable example get quoted in the meeting.

Under pressure, a decision being made tomorrow, people send the view alone, with a lot of feeling.
Don't skip move 3.

A reader who argues with your example is taking you seriously. That's a success, even if they don't
change their mind.
