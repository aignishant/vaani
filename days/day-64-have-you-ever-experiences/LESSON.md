## §1 Where we are

A wedding table, a stranger, and *Have you ever been to Kerala?* The speaking folder is about answering
in three moves: *Yes, I have*, the switch to the past for when and what, and a hand back. It's also
about trading six questions with a real person and counting the turns each one opened, and about the
bare *No, never* that ends a friendly conversation.

A skills-sharing group, a volunteer notice at a clinic, and a friend's hotel application. The writing
folder is about the frame in the present perfect and the dated details in the past simple. It's also
about writing a real experience paragraph for a reader who needs it, and about the tense that never
switches, found with a time-word hunt.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-no-never-and-the-silence-after.md), play four real
*no* answers said bare and with a bridge to a listener and ask what they'd say next. RED is when any
bridged answer gets *OK*, nothing or a change of subject. GREEN is when every bridged answer gets a reply
about what you said.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-i-have-visited-jaipur-in-2018.md), give a reader your
real paragraph and a copy that never switches tense, and ask them to underline time words and circle
wrong verbs. RED is when anything is circled in your real paragraph. GREEN is when nothing is, and the
copy has a circle at every time word.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *Yes, I have* and nothing after it.
- The story told in *have*: *I have been there in 2019*.
- *Yes, I did* as the answer to *Have you ever …?*
- Asking all the questions and never handing back.
- A question everyone answers *yes* to.
- A bare *No, never*.
- A bridge that isn't true.
- A time word with *have* in writing.
- All frame and no detail, or all detail and no frame.
- Details the reader doesn't need.
- The hidden time word: *when I was a student*.
