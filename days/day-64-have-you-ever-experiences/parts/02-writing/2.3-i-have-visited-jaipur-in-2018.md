## One-line answer

Rewrite your real experience paragraph on purpose so that every detail stays in the present perfect
with its time word (*I have visited Jaipur in 2018*), and so that the frame loses its summary. Then run
the time-word hunt on both copies with a reader. A tense that never switches looks careless on the
page, and on an application, careless is the first thing a reader notices.

## The story

A friend asks you to check her application for a hotel reception job before she emails it. Her
paragraph:

> I have worked in a hotel in 2020. I have answered the phone and I have checked in guests last year.
> I have visited Jaipur in 2018 and I have helped tourists there.

Everything in it is true and relevant. But every sentence is *have* + a time, and it reads like the
same sentence four times. You underline *in 2020*, *last year*, *in 2018*. Every time word sits next to
a *have*.

She rewrites it:

> I have worked with guests and tourists in two different jobs. In 2020, I worked at the front desk of a
> small hotel. Last year, I checked in about forty guests a day during the wedding season.

She gets the interview. The manager tells her the paragraph was *clear and professional*.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **frame**, **detail**, **the switch rule**: from [part 2.1](2.1-the-frame-and-the-detail.md).
- **time word**: a word that shows when, from
  [day 56](../../../day-56-explaining-why/parts/02-writing/2.1-four-words-that-put-events-in-order.md).
  Here, any word or phrase that says when: *in 2018*, *last year*, *when I was twenty*.
- **time-word hunt**: underlining every time word and checking the verb next to it.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

Two things go wrong when the switch never happens:

| What's wrong | Example | What the reader sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a time word with *have* | *I have visited Jaipur in 2018.* | a grammar error, on every line |
| no frame, only details in *have* | *I have worked … I have answered … I have visited …* | a list with no point |

## Why Vaani needs it

Phase 5 is applications, CVs and cover letters. A careful reader of those marks a time word with
*have*, and if the switch rule isn't automatic yet, it appears in every sentence.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Present perfect](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/present-perfect):
"If we say when something happened, or we feel that that part of our life is finished, we use the past
simple", with "We visited Russia for the first time in 1992."

**Step one: take your real paragraph** from [part 2.2](2.2-write-your-experience-paragraph.md). Keep a
clean copy.

**Step two: make copy N**, deliberately never switched: every detail in *have* + participle, with its
time word kept, and the frame removed. Label it *deliberate*.

**Step three: the time-word hunt, with a reader.** Give both copies to a reader who writes English
well. Ask them to underline every time word and circle any verb next to one that looks wrong.

**Step four: fill the table.**

| Copy | Time words underlined | Circled verbs | Reader's comment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N (deliberate) | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| my real paragraph | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** when the reader circles any verb in your real
paragraph, a time word sitting with *have*, or a missing past form. It is **GREEN** when your real
paragraph has nothing circled, and copy N has a circle next to every time word.

**Step five: repair, and one more.** Fix anything circled in your real paragraph. Then take the last
thing you wrote about your experience, a form, a message or a profile, and run the time-word hunt on it
yourself.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a copy N that keeps the frame**. With the frame there,
it still has a point, and the list problem doesn't show. Take the frame out.

The second is **a reader who doesn't write English well**. They won't circle anything. Choose someone
who does.

The third, in real writing, is **the hidden time word**: *I have worked there when I was a student.*
*When I was a student* is a time, even though it has no number. The hunt includes it.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Hiring managers read many experience paragraphs. A tense error in every line makes a paragraph look
unchecked, whatever it says.

Under pressure, a form to finish quickly, the time-word hunt is the one check worth doing. Every time
word, every verb next to it.

A careful reader also notices the opposite: a frame with no details. That doesn't look wrong, just
empty. Keep both.
