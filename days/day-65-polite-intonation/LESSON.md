## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-thank-you-that-sounded-bored.md), play six real
polite lines, each flat and warm, mixed up to a listener who scores each from 1 (cold) to 5 (warm). RED
is when any warm line scores 3 or lower. GREEN is when every warm line scores 4 or 5 and the flat ones
score lower.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-you-mustnt-bring-anything.md), plant four modal errors,
one a *mustn't* where you mean *don't have to*, and ask a reader what they're allowed to do. RED is when
they read the swap as forbidden, or miss both form errors. GREEN is when a second reader sorts the
repaired note the way you meant.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A flat *thank you*, heard as *go away*.
- A request with no movement, heard as an order.
- The tune so big it sounds like sarcasm.
- Warm at the thank-you, flat at the request.
- The range shrinking when you're tired or nervous.
- *Mustn't* where you mean *don't have to*.
- *Must to*, *should to*.
- *She have to*.
- *Must* on every line.
- A strong rule with no reason.
- Forgetting to say what the reader doesn't need to do.
