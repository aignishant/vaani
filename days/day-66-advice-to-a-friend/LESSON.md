## §1 Where we are

A friend calls late about the rent, and you tell her to move before she's finished. The speaking
folder is about four steps: understand, check, one idea, hand back. It's also about using them with
three real people who bring you real problems, and about the fast advice a friend scores for how
heard they felt.

A cousin alone in a new city, an old friend's tea shop, and a father in hospital. The writing folder is
about the five parts of a reply to a problem: thank, understand, suggest, offer, close. It's also about
a real reply checked by a stand-in reader before you send it, and about the reply that turns into your
own story, found by counting sentences.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-advice-that-came-too-fast.md), answer a friend's
real problem once with fast advice and once with the four steps, and ask how heard they felt from 1 to 5.
RED is when the four-step version scores 3 or lower. GREEN is when it scores 4 or 5 and higher than the
fast one.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-reply-that-was-all-about-you.md), count the sentences
about the friend and about you in your real reply and in a copy full of your own story, and ask two
stand-ins whose problem each is about. RED is when your real reply is mostly about you. GREEN is when it's
mostly about them and the copy isn't.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *You should …* before the friend has finished.
- A list of ideas instead of one.
- A check said flat, which closes the topic.
- Waiting for a big problem and missing the small ones.
- A sympathy line said in a rush.
- A written reply that starts with advice.
- *I understand, it's hard* in place of their real details.
- An offer you can't keep.
- A reply much longer than the problem.
- Your own story filling the reply.
