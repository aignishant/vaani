## One-line answer

Give advice to a friend in four steps: say you understand (*That sounds really hard*), check the real
problem (*So it's the rent that worries you most?*), offer one idea softly (*Why don't you …?*,
*Perhaps you could …*, *If I were you, I'd …*), and hand it back (*What do you think?*). Advice lands
when the friend feels heard first.

## The story

Your friend calls you late one evening. Her landlord wants to raise the rent, and she doesn't know what
to do. Before she's finished her second sentence, you say:

> You should move. Find a cheaper place.

She goes quiet, then says *yes, maybe*, and ends the call. She doesn't move, and she doesn't call you
about it again.

A week later she tells your other friend, who says:

> Oh no, that's really stressful. So is it the money, or do you not want to leave the flat?

> I love the flat. It's the money.

> Hm. Why don't you ask him if you can pay a bit more, but not the full amount? Maybe he'd rather keep a
> good tenant. What do you think?

She tries it, and the landlord agrees to half. The second friend's advice wasn't cleverer. It came
after listening.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **advice**: telling somebody what you think is a good idea. They can choose.
- **suggest**: to offer an idea for the other person to agree with or not, from
  [day 26](../../../day-26-making-plans/parts/01-speaking/1.1-three-ways-to-suggest.md).
- **sympathy**: feeling sorry for somebody, and here saying so, from
  [day 48](../../../day-48-problem-story/parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-problem-story-with-no-you-in-it.md).
- **check**: here, saying back what you think the real problem is, to see if you're right.
- **hand back**: to give the other person the next turn, from
  [day 38](../../../day-38-hobbies-and-free-time/parts/01-speaking/1.2-say-how-often-and-ask-back.md).

Good advice between friends has four steps, and the advice itself is only one of them:

| Step | What you say | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. understand | a sympathy line | *That sounds really stressful.* |
| 2. check | the problem, said back as a question | *So it's the money, not the flat?* |
| 3. one idea | a soft suggestion | *Why don't you ask him for less?* |
| 4. hand back | a question | *What do you think?* |

Steps 1 and 2 are what make step 3 welcome.

## Why Vaani needs it

Giving advice is one of the most common things friends do. It sits on everything in this phase so far:
an opinion, disagreeing gently, a warm voice. In phase 5 the same four steps help with a colleague's
problem or an upset customer.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Giving advice](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking/b2/giving-advice). In its
video, two colleagues listen to a friend's problem at work before offering ideas, and use these phrases:

> If I were you, I'd email and ask her to stop sending them.
> Perhaps you could try talking to Yuna about it?
> Why don't you try doing nothing?

**Three soft ways to offer an idea**, gentlest first:

| Phrase | How it sounds |
| --- | --- |
| *Perhaps you could …* | very gentle: just an idea |
| *Why don't you …?* | friendly, a little more sure |
| *If I were you, I'd …* | you'd really do this in their place |

*You should …* is fine between close friends, but it sounds more like a rule. Save it for when they ask
*what should I do?*

**The voice.** The sympathy line needs a warm, falling tune: *That sounds really HARD.* ↘ The idea needs
the fall-rise, which leaves room for *no*: *Why don't you ASK him?* ↘↗

**One idea at a time.** If they don't like it, offer a second one. Five ideas at once sound like you're
not listening.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **advice first**:

> You should move.

Before the friend has finished explaining. They feel you've solved a problem they haven't told you yet.

The second is **the list of ideas**:

> You should move, or talk to him, or get a flatmate, or ask your parents …

The friend can't pick one, and it sounds like you want the conversation to end.

The third is **the check that isn't a question**: *So it's the money.* Said flat, it closes the topic.
Let your voice rise: *So it's the MONey?* ↗

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard you make.

## In real life

Good friends often spend most of a conversation on steps 1 and 2. The idea comes late, and sometimes the
friend finds it themselves while you're checking.

Under pressure, when a friend is upset, people rush to fix it because the silence is uncomfortable. One
sympathy line and one check buy you time and make your idea better.

A native listener hears *If I were you* as serious and caring. They hear *You should* straight after a
problem as a little bossy, even from a friend.
