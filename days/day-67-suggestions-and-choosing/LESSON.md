## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-anything-is-fine.md), make the same decision with a
friend as a deliberate loop and with the four moves, count the turns and ask who did the work. RED is
when the four-move version doesn't end in a decision somebody could act on, or your friend says they
still did the work. GREEN is when it ends in a decision in fewer turns, with the work shared.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-if-it-will-rain.md), plant four conditional errors, one a
*when* on something uncertain, and ask a reader which things will definitely happen. RED is when they
call the planted *when* line certain, or miss the *will* after *if*. GREEN is when a second reader sorts
the repaired message the way you meant.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *I don't mind* back and forth, and no decision.
- A preference with no reason.
- Three or more options, so it's a menu, not a choice.
- A close that nobody acts on.
- Offering an option you'd hate.
- *If it will rain*: *will* in the *if* clause.
- *If it rains, we stay in*: no *will* in the result.
- No comma after a first *if* clause.
- *When* for something that might not happen.
- A plan with no *if* lines, so the phone rings.
