## One-line answer

Talk a real person through your coming week out loud, with every uncertain thing on its true step of the
ladder and a reason for each. Then ask them to tell you back how sure you are about each thing. If their
steps match yours, your ladder words were heard.

## The story

Your mother wants to know if you're coming home for your cousin's engagement on Sunday. You call her and
go through the week honestly:

> I'll definitely come on Sunday morning. I might come on Saturday night too, if I finish work early,
> but probably not, because we have stock-taking. And I probably won't stay for dinner, because the last
> bus is at eight.

She says it back: *So Sunday morning for sure, Saturday night probably not, and you'll leave before
dinner.* Exactly right. She tells your aunt not to count you for dinner.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have heard you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **the certainty ladder**: *will definitely*, *will probably*, *might*, *probably won't*, *definitely
  won't*, from [part 1.1](1.1-the-certainty-ladder.md).
- **say back**: when a listener tells you in their own words what they understood, from
  [day 59](../../../day-59-mistake-you-made/parts/01-speaking/1.2-tell-two-real-mistakes.md).
- **honest step**: the step on the ladder that matches what you really think.

This part is about being honest with the ladder, out loud, to someone who's going to plan around it.

## Why Vaani needs it

Plans with family, friends and colleagues depend on this all the time. The say-back in this part is the
same check a manager does in your head when you give an update at work in phase 5.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **all *maybe***: every uncertain thing said with *maybe*. The listener
can't tell the likely ones from the unlikely ones.

The second is **the missing reason**. *I probably won't stay for dinner* with no *because* sounds like
you don't want to. The reason protects the relationship.

The third is **talking to someone who doesn't care about your week**. They won't listen carefully enough
to say it back. Choose somebody who's planning around you.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows in
it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard you make.

## In real life

Families and teams run on this kind of talk: who's coming, who might be late, who probably can't. People
who give honest steps with reasons are the people others find easy to plan with.

Under pressure, when the honest step is disappointing, *to be honest* and a reason make it easier to say,
and easier to hear.

A native listener who says your week back correctly will plan exactly by it. That's what makes honest
steps worth the effort.
