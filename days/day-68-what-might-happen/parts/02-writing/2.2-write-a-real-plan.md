## One-line answer

Write a real plan for a real group with a goal line, four to six numbered steps with a person and a date
each, and a backup line. Then give it to one of the people in it and ask *what are you doing, and by
when?* If every person can answer from the plan alone, it works.

## The story

Your building's water tank needs cleaning, and the secretary asks you to organise it. You write:

> **Goal: clean water tank, with water back on, by Sunday the 12th.**
>
> 1. Get two quotes from cleaning companies. — Mr Das, by Wednesday the 8th.
> 2. Choose one and book it for Saturday the 11th. — Secretary, by Thursday the 9th.
> 3. Put up a notice about the water cut. — Me, by Thursday the 9th.
> 4. Fill buckets and the overhead drums. — Every flat, Friday night.
> 5. Check the water is clean and turn it back on. — Mr Das, Saturday evening.
>
> If the company can't come on the 11th, we'll clean it on the 18th and I'll put up a new notice.

You show it to Mr Das. He reads it and says: *So I get quotes by Wednesday, and I check the water on
Saturday evening.* Exactly right. Nobody phones you all week.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **goal**, **step**, **who**, **deadline**: from [part 2.1](2.1-goal-steps-who-when.md).
- **backup line**: the *if* line for the likeliest problem, from
  [part 2.1](2.1-goal-steps-who-when.md).
- **owner**: the one person responsible for a step.

A plan is tested by the people in it. Each person should be able to find their own line, and know
exactly what to do and by when, without asking you.

## Why Vaani needs it

Every plan you write for other people in English will be tested this way, by them, whether you ask or not.
Asking first is how you find the gaps before they cost anything.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Action plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_plan): "An action plan is a detailed plan outlining
actions needed to reach one or more goals"; "Action steps should be clear and actionable versus vague
ideas or thoughts"; "Responsibility: each action step needs to have one person responsible";
"Completion date".

**Step one: choose a real goal** that needs at least three people or at least three days. Name the
people.

**Step two: list the actions** in time order, before you write the plan.

| Action | Owner | By when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**Step three: write the plan** in the shape from part 2.1: goal line, numbered steps, backup line.

**Step four: the three-check.** For every step: one action? One owner? A real date?

**Step five: the owner test.** Give the plan to at least two of the people in it. Ask each:

> What are you doing, and by when?

Write down their answers. If anyone can't answer, or gets it wrong, fix that step.

**Step six: send it**, if it's for real.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the plan written in paragraphs**: *First Mr Das will get quotes, and then
the secretary can choose, and I'll put up a notice …*. The reader has to hunt for their own name. Numbered
lines let them find it.

The second is **the plan with no backup**. The company cancels, and everyone asks you what now.

The third is **too many steps**: fifteen lines for a small job. Group the small ones. Four to six is usually
enough.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows in
it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Plans like this get pinned on notice boards, saved in family groups and printed at work. People come back
to them to check their own line, again and again, so each line has to stand on its own.

Under pressure, organising something in a hurry, write the plan anyway, even a short one. Three
lines with names and dates save a dozen phone calls.

An owner who can tell you their line without looking at it has read the plan properly. That's the best
sign it works.

## Check yourself

- **Write:** `TODO(me)` Choose a real goal that needs at least three people or three days, and fill the
  action table in time order. Write the plan with a goal line, four to six numbered steps and a backup
  line. Do the three-check on every step. Give it to at least two of the people in it, ask what they're
  doing and by when, write down their answers, and fix any step they couldn't answer. Send it if it's for
  real.
- **Say out loud:** Read your backup line. Say which problem it's for and why that one is the likeliest.
