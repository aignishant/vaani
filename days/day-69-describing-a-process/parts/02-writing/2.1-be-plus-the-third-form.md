## One-line answer

Write the passive with *be* + the past participle: *The pickle is made by hand. The temple was built in
1890.* Use it when the thing is more important than who did it, or when you don't know who did it. Add
*by* + the doer only if the doer matters.

## The story

Your aunt sells mango pickle from her kitchen, and she wants a small label for the jars. Her first try:

> I make this pickle by hand. I use mangoes from our village. I add no chemicals.

Customers don't know her, so *I* means nothing to them. The label is about the maker, not the
pickle.

You rewrite it:

> This pickle is made by hand in small batches. The mangoes are grown in our village. No chemicals are
> added.

Now every sentence starts with what the customer cares about: the pickle, the mangoes, the chemicals.
Nobody needs to know who *I* was. The jars sell out at the temple fair.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **past participle**: the third form of a verb, the one after *have*: *made*, *grown*, *built*, from
  [day 63](../../../day-63-weekend-and-year-plans/parts/02-writing/2.1-have-or-has-and-the-third-form.md).
- **subject**: the person or thing a sentence is about, usually the first thing in it, from
  [day 11](../../../day-11-asking-what-where-who/parts/02-writing/2.1-questions-with-be.md).
- **object**: the person or thing the verb lands on, from
  [day 37](../../../day-37-i-didnt-catch-that/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-word-that-comes-after-the-verb.md).
- **active**: a sentence where the subject does the action: *My aunt makes the pickle.*
- **passive**: a sentence where the subject receives the action: *The pickle is made.*
- **doer**: the person or thing that does the action.

The same fact can be written two ways:

| | Subject | Verb | What comes first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| active | *My aunt* | *makes the pickle.* | the doer |
| passive | *The pickle* | *is made (by my aunt).* | the thing |

The passive moves the thing to the front. The doer moves to the end with *by*, or disappears.

## Why Vaani needs it

Explaining a process in writing, tomorrow's writing day, is full of passives: *the rice is soaked, then it
is ground*. Labels, notices, reports and formal letters in phase 5 use it constantly, because they're
about things and results more than about people.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Passives](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/passives). It gives the
form as "be + past participle", and says "Only the form of be changes to make the tense. The past
participle stays the same." It lists three reasons to use it: when "we prefer not to mention who or what
does the action", to "start a sentence with the most important or most logical information", and in
"more formal or scientific writing". On the doer it says: "It is not always necessary to add who or what
did the action." Its examples include:

> A lot of olive oil is produced in Italy.
> The suspect will be released tomorrow.

**The two tenses you need first.**

| Tense | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| present | *am / is / are* + past participle | *The pickle is made by hand.* · *The mangoes are grown here.* |
| past | *was / were* + past participle | *The temple was built in 1890.* · *The roads were repaired last year.* |

**Only *be* changes.** *Is made*, *was made*, *are made*, *were made*. *Made* stays *made*.

**Match *be* to the subject.** *The pickle is*, *the mangoes are*, *the temple was*, *the roads were*.

**Add *by* + doer only when it matters.** *The school was opened by the chief minister* tells the reader
something. *The pickle is made by people* doesn't. Leave it out.

**The negative.** *Not* after *be*: *No chemicals are added.* · *The road wasn't repaired.*

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the missing *be***:

> The temple built in 1890.

A reader can guess the meaning, but it reads as if the temple did the building.

The second is **the base form instead of the participle**:

> The pickle is make by hand.

The third is **the wrong participle**: *The road was builded.* · *The mangoes are grow here.* Check the
third form in the table from day 63.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows in
it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Labels, signs, notices and reports are full of passives: *Made in Kerala. Tickets are sold at the gate.
This building was opened in 1972.* They're about the thing, and the reader doesn't need to know who.

Under pressure, writing a quick notice, people often drop *be* because the sentence is short. *Shop
closed on Sunday* is fine on a sign. In a full sentence, write *The shop is closed on Sunday.*

A formal reader expects the passive in reports and official letters. Too many *I did this, we did that*
can read as personal where the reader wanted facts.
