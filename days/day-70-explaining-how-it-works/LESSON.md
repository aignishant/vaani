## §1 Where we are

A grandmother asks how paying by phone works, and your nephew starts with the server. The speaking
folder is about four moves: what it's *for*, what it's *like*, *how* it works in two or three stages, and
a say-*back* about the listener's own life. It's also about explaining two real things to two real
beginners, and about the explanation that starts inside, stopped after three sentences.

A new colleague who doesn't know how leave works, a building's shared water bill, and a stock check with
no end. The writing folder is about the four parts of a written process: a title, a first sentence with
the purpose and result, numbered stages, and an end line. It's also about a real page for a real
newcomer tested with three questions, and about the page with no purpose and no end that leaves a reader
stranded.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-explanation-that-started-inside.md), play the first
three sentences of an inside-first version and a four-move version of one real explanation to two
beginners, and ask what the thing is for. RED is when the four-move version's beginner can't say what it's
for, or can't say back how they'd use it at the end. GREEN is when they can, and the inside-first
beginner can't.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-process-with-no-result.md), give your real page and a
copy with no first sentence and no end line to two readers, and ask what it's for and how they'd know
they'd finished. RED is when your real page's reader can't answer both. GREEN is when they can, and the
copy's reader has to guess.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Starting inside: the parts before the purpose.
- A comparison from your world, not theirs.
- *Do you understand?* in place of a say-back.
- A listener who already half-knows the thing.
- Correcting a beginner's right say-back into your words.
- A proper name used as if it explained itself.
- A written process with no first sentence.
- A written process with no end line.
- One stage that runs to four lines.
- Writing in your own words for things, not the newcomer's.
