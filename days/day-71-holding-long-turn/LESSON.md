## §1 Where we are

At a family lunch, your aunt asks why you changed jobs, and you say one sentence with your voice
falling to the floor. Your cousin takes over. The speaking folder is about the small words, *well*,
*so* and *the thing is*, and the voice that stays up until the last sentence. It covers three real
turns tried on a listener who's been told to jump in, and one turn dropped on purpose to find out
where you lose it.

A neighbour asks what you'd do about the noisy family upstairs. The writing folder is about *if* + a
past verb and *would* + a base form, for things that aren't real. It covers a real reply with *If I
were you*, and a broken copy that leaves a reader unsure what's imagined.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- Every sentence falling to the bottom, so each one sounds like the last.
- *So, so, well, so*: a marker used as a filler.
- *The thing is* with no main point after it.
- Reading the turn map with your eyes down and your voice flat.
- A voice held so high that every sentence sounds like a question.
- A jumper who is too kind to jump.
- *Would* in the *if* clause: *If I would have*.
- *If I won, I will*: two conditionals mixed in one sentence.
- *If I am you*, or *If I was you* on a page.
- Advice with no action in it: *If I were you, I'd be happy.*
