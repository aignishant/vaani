## §1 Where we are

At a tea stall late at night, a friend asks what you'd do if you could do anything. One person says
*travel* and the talk moves on. Another names a bakery, a grandmother and a name for the shop, and
everyone leans in. The speaking folder is about the three pieces of a dream said out loud: the dream,
the reason and a detail. It's about the small *d* in *I'd* that keeps a dream from sounding like a
plan, three dreams told to three real people, and one dream said like a plan on purpose.

At New Year, the family sticks a sheet on the fridge and everyone writes a wish. The writing folder
is about the verb after *wish*, which moves back even when you mean now. It covers the five-sentence
wish paragraph that ends with something real, and a broken copy where the reader can't tell the wish
from the news.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- The one-word dream: *travel*.
- *I'd* said so fast the *d* disappears.
- *I'll* where you mean *I'd*: a dream heard as a plan.
- A dream with only reasons against it.
- Forgetting the question back.
- *I wish I have*, *I wish I can*: a present verb after *wish*.
- *I wish I will*: a hope written as a wish.
- A list of three wishes, none explained.
- A wish paragraph with no real last sentence.
- A reader who already knows the wish.
