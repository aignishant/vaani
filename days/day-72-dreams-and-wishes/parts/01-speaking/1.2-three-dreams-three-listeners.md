## One-line answer

Tell three real dreams of different sizes to three real people: a small one, a big one and an
impossible one. Each has the dream, a reason and a detail, and ends with a question back. Then write
down the first question each listener asked. If they asked about your detail, your dream landed.

## The story

At the same tea stall a week later, the talk turns to dreams again. Three friends each try.

The first says, *I'd love a holiday*. Nobody asks anything.

The second says, *If I had a month off, I'd go back to my village and paint my grandparents' house
blue, because it's been grey for twenty years*. Two people ask *why blue?* at once.

The third says, *If I could fly, I'd go to work over the traffic*. Everyone laughs, and someone says
*me too, every morning*.

A small dream, a big dream and an impossible one. The same three pieces made each of them worth
answering.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **dream**, ***I'd***: from [part 1.1](1.1-if-i-could-and-why.md).
- **small dream**: something you could really do soon, if a little thing changed.
- **big dream**: something possible, but far away or expensive.
- **impossible dream**: something that can't happen, like flying. It's still fun to talk about.
- **question back**: a question to the listener about their own dream, so they get a turn.
- **landed**: here, when a listener understood and cared enough to ask about it.

Different sizes of dream need slightly different openings:

| Size | Opening | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| small | *I'd really like to …* | *I'd really like to learn to swim this year.* |
| big | *If I had …, I'd …* | *If I had the money, I'd …* |
| impossible | *If I could …, I'd …* | *If I could fly, I'd …* |

## Why Vaani needs it

A dream is a short opinion about your own life: *this matters to me, and here's why*. The phase 4
gate asks for an opinion with reasons. Today the reasons are yours and the stakes are small, so it's
a good place to practise giving a reason that someone else wants to hear.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **three dreams that are really one**: *a good job, a bigger house,
more money*. Choose three different parts of your life.

The second is **forgetting the question back**. You tell your dream, the listener says *nice*, and
the conversation stops. The question back is what makes it a conversation and not a speech.

The third is **the impossible dream said as a joke with no reason**: *If I could fly … ha*. Even an
impossible dream needs its *because*. That's what people laugh with.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Among friends, dreams are traded: I tell you mine, you tell me yours. The best ones are specific and
a little bit silly.

Under pressure, with someone you've just met, people give the safest, most general dream. It's
polite and forgettable. One real detail is still polite, and it gets remembered.

A native listener asks about the detail almost every time. If they don't ask anything, there was
nothing to ask about.
