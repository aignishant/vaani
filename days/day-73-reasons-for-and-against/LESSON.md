## §1 Where we are

Your family is deciding whether to buy a second-hand scooter, and your brother piles up six reasons
in one breath. Nobody knows which side he's on. The speaking folder is about the four pieces of a
weighed decision: the question, the reasons for, a stop and *on the other hand*, and a verdict. It
covers three real decisions said back by real listeners, and a deliberate pile that nobody can sort.

The residents' association wants to lock the gate at ten, and one page arrives as a single block.
The writing folder is about two paragraphs with a topic sentence each, one for and one against. It
covers a real proposal written fairly for both sides and checked with the cover test, and an against
paragraph that slides back to *for* halfway through.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-reasons-with-no-weight.md), play a deliberate
pile and a weighed version of one real decision to two listeners, who sort what they heard into for,
against and verdict. RED is when the weighed version is sorted wrongly or has no verdict heard. GREEN
is when it's sorted right and the pile isn't.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-against-paragraph-that-agreed.md), give a
deliberately slid copy and your page to two readers, who mark each sentence F or A. RED is when your
page gets any mark on the wrong side or a sentence they can't place. GREEN is when it's all F then all
A and the slid copy isn't.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The mixed pile: every reason joined with *and*.
- *On the other hand* with no stop before it.
- *So … yeah*: no verdict.
- A fake other side that isn't really against.
- Five reasons on one side, one on the other.
- Both sides in one paragraph.
- A paragraph with no topic sentence.
- *For example* followed by the reason again, not a real case.
- Your own verdict inside the two paragraphs.
- The slide: *But* inside the against paragraph, arguing for.
