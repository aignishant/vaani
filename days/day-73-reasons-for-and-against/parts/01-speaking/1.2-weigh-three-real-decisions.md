## One-line answer

Weigh three real decisions out loud, one small, one medium and one big, each with reasons for,
*on the other hand*, reasons against and a verdict. Say each to a real listener and ask them to say
back one reason on each side and your verdict. If they can say all three, your turn was clear.

## The story

Your cousin is deciding whether to move back home from the city. Everyone gives her advice at once.
She asks you to just list the good and bad for her.

You say it once, in a jumble, and she says, *Wait, which side was that?* You try again, slowly:

> Well, there are two sides. One good thing is you'd save rent. Also, you'd be near your mother.
> (stop) On the other hand, your job is in the city, and you'd have to find a new one. So, on
> balance, I'd stay one more year and save first.

She repeats it back to you, both sides and the verdict, and says she'll think about the one-year
idea. She heard a decision she could argue with, not a pile of opinions.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **for**, **against**, **the pivot**, **on balance**: from
  [part 1.1](1.1-on-one-hand-on-the-other.md).
- **say-back**: the listener says in their own words what they heard.
- **weigh**: here, think about how important each reason is.
- **reason card**: a small piece of paper with the decision at the top and two columns, *for* and
  *against*.

Different decisions need different sizes of reason, but the shape stays the same:

| Size | Example | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| small | *walk or take the bus to work?* | exercise | late in the rain |
| medium | *buy a second-hand phone?* | half price | no guarantee |
| big | *move to another city?* | a better job | far from family |

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 4 gate needs three reasons for your view and one against, answered. Weighing real
decisions is how you find reasons that matter to a listener: yours, from your life.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the fake other side**: *On the other hand, it's a bit expensive,
but not really*. Give the against side a real reason, one somebody who disagrees would say.

The second is **one side of five reasons, the other of one**. The listener hears a speech, not a
weighing. Keep them close in size.

The third is **the verdict that hides**: *so, it depends*. Choose, even if you add *but only if …*.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

People bring decisions to the person who can lay them out calmly. Doing it well makes you the one
they ask next time.

Under pressure, with a decision that's yours and that you're nervous about, you'll rush the side
you don't like. Say it at the same speed as the other.

A native listener often says back the verdict first, *so you think she should stay*, and then argues
with one reason. That's a sign your turn was clear.
