## §1 Where we are

The ward councillor walks down your lane asking about problems. One man says *everything is bad*.
The woman next door names the broken light by the temple, her daughter walking home at eight, and
what the council should do this month. The speaking folder is about those three pieces: the problem
made concrete, who it hurts, and what should change and who should do it. It covers three real
problems said to real listeners, and a deliberate general complaint that nobody can act on.

At the lost-property counter, *a black bag* matches eleven bags on the shelf. The writing folder is
about *who*, *which* and *that*, the short clause that points at the one you mean. It covers a real
note that someone uses to find things, and a broken copy with *the man which* and a clause that
points at the wrong thing.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- *Everything is bad*: a feeling, not a problem.
- A change with nobody named to do it.
- *SHOULD* pushed hard, so the opinion sounds angry.
- A change so big nobody can picture it.
- *People* where one real person belongs.
- *Which* for a person.
- *The bag that I left it*: the pronoun said twice.
- A clause far from its noun, pointing at the wrong thing.
- A clause that narrows nothing: *the plant that is green*.
- A clause on every noun, even when there's only one.
