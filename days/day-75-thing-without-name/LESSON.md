## §1 Where we are

At the hardware shop, you need the little rubber ring inside the tap, and *a thing for the tap* gets
you a spanner. The speaking folder is about four moves: what kind of thing it is, what you use it
for, what it looks like, and where it goes. Then you ask *What do you call it?* It covers five real
things you can't name, described to a guesser one move at a time, and a phone call where *that
thing* points at nothing.

At the community kitchen, a new volunteer brings the sieve when the list asked for the ladle. The
writing folder is about a definition: the group a thing belongs to, then the difference that makes
it this one. It covers a real word list tested with the word covered, and an old list where the
definitions go in circles.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- *I need a thing*: a pointing word with nothing to point at.
- The look only: *small and round and black*.
- All four moves in one breath, so the guesser can't guess.
- Choosing things you can already name.
- A listener who can see what you're describing.
- *A cooker is a thing that cooks*: the circle.
- *A kitchen thing*: a group too wide to rule anything out.
- A description with no definition in front of it.
- A group word the reader doesn't know.
- A difference that only works in your own house.
