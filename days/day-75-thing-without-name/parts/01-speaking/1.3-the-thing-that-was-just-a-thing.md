## One-line answer

Describe one real thing on a phone call twice: once deliberately with only pointing words, *that
thing, the thing, this*, and once in four moves. Ask two listeners who can't see it to name or draw
it. The pointing version gets nothing. The four-move version should get the thing.

## The story

Your sister phones from a shop. Her gas stove needs a new part, and the shopkeeper is waiting. She
says:

> It's that thing, the thing on the side. You know. The thing that goes click.

You don't know. You're not there. She's pointing at it in the shop, and you can't see her hand.

You say, *Tell me what kind of thing it is.* She tries:

> It's a kind of small knob, black plastic, on the front. You turn it to make the fire start. It
> clicks.

You say, *The knob you turn to light it, the one that clicks?* The shopkeeper hears you through the
phone, nods, and takes one off the shelf.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have heard from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **the four moves**: kind, use, look, place, from
  [part 1.1](1.1-kind-of-used-for-looks-like.md).
- **pointing word**: a word like *this*, *that* or *thing* that only works when the listener can see
  what you mean.
- **phone test**: describing a thing to someone who can't see it, and asking them to name or draw it.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

On a call, pointing words point at nothing.

| Version | Words | What a listener who can't see gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P, pointing (deliberate) | *that thing, the thing, this, you know* | nothing to picture |
| F, four moves | kind, use, look, place | a picture they can name or draw |

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a P that slips in a move**: *that thing, the plastic
one*. *Plastic* is a look. Keep P empty.

The second is **a listener who's in the room**. They see what you're looking at. Use a real call, or a
recording.

The third, in real speech, is **the sound effect in place of a word**: *the thing that goes
click-click*. It's a good extra, but on its own it's still a pointing word. Put the kind and the use
first.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Help lines, shop phone orders and calls home from a shop are full of *that thing*. The person on the
other end asks *what kind of thing?* every time. Answering that before they ask saves the call.

Under pressure, on the phone, people point with their hands out of habit, even though nobody can see.
Notice your hand moving, and put it into words.

A listener who draws the right thing from your words alone has heard a description that works
without eyes.
