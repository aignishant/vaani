## One-line answer

Make a copy of your word list with each definition deliberately broken in one way: one circular, one
with a group that's too wide, and one with a description and no definition. Run the cover-the-word
test with two readers. The broken copy gets wrong guesses. Your list should get the right ones.

## The story

The community kitchen's old word list, written years ago, is still on the wall:

> Strainer: a thing for straining.
> Tongs: a kitchen tool.
> Grater: it is made of metal and has holes.

A new volunteer is asked for the grater and brings the strainer. Both are metal, and both have
holes. She's asked for the tongs and brings a spatula, which is also a kitchen tool.

The cook takes the list down and asks you to fix it.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly write. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **definition**, **group**, **difference**, **circular**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-group-then-difference.md).
- **cover-the-word test**: hiding the word and asking a reader to guess it, from
  [part 2.2](2.2-define-three-things-for-a-stranger.md).
- **too wide**: fitting many things, not only this one.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

| Deliberate break | Example | What a reader guesses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| circular | *a strainer is a thing for straining* | nothing new, or anything with holes |
| too wide | *tongs are a kitchen tool* | any kitchen tool |
| description only | *it's made of metal and has holes* | the wrong metal thing with holes |

## Why Vaani needs it

A definition that goes in a circle looks like a definition and does nothing. In phase 5, a colleague
or customer who acts on a vague definition gets the wrong thing, and the mistake is yours. The gate
argument has to define its topic for a stranger in one clear sentence.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definition). It says a definition "Should avoid
circularity", and gives the example of defining a horse as "a member of the species equus", which
"conveys no information". It "Must not be too wide or narrow", and it "Must set out essential
attributes of the thing defined".

**Step one: make copy B** from your list in
[part 2.2](2.2-define-three-things-for-a-stranger.md). Break entry 1 so it's circular, entry 2 so
its group is too wide, and entry 3 so it has only a description. Write *deliberate* at the top.

**Step two: two readers.** Give copy B, with the words hidden, to one reader. Give your list, with
the words hidden, to a different reader. Ask each to write the word, point, or draw.

**Step three: fill the table.**

| Entry | Copy B guess | Right? | Your list's guess | Right? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| 2 | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| 3 | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** when your list gets any wrong guess. It is
**GREEN** when your list gets all three right and copy B gets at least one wrong.

**Step four: the repair.** If your list went red, look at the wrong guess. It belongs to the same
group as your thing, so your difference didn't rule it out. Add the one thing that makes yours
different, such as what it's used for, its shape or its place. Test the fixed entry on a new reader.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a reader who was there** when you wrote the list.
They know the words already. Use a stranger.

The second is **a copy B that accidentally works**: *a strainer is a bowl with holes that you pour
rice water through*. That's no longer circular. Keep B's circle closed.

The third, in real writing, is **the too-narrow definition**: *a tawa is the pan my mother uses*. It
fits only one pan in the world, and a reader in another house can't find theirs. The group and the
difference should fit every tawa, and nothing else.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Signs, labels and word lists with circular definitions are everywhere. They were written by people
who already knew the word. The reader who didn't know it gets nothing.

Under pressure, when you know a word very well, it's hard to imagine not knowing it. That's when
circles happen. Read your definition as if you'd never heard the word.

A stranger who guesses every covered word right from your list has been given definitions that do
real work.
