## §1 Where we are

On a bus, you give your customer number to the gas agency as *fifteen-forty-eight-sixty*, and the
cylinder goes to somebody else's flat. The speaking folder is about four tools for a bad line:
small chunks with pauses, numbers digit by digit, a word for each letter that blurs, and a
read-back. It covers five real details given to someone standing in real noise, and one set said
fast on purpose until a number changes.

The landlord says *I'll send the plumber tomorrow*, and you leave a note for your flatmate, who reads
it two days later. The writing folder is about reported speech: *said* or *told me*, the verb one
step back, the people changed, and *tomorrow* changed to a day. It covers a real message passing on
what three people said, and a frozen copy that sends its reader to the wrong day.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-number-that-changed.md), play a deliberately
quick and a careful version of one detail set through real noise to two listeners who write exactly
what they hear. RED is when the careful version arrives with any wrong digit or letter. GREEN is when
it arrives exactly and the quick one doesn't.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-she-said-she-will-come-tomorrow.md), give a frozen
copy and your message to two readers, telling each it was written two days ago, and ask when and who.
RED is when your message's reader gives any wrong day or person. GREEN is when they're all right and
the frozen copy's reader gets one wrong.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *Fifteen-forty*: teens and tens that swap on a bad line.
- Shouting the same fast string louder.
- Bare letters that blur: *B, D, P, T*.
- A word for every letter, even the clear ones.
- No read-back, or a read-back you don't listen to.
- A noise partner in a quiet room.
- *He said he will come tomorrow*, read two days later.
- *She said I am tired*: the person not changed.
- Speech marks around reported words.
- A report with no speaker named.
