## One-line answer

Make a copy of your message with the reports deliberately left as they were spoken: *will*, *I*,
*tomorrow*. Give it to a reader and tell them it was written two days ago. Ask *when is it
happening, and who is doing it?* The copy gives the wrong day or the wrong person. Your message
should give the right ones.

## The story

On Monday, the gas company tells your neighbour, *I'll come tomorrow at nine*. She leaves a note for
her husband, who's away until Wednesday:

> The gas man said I will come tomorrow at nine.

On Wednesday evening her husband reads it and waits in all Thursday morning. He also wonders, for a
moment, whether his wife has started working for the gas company. The gas man came on Tuesday, and
nobody was in.

The note needed three changes: *he*, *would*, and a day instead of *tomorrow*.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly write. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **reported speech**, **backshift**: from [part 2.1](2.1-said-that-and-the-step-back.md).
- **who-what-when test**: from [part 2.2](2.2-write-what-they-said.md).
- **frozen copy**: here, a copy of your message with the speakers' words left exactly as they were
  spoken, with no marks.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

| What stays frozen | Example | What a later reader gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *I* | *The gas man said I will come* | *who's coming: you?* |
| *will* | *… said he will come* | *is it still going to happen?* |
| *tomorrow* | *… come tomorrow* | the day after they read it |

## Why Vaani needs it

A written message is read later than it's written, sometimes days later. Reported speech that moves
its people, verbs and time words stays true. A frozen copy goes wrong the moment the day changes.
Meeting notes and emails in phase 5 depend on this.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Reported speech: statements](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/reported-speech-statements).
It says we "often use a tense which is 'further back' in the past", that "tomorrow changes to the
next/following day", and gives "'I'll phone you tomorrow,' he said" → "He said he'd phone me the next
day."

**Step one: make copy F** from your message in [part 2.2](2.2-write-what-they-said.md). For every
report, put back the speaker's exact *I*, verb and time word, with no speech marks. Write
*deliberate* at the top.

**Step two: the later-reader test.** Give copy F to one reader and your message to a different
reader. Tell each: *This was written two days ago.* Ask:

> When is each thing happening? Who is doing it?

**Step three: fill the table.**

| Copy | Days they gave | People they gave | Wrong answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F (deliberate) | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| your message | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** when your message's reader gives any wrong
day or wrong person. It is **GREEN** when every answer is right and copy F's reader gets at least one
wrong.

**Step four: the repair.** If your message went red, find the report they got wrong. A wrong day
usually means a time word is still in it, so replace it with the day's name or date. A wrong person
means an *I* or *you* didn't change. Fix it and give the message to a new reader, with the same *two
days ago*.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **forgetting to say *two days ago***. Without it, the
reader reads *tomorrow* as their tomorrow and happens to be right. The test depends on the gap.

The second is **copy F with speech marks**. With marks, it's correct direct speech, and a careful
reader can work it out. Leave the marks off.

The third, in real writing, is **stepping back something that's still true**: *He said the office was
on the second floor.* If it still is, *is* is fine, as the page says. A reader might wonder whether
the office has moved.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Frozen reports cause missed appointments, deliveries and calls every day, because a note is read on
a different day from the one it was written.

Under pressure, writing a note just before you leave, people copy the words still ringing in their
ears. Read your note as if you were reading it next week.

A reader who gets every day and every person right, days after you wrote it, has been given reported
speech that stays true.
