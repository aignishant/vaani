## §1 Where we are

You come home from a parent-teacher meeting and start with *she said hello, and I said hello*. Your
partner waits for the news. The speaking folder is about the three pieces of a spoken report: who
you talked to, the point in your own words with a reporting verb like *told me to* or *suggested*,
and *so* for the listener. It covers three real conversations reported to the people they affect,
and a deliberate replay that leaves the listener guessing.

Six people talk on a family call about a birthday, and the aunt who missed it gets a script. The
writing folder is about a summary in four parts: the frame, what was decided, who does what by when,
and what's still open. It covers a real summary checked by someone who was there and used by
someone who wasn't, and a script copy that hides the decision.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §7 Traps

- *And then she said, and then I said*: the replay.
- A report with no *so* at the end.
- *She said something about …*: a reporting verb that carries nothing.
- Your opinion reported as theirs.
- The same report for everyone.
- A written script in place of a summary.
- An action with no owner or no date.
- No *still open*, so the reader thinks it's all decided.
- A summary that invents a decision nobody made.
- Labels on top of a script.
