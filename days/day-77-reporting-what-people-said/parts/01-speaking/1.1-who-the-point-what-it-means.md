## One-line answer

To report a conversation out loud, say who you talked to, then the point of what they said in your
own words with a reporting verb, *she told me to*, *he suggested*, *they promised*, and then what it
means for your listener. Don't replay the whole conversation. Give the point and what happens next.

## The story

You come home from your son's parent-teacher meeting. Your partner asks how it went. The first time,
you tell it like this:

> So I went in, and she said hello, and I said hello, and she said sit down, and then she said he's
> good at maths, and then she said something about reading, and then …

Your partner is still waiting for the news.

The second time, after a breath:

> I talked to his class teacher. She said he's doing well in maths, but she's worried about his
> reading. She suggested reading with him every evening. So I think we should start tonight, after
> dinner.

Your partner nods and goes to find a book.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **quote**: say somebody's words again the way they said them, from
  [day 49](../../../day-49-what-someone-said/parts/01-speaking/1.1-he-said-and-the-voice-change.md).
- **reporting verb**: a verb that tells the listener somebody spoke, such as *said*, *asked*, *told*,
  from [day 49](../../../day-49-what-someone-said/parts/01-speaking/1.1-he-said-and-the-voice-change.md).
- **headline**: the most important thing, said first, from
  [day 55](../../../day-55-passing-on-news/parts/01-speaking/1.1-did-you-hear-then-the-headline.md).
- **report**: here, tell someone the point of a conversation they weren't in.
- **the point**: what the person really meant or wanted, in a sentence or two.
- **so for us**: what the report means for the listener: what happens next.

On day 49 you quoted people, with their words and a change in your voice. Today is different. You
give the point, not the words.

| Quoting (day 49) | Reporting (today) |
| --- | --- |
| *She said, "He should read every night."* | *She suggested reading with him every evening.* |
| exact words, voice change | the point, your words, your voice |
| good for a story | good for news someone needs to act on |

A report has three pieces:

| Piece | What it says | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| who | who you talked to | *I talked to his class teacher.* |
| the point | what they meant, with a reporting verb | *She's worried about his reading. She suggested …* |
| so for us | what it means for your listener | *So I think we should start tonight.* |

## Why Vaani needs it

At home and at work, people rely on you to pass on what a doctor, a teacher, a landlord or a manager
said. In phase 5 you'll give updates in meetings: *I spoke to the client; they asked us to …* The
point and the *so* are what the listener needs.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Reported speech: reporting verbs](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/grammar/b1-b2-grammar/reported-speech-reporting-verbs).
It gives patterns with examples, including:

> She promised to do the report by Friday.
> They advised us not to write our passwords down.
> She suggested taking a break.

and "The guide told us to wait for her in reception" and "The police officer warned us not to go in
there."

**Choose the reporting verb that carries the point.** One verb can say what the person *wanted*:

| Verb | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *told me to* | *told* + person + *to* + verb | *The doctor told me to rest.* |
| *warned* | *warned* + person + *not to* + verb | *The guard warned us not to park there.* |
| *advised* | *advised* + person + *to* / *not to* | *She advised us not to wait.* |
| *suggested* | *suggested* + *-ing* | *He suggested meeting on Monday.* |
| *promised* | *promised* + *to* + verb | *They promised to call back.* |

**Say the point in one or two sentences.** Leave out the hellos, the small talk, and who said *okay*.

**End with *so*.** *So we need to …*, *So I think …*, *So nothing changes*. The listener hears the
point landing on them.

**Say the report in your own voice.** No voice change. It's your summary, not an act.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the replay**:

> And then she said … and then I said … and then she said …

The listener has to find the point themselves, and usually can't.

The second is **the point with no *so***: you report everything well and stop. The listener asks *so
what do we do?*

The third is **your opinion hidden as theirs**: *She said he's lazy*, when she said he's *tired in
the mornings*. Reporting means keeping their point, even when you'd put it differently. Your view
goes after *so*.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Families and teams run on reports like this: what the doctor said, what the bank said, what the boss
said. The people who report well are asked to go to the next meeting.

Under pressure, just back from something stressful, you replay it because it's still in your head in
order. Take one breath and ask yourself *what was the point?* first.

A native listener who answers your report with a plan, *okay, I'll call them*, has heard the point
and the *so*.
