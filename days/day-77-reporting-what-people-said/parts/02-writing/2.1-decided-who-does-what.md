## One-line answer

A written summary of a conversation isn't a script. It has four parts: one line saying who talked,
when and about what; what was decided; who will do what, by when; and what's still open. Write it in
reported speech, in the past, and leave out everything nobody needs to act on.

## The story

Your family has a long evening phone call about your grandmother's eightieth birthday. Six people
are on it. Afterwards, your aunt, who couldn't join, asks what happened. Your cousin sends her this:

> Mum said hi. Uncle said the hall is expensive. Then Priya said what about the terrace. Then Mum said
> it might rain. Then everyone talked about food …

Your aunt still doesn't know where the party is, or whether she has to do anything. You send her
this:

> **Grandma's birthday call, Sunday evening.** Six of us talked about the party.
> **Decided:** it's on the 14th, on our terrace, with a tent in case of rain.
> **Who does what:** Uncle Ravi books the tent by Friday. Priya orders the food. You're asked to bring
> the photo album.
> **Still open:** whether to invite the neighbours. We'll decide next Sunday.

Your aunt replies: *Album. Got it.*

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **reported speech**: telling what somebody said without their exact words, from
  [day 76](../../../day-76-speaking-on-bad-line/parts/02-writing/2.1-said-that-and-the-step-back.md).
- **the most important thing first**: putting the news before the background, from
  [day 55](../../../day-55-passing-on-news/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-most-important-thing-first.md).
- **owner**: the one person responsible for a step, from
  [day 68](../../../day-68-what-might-happen/parts/02-writing/2.2-write-a-real-plan.md).
- **summary**: a short piece of writing that gives only the main points of something longer.
- **transcript**: every word, written down as it was said.
- **decision**: something the group agreed to do.
- **still open**: a question the group hasn't answered yet.

A summary and a transcript are opposites:

| A transcript | A summary |
| --- | --- |
| every line, in order | only what changes something |
| *Mum said … then Priya said …* | *Decided: …* |
| as long as the conversation | a few lines |
| for someone checking exact words | for someone who has to act |

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minutes). It describes minutes as "the instant written record
of a meeting or hearing", which may include "a list of attendees, a statement of the activities
considered by the participants, and related responses or decisions". It quotes a guideline that "the
minutes should contain mainly a record of what was done at the meeting, not what was said by the
members", and says "a verbatim report (transcript) is typically not useful". *Verbatim* means word for
word.

A family call isn't a formal meeting, but the rule is the same: what was done, not what was said.

**The four parts.**

| Part | What it says | Starts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. the frame | who talked, when, about what | a heading, or *On Sunday, six of us talked about …* |
| 2. decided | what was agreed | *Decided:* or *We agreed that …* |
| 3. who does what | one owner per action, and a date | *Ravi books the tent by Friday.* |
| 4. still open | what nobody decided yet, and when it will be | *Still open:* or *We haven't decided …* |

**Reported speech, sparingly.** Use it where *who said it* matters: *Mum said she'd pay for the cake.*
Otherwise, write the decision as a plain fact: *The party is on the 14th.*

**Dates, not *tomorrow*.** The summary will be read later.

**Short labels are fine.** *Decided:*, *Who does what:*, *Still open:* help a reader find things. Put a
colon after each label.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the transcript**: *Mum said … then Uncle said …*. Every word is true
and nothing is findable.

The second is **actions with no owner**: *Someone will book the tent.* Nobody does.

The third is **no *still open***. The reader thinks everything was decided and acts on something that
wasn't.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Family group messages, society meeting notes, and emails after work calls are all summaries. The ones
people actually read are short and have the decisions near the top.

Under pressure, straight after a long call, people write it in the order it happened. Wait a moment.
Ask yourself: *what did we decide, and who's doing what?* Write that first.

A reader who wasn't there and can act on your summary without calling anyone has been given the
right four parts.
