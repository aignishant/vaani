## One-line answer

Write a real summary of a real conversation for someone who wasn't in it. Check it twice: once with
someone who was there, who says whether anything is wrong or missing, and once with someone who
wasn't, who says what was decided and what they have to do.

## The story

After the birthday call, your cousin writes the summary for the family group. He sends it, and almost
at once your uncle replies: *I didn't say I'd book the tent, I said I'd ask about it.* Two people
have already started looking for tents.

The next time there's a call, you write the summary and send it first to one person who was on the
call. She corrects one line: *Priya orders the food* should be *Priya gets three quotes for the
food*. Then you send it to everyone. Nobody replies with a correction. Your aunt, who wasn't there,
replies: *So I bring the album on the 14th.*

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **summary**, **decision**, **still open**, **owner**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-decided-who-does-what.md).
- **check reader**: someone who was in the conversation and checks that the summary is true.
- **act reader**: someone who wasn't there and tells you what they'll do after reading it.
- **quote**: here, a price someone offers before you agree to buy.

A summary has to pass two readers:

| Reader | Question | What a pass looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| check reader, who was there | *Is anything wrong or missing?* | *No*, or one small fix |
| act reader, who wasn't | *What was decided, and what do you have to do?* | the right decision and the right action |

## Why Vaani needs it

A summary that's wrong is worse than none. People act on it. In phase 5, meeting notes are checked by
the people who were there before they go to everyone else. That's the habit this builds.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **a summary that makes a decision nobody made**. *Ravi books the tent*,
when he only said he'd ask. The check reader is there to catch this. Write *Ravi will ask about a tent*
if that's what happened.

The second is **your opinion inside the summary**: *We decided on the terrace, which is a bad idea.*
The summary reports. Your view can go in a separate message.

The third is **the frame missing**: the reader doesn't know which conversation this is. One line: who,
when, about what.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

At work, someone always writes up the call, and everyone quietly checks whether their own action is
right. Getting someone's words wrong in a summary damages trust fast.

Under pressure, when people are waiting for the summary, skip the check reader and you'll often get
one correction in front of everybody. One private check first saves the public correction.

An act reader who does the right thing without asking you anything has been given a summary that
works.
