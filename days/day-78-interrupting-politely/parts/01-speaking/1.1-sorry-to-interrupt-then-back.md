## One-line answer

To interrupt politely, wait for a breath, start with *Sorry* or *Excuse me*, say why in a few words,
make your point short, and hand the turn back. To get back on track after an interruption, yours or
someone else's, say *Anyway, as I was saying …* and pick up the last thing you said.

## The story

At a residents' meeting, one neighbour has been talking about parking for a long time. You need to
say that the water tank is leaking now. The first time, you just start talking over him:

> No, no, wait, the tank —

He talks louder. Two people look at you as if you were rude. Nobody hears *tank*.

The second time, you wait until he takes a breath:

> Sorry to interrupt, but it's urgent. The water tank on the roof is leaking right now. Sorry, please
> go on.

Someone goes up to the roof. The neighbour says, *Anyway, as I was saying, the parking …* and carries
on. Everybody got what they needed.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **turn**: your time to speak, and **hold the turn**: keep speaking without someone else taking
  over, from [day 71](../../../day-71-holding-long-turn/parts/01-speaking/1.1-well-so-the-thing-is.md).
- **warm**: sounding friendly and kind, from
  [day 65](../../../day-65-polite-intonation/parts/01-speaking/1.1-start-high-let-it-fall.md).
- **interrupt**: start speaking while someone else is still speaking.
- **barge in**: interrupt without any sign that you know you're interrupting.
- **hand back**: give the turn back to the person you interrupted.
- **back on track**: returning to what was being said before an interruption.

Interrupting and getting back are two halves of the same skill:

| Move | When | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| get in | you need to say something now | *Sorry to interrupt, but …* · *Excuse me, can I add something here?* |
| say why, short | straight after | *It's urgent.* · *I have a quick question.* |
| hand back | when you've finished | *Sorry, please go on.* |
| back on track | after someone interrupted you | *Anyway, as I was saying, …* |
| help them back | after you interrupted someone | *You were saying …?* |

## Why Vaani needs it

In phase 5 you'll be in meetings where you have to get a word in, and where people will interrupt
you. Knowing how to get in and how to get back means you'll say what you came to say, without
seeming rude and without being talked over.

## The rule

**The pages checked, all opened on 2026-09-23.**
[How to Interrupt Someone in a Nice Way](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/a/how-to-interrupt-someone-in-a-nice-way/4735900.html)
says: "when we interrupt a speaker for any reason, we almost always begin with 'Sorry,' 'I'm sorry'
or, for some kinds of interruptions, 'Excuse me.'" Its phrases include:

> Sorry to interrupt, but may I ask a question?
> Excuse me, but may I jump in here?
> May (or) Can I add something here?

[anyway](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/anyway): "Used to indicate a change of subject."
[as I was saying](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/as_I_was_saying): "Used to refer back to one's own
previous statement in a discourse", with *you were saying* as a related term.

**Wait for a breath.** Most people breathe at the end of a sentence. That's where you come in, not in
the middle of a word.

**Say *sorry* warm.** A high start and a gentle fall, the warm tune from day 65. A flat *sorry* sounds
like a formality. A warm one sounds like you mean it.

**Say why in a few words.** *It's urgent*, *quick question*, *just one thing*. It tells them the
interruption is short.

**Make your point short, and hand back.** One or two sentences, then *Sorry, please go on.*

**Getting back on track.** When someone interrupts you, let them finish. Then:

> Anyway, as I was saying, the parking …

Say the last few words you'd said before the interruption. The listeners pick up exactly where they
were.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the barge-in**:

> No, wait — but —

No *sorry*, no reason, in the middle of their sentence. They talk louder, and you sound rude.

The second is **the interruption that never ends**: *Sorry to interrupt, but* … and then you talk for
the rest of the meeting. That's taking the turn, not interrupting.

The third is **never getting back**: someone interrupts you, and you let your point go. It was
probably the point that mattered. Say *anyway, as I was saying* and finish it.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

In busy meetings, people interrupt all the time. The ones who do it well are hardly noticed. They
say *sorry*, add one thing, and hand back.

Under pressure, when something urgent needs saying, people forget the *sorry*. It's the one word that
turns a barge-in into a polite interruption. Say it even when it's urgent.

A native listener who's interrupted with *sorry to interrupt* and handed back with *please go on*
usually doesn't mind at all. Many will thank you.
