## One-line answer

The second read is a read of your own page after a gap, out loud, as if a stranger wrote it. Go
through it three times, each time looking for one thing: first *does it make sense and do its job?*,
then *does every sentence read once?*, then *spelling, capitals and full stops*. Fix the big things
before the small ones.

## The story

Late at night you write a note to your daughter's class teacher, asking for her to leave early on
Friday. You read it once, silently, and it looks fine.

In the morning, before you put it in her bag, you read it aloud at the kitchen table:

> I am writing to request that my daughter to leave early on Friday for a a family function. She will
> be collected by her by her uncle.

Two extra words, a doubled *a* and a doubled *by her*, and no time. At night your eyes read what you
meant. In the morning, out loud, you heard what you'd written. You fix it before breakfast.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **proofread**: read a page carefully to find and fix mistakes, from
  [day 41](../../../day-41-what-you-did-yesterday/parts/02-writing/2.3-i-stoped-and-tryed.md).
- **pass**: one read-through of a page looking for one thing only, from
  [day 60](../../../day-60-gate-story-from-life/parts/02-writing/2.2-write-the-page.md).
- **edit**: change a page so it does its job better: cut, move, rewrite, fix.
- **the second read**: reading your own page again after a gap, out loud, to edit it.
- **distance**: time away from a page, so you read it more like a stranger would.

The second read isn't the same as a quick check. It has three passes, from big to small:

| Pass | You're looking for | Typical fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. sense | does it do its job? is the point clear? anything extra or missing? | cut a sentence, add a missing fact, move the point up |
| 2. sentences | does each sentence read once, aloud? | split a long one, add a missing word, remove a doubled one |
| 3. surface | spelling, capitals, full stops, commas | the small marks |

Fix pass 1 first. There's no point correcting the spelling in a sentence you're about to cut.

## Why Vaani needs it

Tomorrow you'll write a hundred-and-fifty-word opinion piece, and on day 80 the gate argument.
Both will be read by someone else. From phase 5 on, every formal email and letter needs a second read
before it goes. Phase 6 ends with a proofreading checklist and a full rewrite.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[Proofreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proofreading). It says proofreaders should "get some
distance from the text before attempting to proofread it, and best not to proofread one's own text".
It describes one method where a reader "reads the text aloud literally as it appears". It
separates this from copy editing, where editors do "sentence-by-sentence analysis" to improve
"grammar, spelling, punctuation, and style".

You usually can't give your page to a professional. The second read is the nearest you can get: a
gap for distance, and your voice for *literally as it appears*.

**Make a gap.** Sleep on it if you can. If you can't, do something else first: cook, walk, make a
call.

**Read it aloud, slowly, with a finger or pen under each word.** Your voice will stop where the page
is wrong. Your eyes alone won't.

**Three passes, one thing each.** Don't fix spelling in pass 1. Mark it and move on.

**Mark changes clearly.** Cross out with one line, so you can still read it. Write the new words above.
Put a small mark in the margin next to every change.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the second read straight after writing**. Your head still holds
what you meant, and it reads that instead of what's on the page.

The second is **the silent read**. Missing and doubled words slip past the eye. The mouth trips over
them.

The third is **starting with the commas**. You polish every sentence of a paragraph that shouldn't be
there. Sense first.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Careful writers don't send a first draft of anything that matters. Reading an important email aloud
before sending it, especially one to someone senior, is a habit worth stealing.

Under pressure, with no time for a gap, even a short one helps. Stand up, get water, and read it aloud
when you sit down.

A reader who never trips on your page doesn't notice the second read at all. That's what it's for.
