## One-line answer

A hundred-and-fifty-word opinion piece has four short paragraphs: an opening that gives the topic and
your view, one paragraph for your first reason with an example, one for your second reason with an
example and the other side, and a closing paragraph that sums up. Start them with *First of all*, *In
addition* and *To sum up*. Count the words, and spend them on reasons, not on repeating your view.

## The story

Your area's community newsletter asks residents: *Should the park close at sunset?* The best letter
will be printed. A retired teacher on your street sends two lines: *The park should stay open. It is
our park.* A young father sends a page and a half about everything wrong with the council.

Neither is printed. The one that's printed is about a hundred and fifty words. It says in the first
two lines what the writer thinks. It gives one paragraph to the evening walkers and one to the
children's football, each with a real example. It admits the worry about safety in one sentence. It
ends with a clear view. People discuss it on the bench for a week.

This is a practice scene. It is not a mistake we have seen you make.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **opinion**: what you think or feel, as opposed to what happened, from
  [day 55](../../../day-55-passing-on-news/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-most-important-thing-first.md).
- **the other side**: what people who disagree with you think, from
  [day 62](../../../day-62-agreeing-disagreeing/parts/02-writing/2.1-view-reason-example-other-side-close.md).
- **word count**: how many words a piece has, counted one by one, from
  [day 58](../../../day-58-retelling-story-heard/parts/02-writing/2.2-retell-a-real-story-on-paper.md).
- **break**: where one paragraph ends and the next begins, from
  [day 57](../../../day-57-pausing-right-place/parts/02-writing/2.1-a-new-idea-a-new-paragraph.md).
- **opinion piece**: a short piece of writing that gives your view on a topic, with reasons, for
  readers who may disagree.
- **word budget**: how many of your words you plan to spend on each paragraph.

On day 62 you wrote one opinion paragraph. An opinion piece stretches the same moves across four
paragraphs:

| Paragraph | Starts | Carries | Word budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. opening | the topic, then *I think* or *In my opinion* | the topic and your view | about 25 |
| 2. reason 1 | *First of all, …* | one reason and a real example | about 45 |
| 3. reason 2 | *In addition, …* | a second reason, an example, and the other side in one sentence | about 50 |
| 4. closing | *To sum up, …* | your view again, in new words | about 30 |

The budgets are a guide, not a rule. Together they come to about a hundred and fifty. In this course,
*a hundred and fifty words* means between 140 and 160, counted.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-23:**
[An opinion essay](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing/b1-writing/opinion-essay).
Its tips say: "Write your essay in clear paragraphs. Use phrases like First of all, In addition and To
sum up to start each paragraph", and "Express your own opinion using I think, In my opinion or I
believe. Mention other viewpoints with phrases like Some people think". Its example paragraphs open:

> First of all, I think that active video games are a good way to keep fit.
> In addition, you can play them whenever you want.
> To sum up, I believe that video games are a fun and social way to keep fit.

**Paragraph 1 gives the topic and your view.** The reader should know what you think by the end of the
second sentence.

**Paragraphs 2 and 3: one reason each, with a real example.** A number, a place, a person, something
you saw. The other side goes in paragraph 3 in one sentence: *Some people think …, but …*.

**Paragraph 4 sums up.** Your view again, in new words, and nothing new.

**How to count.** Every word between two spaces is one word: *I*, *a*, *park's*, *24*. A contraction
like *don't* is one word. The title doesn't count.

**Commas.** After *First of all*, *In addition*, *To sum up* and *In my opinion* at the start of a
sentence, and before *but* when it joins two full sentences.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the view only at the end**. Three paragraphs of background, then
*So I think the park should stay open*. A newsletter reader has stopped by then.

The second is **one long block**. A hundred and fifty words with no breaks is hard to read, even when
every sentence is good.

The third is **a closing paragraph with a new reason**. The reader expects a summing up, not a
surprise. Put the new reason in paragraph 2 or 3, or leave it out.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Letters to newsletters, comments on local notices and short opinion pieces at work all fit this size.
Readers give a short piece their full attention if the view comes early.

Under pressure, with a word limit, people spend half their words on the opening. Keep the opening
short and spend your words on the reasons.

A reader who can tell a friend your view and your two reasons after one read has read a piece that
works.
