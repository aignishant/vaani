## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-take-with-no-answer.md), play a take with the
counter-argument deliberately unanswered and the gate take to two strangers, and ask what the speaker
thinks now. RED is when the gate take's stranger gets your view wrong or says you're unsure. GREEN is
when they get it right and sure. To make it go red on purpose, stop straight after the
counter-argument.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-paragraph-with-no-topic-sentence.md), show two
readers only the first line of each paragraph, from a copy with the topic sentences removed and from
the fair copy, and ask which side each argues. RED is when the fair copy's reader gets a side or the
question wrong. GREEN is when they get both sides and the question.

**And the gate itself**, which is the plan's §7 and is not restated more kindly here:

> Speaking: a recorded opinion with three reasons and one counter-argument answered.
> Writing: a two-paragraph argument, one for and one against, with a topic sentence in each.

A gate is never passed because time ran out. If it goes red, that's the gate working: name two or
three days from days 61 to 79 to re-run, and give each re-run its own `PROGRESS.md` row.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A question nobody disagrees with, so the counter-argument is weak.
- Three reasons that are really one.
- A straw man: a version of the other side nobody really holds.
- A counter-argument said and never answered.
- An answer that attacks, with no reason in it.
- Stopping and restarting the take, or editing takes together.
- A transcript written from memory.
- An *against* paragraph with no real example.
- The slide: *But …* inside the against paragraph.
- A topic sentence that's an example, or that promises both sides.
- A third paragraph with your own opinion.
- A fair copy that's checked and never given to anybody.
