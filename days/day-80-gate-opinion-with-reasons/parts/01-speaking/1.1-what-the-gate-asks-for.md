## One-line answer

The phase 4 speaking gate is a recorded opinion with three reasons and one counter-argument answered.
This part teaches nothing new, because a gate that teaches isn't a gate. It sets up the recording so
that when you press record, there's nothing left to decide.

## The story

At a family wedding lunch, your uncle leans across the table and says:

> So, what do you think? Should your cousin take the job in the city, or stay here?

Everyone at the table has an opinion, and several of them disagree with yours. Your aunt has already
said the city is too expensive. You have one turn, and the people who disagree are listening for
the weak spot.

That's today. Nineteen days of this folder have taught the pieces: the opener, agreeing and
disagreeing, plans, experiences, the warm voice, advice, suggestions, what might happen, processes,
explaining, the long turn, dreams, for and against, problems, words you don't know, the bad line,
reporting, interrupting, and the frame for any topic. None of it is repeated here, and none of it is
made easier.

**This is a rehearsal, and it says so.** This day was written before your error ledger had rows in
it. Until the `docs/ERRORS.md` re-read on the checklist is ticked, the checks below are the general
ones rather than your own recurring mistakes, and the gate is not yet a gate.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **gate**: the check at the end of a phase, which the whole phase was for, from
  [day 20](../../../day-20-gate-self-introduction/parts/01-speaking/1.1-what-the-gate-asks.md).
- **take**: one recording, from start to finish, from
  [day 20](../../../day-20-gate-self-introduction/parts/01-speaking/1.2-record-the-introduction.md).
- **transcript**: every word of a recording, written down as it was said, from
  [day 60](../../../day-60-gate-story-from-life/parts/01-speaking/1.1-what-the-gate-asks-for.md).
- **opinion**: what you think about something, which other people might not share, from
  [day 61](../../../day-61-giving-opinion/parts/01-speaking/1.1-opener-reason-example.md).
- **the pivot**: the moment you turn from one side to the other, from
  [day 73](../../../day-73-reasons-for-and-against/parts/01-speaking/1.1-on-one-hand-on-the-other.md).
- **counter-argument**: the strongest reason someone who disagrees with you would give.
- **answer** (a counter-argument): say why your view still stands, after saying their reason fairly.

The gate wording is in the plan's §7, and it's copied here without a word changed:

> Speaking: a recorded opinion with three reasons and one counter-argument answered.

Four conditions, and all four can be checked.

| Condition | What it means | How it's checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| recorded | one take, kept, not edited together | the file exists, with a `RECORDINGS.md` row |
| an opinion | your view on a real question, stated | a stranger says your view back in one sentence |
| three reasons | three different reasons, each one yours | the transcript shows three; a stranger can list them |
| one counter-argument answered | the other side said fairly, then your answer to it | a stranger says what the other side was, and how you answered it |

## Why Vaani needs it

This is the point of the phase. Nineteen days of opinions, reasons, plans and explaining exist so
that one view can be carried through three reasons and a real disagreement without falling apart.
Until it's recorded, nobody knows whether it can be.

Phase 5 builds on it. An interview answer is an opinion about yourself with reasons, and a meeting
is full of counter-arguments that need answering on the spot.

## The rule

There's no new rule today. What follows is the setup.

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-23.**
[Discussion](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/exams/speaking-exams/discussion). Among its
tips: "Think about your opinion before the discussion starts" and "Say what you really think about
the topic and explain why you think that." Among its *don'ts*: "Talk about things that are not
relevant to the topic."
[Opinions](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/exams/speaking-exams/opinions) lists openers
including "I think... / I don't think...", "In my opinion," and "Personally, I think...".

**Choose the question before anything else.** It must be real, and people you know must disagree
about it. Good kinds: a decision in your family, a rule where you live or work, a change in your
town, a habit people argue about. If nobody disagrees, there's no counter-argument to answer.

**Fill the gate card. Six lines, a few words each.**

| Line | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| the question and my view | `TODO(me)` |
| reason 1 + a real example | `TODO(me)` |
| reason 2 + a real example | `TODO(me)` |
| reason 3 + a real example | `TODO(me)` |
| the counter-argument, as they'd say it | `TODO(me)` |
| my answer to it, then back to my view | `TODO(me)` |

**Where the counter-argument goes.** After your three reasons, a stop and a pivot: *Some people
think …*, or *On the other hand, …*. Say it the way someone who disagrees would say it. Then your
answer, starting *But …*, and then back to your view: *So that's why I still think …*.

**What must be in the recording**, because these are what the phase taught:

- an opener before your view;
- three reasons, each with a real example, joined with markers;
- a voice that stays up until the last sentence;
- a real stop before the counter-argument;
- the counter-argument said fairly, then answered with *but*;
- your view again at the end, in new words.

**Set the phone down and don't stop it.** One take, recorded straight through. Mistakes stay in.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the question nobody disagrees with**: *Is it good to be kind?* There's
no real counter-argument, so the one you invent is weak, and a stranger hears that.

The second is **three reasons that are one**: *it's cheaper, it saves money, it costs less*. A
stranger counts one.

The third is **the counter-argument that's a straw man**: a version of the other side nobody really
holds, made easy to knock down. A stranger who disagrees with you notices at once.

The fourth is **stopping to restart**. One word goes wrong, you stop, you start again. Record through.

These are errors beginners commonly make in a first gate recording. This day was written before your
own error ledger had rows in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard you make.

## In real life

A real opinion at a family table or in a meeting has this shape more often than you'd think: a view,
a few reasons, *I know some people say …, but …*, and the view again. The gate asks you to produce
that shape on purpose.

Under pressure, the counter-argument is the part that gets dropped, because it feels like helping the
other side. Saying it fairly is what makes a listener who disagrees actually listen to your answer.

What a native listener notices is whether you heard the other side. The fair counter-argument tells
them you did.
