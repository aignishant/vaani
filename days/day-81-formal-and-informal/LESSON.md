## §1 Where we are

Your electricity bill is double what it should be. At home you say *Can you check this?* to your
brother and he does. At the office counter you say the same words, and the clerk points at a pile of
forms. The woman behind you says *Sorry to trouble you, would you mind checking this?* and gets
served. The speaking folder is about the ladder every request stands on, five real requests said
twice, and a request said in the wrong room on purpose.

The school trip fee has gone up. You message another parent, *Hi Meena! Did u see the trip thing?*,
then start writing to the head teacher the same way and stop. The writing folder is about the five
switches between those two messages, one real message written twice, and a message that changes
voice halfway through.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-request-in-the-wrong-room.md), play your
right-room versions and two deliberately wrong-room versions to two listeners and ask how the speaker
feels about the person they're asking. RED is when a listener hears your formal version as rude or
cold, or your informal one as distant. GREEN is when both right-room versions fit and at least one
wrong-room version doesn't.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-message-in-two-voices.md), give a deliberately
mixed copy and your formal message to two readers, who underline every change of voice. RED is when
your formal message has a real informal piece underlined. GREEN is when it has none and the mixed
copy's reader finds at least two of its three breaks.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Step 1, *Can you …?*, at an office counter, with no opener and no reason.
- *Would you mind to check*: after *would you mind*, the verb takes *-ing*.
- Climbing so high with family that it sounds like anger.
- Dropping down the ladder when the queue is slow.
- Four switches out of five: a formal message that ends *Thanks!!*
- One contraction hiding in the middle of a formal message.
- *Dear Mrs Anita*: a title with a first name.
- A formal version so polite that the date or the reason has gone.
- Editing the informal version instead of writing the formal one from the facts.
