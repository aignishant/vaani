## §1 Where we are

Your first morning at a new job. At the tea point, someone asks *Are you new?*, and you tell them your
town, your family and your hobby. At lunch they ask somebody else what you actually do. The speaking
folder is about the introduction a colleague really needs: your name, your role, the link between
you, and a question back. It comes in three sizes, and then you break it on purpose.

You email a college about an evening course and put *Hello* in the subject box. Two weeks later, the
office can't find it. The writing folder is about the frame every formal email sits in: the subject
line, the greeting, the reason line, the closing line and the sign-off that matches the greeting. You
write one real email in it, then break the frame on purpose.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-introduction-with-no-job-in-it.md), two
listeners hear your personal copy, your rushed copy and your real introduction, and say back the name
and the role of each. RED is when either listener can't give your name and role from your real one.
GREEN is when both can, and the deliberate copies lose at least one.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-email-in-the-wrong-frame.md), two readers see
your broken copy and your real email as an inbox, pick one to open first, and say what each wants.
RED is when a reader opens the broken copy first, can't say what your real email wants, or when your
real email's greeting and sign-off don't match. GREEN is when your real email wins on all three.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The personal introduction brought to work: town, family, hobby, no job.
- Your own name, rushed, because you've said it all your life.
- A role with no team: *I'm new*, but new where?
- A link that's about you, not about them.
- One size of introduction for every room.
- *Hello* in the subject line, or no subject at all.
- *Dear Sir or Madam* with *Yours sincerely*, or a surname with *Yours faithfully*.
- The reason line buried after three sentences of background.
- An email that stops after the last fact, with no closing line.
- *URGENT* in capitals instead of the reason in words.
