## One-line answer

Build your own work introduction in three sizes. A **short** one is two moves, for going round a
table. A **middle** one is all four moves, for a colleague at the tea point. A **formal** one is four
moves one step higher, for a senior person or a visitor. Record all three, then play each one to a
listener. Ask them to tell you back your name, your role, and why they'd see you again.

## The story

At the end of your first week, the team has its Monday meeting. The manager says, *We've got a new
face, let's go round.* Eight people each say one line: *Anjali, dispatch. Farhan, I do the drivers'
rotas.* Then it's your turn, and you give the full four moves, with the question on the end. There's
a short, kind laugh. Nobody answers the question.

After the meeting, the manager's own boss, visiting from head office, stops you in the corridor:
*You're the new one in accounts?* You say *Yes*, and nothing else, because the two-move version is
still in your head.

Three rooms in one morning, and each one wanted a different size.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **role**, **link**, **question back**: from [part 1.1](1.1-name-role-link-question.md).
- **formal**, **the ladder**: from
  [day 81](../../../day-81-formal-and-informal/parts/01-speaking/1.1-the-request-ladder.md).
- **going round the table**: in a meeting, when each person introduces themselves in turn.
- **senior**: higher than you at work, such as your manager's manager.
- **tell-back test**: playing your introduction to a listener, then asking them to tell you what
  they remember.

| Size | Where | Moves | Model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short | going round the table | name + role | *Ravi, accounts. I joined on Monday.* |
| Middle | tea point, corridor, next desk | name + role + link + question | *Hi, I'm Ravi. I've just joined accounts. I think I'll be checking your forms. Which team are you in?* |
| Formal | a senior person, a visitor, a client | all four, one step up the ladder | *Good morning. I'm Ravi Kulkarni. I joined the accounts team on Monday. I'll be handling the expense claims. Have you come from head office today?* |

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **one size for every room**. It's usually the middle one, and it
sounds long round a table and too casual in the corridor with a senior person.

The second is **a role with no team**: *I'm new.* New where? *I'm new in dispatch* costs two words and
answers the question.

The third is **a link that's about you**: *I'm looking forward to learning a lot.* That's a nice
sentence, but it tells them nothing about what you'll do together. A link has *you* or *your team* in
it: *I'll be sending your team the weekly figures.*

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers pick the size from the first thing they hear. *Let's go round* means short. *Oh,
hello, are you new?* means middle. A handshake and a full name means formal. Listen to their first
line and match it.

Under pressure, with a senior person, beginners often freeze at *Yes*. The rescue is one line you
can always add: *Yes, I joined on Monday. I'm doing the expense claims.* That's the role, and it's
enough.

A native listener will often give their own introduction back in the same size you used. If you
go short, they go short. If you give a link, they usually give one too, and that's when a real
conversation starts.
