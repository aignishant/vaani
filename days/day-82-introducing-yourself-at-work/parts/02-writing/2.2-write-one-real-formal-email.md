## One-line answer

Write one complete formal email to a real office, about a real thing you need, inside the
seven-piece frame. Then run two checks. First, tick the frame piece by piece. Second, give a reader
**only the subject line** and ask what the email is about. If they can tell you without opening it,
the subject line works.

## The story

After the phone call with the admissions office, you write the email again. This time the subject
says *Evening computer course (Tuesday batch) – fees and start date*. The first line says *I am
writing to ask about the fees and the start date for the Tuesday evening computer course.* The
sign-off matches the greeting.

The reply comes the next morning, and it begins *Dear Ms Sharma, Thank you for your enquiry about the
Tuesday evening course.* The words of their answer are the same words as your subject line. They
knew what you wanted before they opened it.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **frame**, **reason line**, **closing line**, **matching pair**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-the-formal-frame-top-to-bottom.md).
- **subject line**, **greeting**, **sign-off**: from
  [day 29](../../../day-29-weather-and-small-talk/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-five-parts-of-a-friendly-email.md).
- **contraction**: two words joined with an apostrophe, such as *I'm*, from
  [day 35](../../../day-35-contractions-aloud/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-mark-that-holds-the-missing-letters.md).
- **enquiry**: a formal word for a question you send to an office.
- **subject-line test**: showing a reader only your subject line and asking what the email is about.

The body of this email is short: two paragraphs at most. What you're practising is the frame around
it, until it's automatic.

A frame you've only labelled on paper won't come out right when you're writing a real complaint on

[Unit 4: Starting and finishing emails](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/business/english-emails/unit-4-starting-finishing-emails):
the formal greetings and sign-offs, with a comma after each.
[Email writing](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/teaching-resources/teaching-adults/lesson-plans/intermediate-b1/email-writing):
"Use an informative subject line, which clearly says what the email is about".

**Step one: choose a real matter.** Something you really need from an office you don't know
personally: a course, a bill, a repair, an appointment, a document. Don't use the computer course.
Use your own.

**Step two: write the facts first.** Before any sentence, list the facts the office needs: what,
which one, any date or number, what you want them to do.

**Step three: write the email, top to bottom.** All seven pieces, in order, with no contractions.
The body is one or two short paragraphs.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the subject line that repeats the greeting's job**: *Dear sir
please help*. The subject is a label, not a sentence to the reader.

The second is **a body that's longer than the matter**, three paragraphs of background before the
request. Your reason line said why. The body only needs the details that answer *which one*,
*when* and *what next*.

The third is **the frame written from memory, with one piece missing**, and it's usually the closing
line. The email just stops after the last fact. The tick table exists to catch that.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Offices answer in the order they understand, not the order they receive. A clear subject line and a
clear reason line often mean an answer the same week.

Under pressure, for something urgent, people write *URGENT* in capitals. The etiquette page in the
same email course,
[Unit 9: Email etiquette](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/business/english-emails/unit-9-email-etiquette),
opened on 2026-09-24, warns that writing in capitals means "you are basically shouting at your
reader". Put the reason for the urgency in words instead: *Water leak at Flat 12 – repair needed
today*.

A formal reader often copies your subject line into their reply. If your subject line is good, their
reply is easy to find too.
