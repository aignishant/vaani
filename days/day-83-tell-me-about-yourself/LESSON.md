## §1 Where we are

A hotel interview. *So, tell me a little about yourself.* You start with where you were born, and by
the time you reach college, the interviewer is looking at the clock. The speaking folder is about the
answer they actually want: what you do now, one or two things from before that fit the job, and a last
sentence that lands on the job in front of you. You build it for a real advert, then break it on
purpose.

At the print shop, the owner reads your CV's first line aloud and laughs: *Everyone writes that.* The
writing folder is about a CV in five blocks, where every work line is a plain sentence someone can
picture. You write one for a real advert, then fill a copy with empty words on purpose.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-answer-that-started-at-school.md), two listeners
hear your school copy, your list copy and your real answer, and say why you'd be good at the job. RED
is when either can't give a reason from your real answer that comes from something you did. GREEN is
when both can, and at least one struggles with a deliberate copy.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-cv-made-of-empty-words.md), two readers each name
one thing the person on a CV actually did. RED is when your real CV's reader can't, or when you leave an
empty word in it unproved. GREEN is when they can, the empty copy's reader struggles, and every empty
word in your CV is gone or proved.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *I was born in …* as the first sentence of an interview.
- A list of good words: *hardworking, honest, punctual*, and no example.
- An answer with no land, so the interviewer has to guess why you applied.
- One answer used for every job.
- Reading key words as a list instead of turning them into sentences.
- *Responsible for …* instead of what you did.
- A copied introduction: *Seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation*.
- The oldest job at the top.
- Past and present mixed inside one job.
- Dates stretched to hide a gap.
