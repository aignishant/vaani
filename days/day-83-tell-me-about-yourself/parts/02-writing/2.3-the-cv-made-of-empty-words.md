## One-line answer

Make a copy of your CV and fill it with empty words on purpose. Use a copied introduction, duties
instead of doings, and a list of good adjectives. Give that copy and your real CV to two readers, and
ask each to tell you **one thing this person actually did**. The empty copy leaves them with nothing
to say. Your real CV should give them something they can picture.

## The story

The print-shop owner is hiring. He shows you two CVs from the pile on his counter.

The first says: *Dynamic and result-oriented professional with excellent communication skills.
Responsible for handling customers. Team player. Hardworking.*

The second says: *Worked the counter at a stationery shop for a year. Opened the shop at 8 on
weekdays. Took phone orders from two schools and delivered them by scooter.*

> Which one would you call? he asks. Then, before you answer: *I don't even know what the first one
> did. The second one, I know he can open a shop on his own.*

The first writer might be better at the job. You can't tell from the page.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **CV**, **plain sentence**: from [part 2.1](2.1-five-blocks-one-page.md).
- **picture test**: from [part 2.2](2.2-write-your-cv-lines.md).
- **empty word**: a word that sounds good but that anyone could write about themselves, such as
  *dynamic*, *hardworking* or *excellent*.
- **duty**: what a job was supposed to include, written as *Responsible for …*, instead of what you
  actually did.
- **one-thing test**: a reader tells you one thing the person on the CV actually did.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

| Copy | What's in it | What a reader should be able to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X, empty (deliberate) | copied introduction, duties, adjectives | nothing real |
| C, your CV | your real CV from [part 2.2](2.2-write-your-cv-lines.md) | at least one thing you did |

## Why Vaani needs it

The cover letter tomorrow and the phase 5 gate letter both point at the CV. If the CV is empty words,
the letter has nothing to point at. This part trains you to spot an empty word in your own writing
before an employer does.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[How to write a CV](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections): work history is
"what you did"; the introduction should "sum up who you are and what you hope to do".
[The STAR method](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice/the-star-method):
the Action is "the action you took", and the Result is "what happened as a result of your action".

**Step one: make copy X.** Copy your CV, and write *deliberate* at the top. Change three things:

1. Replace your introduction with a copied-sounding one: *Seeking a challenging position in a reputed
   organisation where I can utilise my skills.*
2. Replace every work line with a duty: *Responsible for …*.
3. Add a line of empty words: *Hardworking, dynamic, team player, excellent communication skills.*

Keep the headings, the dates and the job titles the same.

**Step two: the one-thing test.** Give X to one reader and C to a different reader. Ask each:

> Tell me one thing this person actually did.

| C | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**Step three: circle the empty words in C.** Read your real CV once, looking only for words anyone
could write about themselves. Circle each one. For each circle, either delete the word or add the thing
you did that proves it.

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** if C's reader can't name one real thing you
did, or if step three found an empty word you then left unproved. It's **GREEN** when C's reader names a
real thing, X's reader struggles, and every circled word in C was deleted or proved.

**Step four: the repair.** If C went red, look at your newest job. Rewrite its first line as a doing
word + a thing + a number. Then give C to a new reader.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **an X that isn't empty enough**. Beginners keep one
real line out of habit, and the reader picks it. Make every line a duty.

The second is **a reader who repeats the adjective**: *X is hardworking.* Ask: *What did they do that
was hardworking?* If the reader can't say, the test has worked.

The third, in a real CV, is **the proof that's another adjective**: *Hardworking: always gave 100%.*
That's still an empty phrase. Proof is a fact: *Worked six days a week through the festival season.*

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Empty words are often copied from a template, a friend's CV or a website. The words aren't wrong. The
trouble is that the reader has seen them so often that they skip them, and then nothing is left.

Under pressure, writing a CV the night before a deadline, beginners reach for the template. Keep your
own list of things you did from part 2.2. It's faster than a template, and it's yours.

A formal reader doesn't hold *hardworking* against you. They just don't count it. Only a thing you did
counts.
