## One-line answer

A cover letter is a short formal letter that goes with your CV. It has four paragraphs doing three
jobs. It **introduces** you and says which job and where you saw it. It **matches** you to the job,
with two things you did that fit what the advert asks for. It **closes**: it thanks them, points to
the CV and says you're available. It sits inside the formal frame, with a matching greeting and
sign-off and no contractions. It's written for one job at one place, and it names both.

## The story

Your neighbour manages a pharmacy on the main road. She's hiring a counter assistant, and she has
forty envelopes on her desk. She lets you look through a few.

Most of them hold only a CV. Some have a letter on top that says *Please find attached my resume for
your kind consideration.* One letter says:

> I am writing to apply for the post of counter assistant at your Station Road branch, which I saw
> advertised on your shop window. For two years I have worked at a busy medical store, where I
> handled prescriptions and helped older customers with their dosage instructions.

Your neighbour taps it. *This one I've already called. I knew from the first paragraph she'd done the
job before.*

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **formal**, **contraction**: careful writing for someone you don't know; two words joined with an
  apostrophe, such as *I'm*, from
  [day 81](../../../day-81-formal-and-informal/parts/02-writing/2.1-five-switches.md).
- **frame**, **reason line**, **closing line**, **matching pair**: from
  [day 82](../../../day-82-introducing-yourself-at-work/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-formal-frame-top-to-bottom.md).
- **CV**, **plain sentence**: from
  [day 83](../../../day-83-tell-me-about-yourself/parts/02-writing/2.1-five-blocks-one-page.md).
- **cover letter**: a short formal letter or email that goes with a CV and asks the employer to
  consider you. Also called a *covering letter*.
- **advert words**: the exact words a job advert uses for what it needs, such as *patients* or
  *able to work weekends*.
- **tailored**: written for one particular job, not for any job.

| Paragraph | Job | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | introduce | which job, at which place, and where you saw it |
| 2 | match | one thing you did that fits the advert, as plain sentences |
| 3 | match | a second thing, or why this particular place |
| 4 | close | thank them, point to the CV, say you're available |

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 5 gate writing is "a formal email and a cover letter, both with the correct greeting,
sign-off and no contractions". This is the cover letter. Everything from the last three days meets
here: the register, the frame and the CV.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-24.**
[How to write a cover letter](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/covering-letter)
says: "A cover letter introduces you to an employer and asks them to think about your application.
It's a short letter, usually 3 to 5 paragraphs long." For the parts, it says "Introduce yourself and
explain how you found the advertised job" and "Highlight the skills and experience you have that match
what the employer is looking for". It says "Thank the employer for considering your application. Let
them know that they can get more details from your CV." On the sign-off: "If you know the name of the
person you're writing to, you should end the letter with 'Yours sincerely'. If you've addressed the
letter 'Dear Sir or Madam', you should end the letter with 'Yours faithfully'." Its tips include
tailoring each letter to the specific role.

**The letter on the page:**

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Subject: Application – Counter Assistant, Station Road    │  (if sent as an email)
│                                                          │
│ Dear Ms Iyer,                                            │  name if you can find it
│                                                          │
│ ¶1 I am writing to apply for the post of … at … ,        │  INTRODUCE
│    which I saw advertised …                              │
│                                                          │
│ ¶2 For two years I have … . I …                          │  MATCH 1 (advert words)
│                                                          │
│ ¶3 I would particularly like to work at … because …       │  MATCH 2 / why here
│                                                          │
│ ¶4 Thank you for considering my application. My CV is    │  CLOSE
│    attached, and I am available for interview at any    │
│    time.                                                 │
│                                                          │
│ Yours sincerely,                                         │  matches the greeting
│ Anita Sharma                                             │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Paragraph 1: introduce.** The reason line from day 82, made specific: *I am writing to apply for the
post of [job title] at [place], which I saw advertised [where]*. That's one sentence, and it names
both the job and the place.

**Paragraphs 2 and 3: match.** Take the advert words, and for each one, write what you *did* that shows
it. These are your CV's plain sentences, told as a short story in full sentences with *I*. One match
per paragraph. If you have only one strong match, use paragraph 3 for why this place: something true
about them, not *your esteemed organisation*.

**Paragraph 4: close.** Thank them, point to the CV and say when you can come: *Thank you for
considering my application. I have attached my CV, and I would be glad to attend an interview at any
time.*

**The whole thing is formal.** No contractions: *I have*, not *I've*. As on day 81, that's this
course's choice. It's never wrong, and the phase 5 gate asks for it.

**Name the trap: the greeting.** Try to find a name. Look on the advert, on the shop's website, or ask
at the counter who's hiring. *Dear Ms Iyer* reads as a letter to her. *Dear Sir or Madam* reads as a
letter to anyone. If you can't find a name, *Dear Sir or Madam* with *Yours faithfully* is correct.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the one-line note**:

> Please find attached my resume for your kind consideration.

It does none of the three jobs. It doesn't say which job, it gives no match, and it doesn't close.

The second is **the CV again, in sentences**: every job you've ever had, in order. The letter picks
two things. The CV holds the rest.

The third is **the letter with no place in it**: *I am writing to apply for the vacancy in your
organisation.* Which vacancy? Which organisation? It could have been sent to forty shops, and the
reader can tell.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

A busy reader with forty envelopes may read only your first paragraph before deciding whether to
read on. So paragraph 1 names the job, and paragraph 2 starts with your strongest match.

Under pressure, applying to several jobs in a week, people write one letter and change only the
company name. The page above says to tailor each letter to the role. At the very least, change the
first paragraph and the advert words in paragraph 2.

A formal reader notices at once when the advert's words come back to them. If they asked for
*patients* and your letter says *patients*, they know you read the advert.
