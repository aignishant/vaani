## §1 Where we are

At the housing society meeting you tell everyone the tank-cleaning date. You know it perfectly, and
someone at the back still calls *Sorry, which Saturday?* The man after you says less and gets no
questions. The speaking folder is about three dials: loud to the last word, slow on the key word, and
a silent pause instead of *um*. You turn them one at a time, test them from another room, and then
let your voice run away on purpose.

On a job application you write *I worked at Sharma Electronics for three years*, and the company thinks
you've left. The writing folder is about two questions: is the time finished, or is it still true now?
You write your own work history so that its verbs tell the truth. Then you flip every tense on purpose
and watch a reader believe the wrong facts.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-voice-that-ran-away.md), two listeners score how
sure your nervous take and your confident take sounded, and write the key facts. RED is when either
misses a fact from your confident take, or scores it 3 or below. GREEN is when both get every fact and
score it 4 or 5, and the nervous take scores lower.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-job-you-seemed-to-leave.md), a reader says for each
job in your paragraph whether you're still doing it. RED is when your real paragraph's reader gets any
fact wrong. GREEN is when they get every fact right, and the flipped copy's reader gets at least one
wrong.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A falling tune mistaken for a fading voice: the pitch falls, the volume stays.
- Speeding up on the numbers, exactly where you should slow down.
- A pause so short it disappears.
- Marking every word as a key word.
- Turning all three dials at once before any one is a habit.
- *I am working here since 2022*.
- *I have completed my diploma in 2021*.
- *I worked there for three years*, when you still do.
- *I attached my CV*, with no time, in an email.
- A finished time hidden in a phrase: *when I was at college*.
