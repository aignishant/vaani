## One-line answer

The first answer to a complaint is often *no*. Practise holding your request through two *no*s without
getting louder or giving up. Say *I understand*, then say the request again, a little more fully: *I
understand, but the cooker is only three days old. I'd still like a replacement.* Record a role-play
with a friend who refuses twice, then ask them how you sounded.

## The story

Back at the shop, a different day and a different problem: the mixer you bought makes a burning smell.
The man at the counter says *We don't do replacements. You'll have to go to the service centre.*

Last time, you'd have said *Oh, OK*. This time you say:

> I understand. But I bought it here four days ago, and it's still inside the shop's return period on
> the bill. I'd still like a replacement, please.

He says *I'll have to ask the manager*. You say *Thank you, I'll wait*. When the manager comes out, you
say the three moves again, and you walk out with a new mixer.

You didn't argue. You just didn't let the request disappear.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **complaint**, **polite**, **firm**, **proof**, **request**: from
  [part 1.1](1.1-problem-proof-request.md).
- **role-play**: acting out a real conversation, with someone else playing the other person.
- **push-back**: the other person's first *no*, or the reason they give for not helping.
- **hold**: say your request again after a *no*, calmly, without dropping it.
- **escalate**: ask for someone with more power to decide, such as a manager.

The hold has three pieces, and they come back each time you hear *no*:

| Piece | Sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| Show you heard them | *I understand.* / *I see.* |
| Add one more fact | *But it's only four days old.* / *And the bill says seven days.* |
| The same request | *I'd still like a replacement, please.* |

After the second *no*, escalate, still politely: *Could I speak to the manager, please?*

## Why Vaani needs it

Every formal conversation in this phase can meet a *no*: a complaint, a negotiation on day 92, an
office on day 94. Holding a request calmly is what keeps *no* from being the end. It's also exactly
what you'll hear from the other side on day 95.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the fold**: after one *no*, *Oh, OK*. The request was fine. It just
wasn't said twice.

The second is **the climb**: each *no* makes you louder, until the third try is a shout. Firm isn't
loud. Firm is the same thing, said again, at the same volume.

The third is **arguing with their reason**: *That's a stupid rule.* Now you're discussing the rule, not
your cooker. Show you heard it, add your fact, repeat the request.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Counter staff often aren't allowed to say yes, even when they want to. Asking for the manager isn't an
attack on them. Often it's a relief. Say it kindly: *I know it's not your decision. Could I speak to
someone who can decide?*

Under pressure, with a queue behind you, the pull to fold is strongest. Step to one side and say *I'll
wait*. The queue moves, and your complaint is still open.

A native listener hears *I understand, but …* as reasonable, and a raised voice as the moment to stop
helping. Keep your volume where it was.
