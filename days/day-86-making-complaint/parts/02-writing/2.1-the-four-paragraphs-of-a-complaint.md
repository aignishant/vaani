## One-line answer

A complaint letter sits inside the formal frame and has four paragraphs. First, **why you're writing**.
Second, **what happened**: only the facts that matter, with dates and numbers. Third, **the effect**,
and anything you've already tried. Fourth, **what you want** them to do, and by when. It's firm in
content and calm in tone. Blame the problem, not the reader, and use the passive where a *you* would
sound like an accusation: *I was sent a damaged item*, not *You sent me a damaged item*.

## The story

You ordered a mixer grinder online. It arrived with a cracked jar. You phoned the helpline twice, and
both times someone said *We will look into it*. Nothing happened.

So you write. Your first draft starts *I am very very disappointed with your company* and goes on for a
page about how long you've been a customer, how your mother's birthday was ruined, and how you'll never
order again. It ends *Please do the needful*.

Reading it back, you realise it never says the order number, the date, what was broken or what you
want. Somebody at the company would have to write back to ask all four. Then the letter would go to the
bottom of the pile.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **formal**, **contraction**: careful writing for someone you don't know; two words joined with an
  apostrophe, from
  [day 81](../../../day-81-formal-and-informal/parts/02-writing/2.1-five-switches.md).
- **frame**, **reason line**, **closing line**, **matching pair**: from
  [day 82](../../../day-82-introducing-yourself-at-work/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-formal-frame-top-to-bottom.md).
- **passive**: a sentence built from *be* and the third form of the verb, so that the thing done comes
  first: *The jar was cracked.* It's from
  [day 69](../../../day-69-describing-a-process/parts/02-writing/2.1-be-plus-the-third-form.md).
- **complaint letter**: a formal letter or email telling a company or office that something went wrong,
  and asking them to put it right.
- **reference number**: the number a company uses to find your order, account or earlier complaint.
- **action**: what you want the reader to do: refund, replace, repair or explain.
- **deadline**: the date by which you want an answer or an action.

| ¶ | Job | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | why | *I am writing to complain about …* + the order or account and the reference number |
| 2 | what happened | the facts in order: when, what, what was wrong; only what matters |
| 3 | the effect | what it cost you, and what you have already done (calls, visits, dates) |
| 4 | the action | exactly what you want, and by when; copies of documents attached |

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[A letter of complaint](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/upper-intermediate-b2-writing/letter-complaint)
gives a model letter that opens *I am writing to express my dismay at the service at your Eden Hill
branch on Saturday 14 January.* and ends *I hope you can take steps to make sure this mistake does not
happen again.* before *Yours faithfully,*. Its tips say: "Focus on the most important facts. Don't give
unnecessary background information."; "Complaint letters are usually written in a formal style."; "Use
passives to be less direct and more formal, e.g. *I was served quickly*."; and "Use *Yours faithfully*
to sign off if you don't know the name of the person you're writing to."
[How to write an effective complaint letter](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2015/09/how-write-effective-complaint-letter)
says: "Describe the item or service you bought and the problem."; "State exactly what you want done and
how long you're willing to wait for a response."; "Don't write an angry, sarcastic, or threatening
letter."; "Include copies of relevant documents … Keep your originals."; and "If an account is
involved, be sure to include the account number."

**The letter, on the page:**

```text
Subject: Complaint – order 55-81727 – damaged mixer grinder

Dear Sir or Madam,

¶1  I am writing to complain about order 55-81727, which was delivered
    on 3 September.                                               WHY

¶2  When I opened the box, the large jar was cracked. The lid was
    also missing. I reported this to your helpline on 4 and 8
    September.                                                    WHAT HAPPENED

¶3  I was told both times that the matter would be looked into,
    but I have not been contacted since.                          EFFECT / TRIED

¶4  I would like a full replacement of the mixer grinder. I would
    be grateful if you could arrange this by 30 September. I have
    attached a photograph of the damage and a copy of the invoice. ACTION + BY WHEN

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully,
Anita Sharma
```

**¶1: why.** *I am writing to complain about …*, then name the thing and give the reference number. The
reader should know which file to open from the first line.

**¶2: what happened.** Dates, what, and what was wrong, in order. Leave out everything the reader
doesn't need to act: your mother's birthday stays out.

**¶3: the effect.** What you've already done, with dates. This is where the passive helps: *I was told
that the matter would be looked into* is calmer than *Your staff told me …*, and it's just as clear.

**¶4: the action.** Name one action and one date: *I would like a full refund. I would be grateful if
you could arrange this by 30 September.* Say what you've attached.

**The tone.** No capitals for shouting, no exclamation marks, no sarcasm. The anger shows in the facts:
*This is the second time I have reported this problem.*

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the letter with no action**:

> I am very disappointed with your service. Please do the needful.

*Do the needful* asks the reader to decide what you want. They'll decide on the cheapest thing, or
nothing.

The second is **the story in place of facts**: a page of background, and the order number never
appears. The page above says "Don't give unnecessary background information."

The third is **the accusation**: *Your careless staff sent me a broken product and lied to me on the
phone.* Even if it feels true, the reader stops reading as a helper and starts reading as a defender.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

A company has to find your order before it can fix it. The reference number and the subject line are
how it finds it. A complaint without them has to be puzzled over before anyone can act.

Under pressure, angry, people write the letter in one go and send it at once. Write it, then read it
the next morning before you send it. The facts will still be true, and the tone will be calmer.

A formal reader deals with many complaints. A short, factual, calm letter that ends with one clear
request and a date is the easiest one to act on. A long, angry letter has to be read twice before the
reader can find what to do.
