## One-line answer

Write a complete complaint letter about a real problem, in the formal frame, with the four paragraphs.
Then run the **act-on-it test**. Give the letter to a reader and ask them to pretend they work at the
company, and to write down four things: which order or account, what went wrong, what you want, and by
when. If they can fill all four from your letter, it's ready to send.

## The story

You rewrite the mixer grinder letter. It's now nine sentences long. You give it to your cousin, who
works in a call centre, and ask her to read it as if it had landed on her desk.

She writes on the back of an envelope: *Order 55-81727. Jar cracked, lid missing. Wants replacement. By
30 Sept.* Then she says, *I could process this in one go. Most of ours need three emails before we even
know what the customer wants.*

You send it that evening. The replacement is booked by the end of the week.

**Step two: write the letter.** The formal frame: a subject line with the reference number, *Dear Sir or
Madam* or a name, ¶1 to ¶4, a closing line, and the matching sign-off. No contractions.

**Step three: read it once for tone.** Circle any capitals used for shouting, any exclamation marks,
any *you* that blames the reader, and any sarcasm. Rewrite each circle as a plain fact, or with the
passive.

write down four things from the letter alone:

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the action that's a hope**: *I hope you will do something about this.*
The reader's *something* won't be yours. Name it.

The second is **two actions at once**: *I would like a refund or a replacement or at least an apology
and a discount on my next order.* Choose the one you really want. A second choice can come in the reply.

The third is **a deadline that's a threat**: *If I do not hear from you by Friday, I will take legal
action.* Save that for a real third letter. In the first, a date is enough.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Many companies now use a web form instead of an email address. The same four paragraphs work in a form.
Paste them into the box, and put the reference number in the first line.

Under pressure, people complain on social media first. A public post might get attention, but it rarely
has room for the order number, the dates and the action. Send the letter as well.

A formal reader processes the complaint they can understand first. A letter that fills the four boxes
of the act-on-it test is a letter they can finish in one go.
