## One-line answer

Meeting notes are written for the person who **wasn't there**. So they carry four things that person
can't guess. A **header**: what meeting, the date, who came, who couldn't. **Numbered items**, one per
topic, each with what was decided. An **actions table**: what, who, by when. And the **next meeting**.
Someone who missed the meeting should be able to read the notes and know exactly what to do, without
asking anybody.

## The story

You're on the committee of your local cricket club. The secretary is ill, so you're asked to take notes
at the meeting about the tournament. You write down what people say, as fast as you can: *Mr Iyer said
the ground is booked. Farhan said maybe we need more balls. Discussion about umpires. Ok.*

Two days later, the secretary, now better, reads your page and phones you. *So who's buying the balls?
Did anyone confirm the umpires? When's the next meeting?* You don't know. It was said, probably. It
isn't on the page.

The notes made sense to you because you were there. They were meant for someone who wasn't.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **summary** (of a conversation): what was decided, who does what, and what's still open, from
  [day 77](../../../day-77-reporting-what-people-said/parts/02-writing/2.1-decided-who-does-what.md).
- **imperative**: the verb form that tells someone to do something, such as *Buy*, *Send* or *Call*,
  from [day 32](../../../day-32-giving-instructions/parts/02-writing/2.1-the-imperative-on-paper.md).
- **date**: written in full, so nobody can misread it, from
  [day 10](../../../day-10-numbers-times-dates/parts/02-writing/2.1-writing-the-date.md).
- **meeting notes**: a short written record of a meeting: who came, what was decided and who does what.
  Formal ones are called *minutes*.
- **header**: the block at the top that says which meeting, when, and who was there.
- **apologies**: in meeting notes, the people who couldn't come and said so.
- **agenda**: the list of topics for the meeting, in order.
- **action**: a task someone agreed to do, with their name and a date. It's also called an *action
  point* or *action item*.
- **owner**: the one person responsible for an action.

On [day 77](../../../day-77-reporting-what-people-said/parts/02-writing/2.1-decided-who-does-what.md) you
summarised a conversation for people who'd been in it. Today's notes are for someone who wasn't. That
changes three things: the header, one numbered item per topic, and an actions table they can act on
alone.

## Why Vaani needs it

This phase is about writing that works in a formal place. Meeting notes are one of the first formal pages a
new person at work is often asked to write. Tomorrow's
status update is built from the same actions: what's done, what's next, who owns it.

## The rule

**The pages checked, all opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minutes) says minutes may include "the place, date, list of
people present", and that "the minutes should contain mainly a record of what was *done* at the meeting,
not what was *said* by the members". It says "a verbatim report (transcript) is typically not useful".
[Action item](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_item) defines an action item as "a documented event,
task, activity, or action that needs to take place". It lists its owner, "Who is responsible for
actively working the issue?", and its due date, "When will this issue be solved?".
[Managing meetings](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/business/business/magazine/managing-meetings)
says to "ensure that action points – tasks to be done and who will do them – are recorded in the
minutes", and that "An agenda is a list of points to be dealt with at the meeting, in the order in which
they will be discussed."

**The page:**

```text
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Riverside Cricket Club – committee meeting                    │ HEADER
│ Date: 21 September 2026   Place: the pavilion                  │
│ Present: A. Sharma (notes), R. Iyer (chair), F. Khan, M. Rao   │
│ Apologies: S. Das (secretary)                                  │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Tournament ground                                          │ ITEMS
│    The ground is booked for 12 and 13 October.                │ numbered,
│ 2. Equipment                                                  │ one per topic,
│    We need twelve new balls. F. Khan will buy them.           │ what was decided
│ 3. Umpires                                                    │
│    Not yet decided. Two names to be checked (see actions).    │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Actions                                                       │ ACTIONS
│  What                         Who        By                    │
│  Buy twelve balls             F. Khan    5 October             │
│  Ask two umpires, confirm     M. Rao     28 September          │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Next meeting: 30 September, the pavilion                       │ NEXT
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**The header.** The meeting's name, the date in full, the place, who was present and who sent apologies.
Put *(notes)* after your own name so readers know who to ask.

**The items.** Number them in the order of the agenda. Under each, write what was *decided*, not who said
what. If nothing was decided, say so: *Not yet decided.* That's a decision too, and the reader needs it.

**The actions table.** Every action has a verb, one owner and a date. Start the *What* column with a verb,
as an instruction: *Buy*, *Ask*, *Send*. One owner, never *everyone* or *the committee*. A date, never
*soon*.

**The next meeting.** Date and place. It's the line the absent person looks for first.

**Grammar on the page.** Items are in full sentences in the past or present: *The ground is booked.* *We
agreed to …*. The *What* column uses the imperative, without a full stop, because it's a table cell, not a
sentence. Use one style for every row.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the transcript**:

> Mr Iyer said the ground is booked. Farhan said maybe we need more balls. Then there was a long
> discussion about umpires.

It's everything that was said, and nothing the reader can act on.

The second is **the action with no owner**: *Balls to be bought.* By whom? An action without a name is an
action nobody does.

The third is **the header with no date and no apologies**. The notes get filed, and months later nobody
knows which meeting they were from, or why the secretary didn't object.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

At work, notes are often sent by email soon after the meeting. Many readers open them only to find the
actions, so some teams put the actions table first and the items after. Either
works, as long as the actions are easy to find.

Under pressure, while taking notes, you'll try to write everything. Write only three things as they
happen: decisions, names and dates. Fill in the rest straight after the meeting, while you still
remember.

A reader who missed the meeting reads the actions table and looks for their own name. If it's there,
with a date, they know what to do. If it isn't there, they assume they have nothing to do.
