## One-line answer

Make a copy of your meeting notes that only you could read, on purpose. Take out the header, turn the
items into a transcript of who said what, and give the actions no owner and no date. Give that copy and
your real notes to two readers who weren't at the meeting, and run the absent-reader test. The broken
copy should leave them guessing. Your real notes should answer every question.

## The story

A year later, the cricket club has a dispute about who paid for the balls. The treasurer digs out the
old notes. One page from that season says:

> Balls — Farhan? Ground ok. Umpires TBC. Next time.

Nobody can say which meeting it's from, whether Farhan agreed or was only suggested, or who *TBC* was
waiting for. The person who wrote it has left the club.

Your page from the same season has a date, a list of who was there, and *Buy twelve balls — F. Khan —
5 October*. The dispute is over in one phone call.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **meeting notes**, **header**, **action**, **owner**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-notes-for-the-person-who-was-not-there.md).
- **absent-reader test**: from [part 2.2](2.2-write-notes-from-a-real-meeting.md).
- **transcript**: a record of who said what, in order, instead of what was decided.
- **TBC**: short for *to be confirmed*. It means not decided yet, but on its own it doesn't say who will
  confirm it, or when.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

| Copy | What's in it | What the absent reader should manage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P, private (deliberate) | no header; items as *X said …*; actions with no owner or date; *TBC* | guesses, or *don't know* |
| N, notes | your full notes from [part 2.2](2.2-write-notes-from-a-real-meeting.md) | every answer right |

## Why Vaani needs it

A formal page isn't formal because of long words. It's formal because a stranger can use it. This part
shows you, with your own notes, the difference between a page for yourself and a page for someone else.
The phase 5 gate email has to pass that same test.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minutes): minutes record "mainly a record of what was *done* at
the meeting, not what was *said*", and "a verbatim report (transcript) is typically not useful".
[Action item](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_item): the owner, "Who is responsible for actively
working the issue?", and the planned completion date.
[TBC](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/TBC): "Initialism of to be confirmed."

**Step one: make copy P.** Copy your notes, and write *deliberate* at the top. Then:

1. Delete the whole header.
2. Rewrite each item as who said what: *Farhan said maybe we need balls. Mr Iyer said ok.*
3. In the actions, delete every owner and every date, or write *TBC*.

**Step two: the absent-reader test, twice.** Give P to one reader and N to a different reader. Neither
was at the meeting. Ask each the same three questions as in part 2.2, and add one more:

> Which meeting is this from, and who was there?

| Question | P reader (deliberate) | N reader | True answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First action: who, by when? | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| Second item: what was decided? | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| Next meeting: when, where? | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| Which meeting, who was there? | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** if N's reader gets any of the four questions
wrong or says *don't know*. It's **GREEN** when N's reader gets all four right and P's reader misses at
least two.

**Step three: the repair.** If N went red, the missing answer points to the missing piece: the header,
a decision, an owner, a date or the next meeting. Add it and give N to a new reader.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a P reader who was at the meeting**. They'll answer
from memory. Choose readers who really weren't there.

The second is **an N with *TBC* in it**. Sometimes things really aren't decided. Write who will confirm
and by when: *Umpires: to be confirmed by M. Rao by 28 September.* That's not a failure. It's an action.

The third, in real notes, is **initials only**: *FK to buy. MR to check.* Everyone at the meeting knows
who FK is. The new treasurer next year doesn't. Use initial and surname, at least once, in the header.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Notes are read long after the meeting: when someone new joins, when there's a disagreement, when a
task is late. That reader has only your page. Write for them.

Under pressure, busy people write private notes and promise themselves to tidy them later. Later rarely
comes. The full notes take less effort straight after the meeting than at any other time.

A formal reader, such as a treasurer, an auditor or a new manager, judges a group by its notes. Clear
notes make a group look organised, even when the meeting itself was messy.
