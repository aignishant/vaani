## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-update-that-became-a-diary.md), two listeners each
hear one version of your behind-or-stuck update and say whether there's a problem, what's needed and
from whom. RED is when your real update's listener misses any of the three. GREEN is when they get all
three, and the diary's listener misses one.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-bad-news-at-the-bottom.md), two readers see only the
subject and first line of an update and say whether the work is OK and whether they must act. RED is
when your true update's reader says *fine* or *nothing to do* when there's a blocker. GREEN is when they
get both right, and the hidden copy's reader gets one wrong.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The diary: *first … then … then …*.
- The blocker mentioned last, quickly, as people turn away.
- A blocker with no ask, or an ask to nobody.
- *Mostly fine* when it's behind.
- Activities in place of results: *worked on*, *talked to*.
- No status line: *Please find below the update*.
- *On track* in the status line and a deadline problem under *Needs*.
- *Done* in the past simple with no date, or the present perfect with a date.
- A colour on its own, with no word.
- No date for the next update.
