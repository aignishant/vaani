## One-line answer

Take your *not on track* update and record it as a diary on purpose, **D**: every activity in order,
*first … then … then …*, with the blocker mumbled at the very end. Then record your real update, **U**:
headline first, blocker said plainly, with the ask. Play each to a different listener and ask one
question: *Is there a problem, and what's needed?* D should hide the problem, and U should make it
impossible to miss.

## The story

Back at the courier depot, the supervisor tells you later why van three was late. *He did say it. I
checked the recording from the morning call. It's at the very end, after lunch and the gate and
Suresh. I'd stopped listening by then. Everyone had.*

The problem was reported. It just wasn't heard. The diary had buried it.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have heard from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **update**, **headline**, **blocker**, **the ask**: from
  [part 1.1](1.1-headline-done-next-blocker.md).
- **four-box test**: from [part 1.2](1.2-three-updates-from-your-week.md).
- **diary**: here, a list of everything you did, in time order, with no headline.
- **buried**: said so late or so quietly that the listener misses it.
- **problem test**: a listener hears one update and says whether there's a problem, and what's needed.
- **RED / GREEN**: a check that failed, and a check that passed.

| Take | What's in it | What a listener should report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D, diary (deliberate) | *first … then … then …*, the blocker last and quiet | no problem heard, or no clear need |
| U, update | headline first, done, next, blocker with the ask | the problem and the need, both right |

## Why Vaani needs it

At work, a buried blocker is how small problems become late deliveries, missed deadlines and hard
conversations. In the phase 5 gate interview, a *time when something went wrong* answer that becomes a
diary loses the interviewer the same way. This part lets you hear the burial in your own voice.

**Step one: record D.** Say *deliberate* first. Take your behind-or-stuck work from part 1.2 and tell it

ask. Record U again and try a new listener.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a D that's still too tidy**. You can't help putting a
headline on it. Start D with *So, yesterday morning …* and the diary comes naturally.

The second is **a U that apologises before the headline**: *Sorry, I'm really sorry, but …*. One
*sorry* is fine. The headline must still be the first real fact.

The third, in real life, is **the half-diary**: a headline, then a diary. It's better, but the blocker
still ends up at the bottom. After the headline, go straight to the blocker if there is one.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

When things are going badly, a diary feels safer, because it shows how hard you worked. The listener
doesn't need to know how hard you worked. They need to know what's wrong and what to do.

Under pressure, in a round of updates, the person before you may give a long diary. Don't copy their
length. Short is fine, even when everyone else is long.

A native listener, especially a busy one, remembers the first sentence and the ask. Everything in the
middle fades. Put what matters at the two ends.
