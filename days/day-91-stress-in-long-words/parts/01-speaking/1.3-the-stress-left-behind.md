## One-line answer

Say three long words wrong on purpose by leaving the stress where it was in the family's first word:
*PHOtography*, *DEMocracy*, and one of your own. Put each one in a real sentence and record it as take
**L**. Record the same sentences with the right stress as take **R**. Play them to two listeners and ask
them to write down the long word they heard. L should cause wrong words, pauses or *sorry?* R should be
understood at once.

## The story

In an interview for a job at a photo lab, you're asked about your experience. You say:

> I did a course in PHOtography, and I've worked with PHOtographic equipment.

The interviewer writes something, then asks, *A course in … photographs? Taking them, or printing
them?* You explain, and it's fine, but the interview has spent a question on a word instead of on you.

The next candidate says *pho-TO-graphy* and *photo-GRA-phic*. The interviewer doesn't notice the words at
all. That's what right stress sounds like to a listener: nothing.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have heard from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **write-the-word test**: a listener hears a sentence and writes down the long word in it.

## Why Vaani needs it

In the phase 5 gate interview, you'll say the long words from your own work. An interviewer who
stumbles over one of them is spending attention on the word instead of on you. This part lets you hear
that stumble on a listener's face, on purpose, before it happens by accident.

## The rule

**The pages checked, all opened on 2026-09-24.**
[photography](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/photography): /fəˈtɒɡ.ɹə.fi/, with audio.
[democracy](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/democracy): /dɪˈmɒk.ɹə.si/, with audio.
[Stress and vowel reduction in English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_and_vowel_reduction_in_English):
in *photography*, "the first syllable now contains schwa while the second syllable contains a full
vowel".

**Step one: choose three words.** *Photography*, *democracy*, and one word from your own families in part
1.2 whose stress moved from another word in its family.

**Step two: write three real sentences**, one for each word, about your own life.

**Step three: record L.** Say *deliberate* first. Say each sentence with the stress left behind: *PHO*
in *photography*, *DEM* in *democracy*, and the wrong syllable in your own word. Keep the full vowel on
the first syllable, too. That's what really happens when the stress is left behind.

**Step four: record R.** The same sentences, with the checked stress and the weak vowel in the right
place.

**Step five: the write-the-word test.** Two listeners, one at a time. Play L and R sentences in a jumbled
order. After each, the listener writes the long word they heard, and you note any pause or *sorry?*

**The check, and how it goes RED.** This part is **RED** if either listener writes a wrong word, hesitates
or asks again for any R sentence. It's **GREEN** when both write every R word correctly without a pause,
and at least one L sentence causes a wrong word, a pause or a *sorry?*

**Step six: the repair.** If an R word went red, play it next to the dictionary page's audio. Is the loud
syllable really louder? Is the syllable before it weak? Fix it, record that sentence again, and try a new
listener.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **an L listener who hears the same sentence as R first**.
Once they've heard the right word, they'll recognise the wrong one. Play L first, or use listeners for L
who haven't heard R.

The second is **an L that sounds like a joke**. If you exaggerate too much, the listener laughs and
guesses the word from the silliness. Say L as if you meant it.

The third, in real speech, is **the half-move**: the loud syllable in the right place, and the first
vowel still full. *foh-TO-gra-fee*. It's understood, just slowly. That weak first vowel is the last
thing to learn.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

If you only have attention for one thing in a long word, give it to the loud syllable. The weak vowel
next to it matters too, but the loud syllable is what the listener hears first.

Under pressure, your first language's stress habits come back. If your language stresses the first
syllable, *PHOtography* will return when you're nervous. Know your habit, and slow down on the long word.

A native listener who hears a long word with the stress left behind often thinks of the family's other
word first: *photograph* for *PHOtography*. That's the clerk's *photograph course* from part 1.1.
