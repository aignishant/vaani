## One-line answer

In formal writing, the passive does three jobs. It writes **rules and notices** without pointing at a
person: *Payment must be made by 30 September.* It writes **reports**, where what was done matters more
than who did it: *The meter was checked on 12 September.* And it gives **careful news**: *Your
application has not been successful.* The form is always *be* + the third form, and only *be* changes:
*is made*, *was made*, *has been made*, *must be made*, *will be made*. Used for one of these three jobs,
it sounds formal. Used everywhere, it hides who is responsible.

## The story

The clinic where you work at the front desk is changing its appointment rules, and the doctor asks you
to write a notice for the waiting room. You write:

> We tell all patients you must come early for your appointment. We will cancel your appointment if you are late.
> We don't accept cash any more. Doctor is saying pay by card.

The doctor reads it and winces. *It sounds like we're shouting at them. And "Doctor is saying" — it's not
me personally, it's the clinic's rule.* A notice at the bank round the corner says: *Customers are
requested to arrive before their appointment time. Late arrivals may be asked to rebook. Payment
is accepted by card only.* It's the same rules, without pointing at anyone.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **passive**: a sentence built from *be* and the third form of the verb, so the thing done comes first:
  *The form was signed.* From
  [day 69](../../../day-69-describing-a-process/parts/02-writing/2.1-be-plus-the-third-form.md).
- **active**: the ordinary order, where the doer comes first: *The doctor signed the form.*
- **past participle**: the third form of a verb, such as *made*, *checked*, *accepted*, from
  [day 85](../../../day-85-sounding-confident/parts/02-writing/2.1-finished-or-still-going.md).
- **formal**, **contraction**: from
  [day 81](../../../day-81-formal-and-informal/parts/02-writing/2.1-five-switches.md).
- **doer**: the person or thing that does the action. Grammar books call it the *agent*.
- **notice**: a short formal text on a wall, a door or a screen, telling people a rule or a change.
- **modal**: a helper word such as *must*, *will*, *should*, *may* or *can*.

On [day 69](../../../day-69-describing-a-process/parts/02-writing/2.1-be-plus-the-third-form.md) you used
the passive to describe how something is made. Today is about where it belongs in formal writing, and
where it doesn't.

## The rule

**The pages checked, both opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Passives](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/passives) says: "We
use the passive voice to change the focus of the sentence". It also says we use it "when we prefer not to
mention who or what does the action", "so that we can start a sentence with the most important or most
logical information", and "in more formal or scientific writing". For the form: "We make the passive
using the verb *be* + past participle", and "Only the form of *be* changes to make the tense. The past
participle stays the same." Its examples include "The work will be finished next week".
[English passive voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_passive_voice) says the passive "may
therefore be used when the agent is unknown or unimportant, or the speaker does not wish to mention the
agent". It gives *Mistakes have been made* as a criticised example of avoiding responsibility.

**Job 1: rules and notices.** The rule is for everyone, so no *we* and no *you*:

> Payment must be made by 30 September.
> Visitors are requested to sign in at reception.

**Job 2: reports.** What was done, and when. The doer doesn't matter or is obvious:

> The meter was checked on 12 September. No fault was found.

**Job 3: careful news.** When the news is bad, the passive keeps it from sounding personal:

> Your application has not been successful on this occasion.

**The form: only *be* changes.**

| Time | Passive | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| now, always | *is / are* + third form | *Cash is not accepted.* |
| finished | *was / were* + third form | *The meter was checked.* |
| result now | *has / have been* + third form | *Your form has been received.* |
| future | *will be* + third form | *The lift will be repaired on Monday.* |
| rule | *must be* + third form | *Forms must be signed.* |
| advice | *should be* + third form | *Bags should be left at the door.* |

After a modal, *be* stays as *be*. The third form never changes.

**When not to use it.** If the reader needs to know *who*, name them. *The form must be signed* is fine.
*It has been decided that your hours will be reduced* hides who decided, and the reader can't argue with
nobody. The reference page's criticised example, *Mistakes have been made*, is exactly that.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the missing *be***:

> Payment must made by 30 September.
> Your form has received.

Without *be*, the sentence says the payment is doing the making, or the form did the receiving.

The second is **the wrong form after *be***: *The meter was check.* / *Cash is not accept.* After *be*,
it's always the third form: *checked*, *accepted*.

The third is **the passive that hides the doer the reader needs**: *It has been decided that the canteen
will be closed.* By whom? Who can the reader ask? Name the doer, or give a contact.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Notices, forms and official letters are where you'll see the passive most. *Applications are invited*,
*Fees must be paid*, *Your request has been received*. Reading them with the three jobs in mind makes
them easier to understand, and easier to copy.

Under pressure, writing a formal note quickly, people mix active and passive in one sentence: *Payment
must be made and we will send receipt.* That's fine. The passive is a tool for the sentence that needs
it, not a rule for the whole page.

A formal reader expects notices and reports to be impersonal, and expects bad news to be careful. What
they don't forgive is not being able to find out who's responsible. So every formal page with passives
needs one active line with a name or a contact: *Please contact the reception desk with any questions.*
