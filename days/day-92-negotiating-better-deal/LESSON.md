## §1 Where we are

A carpenter says *twelve thousand*. You say *too much, ten*, and end up paying twelve. Your neighbour
says she likes his work, names her budget, offers half the money today, and gets the handles thrown
in. The speaking folder is about the deal as a trade: give something small, ask for something back,
then say the whole deal out loud so both sides heard the same thing.

You need to start late two mornings a week. Your email tells the story first, and your manager replies
with kind words and no answer. The writing folder is about the request email that gets a yes: the ask
in the subject line and the first paragraph, a short reason, and a paragraph that answers the
reader's worries before they have them.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-thing-you-gave-for-nothing.md), two listeners
each write down the final deal from one take. RED is when the trading take's listener can't fill the
number, what's included and the date, or writes a different deal from the one you meant. GREEN is when
they write it exactly, and the giving take's listener shows a worse deal or an empty box.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-ask-in-paragraph-three.md), two readers see only
the subject line and ¶1. RED is when your real email's reader can't say the ask with its dates or
numbers. GREEN is when they can, and the buried copy's reader can't.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The number thrown back: *Too much. Ten.*
- A gap with no reason.
- A free concession: *I can pay now*, with nothing asked back.
- Round two that repeats round one, louder.
- A say-back with a question tune.
- Going below your walk-away point to end a silence.
- The story first and the ask at the bottom.
- A subject line that says *Hello* or *Request*.
- An ask with no dates or numbers.
- A ¶3 that promises what you can't keep.
- Contractions in a formal email.
