## One-line answer

Prepare one real deal on paper: what you want, what you can give, and the point where you'd walk
away. Then ask a friend to play the other side and say *no* to your first if-trade. Record it. You
offer a second trade on something else, and you finish with a say-back that names every part.

## The story

Your cousin wants to join an evening computer course. The fee is eighteen thousand, all in advance.
She has twelve now and six next month. She's nervous, so she goes into the office with one sentence
ready, *Can I pay in two parts?*

The woman at the desk says *No, sorry, it's all in advance*. Your cousin had nothing after that
sentence, so she said *OK* and left.

At home you sit with her and write three lines: *want: join this month · can give: twelve now, a
cheque for six dated next month, or join the weekend batch · walk away: if they want all eighteen
today*. The next day she tries again. The first *no* comes. She has a second trade ready:

> If I give you a cheque today for the rest, dated the fifth, could I start on Monday?

The answer is *yes*.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **if-trade**, **say-back**, **gap**: from [part 1.1](1.1-give-to-get-the-if-trade.md).
- **role-play**: acting out a real conversation, with someone else playing the other person, from
  [day 86](../../../day-86-making-complaint/parts/01-speaking/1.2-hold-the-request.md).
- **walk-away point**: the worst deal you'll still say yes to. Below it, you say *thank you* and
  leave.
- **round**: one if-trade and the answer to it.
- **deal card**: three lines on paper: what you want, what you can give, and your walk-away point.

A second round is where most deals are won, because the first answer to anything is often *no*.

## Why Vaani needs it

The phase 5 gate is an interview role-play, and an interview can end with *What salary are you
expecting?* or *Can you start next week?* Both are deals. This part is where you practise having a
second thing ready when the first answer is *no*.

## The rule

**Step one: the deal card.** Write three lines for one real deal, past, present or coming soon:

| Line | Yours |
| --- | --- |
| I want | `TODO(me)` |
| I can give (at least two things) | `TODO(me)` |
| I walk away if | `TODO(me)` |

Things you can give are often not money: time, a date, doing part of the work, paying early, telling
friends, a longer contract, taking the plainer version.

**Step two: brief your friend.** Tell them the situation and the first price. Ask them to say *no* to
your first trade, with a reason, and then to be fair after that. Don't tell them your walk-away point.

**Step three: record the role-play.** Recorder on. Two rounds:

> Round 1: warm → gap with a reason → if-trade 1 → their *no*
> Round 2: *I understand.* → if-trade 2 on something else → their answer
> Close: say-back, or *Thank you, I'll think about it* at your walk-away point

**Step four: listen back and fill the table.**

| Check | Yes / no | Where on the recording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I said a warm line before the number | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| My gap had a reason | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| Both trades had an *if* part and a *could you* part | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| Trade 2 was about something different from trade 1 | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |
| My say-back named the number, what's included and the date | `TODO(me)` | `TODO(me)` |

**Step five: your friend's verdict.** Ask them: *Did you feel you got something too?* Write their
answer down in their words.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the second round that repeats the first**, louder:

> Please, if I pay half, could you do it for less? Please?

A repeated trade is a repeated *no*. Round two needs a new *if* part.

The second is **trading below your walk-away point** because the silence got too long. Look at your
card. If the deal is under the line, say *Thank you, I'll think about it*.

The third is **a friend who's too kind** and says *yes* at once. Ask them to play it again, harder.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers keep the card in their head, not in their hand, but they have one. In a real shop or
office you can glance at your phone. That's normal.

Under pressure, people forget round two and leave after the first *no*. Say *I understand* after the
*no*. It buys you a breath to find the second trade.

Many people who sell things are allowed to move a little and expect you to ask. A calm second round
often lands on something they can say yes to, even when the price itself can't move.
