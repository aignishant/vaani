## §1 Where we are

A colleague asks you to cover her Saturday. You say *hmm, maybe, I'll see*, and she tells the manager
it's sorted. The speaking folder is about the four moves of a polite *no*: thanks, a clear *no* with a
falling tune, one short reason, and an offer only if you mean it. You practise three sizes of *no*,
then record it too soft and too cold on purpose and let listeners tell you what they heard.

Your friend turns down a job by email. Every sentence is kind, and the company sends her a contract.
The writing folder is about the written decline: the thanks and the decline sentence together in the
first paragraph, a short reason or none, and a door left open only if you mean it. Then you hide the
*no* on purpose and see what a reader decides.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-no-that-sounded-like-maybe.md), three listeners
each hear one take and say *yes*, *no* or *maybe*, and score warmth from one to five. RED is when your
real take is heard as *maybe* or *yes*, or scores 3 or below. GREEN is when it's a clear *no* at 4 or 5,
and a deliberate take does worse.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-no-nobody-found.md), two readers say *yes*, *no* or
*maybe* and underline the words that told them. RED is when your real decline's reader says *yes* or
*maybe*, or underlines something other than the decline sentence. GREEN is when they say *no* and
underline it, and the hidden copy's reader doesn't.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- *I'll see*, *maybe*, *I'll try* when you mean no.
- A bare *No.* with no thanks.
- A reason that grows into a story.
- A rising tune on *can't*.
- A new reason after every push.
- An offer you don't mean.
- A decline letter that is all thanks and no *no*.
- *Might*, *perhaps*, *at this time* inside the decline sentence.
- The decline in the last paragraph.
- An angry reason that closes a door.
- Contractions in a formal decline.
