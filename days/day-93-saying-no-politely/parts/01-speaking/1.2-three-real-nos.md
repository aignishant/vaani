## One-line answer

Say *no* to three real requests of three different sizes: a small favour from someone close, a request
at work or college, and a push from someone selling something. A friend asks each one and pushes once
after your *no*. Record all three. The first two get the four moves. The seller gets a shorter *no*,
said twice, the same way both times.

## The story

In one week your friend had three people ask her for things.

Her aunt wanted her to cook for a family lunch on a day she was working. She said *no*, but kindly, and
offered to bring sweets. Her aunt was fine with that.

Her team leader asked her to stay late on Friday. She said *no* and gave the reason, a train home, and
offered to come in early on Monday. He said *Fine*.

A man on the phone offered her a credit card. She said *No, thank you*. He said *But madam, it's free
for the first year*. She said *No, thank you* again, in exactly the same voice, and he stopped.

Three *no*s, three sizes. She didn't use the same *no* for all of them.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **the no line**, **offer (after a no)**: from [part 1.1](1.1-thanks-no-reason-offer.md).
- **push**: the other person asking again after your *no*: *Are you sure? Just this once?*
- **broken record**: saying the same short *no* again, in the same words and the same voice, when
  someone pushes.
- **size (of a request)**: how close the person is and how much the answer matters.

| Size | Who | What your no needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small, close | family, a friend, a neighbour | warmth, a reason, often an offer |
| Medium, formal | work, college, an office | thanks, a clear *no*, a reason, maybe an offer |
| Pushy, a stranger | a seller, a caller | *No, thank you.* Said again the same way if they push. No reason needed. |

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the new reason after the push**. You say *I'm busy*, they push, and
you say *Well, maybe I could move my other thing …*. The push worked, so the next push will too.

The second is **explaining to the seller**: *No, because I already have a card, and my bank is …*. Each
reason is a new thing for them to answer.

The third is **the offer you don't mean**, said to end the discomfort: *Maybe another time!* If there
won't be another time, leave it out.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers use the broken record with sellers without thinking about it. It isn't rude. It's the
clearest thing you can do.

Under pressure from someone close, the *no* is hardest. Remember that a clear *no* now is kinder than
a *yes* you'll cancel later.

A person who pushes once and hears the same calm *no* usually stops. A person who hears the answer
change keeps pushing, because they can hear that it's working.
