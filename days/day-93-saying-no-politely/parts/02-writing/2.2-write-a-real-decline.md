## One-line answer

Write one real decline in the four-paragraph shape: an offer, an invitation or a request you've had.
Then circle the decline sentence and check it three ways: it's in ¶1, its verb can only mean *no*, and
it has no *might*, *perhaps* or *at this time* softening it into a maybe. Send it if it's live.

## The story

Your neighbour's son was invited to speak at his old college's prize day. He had exams that week and
had to say no. He wrote a long, warm email, and before sending it he circled the sentence that said
*no*. It read:

> I would have been so happy to come, and perhaps it might still be possible if things change.

He looked at it again. *Perhaps it might still be possible* meant the college would keep his name on
the programme. He rewrote it:

> I am afraid I am unable to attend, as I have examinations that week.

The college wrote back the same day, thanked him, and asked him for next year.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **decline**, **the decline sentence**, **leave the door open**: from
  [part 2.1](2.1-thank-decline-reason-door.md).
- **softener**: a word that makes a statement less certain, such as *might*, *perhaps*, *possibly*, or
  *at this time*.
- **no-sentence check**: you circle the decline sentence and check its place, its verb and its softeners.

Softeners are fine in many sentences. In the decline sentence, one softener can turn *no* into *not
yet*.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Politely declining a job offer: What to say and how to say it](https://www.uc.edu/blog/decline-job-offer.html)
begins its sample with thanks: "I would like to express my sincere gratitude for offering me the
position of [Job Title] at [Company Name]." The decline comes in the next sentence.

**Step one: choose a real decline.** A job, an invitation, a meeting, a request to help, a sale. It can
be from the past.

**Step two: write it.** Use the four paragraphs:

- ¶1 thanks and the decline sentence
- ¶2 one short reason, or none
- ¶3 the door, only if you mean it
- ¶4 *Thank you again for …*

Put it in the formal frame: *Dear* + name, a comma, *Yours sincerely* with a name or *Yours
faithfully* after *Dear Sir or Madam*. No contractions.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the softener left in the decline sentence**: *At this time I am
unable to …*. The reader hears *ask me later*. If you do want them to ask later, say that in ¶3, in its
own sentence.

The second is **the thank-you that goes on for three paragraphs**. Keep the thanks to one sentence in
¶1 and one in ¶4.

The third is **a door you don't mean**: *Please do think of me for future roles*, sent to a company you
never want to hear from again. Leave ¶3 out.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

Fluent writers send a decline quickly. A fast *no* lets the other side move on, and that's the kindest
thing in the email.

Under pressure, when you feel bad, you'll want to add softeners. Add warmth to the thanks instead.

A formal reader often forwards a decline to someone else to act on. That person sees only the decline
sentence. Make sure it works alone.
