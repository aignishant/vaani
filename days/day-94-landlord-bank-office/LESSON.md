## §1 Where we are

You go to the bank to change your address. You tell the story, the clerk says *It will be done*, and
three weeks later nothing has changed and nobody has a record. The speaking folder is about four moves
that work with a landlord, a bank or any office: who you are with your reference, what you need in one
sentence, what they need from you, and a next step with a date and a number, said back. Then you leave
without the next step on purpose and hear what that costs.

Your cousin's college form comes back because her name is in small letters, her date of birth is half
there, and one answer says *For job*. The writing folder is about forms and applications: read it all
first, then block capitals, dates in the boxes' order, N/A, full sentences in the free-text box, and
the signature after the declaration. You fill a real form, then fill a copy wrong on purpose and let a
reader play the clerk.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-leaving-without-the-next-step.md), two listeners
answer four questions: what happens next, by when, who or what number, and what they'd say when they
chase. RED is when the next-step take's listener can't answer one of the four. GREEN is when they answer
all four, and the leaving take's listener leaves at least two empty.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-form-sent-back.md), two readers play the clerk and
list every reason to send a form back. RED is when your real form gets any reason. GREEN is when it
gets none, and the deliberate copy gets at least four.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- The story before the ask.
- No reference number in the first two sentences.
- Not asking *What do you need from me?*
- Leaving on *It will be done* or *We'll call you*.
- Accepting *soon* as a date.
- A chase call that starts with the story.
- Filling before reading the whole form.
- Small letters in a block-capitals box.
- A date in the wrong order for the boxes.
- An empty box instead of N/A.
- A one-word answer in a free-text box.
- A printed name in the signature box.
- Private numbers on a practice page.
