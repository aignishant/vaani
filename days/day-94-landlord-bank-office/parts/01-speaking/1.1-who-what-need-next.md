## One-line answer

At a counter or on the phone to a landlord, a bank or an office, use four moves. Say **who** you are
with your reference, say **what** you need in one sentence, ask what **they need** from you, and before
you leave, lock the **next step**: what happens, by when, and a name or reference number. Then say the
next step back to them.

## The story

You go to the bank to change the address on your account. You wait, reach the counter, and say:

> Hello, I moved house, so the letters are going to the old house, and my landlord there is not
> giving them to me, and …

The clerk listens, gives you a form, stamps something, and says *It will be done*. You say *OK, thank
you* and leave.

Three weeks later, the letters are still going to the old house. You go back. A different clerk has no
record of you. You don't know the first clerk's name, the date you went, or any number.

Your friend changed her address at the same bank the month before. She said:

> Good morning. I'm Anjali Rao, account ending 4417. I'd like to change my address. What do you need
> from me?

At the end, she asked:

> So what happens next, and when? Is there a reference number for this?

She left with a number on a slip and a date. When nothing arrived by that date, she phoned and gave the
number.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **read-back**: asking the other person to repeat what you gave them, or repeating what they gave
  you, so both know it's right, from
  [day 76](../../../day-76-speaking-on-bad-line/parts/01-speaking/1.1-slow-chunk-spell-read-back.md).
- **the ladder (of requests)**: *Can you …?*, *Could you …?*, *Would you mind …?*, from
  [day 81](../../../day-81-formal-and-informal/parts/01-speaking/1.1-the-request-ladder.md).
- **counter**: the place in a bank or office where a staff member serves you.
- **reference number**: the number an office uses to find your case, account or request.
- **document**: an official paper, such as an ID card, a bill or a rent agreement.
- **next step**: what happens after you leave, who does it, and by when.
- **lock the next step**: get the next step, a date and a reference number or name before you leave.

| Move | What it does | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Who | lets them find you | *I'm Anjali Rao, account ending 4417.* / *I rent flat 3B at number 12.* |
| 2. What | the one thing you need | *I'd like to change my address.* |
| 3. What you need | stops a second visit | *What do you need from me?* / *What documents should I bring?* |
| 4. What next | locks it | *What happens next, and when? Is there a reference number?* |

## Why Vaani needs it

These are the conversations where English costs you the most if it goes wrong: a deposit not returned,
a form lost, a second trip. On day 97 you'll say the numbers and codes in these conversations clearly.
This part gives them a place to live.

## The rule

**The page checked, opened on 2026-09-24.**
[Checking understanding](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking/a1/checking-understanding)
gives the lines you need when you miss something at a counter: "Could you say that again, please?" and
"Can you repeat that more slowly, please?"

**Move 1: who, with a reference.** Your name, and the one number they use to find you: an account, a
flat, a customer number, a case number. Offices find you by numbers, not by stories.

**Move 2: what, in one sentence.** Start with *I'd like to …* or *I'm calling about …*. The story can
come after, if they ask.

**Move 3: ask what they need.** This one question saves a second visit:

> What do you need from me?
> Do I need to bring anything else?

**Move 4: lock the next step.** Three questions, asked calmly:

> What happens next?
> When should I hear from you?
> Could I have a reference number, or your name?

**Then say it back.** *So you'll send the new card to my new address within ten working days, and the
reference is 55120.* If you've got it wrong, they'll tell you now.

**Name the trap.** At a busy counter, the moment to ask Move 4 feels rude, because the clerk is already
looking at the next person. It isn't rude. It's the question they'd ask in your place.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **the story before the ask**, as in the story. The clerk has to find the
request inside it.

The second is **no reference in Move 1**: *I have an account here*. The clerk has to ask for it, and
you may not have it with you.

The third is **leaving on *It will be done***. That's not a next step. It has no *when* and no number.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers carry the reference in their phone and say it first. They also write the next step
down before they leave the counter, even when they're sure they'll remember.

Under pressure, a queue behind you or a clerk who speaks fast, Move 3 gets skipped. That's the one
that brings you back a second time.

Staff at a counter hear hundreds of stories. They act faster on a reference and one sentence than on a
long explanation.
