## One-line answer

Prepare three real conversations: one with a landlord, one with a bank, and one with an office, such as
electricity, water, a college or a local government office. Write a card for each one: your reference,
your one-sentence ask and the documents you have. Then a friend plays each person, and you record all
three. Each one must end with a next step, a date and a reference or name, said back.

## The story

Your uncle had three things to sort out in one week. His landlord still hadn't returned his deposit.
His bank card had stopped working. And the electricity bill had his old name spelled wrong.

He wrote three small cards the night before, one for each conversation. Each had his reference number,
one sentence of what he wanted, and the papers he had.

At the end of each conversation, he asked the same three questions. He came home with three dates,
two reference numbers and one name. Two things were fixed by the dates he'd been given. For the third,
he phoned, gave the reference, and it was fixed that day.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **reference number**, **document**, **lock the next step**: from
  [part 1.1](1.1-who-what-need-next.md).
- **role-play**: acting out a real conversation, with someone else playing the other person, from
  [day 86](../../../day-86-making-complaint/parts/01-speaking/1.2-hold-the-request.md).
- **deposit**: money you pay a landlord at the start, which they give back at the end if nothing is
  damaged.
- **office card**: a small card with three lines: your reference, your one-sentence ask, and the
  documents you have.
- **working days**: Monday to Friday, not counting public holidays. Offices often give dates this way.

The shape is the same in all three places. What changes is the reference, the ask, and how formal the
person is. A landlord you know may be *Hi, Mr Das*. A bank is *Good morning*.

## Why Vaani needs it

stranger, the one who has something you need. The same calm shape carries both.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **a friend who gives the next step without being asked**. Then you
haven't practised asking. Ask them to hold it back.

The second is **a vague date accepted**: *soon*, *in a few days*, *we'll call you*. Ask once more: *By
which date should I expect it?*

The third is **the landlord said like the bank**, stiff and formal with someone you know. The shape
stays the same. The greeting can be warmer.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent speakers often end with *Thanks, that's really helpful*. Staff remember a thank-you, and it
makes the next call easier.

Under pressure, on hold for a long time or at the end of a long queue, people forget the say-back.
It's the part that catches a wrong date while you can still fix it.

When you phone again, the first thing the office will ask is your reference. Having it ready turns a
long call into a short one.
