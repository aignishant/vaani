## One-line answer

Copy the first part of your real form twice. Fill copy W wrong on purpose: small letters in a capitals
box, the date in the wrong order, one empty box, a one-word free-text answer, and your printed name in
the signature box. Fill copy R the way you did in part 2.2. Two readers play the clerk, one copy each,
and list every reason they'd send it back. W should come back with a list. R should come back with none.

## The story

Your friend's sister works at a passport help desk. She keeps a list on her desk of the reasons forms
come back:

> Name in small letters. Date the wrong way round. A box left empty. "Yes" in the "Please give
> details" box. The name printed where the signature goes. Every week, the same five.

> It's never the hard boxes, she says. It's always the easy ones, done fast.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have seen from you.

## Why Vaani needs it

A wrong form doesn't look wrong to the person who filled it. The five mistakes on the help desk list
are easy to make and hard to see in your own writing. This test shows you what a clerk sees, before a
real office does.

**Step one: make copy W.** Copy the first part of your form: name, date of birth, two more short boxes,
one free-text box and the signature. Write *deliberate* at the top. Then:

1. Write your name in small, joined-up letters in the capitals box.
2. Write your date of birth in the wrong order for the boxes.
3. Leave one box empty that should say N/A.
4. Answer the free-text box in one word.
5. Print your name in the signature box.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a reader who doesn't know what the boxes want**. Show
them the instruction list from part 2.2 before they start.

The second is **W with only one or two changes**. Make all five, or the test can't show which mistakes
a clerk catches first.

The third, in real life, is **correcting a real form with messy crossings-out**. Some offices won't
accept one. Ask for a clean copy instead.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have seen from you.

## In real life

A form sent back costs more than the time to fill it again. It costs the date: the course place, the
appointment, the deadline.

Under pressure, people fill the easy boxes fastest and the hard ones slowest. The help desk list says
the easy ones are where it goes wrong. Slow down on your name and your date of birth.

A clerk isn't judging your English. They're checking boxes against a list. Give them a form that
matches the list.
