## §1 Where we are

A woman at a phone repair counter is shouting because her phone isn't ready. Your cousin says *calm
down, it's not my fault*, and she gets louder. The owner lets her finish, says her problem back, says
sorry for the one thing that went wrong, and promises a time. She calms down. The speaking folder is
about those five moves, with your voice lower and slower than theirs. You practise one customer you can
help and one you can't, then record the defensive version on purpose.

A jar of pickles arrives broken, and the reply blames the courier. The customer posts it online. The
writing folder is about the reply to a complaint in four paragraphs: thanks with the customer's facts,
one specific sorry, a short explanation without blame, and an action with a date. You reply to a real
complaint, then write the defensive reply on purpose and see what a customer does with it.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-calm-down-and-not-my-fault.md), two listeners, as
the customer, score their anger from one to five and say what happens next. RED is when your five-move
take scores 3 or more, or leaves the next step unknown. GREEN is when it scores 1 or 2 with a known next
step, and the defensive take scores higher.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-reply-that-defended-itself.md), two readers, as the
customer, score from one to five whether they'd come back, and say what happens now. RED is when your
real reply scores 3 or below, or has no next step. GREEN is when it scores 4 or 5 with a next step, and
the defensive copy scores lower.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Cutting in before the customer finishes.
- *Calm down.*
- *It's not my fault*, or blaming a supplier.
- *It's company policy*, said as a wall.
- A voice that rises with theirs.
- *Sorry for everything*, or three *sorry*s.
- A promise you can't keep.
- A *can't* with no *can*.
- *We apologise for any inconvenience caused.*
- *This has never happened before.*
- A *but* after the sorry.
- An action with no date.
- Contractions in a formal reply.
