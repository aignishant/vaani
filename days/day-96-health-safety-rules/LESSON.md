## §1 Where we are

A new helper in a restaurant kitchen hears *Hair net. No phone. Gloves.* By Wednesday his phone is
above the fryer. The owner tells him the same rules with a reason and something to do instead, and he
never forgets them. The speaking folder is about saying a health or safety rule in three parts: the
rule, the reason, and the instead. You walk a newcomer through five real rules, then record them as
bare orders on purpose and hear what a listener keeps.

A community hall's rules sheet says *should*, *may not* and *shall*, and every family finds a way
round it. The writing folder is about the four modals of written rules: *must* for required, *must not*
for forbidden, *may* for allowed, *should* for recommended, and the two words to avoid. You write a real
rules sheet, then swap the modals on purpose and see which rules a reader skips.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-rule-with-no-reason.md), two listeners write
down the rules they remember and score from one to five how likely they are to follow them when nobody
is watching. RED is when your walk-through's listener keeps fewer than four or scores 3 or below. GREEN
is when they keep four or more and score 4 or 5, and the bare take does worse.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-should-where-must-belonged.md), two readers underline
every line they'd skip. RED is when your real sheet's reader would skip a line that isn't a *should*.
GREEN is when they skip only *should* lines, and the swapped copy's reader skips a real rule.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Rules read as a list at the door.
- A rule with no reason, or *because it's the rule*.
- A *can't* with no instead.
- A reason that blames someone.
- *You must* in speech for every small rule.
- *Should* on paper for a real rule.
- *May not* for a ban.
- *Shall*.
- *Must to* clean.
- A rule with no doer: *Must be cleaned.*
- Two rules in one line.
- Capitals and *!!!* on a rules sheet.
