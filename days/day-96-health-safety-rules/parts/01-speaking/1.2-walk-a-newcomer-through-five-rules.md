## One-line answer

Choose one real place you know well and five real rules there: at least two for safety and one for
health. Write each as rule, reason, instead. Then walk a friend through them as if it's their first day,
recorded, and answer one question they ask. At the end, ask them to tell the rules back to you.

## The story

Your friend's mother runs a small tailoring workshop with four machines. When a new girl joined, her
mother didn't give her a list. She walked her round the room, one machine at a time:

> This is where you'll sit. You need to switch this off at the wall when you leave, because it heats
> up and it can start a fire. The switch is here, the red one.
>
> You can't eat at the machines, I'm afraid. Oil from your fingers gets on the cloth and it won't come
> out. There's a table for lunch at the back.

At the end she said *Tell me the rules back*. The girl got four out of five. She'd missed the scissors
rule, so her mother said it once more.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **rule**, **safety rule**, **health rule**, **the instead**: from
  [part 1.1](1.1-rule-reason-instead.md).
- **newcomer**: someone on their first day in a place.
- **walk-through**: showing a newcomer a place by moving round it and telling each rule where it
  applies.
- **tell-it-back**: asking the newcomer to say the rules back to you, so you can hear what they kept.

A walk-through says each rule in the place it applies, next to the machine or the door. That's why it's
remembered better than a list read out at the entrance.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version is **five rules said in one breath at the door**. Say each one where it
belongs, with a pause after it.

The second is **the question answered with a bare rule**. They ask *Can I leave my bag here?*, and you
say *No.* Answer with the three parts: *I'm afraid you can't, because it blocks the fire exit. There are
hooks by the door.*

The third is **a tell-it-back you don't listen to**. If they can't say a rule back, you haven't said it
clearly yet. That's information, not their failure.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

Fluent trainers usually say the most dangerous rule first and last. It's the one they most need
remembered.

Under pressure, on a busy first day, the reasons get cut. If you have to cut, cut the instead for the
small rules. Never cut the reason for a safety rule.

A newcomer is taking in the whole place at once: names, faces, machines. That's why this part stops at
five rules. If your place has more, give the newcomer the rest on paper as well.
