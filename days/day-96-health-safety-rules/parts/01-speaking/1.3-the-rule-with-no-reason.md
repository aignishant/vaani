## One-line answer

Record your five rules twice. Take B is bare on purpose: five orders in a row, with no reasons, no
insteads and a flat voice. Take W is the full walk-through with rule, reason and instead. Play each to a
different listener. Afterwards, ask them to write down every rule they remember, and to score from one
to five *How likely are you to follow these when nobody's watching?* B should lose rules and score low.
W should keep them and score 4 or 5.

## The story

Your neighbour's son got a summer job at a warehouse. On the first morning, the supervisor read out a
list at the gate:

> Helmet at all times. No running. No phones on the floor. Lift with your knees. Report all spills.

By lunch, half the new boys had their helmets off, because it was hot and nobody said why they
mattered.

On the second morning, a different supervisor took them to the high shelves:

> Boxes fall from up there sometimes. The helmet's what stops that being a hospital visit. If it's
> too hot, the water cooler's by door three. Take your break there, and keep the helmet on.

Nobody took the helmet off after that.

This is a deliberate exercise. The wrong versions below are ones beginners commonly produce. They are
not ones we have heard from you.

## The idea in plain language

**Words to know**

- **deliberate**: done on purpose.
- **rule**, **the instead**: from [part 1.1](1.1-rule-reason-instead.md).
- **walk-through**, **tell-it-back**: from [part 1.2](1.2-walk-a-newcomer-through-five-rules.md).
- **bare rule**: a rule said as an order, with no reason and no instead.
- **keep-and-follow test**: a listener writes down every rule they remember, then scores from one to
  five how likely they are to follow the rules when nobody is watching.

| Take | What's in it | What a listener should report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B, bare (deliberate) | five orders, no reasons, no insteads, flat voice | fewer rules kept; follow score 1 to 3 |
| W, walk-through | rule, reason and instead for each; a warm tune | all or nearly all rules; follow score 4 or 5 |

## Why Vaani needs it

A bare rule sounds clear to the person saying it. They know the reasons already. Only a listener who
doesn't can show you what's lost, and the loss is both memory and willingness.

## When it breaks

The commonest wrong version of the exercise is **a take W with *because it's the rule***. That's the
bare rule again, wearing a *because*.

The second is **listeners who know the place**. They'll remember the rules because they already knew
them. Use people who've never been there.

The third, in real life, is **the rule nobody follows, repeated louder**. If a rule is broken every
day, the reason is missing or the instead is too hard. Fix those. Don't shout.

These are errors beginners commonly make. This day was written before your own error ledger had rows
in it, so none of them is a mistake we have heard from you.

## In real life

People follow safety rules for two reasons: they understand what could happen, and the safe way is
easy. The reason gives the first. The instead gives the second.

Under pressure, on a busy shift, bare rules are what people shout. That's fine in the moment. The
reasons need to be given before the busy moment comes.

A newcomer often breaks a bare rule by accident, because they didn't know what it was for. A rule with
a reason tells them what to protect, even in a situation the rule didn't cover.
