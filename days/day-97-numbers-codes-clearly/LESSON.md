## §1 Where we are

On the phone to a help line abroad, you say *one lakh twenty-four thousand*, and the agent writes
*1,024*. Your friend reads her application number without the slash and the hyphen, and the college
can't find her. The speaking folder is about the sounds that carry big numbers and codes: group words
and a weak *and*, *point* and each digit after it, *a hundred thousand* for a listener who doesn't use
*lakh*, and the spoken names of the symbols, *slash*, *hyphen*, *dot*, *at*, *underscore*. You say five
real items to a stranger who types them, then say them in one breath on purpose.

A note on the shared washing machine runs the steps together and puts *don't pull the door* after
*press start*. Someone snaps the handle off. The writing folder is about the layout of instructions and
rules: a title, numbered steps with one action each, bullets for rules under a lead-in line, and the
warning before its step. You write a real sheet and test it on a first-timer, then break the layout on
purpose.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.4](parts/01-speaking/1.4-the-code-in-one-breath.md), two listeners type your
five items from one take each. RED is when the careful take's listener gets any item wrong by one
character. GREEN is when they get all five exactly, and the fast take's listener gets at least two
wrong.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-warning-after-the-step.md), two first-timers go
through a sheet one line at a time and stop at their first mistake. RED is when your real sheet's
first-timer makes any mistake. GREEN is when they reach the end with none, and the paragraph copy's
first-timer makes at least one.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A big amount said as a digit string.
- A strong *and* that sounds like a second number.
- *Point seventy-five* instead of *point seven five*.
- *Lakh* to a listener who doesn't use it.
- A pause in the middle of a group.
- Symbols left out of a code.
- *Full stop* in an email address.
- An underscore said as *dash* or *line*.
- Reading a code off a screen at reading speed.
- Steps in a paragraph.
- A rule hidden between two steps.
- The warning after its step.
- Helping the first-timer during the test.
