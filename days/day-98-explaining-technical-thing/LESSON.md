## §1 Where we are

You show your mother how to pay a bill on her phone: *select the biller, it'll autofetch, OK?* She says
*yes*, and next month she can't find the biller. Your brother points to the word *Bills*, asks *Which
one will you press?*, and never touches the phone. The speaking folder is about the words inside an
explanation: find your own jargon, keep and name the words on the screen, replace the rest, check with
a do-question, and keep your hands off. You teach one real thing to one real beginner, then explain it
in your own jargon on purpose.

A note about the home power backup calls it an inverter, a UPS, a backup and a battery, and your cousin
goes looking for four machines. The writing folder is about technical explanation on paper: what it's
for, each term defined at first use, one word for each thing kept all the way through, and one example.
You write a real explanation for a beginner, then vary the words on purpose and see how many things a
reader counts.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-explanation-in-your-own-jargon.md), two
listeners who've never done the task explain it back and score from one to five whether they could do
it now. RED is when your plain take's listener misses a step or scores 3 or below. GREEN is when they
explain every step and score 4 or 5, and the jargon take's listener does worse.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-word-that-changed-halfway.md), two beginners say how
many things the page is about and answer three questions. RED is when your real page's reader counts
wrong or misses a question. GREEN is when they count right and answer all three, and the varied copy's
reader doesn't.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- A run of jargon that is all correct and all unknown.
- *OK? Understood?*
- Taking the phone or the machine out of their hands.
- A screen word called by a different name.
- Talking during the solo run.
- A listener from your own field.
- Jargon in the last sentence.
- Four names for one thing.
- A term defined two paragraphs after it first appears.
- A definition made of more jargon.
- A page word that doesn't match the label on the machine.

explain something technical, my own jargon is the thing I can't hear. I kept the words on the screen
