## §1 Where we are

A neighbour's talk about the water tank opens well and then runs health, cost and the law together with
*and also*, until nobody can say what the second reason was. The next month, a talk about the lift gives
each point a number, an example and a turn, and everyone can list all three. The speaking folder is
about the middle of a talk: point, example, *so*, turn. You give a full talk with an opening, three
points and a close, from a card of notes, then record the middle as a river on purpose.

Your sister starts an exam essay without a plan and ends it arguing the opposite. Her friend spends
half a page first: the question with its key words underlined, one answer sentence, three topic
sentences with example notes, and a conclusion line. The writing folder is about the essay plan. You
plan a real essay and let a reader predict it, then turn the plan into a list of topics on purpose.

Choose either folder first. Each stands on its own. Follow the parts in that folder's order.

## §5 The check that must be able to fail

**Speaking:** In [part 1.3](parts/01-speaking/1.3-the-talk-with-no-turns.md), two listeners count the
points, name them and give an example. RED is when your take with turns is counted as other than three,
or a point can't be named. GREEN is when all three are counted and named with an example, and the river
take's listener does worse.

**Writing:** In [part 2.3](parts/02-writing/2.3-the-plan-that-was-a-list-of-topics.md), two readers see
a plan with the question covered and say what the essay will argue and which side it takes. RED is when
your real plan's reader can't say the answer or names the wrong side. GREEN is when they can, and the
topic list's reader can't.

Only record what you actually heard or saw. A deliberate exercise is labelled deliberate.

## §7 Traps

- Points joined by *and also* and *anyway*.
- A point with no example.
- An example longer than its point.
- No *so* sentence to close a point.
- No pause before a turn.
- A talk read from a page.
- A card of full sentences.
- No plan.
- A plan with no answer line.
- A thesis on the fence when the question asks your opinion.
- Keeping every brainstormed idea.
- A plan in full sentences everywhere.
