# ADR-0003 — A hard ceiling of one part document per day

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-09-07

## Context

The reference curriculum this repository is shaped after runs to ninety-seven days and produces
a hub plus many part documents per day, each carrying eleven sections. That is the right shape
for a technical subject built once and defended in an interview.

It is the wrong shape here, and the learner said so directly: they did not want a hundred-plus
days with twenty documents each.

Speaking is also a different kind of skill. Reading more about intonation does not improve
intonation. Only saying it does. Volume of explanation is, past a low threshold, actively
harmful — it converts speaking practice into reading practice, which is the failure mode of
almost every English course the learner has already tried.

## Decision

**One part document per day. Two on rehearsal days. Never three.**

Parts carry seven sections rather than eleven, and three of the seven are reps rather than
explanation: `Say it now`, `Say it yours`, and the deliberate failure.

A day that appears to need three parts is two days.

## Consequences

**Coverage per day drops.** Compensated by cutting scope hard: eighty concepts, chosen against
the portfolio artifact rather than against a syllabus. Anything whose removal would not change a
recording was cut. That is why there is no day on the past perfect continuous.

**The ceiling is the single most load-bearing rule in the repository**, and the easiest to erode
— every individual day will feel like it could carry one more part. The day skill names this as
the worst violation available to it, and the depth contract puts it at rule 2.
